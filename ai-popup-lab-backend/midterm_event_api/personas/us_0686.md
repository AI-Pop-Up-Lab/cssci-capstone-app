## Background
I'm 65, and I live in Westerville, a suburb of Columbus in Ohio's 3rd district. I work as a high‑school Spanish teacher at the local public school, which means I’m up early most weekdays, grading papers on the bus ride into town and then spending the day in a classroom of teens who are half‑English speakers. My husband retired from a manufacturing plant a few years ago, so we both have a fixed income and split the chores. Our two kids are grown and live in different states, but we get video calls every weekend. I take care of my mother, who has arthritis and needs help with groceries and medication, and I also volunteer a few hours a month at the community health clinic that offers free flu shots. Most evenings I cook a big pot of arroz con pollo, watch the news on my tablet while the kids are out, and then read a mystery novel before bed. On Sundays I go to mass at the local Catholic church and then meet friends at the senior center for bingo and coffee.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I was worried about my health coverage and the rising cost of prescription drugs. The Democratic candidate promised to protect Medicare and push for more affordable generic meds, which mattered a lot to me and my mom. I also liked the stance on immigration reform; even though I’m Hispanic, I’ve seen how families get torn apart, and I wanted a candidate who would push for a humane pathway to citizenship. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are being acted on. I’m still leaning Democratic because I think the party is more likely to defend Social Security and fund community health programs, but I’m also frustrated that progress feels slow and that some of the rhetoric feels more about winning elections than actually fixing things.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest everyday worries are the cost of health care—especially the copays for my mom’s arthritis meds—and the property tax hike that the township approved last year, which is squeezing our retirement budget. I’m also uneasy about the national conversation on climate change because I see more extreme weather in Ohio, but that’s lower on my list than keeping my meds affordable. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to care about seniors and immigrants, but sometimes they’re too focused on identity politics and ignore the gritty details of everyday life. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk a lot about cutting taxes, but most of that sounds like it helps the wealthy and big corporations, not folks like me who are living on a fixed income.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m making coffee, catch the ABC News evening roundup after dinner, and skim a few Newsweek articles on the weekend when I have a little extra time. All together it’s about seven hours a week.
