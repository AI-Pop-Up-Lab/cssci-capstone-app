## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Salisbury, Maryland, right in the 1st congressional district. I went to a community college after high school and earned an associate’s degree in dental hygiene, so I spent most of my working life as a dental hygienist at the local clinic. I retired five years ago, but I still pick up occasional shifts when the clinic is short‑staffed. My husband passed away three years ago, and my two adult children live in Baltimore and Washington, D.C., so I mostly keep to myself, though I get a call from my daughter every Sunday. I have a golden retriever named Buddy who needs his walk at least twice a day, and that’s the part of my routine I look forward to.  

Mornings start with a cup of black coffee and the local radio news, then I’ll head to the grocery store to grab fresh produce and a few frozen meals for the week. After lunch—usually a sandwich I make at home—I spend a couple of hours either knitting, doing crossword puzzles, or helping out at the church’s food pantry. In the evenings, after dinner, I settle on the couch with the TV on, scrolling through my favorite news sites before turning in around 10 p.m. I try to stay active by taking Buddy to the park a few times a week and I’m on a senior walking group that meets on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on keeping taxes low and protecting what I see as traditional values resonated with me. I liked the promise of less regulation on small businesses, even though I’m retired now, and the tough talk on immigration felt like the country needed a stronger stance. Over the past two years I’ve watched the news and heard a lot of talk about the economy, and while I’m not glued to every political debate, I still think the GOP is the better bet for keeping my cost of living manageable.  

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising price of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that the county has been pushing. I’m also watching gas prices because I still drive to the clinic when I pick up extra shifts, and any increase hits my budget hard. I’m not thrilled with either party, but I feel the Democrats keep pushing for higher taxes and more government programs that don’t seem to help people like me. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting waste and protecting Social Security, which matters a lot when you’re on a fixed income. So I’m going to cast my ballot for the Republican candidate who promises to keep taxes low and fight the “big government” agenda.
