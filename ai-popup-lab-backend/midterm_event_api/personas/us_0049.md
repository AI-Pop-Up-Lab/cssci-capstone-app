## Background
I'm 49 and I work as a shift supervisor at the big-box home‑improvement store in Bentonville. I took a few courses at the community college after high school but never finished a degree, so most of what I know about the job comes from on‑the‑job training. My husband runs a small landscaping business, and we have two kids: a 17‑year‑old senior at the local high school and a 20‑year‑old who just started college out of state. Most weekdays I get up around 5:30 am, make coffee, and drop the younger one off at the bus stop before heading to the warehouse. My shift starts at 7 and runs until about 3 pm, then I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from school, help with homework, and try to fit in a quick workout at the community center. Evenings are usually family time—dinner, a bit of TV, and sometimes a church youth group meeting on Wednesdays. On weekends we do chores, run errands, and occasionally go to the farmers’ market downtown. The cost of living has crept up, so we keep a close eye on the budget, especially when the kids need new clothes or school supplies.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best represented my concerns about keeping taxes low, protecting the border, and supporting small businesses like my husband's. The Democratic platform seemed focused on big‑city issues that didn't resonate with life out here in the Ozarks. I also liked the talk about deregulating energy and agriculture, which matters for the local farms and the manufacturing plants that employ a lot of people in the district. Since then, I've watched the news and heard more talk about inflation and the national debt, which makes me nervous. Still, I think the GOP is the safer bet for keeping the economy stable and preserving the values I grew up with.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm voting. My top everyday worries are the rising price of groceries and the property tax hikes that are squeezing our household budget. I'm also watching health‑care costs because my mom needs regular medication. I see the Republican Party as the one that understands the challenges of working families in a rural‑suburban setting, while the Democrats often sound like they're trying to rewrite the rules in a way that hurts small‑town America. I don't expect miracles, but I think a conservative approach gives us the best chance to keep jobs and keep the cost of living from spiraling out of control.
