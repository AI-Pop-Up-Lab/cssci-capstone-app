## Background
I'm 55, and I live in Lake City, Minnesota, right on the edge of the Mississippi River. I work as a medical assistant at the small clinic downtown, clocking in at 8 a.m. and usually finishing around 4 p.m. My husband retired from the railroad a few years ago, so he's home most afternoons, tinkering with his old pickup and watching sports on the TV. We have two grown kids—one in Minneapolis studying law and the other in Denver working as a software developer—so the house is pretty quiet most evenings. My mother, who lives a couple of hours away in St. Paul, comes to stay with us every other weekend because she needs help with her meds and the stairs. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan to work, stop at the grocery store on the way back, and pick up a bag of frozen veggies for dinner. Sundays are church, potluck, and then a quick walk along the river trail before the week starts again. I try to keep the house tidy, but the laundry pile never seems to shrink, and I’m always on the lookout for a good deal on household supplies.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for real expansion of Medicaid and trying to keep prescription drug prices from spiraling out of control. The clinic where I work was swamped with patients who couldn’t afford their meds, and I saw firsthand how a little more coverage could make a huge difference. I also liked the candidate’s stance on funding community health centers, which directly helps people in our town. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the state legislature is handling the new health‑care proposals, and I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the ones more likely to protect the safety net I rely on.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of my prescription drugs and the property tax hike the county council approved last year—both are squeezing my budget tight. I’m also concerned about the lack of decent jobs for younger folks here; a lot of my friends’ kids end up leaving for the Twin Cities because there’s not enough work locally. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to care more about health care and education, even if they sometimes talk a lot of nonsense. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like they’re ignoring the everyday struggles of working‑class families like mine.
