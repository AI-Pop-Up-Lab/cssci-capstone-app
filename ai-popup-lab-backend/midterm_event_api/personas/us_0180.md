## Background
I'm 30, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in St. Petersburg, right in the middle of Florida's 11th district. After a couple of years at community college I landed a job as an IT support technician for a regional health‑care network. My shift starts at 8 a.m., so I usually grab a coffee from the corner bodega, hop on the bus, and spend about thirty minutes scrolling through my phone before the first ticket pops up. The work is a mix of fixing printer jams, resetting passwords, and the occasional frantic call when a doctor’s system crashes. I get home around 5 p.m., try to squeeze in a quick gym session at the community center, then cook something cheap—usually a stir‑fry with frozen veggies and rice—while catching up on a few episodes of a streaming series. I share the place with a roommate from Brazil who works as a bartender; we split rent and utilities, and we both keep an eye on the meter because the electric bill spikes in the summer. On weekends I sometimes drive out to the beach, but more often I’m at a local karaoke bar with friends, trying to improve my Mandarin and singing the occasional 90s pop hit. I’m not married, don’t have kids, and my parents live back in Miami, so I call them mostly on Sundays when I have a few free minutes.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole process felt like a circus—ads everywhere, candidates shouting over each other, and a feeling that none of them really cared about everyday folks like me. Between long work hours, a cramped budget, and trying to keep my Wi‑Fi from crashing, voting just seemed like another chore I could skip. I also felt that both parties were talking past each other, promising the same vague “jobs” and “growth” without any concrete plan for a guy who’s scrambling to pay rent and keep up with grocery prices. That cynicism hasn’t faded; unless something dramatic happens—like a major power outage that hits my building or a local scandal that directly affects my job—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out as well. My top concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing; the last thing I need is an unexpected bill that forces me to choose between food and a phone plan. As for the parties, I see Democrats as too focused on big‑picture climate talks and identity politics, while Republicans seem stuck on tax cuts that don’t trickle down to people on the lower end of the income scale. Both feel disconnected from the grind of a 30‑something tech worker trying to get by.
