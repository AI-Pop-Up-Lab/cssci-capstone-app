## Background  
I'm 30 and work full‑time as a sales associate at a big‑box home‑goods store in Metairie, just outside New Orleans. My high school diploma got me the job, and after a few years I moved into the night shift so I can take a daytime class at the community college, though I haven’t finished a degree yet. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with my younger sister; we split rent and utilities, and we both keep a small kitchen garden on the balcony to stretch the grocery budget. My days start early—around 5 a.m. I grab a coffee from the corner stand, catch a 15‑minute bus, and be on the sales floor by 7. After work I either head straight home or stop at the gym for a quick class before cooking dinner. Weekends are a mix of laundry, grocery runs, and helping my sister with her evening shift. When I have a spare hour, I scroll through social media or watch a few clips from the news outlets I follow.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two jobs, caring for my sister, and trying to keep up with rent, the whole voting thing felt like another box I couldn’t fit into. I also get the sense that the candidates were all saying the same things, and none of them seemed to care about the day‑to‑day grind of people like me. That cynicism stuck, and unless something hits close to home—like a major utility outage in my building or a sudden rent hike—I’m leaning toward sitting out the 2026 midterms too. I’m definitely not planning to cast a ballot this time; I’ll probably stay home and watch the results on TV instead.  

My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent and the rising price of groceries. Every time I go to the store I see the price tags creeping up, and the landlord’s notice about a possible increase next lease term feels like a punch to the gut. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as promising a lot of social programs but often falling short on delivering concrete help to neighborhoods like mine. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk about “law and order” and cutting taxes, but I feel they ignore the realities of a city that’s still rebuilding after the storms. Both sides feel distant, but if I had to pick one I’m more annoyed at the Democrats because I think they’ve taken my community for granted.
