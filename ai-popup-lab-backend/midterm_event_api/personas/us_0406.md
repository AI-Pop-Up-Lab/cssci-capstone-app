## Background

I'm 70, and I work as a certified nursing assistant at a small long‑term care facility in Augusta. I grew up in a bilingual household; my parents came to the U.S. from Puerto Rico when I was a kid, so Spanish is still the language we speak at family gatherings. I live in a two‑bedroom duplex that I rent from a longtime neighbor, and I keep a modest garden out back where I grow tomatoes and beans when the weather lets me. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two adult children live in Boston and Portland, so most weekends I either host my grandchildren when they can drive up or I call them on the phone. 

My day starts early—around 5:30 a.m.—because I have to be at the nursing home for the 7 a.m. shift. I spend most of the morning helping residents with bathing, dressing, and medication reminders. After my shift ends at noon, I swing by the grocery store to pick up a few things for dinner and sometimes stop at the local library to read the community bulletin board. In the evenings I either help my sister with her Spanish‑English tutoring sessions or attend the weekly senior‑center bingo night. I’m not tech‑savvy, but I do have a tablet that I use to watch the news and video chat with my kids.

## Politics and this election

I voted Democratic in 2024 because the party’s stance on Medicare and Social Security felt like the only thing that could keep my retirement income from disappearing. The candidate talked about protecting nursing home funding, which mattered to me and my coworkers. I also appreciated the emphasis on climate action for Maine’s coastal towns—my sister lives in a fishing village that’s already seeing more storms, and that hit close to home.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that my property taxes have jumped again after the town reassessed its values. I also worry about broadband; my niece tried to set up a telehealth appointment last month and the connection kept dropping, which made me nervous. I think the Democrats still have the best record on protecting seniors, but I’m frustrated by the constant infighting and the way some of the newer progressive ideas seem out of touch with folks like me who just want reliable health care and a stable bill. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about cutting taxes for big businesses and being tough on immigration, which feels like they’re ignoring the everyday struggles of older folks who are already on fixed incomes. I do respect a few Republican lawmakers who actually push for rural broadband, but overall I feel the party doesn’t understand what matters to someone who’s been working the night shift for decades.

## Media Diet

I usually listen to NPR while I’m getting dressed in the morning, scroll through CBS News on my tablet after my shift, and check ABC News headlines once before bed.
