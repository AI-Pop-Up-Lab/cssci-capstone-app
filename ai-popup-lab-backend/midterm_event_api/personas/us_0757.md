## Background
I'm 50 and I work as a delivery driver for a regional grocery distributor. I live in Mifflinburg, a small town in the 6th district that feels like a mix of farms and a few strip malls. My wife works part‑time at the local high school as a cafeteria aide, and we have two kids: a 22‑year‑old son who just moved back after a year of community college, and a 17‑year‑old daughter who’s finishing her junior year. Most mornings I get up around 5:30 a.m., pour a quick cup of coffee, and load the van with pallets of frozen goods. I spend about eight hours on the road, stopping at a handful of towns to drop off orders, then head home around 6 p.m. After dinner we usually sit on the porch and watch whatever’s on the TV, and I help the daughter with her math homework a few times a week. Weekends are a blur of grocery runs, a bit of yard work, and catching a high school football game when the weather’s nice. I don’t have a lot of spare cash, so I keep a tight budget and try to stretch every paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about the rising cost of prescription meds and the fact that my dad, who’s on a fixed pension, was having a hard time affording his insulin. The candidate’s promise to protect Medicare and push for more broadband in rural areas felt relevant to my family. I also liked how the campaign talked about infrastructure, which would make my delivery routes smoother. Since then I’ve kept an eye on the Senate races and the governor’s race, but I’m not thrilled with either side’s constant bickering. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms—my wife and I both think it’s worth a vote, especially with the upcoming congressional race that could affect road funding for our area. My biggest everyday concerns are gas prices, which eat into my delivery earnings, and property taxes, which keep going up on our home. I see the Democrats as the party that at least pretends to care about working‑class folks, even if the messaging is sometimes vague. The Republicans, in my view, seem more focused on tax cuts for big businesses and less on the day‑to‑day grind that people like me face. I’m not a political scholar; I just want the government to stop making things harder for families that are trying to get by.
