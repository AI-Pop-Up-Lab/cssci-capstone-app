## Background
I'm 45 and I live in Nampa, Idaho, right in the heart of the 2nd congressional district. I work as a sales manager for a farm‑equipment dealership on the edge of town; most of my day is spent talking to ranchers, making sure we have the right tractors and parts in stock, and handling paperwork. I went to college in Utah, got a B.A. in business, and came back to Idaho after marrying Carlos, who runs a small cattle operation just outside the city. We have two kids—Mia, 12, and Luis, 9—who both go to the local public school. Mornings are a blur of loading backpacks, dropping the kids at the bus stop, and then driving a half‑hour to the dealership. After work I usually pick up the kids, help with homework, and try to get a quick dinner on the table before we all collapse on the couch. On weekends I help Carlos with fence repairs, take the kids to the community pool, and sometimes go hunting with my brother. I’m not the type to travel far; most of my free time is spent around the house, the ranch, or the local grocery store.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party cared about the things that matter to my family: low taxes, less regulation on small businesses like the one I manage, and protecting our right to own guns. The economy felt shaky, and the Democratic message about higher taxes and more government control just didn’t sit right with me. I also liked the talk about securing the border; as a Hispanic family we’re proud of our heritage, but we want the system to work fairly for everyone.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of groceries and gas, and the ever‑increasing property taxes that make it harder to keep the ranch running. I’m also watching water‑rights legislation because if we can’t get enough water for the fields, everything else falls apart. I think the Republican Party is the one that understands what folks like me need—steady jobs, affordable living, and the freedom to run a small business without too many hoops. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with rural Idaho life. I’m not a political scholar, I just want policies that keep my family fed and my husband’s cattle healthy.
