## Background  
I'm 45 and I work as a forklift operator at a big distribution center just outside Springdale. My wife runs a small home‑based catering business, and we have two kids – a 13‑year‑old son who’s starting high school and a 9‑year‑old daughter who’s in third grade. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30, make coffee, and watch the local news on the TV while the kids get ready. I grab a quick breakfast, hop in my pickup, and drive the ten‑minute stretch to the warehouse where I clock in for a 10‑hour shift, usually with a short lunch break. After work I pick up the kids from after‑school programs, help my son with his math homework, and we all eat dinner together around 6:30. Weekends are a mix of family chores, a little yard work, and the occasional visit to my parents’ house on the reservation, where we keep up with tribal gatherings and cultural events. I don’t have a college degree, but I’ve taken a few night classes on basic computer skills to keep up with the warehouse’s inventory system.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. The whole thing just felt like a circus – the ads were all shouting at each other, the candidates seemed more interested in scoring points than fixing anything that mattered to people like me. I work long hours, and by the time the polls opened I was already exhausted, so I just stayed home. I also felt that none of the choices really represented the concerns of a working‑class family in a small Arkansas town. That feeling hasn’t really changed, so I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something huge blows up locally – say a major factory closing or a scandal that hits my community directly – I don’t see a reason to drive to the polls.

My biggest worries day‑to‑day are keeping up with grocery prices and making sure the property taxes don’t eat up the little extra money we manage to save. The job market feels shaky; the warehouse has been cutting overtime, and I hear rumors about automation. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re living in a different world, always talking about climate goals and social programs that never reach my town. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep fussing over culture wars and national scandals while ignoring the real cost of living issues that hit families like mine. Both sides seem to forget the people who are just trying to put food on the table.

## Media Diet  
I usually listen to NPR podcasts on my commute, skim CBS News headlines during lunch, and check the New York Post for quick political snapshots in the evenings. I spend about six hours a week juggling those three sources.
