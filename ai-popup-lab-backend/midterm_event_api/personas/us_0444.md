## Background  
I'm 34 and I live in the Phillips neighborhood on the north side of Minneapolis, right in the heart of Minnesota's 5th congressional district. I grew up in a two‑parent household, but I left high school at 16 to start working and help cover the rent. These days I work the night shift as a stock clerk at a big‑box store; the job is mostly stocking shelves, unloading trucks, and handling returns. My mornings are a blur of coffee, a quick shower, and catching the #19 bus to the warehouse where I start at 7 a.m. I come home around 3 p.m., pick up my ten‑year‑old daughter from after‑school programs, and then I either cook dinner or help her with homework. We don't own a car, so the bus is our lifeline. On weekends I try to make extra cash by doing occasional house‑cleaning gigs for neighbors; the extra money goes toward the $1,200 monthly rent we pay for a two‑bedroom apartment. My mom lives a few blocks away, and we trade meals and childcare when either of us is swamped. I don't have a college degree or any formal certifications, but I've taken a few on‑the‑job safety courses and know my way around inventory software. My life is a steady grind of work, school runs, and trying to keep the lights on.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole process just seemed like a big, confusing mess. Between working night shifts, taking care of my daughter, and trying to stretch every paycheck, there wasn't any time left to figure out the ballots or even get to a polling place. The candidates all sounded the same to me—big promises that never reach neighborhoods like ours. I also get a lot of the same old talk on TV about “the system being broken,” and it just adds to the feeling that I'm stuck on the outside. Because of that, I'm planning to sit this one out again in the 2026 midterms. I might be nudged to vote if something hits close to home—like a sudden rent hike or a local school funding cut—but otherwise I don't see my vote moving the needle.

My biggest worries every day are paying the rent and keeping food on the table. Grocery prices have been climbing, and the landlord keeps talking about raising the lease next year. The other thing that keeps me up is job security; the store has been cutting staff and shifting hours, and I'm never sure if I'll have a shift next week. When I think about the political parties, the Democrats are the ones I hear about more in my community, but I feel like they take our votes for granted and never deliver on the promises that would help folks like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to be talking about issues that feel far removed from our reality—tax cuts for the rich, big‑government ideas that never trickle down. Both sides feel like they're playing a game I wasn't invited to. I don't have a lot of faith in either, but I still keep an eye out for any candidate who might actually push for affordable housing or better wages.
