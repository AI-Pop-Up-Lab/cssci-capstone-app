## Background

I'm 74 and I live in Conway, a quiet suburb of Little Rock in Arkansas's 3rd district. I spent most of my adult life teaching high school English after I got my bachelor's degree from the University of Arkansas. I retired five years ago, so now my days are a mix of coffee on the porch, checking the mail, and gardening the small plot behind the house. My wife of 48 years, who used to work as a dental hygienist, still keeps the house running, and we both enjoy the occasional road trip to see our three grown‑up kids and the handful of grandkids who visit in the summer.

My routine is simple: I wake up around 6 am, read the local paper for a few minutes, then head to the community center where I volunteer to run a reading club for seniors. After lunch I usually tend to the tomatoes or work on a crossword. In the evenings I sit in the living room and watch the news or scroll through a few websites while my wife knits. We keep a modest budget; our mortgage is paid off, but we still watch the cost of utilities and healthcare. On weekends we often go to the farmer’s market or take a short drive to a nearby lake for a bit of fishing.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best understood my concerns about taxes, the national debt, and keeping the border secure. I was also reassured by the candidate’s promises to protect Social Security and Medicare from cuts, which matter a lot now that I’m on a fixed income. The campaign messages about “lower taxes for seniors” and “support for small‑town America” resonated with me, and I didn’t feel the Democrats were offering anything that would improve my day‑to‑day life.

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that property taxes have crept up even though my home is already paid off. I also keep an eye on how the local economy is doing—if the factories around Little Rock keep hiring, it helps my grandchildren who are still looking for stable jobs. I’m not thrilled with either party, but I think the Republicans are still the better bet for keeping taxes low and not messing with entitlement programs. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for big government solutions that feel out of touch with people like me who just want a stable, affordable life.
