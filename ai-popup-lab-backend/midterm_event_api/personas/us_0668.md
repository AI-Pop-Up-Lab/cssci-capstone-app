## Background
I'm 30 and I live in a two‑bedroom apartment in the town of Rhinebeck, which falls in NY‑22. I grew up in Queens, went to a community college in the Hudson Valley for a couple of years, but never finished a degree. Right now I work full‑time as a scheduling coordinator for a regional logistics company; most of my day is spent on a computer, juggling driver routes and answering calls from the warehouse floor. I share the place with a roommate from Ohio who works at a nearby coffee shop, so rent is split and we keep each other company when the building’s laundry machines are broken.

My mornings start at 6 am with a quick coffee, a glance at the news on my phone, and a 30‑minute bike ride to the office. After work I usually hit the grocery store on Main Street, then either cook dinner or order takeout when I’m too tired. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a hike in the Catskills, and catching up on a few streaming shows. I’m not married, don’t have kids, and my parents live in Brooklyn; we talk on the phone a few times a week, mostly about family health and the occasional holiday plan.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. The ballot was mailed to me in June, but I was pulling double‑shifts at the warehouse to meet a deadline and the whole thing felt like a distant, noisy background. I’ve always thought the system is set up so that the big parties argue over the same things while my daily worries—paying rent, keeping food on the table—stay the same. That feeling of cynicism made me skip the polls, and I’m leaning toward doing the same in the 2026 midterms unless something dramatic hits my neighborhood, like a sudden rent hike or a major school closure.

My top concerns right now are the cost of rent and grocery prices. The landlord just announced a 5 % increase for the next lease term, and every time I go to the supermarket I see the same items priced a few dollars higher than last year. I’m also watching the local job market; a couple of small manufacturers have closed, and it feels like the safety net is getting thinner.

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Democrats keep talking about climate goals and student debt relief, but the taxes on my paycheck keep climbing and they never seem to address the landlord‑tenant squeeze that hits people like me. The Republicans shout about cutting taxes, yet they ignore immigration issues that affect my extended family and they rarely mention the cost of living in places far from the big cities. I don’t feel a strong allegiance to either side; I just wish someone would focus on the stuff that matters to everyday folks.

I’m sitting this one out. If I do end up voting, it will be because a candidate actually talks about affordable housing or a local issue that hits home, not because I feel compelled by party loyalty.
