## Background
I'm 65 and I work as a senior civil engineer for a regional construction firm that does a lot of bridge and road projects around western Iowa. I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Sioux City, just a few blocks from the Missouri River. My wife, who retired from nursing a few years ago, and I have two grown kids—one lives in Des Moines and the other is finishing a master’s in Chicago. Most mornings I wake up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and skim the headlines on my tablet while the kettle whistles. After that I grab a quick breakfast of toast and fruit, then drive the 15‑minute commute to the office where I spend the day reviewing plans, meeting with contractors, and occasionally heading out to a site to check progress. I still enjoy playing a round of golf on Thursday evenings with a few old colleagues; it’s the best way to unwind after a week of deadlines. Sundays are for church and a potluck with the neighborhood association, where we trade recipes and complain about the rising cost of property taxes. When I’m not working or at church, I’m usually tinkering in the garage, fixing up an old pickup truck or helping a neighbor with a leaky faucet.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate’s promises on lowering taxes and cutting regulation spoke to my experience in the construction business. I’ve watched budgets get tighter at the city level, and the idea of a smaller government footprint felt like it would let projects move faster. I also liked the emphasis on law‑and‑order policies; growing up in a neighborhood that struggled with crime, I appreciate a firm stance on public safety. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the party’s policies have affected my pocketbook—especially the recent hikes in prescription drug prices and the push to raise the state income tax again. Those moves have made me a bit more skeptical, but I still think the GOP is the better bet for keeping taxes low and keeping the regulatory environment friendly to businesses like mine.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the cost of my blood‑pressure medication and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. I’m also watching the local job market; if the state doesn’t attract new manufacturing plants, my firm could lose contracts, and that would hit my retirement savings. I view the Democratic Party as well‑meaning but out of touch with the practical concerns of small‑town workers. Their push for higher taxes and more expansive social programs feels like it would stifle the kind of growth I’ve seen in my career. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least seem to understand that keeping the tax burden low and the regulatory environment stable is crucial for folks like me who are trying to stretch a fixed income into retirement.
