## Background
I'm 30 and work full‑time as a forklift operator at a distribution center just outside Wilmington. My shift starts at 6 a.m., so I’m usually up by 5 to grab a coffee from the corner deli and scroll through the news on my phone while the truck pulls out of the lot. After my eight‑hour shift I head home to a modest two‑bedroom apartment in the Bear area, where I live with my girlfriend and our nine‑month‑old son. We split the rent and utilities, and on weekends I try to squeeze in a quick grocery run at the local market and a couple of hours at the gym—mostly to burn off the junk food I end up eating on late‑night shifts.

When I’m not at work or taking care of the baby, I spend a lot of my free time tinkering with my old pickup truck, listening to classic rock on the radio, and catching up with a few friends from high school who still live in the area. We meet at the bar down the road for a beer after the kids are in bed, and the conversation usually drifts to how hard it is to make ends meet, the rising cost of rent, and whether the next election will actually change anything for people like us. I don’t have a college degree, but I did take a couple of night‑time courses on basic computer skills, mainly so I can handle the inventory software at work.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like my ballot wouldn’t make a dent in the system and I was swamped with overtime and caring for my son. The whole process seemed like a circus—long lines, endless ads, and politicians promising everything while delivering nothing that touched my day‑to‑day life. That feeling of cynicism hasn’t really softened; unless there’s a local issue that directly threatens my job or my family’s housing situation, I’m leaning toward sitting this one out again in 2026.

I’m not planning to vote in the upcoming midterms. My top concerns right now are keeping up with rent and making sure grocery prices don’t push us over the edge. The political parties both feel out of touch: the Democrats keep talking about climate goals and social programs that sound good on paper but never translate into higher wages for warehouse workers, while the Republicans harp on tax cuts that mostly benefit the wealthy and ignore the cost of living spikes that hit folks like me. I’m annoyed that neither side seems to understand the grind of a 9‑to‑5 shift and a newborn at home.
