## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Council Bluffs, right on the Missouri River. After I finished my associate's degree in health sciences, I worked as a dental hygienist for the city clinic for about thirty years before I retired last winter. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two grown kids live in Des Moines and Omaha, so most of my days are spent at home with my dog, Bella, and occasional visits to my grandson who stays with me on weekends while his parents are at work. I get up around 6 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and flip through the local paper—though I mostly read the national sections online. By 8 a.m. I’m usually at the senior center for a bingo game or a low‑impact exercise class; the social part keeps me sharp. In the afternoons I volunteer at the community health fair, handing out flyers about flu shots and free blood pressure checks. Evenings are quiet: a simple dinner, a bit of TV, and then I check my email or scroll through the news before bed. I drive a reliable older sedan, and I’m careful about keeping up with car maintenance because I still need to run errands around town and pick up my grandson from school.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because Medicare was a huge issue for me. My doctor raised my prescription costs after the new drug came out, and the Democratic candidate promised to push back on those price hikes and protect Social Security. I also liked the stance on expanding broadband in rural areas; my granddaughter needed a stable connection for her online classes, and the promise of better internet felt personal. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are being handled. The Republican candidates keep talking about cutting taxes, but I worry those cuts end up favoring big corporations and leave less money for local schools and health programs. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t feel like sitting this one out because the stakes feel real for my pocketbook. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hike the county council approved last year, which is squeezing my fixed income. I think the Democratic Party is more attuned to seniors’ needs and immigration reform, which matters to my family since my parents came here from Mexico. The GOP, in my view, seems more interested in deregulating industries and rolling back environmental protections that keep our river clean. I’m not a political expert, but when I hear about a candidate’s stance on health care, I tend to side with the party that talks about protecting Medicare and keeping drug prices low.

## Media Diet
I spend about eleven hours a week with the news: NPR in the mornings while I have my coffee, ABC News and CBS News in the evenings after dinner, and I skim Newsweek articles on the weekend when I have a little extra time.
