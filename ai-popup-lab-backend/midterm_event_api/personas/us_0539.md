## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Apex, a suburb of Raleigh in North Carolina's 7th district. I work as a senior data scientist for a research lab affiliated with the Research Triangle Park, a job I landed after finishing my PhD in computer science at Duke. Most weekdays I start my day with a quick jog around the greenway, then drive about twenty minutes to the office where I spend eight hours crunching numbers for federal grant projects. My wife of thirty‑four years, a retired schoolteacher, now runs a small online tutoring business from our home. We have two grown‑up children—one in Seattle working as a software engineer, the other back in North Carolina teaching high school math. My parents live in Charlotte; I visit them a few times a year and help with their medical appointments. On weekends I’m usually at the farmers market picking up fresh produce, fixing things around the house, or meeting friends at the local dim sum spot that reminds me of home. I’m pretty comfortable financially, but I still watch my mortgage payments and keep an eye on the cost of health insurance for my parents.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really pushing for affordable health care and protecting funding for the research labs I depend on for my work. The candidate’s stance on STEM education and immigration also resonated with me as the child of immigrants. Over the past two years I’ve watched the Senate grind through a few budget battles and felt the GOP’s push to cut science funding was a direct threat to my livelihood, so I’m planning to cast a ballot again in the 2026 midterms. I’m definitely voting; I’ve already filled out my mail‑in ballot and will drop it off at the post office next week.

My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of health insurance for my aging parents and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on our home’s assessed value. I’m also watching the local job market because the tech corridor is getting hit by a slowdown, and I fear that could ripple out to the whole region. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are at least trying to address the problems that matter to me, even if they’re sometimes all over the place with messaging. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re more interested in cultural battles than in the practical issues that affect my family’s day‑to‑day life. I’m not a fan of the constant partisan bickering, but when it comes down to it I’m leaning toward the side that seems to support science, education, and a decent safety net.
