## Background  
I'm 35 and I live in Weirton, right on the Ohio River in the 1st District of West Virginia. I never finished high school; I dropped out at 16 to help my dad at the steel mill when the plant started cutting back. After the mill closed, I picked up odd jobs—mostly construction labor, loading trucks, and some seasonal work at the local quarry. I work the second shift, so I’m usually up at 2 p.m., grabbing a coffee, then heading to the site around 3 p.m. and staying until 11 p.m. When I’m not on the job, I’m either at the bar with a couple of buddies watching the Steelers, or I’m fixing up the old pickup I drive to get to work.  

I’m married to a woman who works part‑time at the grocery store, and we have a 7‑year‑old son. We rent a modest two‑bedroom house a few blocks from the river; the rent’s a big chunk of what we bring home, so we keep a tight budget. On weekends I try to get the kid to the park or the community pool, but a lot of the time we’re just hanging out at home, watching whatever’s on TV. I don’t have a lot of free time for anything beyond the usual work‑home‑sleep cycle, and I’m not really into clubs or anything like that.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus. The ballots were the same old names, the promises were the same old lies, and I was working a second‑shift job that left me exhausted by the time the polls opened. I also thought my vote wouldn’t change anything; the big‑ticket races feel distant from the day‑to‑day grind of paying rent and keeping the lights on. I’ve got a healthy dose of cynicism about both parties—Democrats seem to be talking about big‑government solutions that never reach a town like mine, and Republicans keep shouting about “draining the swamp” while still giving tax breaks to the same folks they claim to hate.  

I’m still sitting this one out for the 2026 midterms. Unless something blows up locally—a factory closure, a scandal that hits my community hard—I don’t see a reason to make the trip to the polling place. My top concerns are plain and practical: keeping my job when the construction market dips, and making sure the price of gas and groceries don’t keep climbing. I also worry about property taxes; the county keeps asking for more money to fund schools that feel like a waste of my kid’s time.  

My view of the parties is pretty lopsided. I think Democrats are out of touch with working‑class folks in Appalachia, always talking about climate goals while the coal mines close and no one replaces the jobs. The GOP talks tough on “law and order” and “energy independence,” but they rarely follow through with anything that helps a guy who’s trying to keep a roof over his family’s head. I’m not a fan of the political theater; I just want something that makes it easier to put food on the table.
