## Background

I didn't finish high school—got a GED after a few years of working odd jobs, so most of my skills are on the warehouse floor and fixing things around the house. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in the town of Snohomish, just inside the 6th district. My roommate, Carlos, is from Mexico and we split rent and utilities; we both send a little money back to families down south. My days start early: I’m up at 5 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner diner, and catch the bus to the warehouse. After a 10‑hour shift I head home, eat whatever's left in the fridge, and try to catch a show or a YouTube video before I’m out cold. On Sundays I help my aunt with grocery shopping and sometimes volunteer at the local community center, handing out food boxes to families that are struggling. I watch Spanish‑language TV with my family sometimes, but most of my news comes from the English outlets I list below.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Honestly, I felt like none of the choices mattered to me. The whole process seemed like a circus, and I was working two jobs at the time, so I couldn’t even get to a poll before my shift ended. I also get a lot of messages from friends saying “why bother? they’ll just raise taxes and make rent higher.” That stuck with me, and I’ve been pretty skeptical of the whole system ever since. I’m sitting this one out too. The midterms don’t feel any more exciting, and unless something blows up in my neighborhood—like a big layoff at the warehouse or a sudden spike in rent—I’ll probably stay home.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with rent and making sure groceries don’t run out before payday. The warehouse pays okay, but everything else—gas, utilities, my roommate’s tuition—keeps the budget tight. I’m also worried about the local job market; a lot of my co‑workers talk about the plant possibly closing if automation ramps up. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the “talkers” who promise free college and cheap housing but never deliver anything that reaches people like me. The Republicans feel like the “tax‑cut crowd” that doesn’t get why a guy who works night shifts still can’t afford a decent apartment. Both sides seem to forget the day‑to‑day grind of folks who don’t have a degree and are just trying to put food on the table.
