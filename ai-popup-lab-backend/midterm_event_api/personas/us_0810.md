## Background
I'm 45 and I work full‑time as a cashier at the grocery store in Rapid City. The job is mostly standing behind a register, bagging groceries, and trying to keep a smile on my face even when the line backs up. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the edge of town with my teenage daughter, Maya, and my elderly mother, who needs help with her medication and getting around. We get by on my paycheck and a small amount of food stamps; rent is the biggest line item each month, and we’re always watching the utility meter.

Mornings start early. I’m up by 5:30 am, make coffee, and get Maya ready for school before I head to the store for my 7 am shift. My break is a quick sandwich in the back room, then it’s back to ringing up customers until the store closes at 10 pm. On my days off I try to squeeze in a part‑time cleaning gig for extra cash, but it’s hard to keep the schedule together. On Saturdays I take Maya to the local community center for basketball practice, and we both go to church on Sundays. Between work, school runs, and caring for my mom, there’s barely any time left for anything else.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two jobs, caring for my kids and my mother, and dealing with constant bills, voting just felt like another thing I couldn’t fit into the day. The whole process seemed distant: long lines, confusing ballot instructions, and a feeling that none of the candidates really understood what life is like for a Black family in a mostly white, rural part of South Dakota. I was also fed up with the endless ads that promised change but never delivered anything that touched my grocery budget or my mom’s health care.

I’m sitting this one out again in 2026. Unless something blows up locally—a clinic closing, a school funding crisis, or a major price hike on food—I don’t see a reason to carve out the time. My top everyday worries are keeping up with grocery prices and making sure my mother can afford her prescription meds. I’m also constantly thinking about whether Maya will have a decent job after high school; the local economy just isn’t booming.

When I think about the parties, I’m annoyed on both sides. The Democrats talk a lot about racial justice, but they never seem to address the lack of jobs or the high cost of living in places like ours. The Republicans keep shouting about “family values” and “lower taxes,” yet they ignore the very real concerns of Black families and rural workers. It feels like they’re both playing to their base and not to folks like me who are just trying to get by.

## Media Diet
I spend about seven hours a week with the news: I listen to NPR podcasts while I’m driving my daughter to school, and I scroll through ABC News and CBS News on my phone during lunch breaks and in the evenings after I get home.
