## Background

The job is hard, noisy, and the pay is decent enough to keep the lights on and put the kids through school. On weekdays, after my shift ends around 5 p.m., I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and then help my wife with the kids’ homework before we all collapse on the couch. ## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of work I do. The candidate promised to keep regulations from choking the plant, and I liked the talk about securing the border so we don’t have to worry about cheap labor undercutting wages. I also liked the emphasis on “personal responsibility” – it fit with how I try to run my household. Since then, I’ve watched the news and seen the Democrats push for higher taxes and more government programs that, to me, sound like they’ll end up paying for themselves by raising the cost of living. I’m still convinced that a smaller government is the way to go, even if I’m not sure every Republican candidate lives up to that ideal.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of groceries and the property tax hike the county council is debating – both hit our family budget hard. I’m also watching the local job market; if the plant ever cuts a shift, that would be a big blow. I see the Republican Party as the one that at least talks about keeping business costs down and letting folks keep what they earn, while the Democrats seem more interested in expanding welfare programs that I think end up costing taxpayers more than they help. I’m not thrilled by any politician’s promises, but I feel more aligned with the GOP’s focus on the economy and personal liberty.
