## Background
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a cashier at the grocery store in Fairbanks. I grew up in a small Hispanic family that moved up from Anchorage when I was a teen, so I still talk Spanish at home with my parents on the phone. After high school I didn't go to college; I took a job at the store right after graduation and have been there ever since, moving up to the night shift manager last year. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who also works retail, and we split the rent and utilities. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the local diner, walk the short distance to the store, and spend my shift stocking shelves, ringing up groceries, and dealing with the occasional angry customer. After work I usually hit the gym for an hour, then come home, cook something simple—often canned beans and frozen veggies—because fresh food is pricey up here. Weekends are a mix of catching up on Netflix, helping my younger sister with her school projects over video chat, and sometimes driving out to the outskirts to hunt for a cheaper gas price. I’m not religious, but I do attend a community potluck once a month at the local community center, mostly to stay connected.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote didn’t matter; the candidates kept talking about issues that never touch my daily grind—like big‑city infrastructure or foreign policy. Between long shifts, a commute that can be delayed by snow, and trying to stretch a paycheck to cover heating bills, the whole election thing just felt like another thing I couldn’t fit into my schedule. I also got a lot of junk mail and automated calls that made the whole process feel like a corporate sales pitch, so I just ignored it. I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out as well, unless something hits close to home—like a sudden change in state funding for rural broadband or a local controversy that makes the news impossible to ignore.

My biggest worries are the cost of groceries and keeping the house warm during the long winter. Prices keep climbing, and the electric bill spikes every time the wind dies down. I also worry about the shortage of decent jobs that pay enough to afford those basic needs. When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Democrats because they promise big social programs but never seem to allocate enough to remote places like mine. The Republicans get my ire for pushing policies that feel like they ignore the realities of a place where the economy is tied to tourism and fishing, and they keep talking about “law and order” while the real law I need is affordable heating. Both sides feel like they’re talking past people like me.
