## Background
I'm 64 and work as a senior policy analyst for a nonprofit that advocates for Native American issues and environmental justice. I live in the Princeton area of New Jersey’s 12th district, in a modest house I share with my husband and our two grown‑up kids who both live out of state. My days start early with a cup of black coffee while scrolling through the morning headlines on my tablet. I head to the office in Trenton by train, spending a couple of hours each morning drafting briefs, meeting with tribal leaders, and reviewing state legislation. Lunch is usually a quick sandwich in the office kitchen, though once a week I meet a former colleague at a local deli to discuss a new grant proposal. After work I often stop by the community center where I volunteer to teach a computer class for seniors. Evenings are low‑key: I cook dinner, watch a bit of TV, and sometimes video‑call my daughter who lives in California. On weekends I tend to the garden, run errands, and attend the monthly tribal council meeting that rotates between our county’s towns.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because the party’s platform on expanding Medicaid, protecting clean water, and recognizing tribal sovereignty felt like the closest match to the issues that affect my family and community. The candidate’s promise to push for more federal funding for tribal health programs was a big pull for me; I’ve seen how underfunded our clinics are. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are being acted on, and I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for protecting the social safety net I rely on. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—I’ve already filled out my ballot in advance because I don’t want to miss the chance to influence who controls the House and Senate.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the ever‑increasing property taxes that make it hard to keep up with maintenance on our home. I also worry about the state’s plans for a new highway that could cut through sacred land near our reservation; that’s a local issue that feels personal. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more willing to listen to tribal voices and protect programs that matter to seniors like me. The Republicans, in my view, often talk tough on taxes and regulation but rarely address the unique challenges our community faces, and some of their rhetoric feels dismissive of our history. I’m not a political scholar, but I trust my gut that the party that cares more about health care, the environment, and tribal rights is the one I’ll support.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR’s morning news feed while I eat breakfast, catch the ABC News evening roundup after work, and skim a few articles on Newsweek during the weekend; it adds up to about five hours a week.
