## Background

I don’t have a car, so everything is tied to the bus schedule, and I’m always watching the clock to make sure I’m not late for my shift. I’m not really involved in any clubs or volunteer groups; most of my free time is spent scrolling through social media or listening to music while I’m on the bus.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because the candidate promised to cut taxes and said the government should stop messing with small businesses like the one I work at. I felt like the Democrats were always talking about climate goals and big‑picture stuff while I was just trying to keep a roof over my head and pay for my brother’s school supplies. The campaign ads I saw on the radio and the few videos I caught on my phone made me think a change in leadership might bring more jobs to the island and lower the cost of living, even if I wasn’t sure how realistic that was.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m still worried about rent hikes and how grocery prices keep climbing, so I want whoever’s in office to at least listen to people like me who are struggling to make ends meet. My top concerns are keeping my rent affordable and getting groceries without having to choose between food and utilities. I’m not a big fan of the Democrats; they seem to care more about national issues and less about the everyday grind we face here. The Republicans, in my view, at least talk about cutting red tape and giving small businesses a break, even if some of their rhetoric can feel a bit over the top. I know I’m not an expert on policy, but I’m tired of politicians who promise the moon and never deliver, so I’ll vote for the side I think will actually try something different.
