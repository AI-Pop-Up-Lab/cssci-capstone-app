## Background  
I'm 35 and work as a data analyst for a natural‑resources consulting firm that contracts with several mining companies around the Casper area. I grew up in a small town near Laramie, went to the University of Wyoming for my bachelor's, then did a master's in statistics at a university out of state before coming back to work here. My husband is a high‑school teacher, and we have a nine‑year‑old daughter who rides the bus to school while I usually start work at 8 a.m. from a home office. Mornings are a rush of coffee, checking the kids’ schedules, and scrolling through my phone for the latest headlines before the commute to the neighborhood co‑working space. After work I pick up my daughter, help with homework, and we try to squeeze in a hike on the nearby trail on weekends. We own a modest ranch‑style house, and my parents live a couple of hours away in a town near the Tetons, so we see them a few times a year. My spare time is spent mostly on cooking, a little bit of knitting, and catching up on podcasts about tech and public policy.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Green Party candidate for president. I was fed up with the usual choices: the Democrats seemed to want to hammer down on energy projects that keep a lot of jobs here, while the Republicans were pushing policies that felt hostile to the growing diversity in our community and ignored climate concerns. I thought a third‑party voice might force the conversation toward real solutions, even if I knew the chances of winning were slim.  

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m not thrilled about any of the major‑party candidates, but I’ve found a Senate hopeful who’s a moderate with a background in engineering and a clear plan to protect both the state’s mineral revenues and invest in renewable research. My top daily worries are the rising property taxes that are making it hard to keep up with our mortgage, and the grocery bills that keep creeping higher as supply chains tighten. I view the Democratic Party as too quick to impose federal mandates that hurt local businesses, and the Republican Party as increasingly focused on cultural battles that don’t address the economic realities of a state that relies on both energy extraction and tourism. I’m looking for someone who can balance those competing interests without resorting to the usual partisan bickering.
