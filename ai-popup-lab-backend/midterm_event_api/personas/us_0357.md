## Background

I work as a maintenance supervisor at the local chemical plant; the job mostly requires a two‑year technical program and a lot of hands‑on know‑how with pumps, HVAC systems, and safety compliance. My shift starts at 6 a.m., so I’m usually up before sunrise, making a quick coffee, checking the news on my phone, and then driving the 20‑minute commute to the plant. The work is steady, but the plant’s overtime schedule can be erratic, so I’ve learned to keep a spare set of clothes and a lunchbox ready for late‑night calls.

I’m married to a woman who runs a small boutique that sells handmade quilts and home décor; we met at a community event when we were both in our mid‑twenties. We have two kids: a daughter who just finished her associate degree in nursing and moved to New Orleans, and a son who’s still in high school and spends his weekends helping his dad with odd jobs around the house. On Saturdays we usually head to the local fishing pier or the bar down the road for a couple of cold beers and a game of darts. I drive a pickup truck, keep a spare tire in the trunk, and try to stay on top of the rising cost of gasoline and insurance.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that understood the kind of work I do and the taxes that bite into my paycheck. The talk about cutting regulations on manufacturing and keeping energy prices low resonated with me, especially after the plant announced a modest wage freeze last year. I also liked the candidate’s tough stance on border security; it felt like a personal safety issue for my community, which has seen a few incidents at the local ports.

Since then, I’ve watched the headlines and heard the talk at the bar, and I’m still leaning Republican. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms – I’m not one to sit this one out unless something truly crazy happens, like the plant shutting down or a major scandal that directly hits my family. My biggest everyday worries are the price of gasoline and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on our modest house. I also worry about healthcare costs; my wife’s boutique doesn’t have a large group plan, and I’m always checking if the new proposals will make it harder for us to afford her insurance premiums.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones always talking about big government programs that end up raising my taxes, while the Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about keeping taxes low and letting small businesses breathe. I get annoyed when the media spins every issue into a culture war, but I’m still skeptical of the Democrats’ promises on infrastructure – I’ve heard too many promises that never materialize in our town. Overall, I’m hoping the Republican candidates will keep the focus on jobs, lower taxes, and a stable energy market.
