## Background  
I'm 79 and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on a quiet street in Hamburg, a suburb of Buffalo in NY’s 18th district. I retired ten years ago after spending most of my working life as a maintenance supervisor at a local manufacturing plant. I never finished my associate’s degree in mechanical technology, but I learned a lot on the job and can still fix a leaky faucet or a stubborn furnace without calling anyone.  

My wife, Margaret, is 77 and we’ve been married for 55 years. We both have grown‑up kids who live out in upstate New York and one daughter who moved to Florida. They call us every Sunday, and we chat on the phone most evenings.  

My days are simple. I get up around 7 a.m., brew a pot of coffee, and read the newspaper headlines on my tablet while the kettle whistles. After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood with our old Labrador, Buddy, and sometimes stop by the senior center where I help with the chess club. In the afternoon I’ll watch a bit of TV news, tend to the garden, or do some light woodworking in the garage. Dinner is usually something quick—leftovers or a frozen pizza—followed by a bit of TV or a NPR podcast before bed. I try to keep active, but arthritis in my knees makes me careful about overdoing it.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. By then I was already feeling pretty fed up with the whole circus. The campaigns seemed louder than they were useful, and I was convinced my single vote wouldn’t change anything. Between doctor appointments, taking my meds, and caring for Margaret when her blood pressure spikes, I just didn’t have the energy to get to a polling place. The candidates all sounded the same to me—talking about “change” while ignoring the real issues that matter to seniors like me, such as prescription‑drug prices and the rising cost of property taxes.  

I’m sitting this one out too. Nothing in the news has sparked a fire under me, and the local controversies haven’t reached the level that would make me crawl out of the house. My top everyday concerns are keeping my meds affordable and making sure my property tax bill doesn’t keep climbing so I can stay in the house we’ve lived in for decades. I’m also worried about the local hospital’s capacity; if the ER gets too crowded, I’m the one who ends up waiting hours for basic care.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep pushing for big‑government programs that feel out of touch with the budget constraints of retirees, and the Republicans act like they don’t care about healthcare at all. It’s like both sides are playing a game of who can ignore seniors the longest. I wish there were folks who actually cared about the little things that affect us day‑to‑day.

## Media Diet
