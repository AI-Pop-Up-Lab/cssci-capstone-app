## Background  
I'm 35 and live in West Palm Beach, in a modest two‑bedroom condo I share with my wife and our 13‑month‑old son. My parents came here from Vietnam in the late‑80s, and they run a small take‑out place a few blocks from our building. I dropped out of community college after two years to take a job as a warehouse supervisor for a regional distribution center; the pay is decent but the shifts are long, so I’m usually up before sunrise, grab a coffee, and head out at 5 a.m. The job is mostly physical, moving pallets and making sure the inventory system stays in sync, but it gives me enough to cover the mortgage and our kid’s daycare.  

When I’m not at work, I spend evenings with my family—helping my son with his first steps, cooking whatever my wife can manage after a long day at the restaurant, and trying to squeeze in a little TV before bed. On weekends we hit the beach or the local park, and once a month we go to the Buddhist temple with my parents. I’m not a big sports fan, but I do follow the local high‑school football games because the kids are my neighbors. My biggest daily hassle is the traffic on I‑95 during rush hour and trying to keep the condo’s air‑conditioning running in the humid summer.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to the health‑care costs that were eating into my paycheck and the immigration reforms that would help families like mine. My dad talked a lot about the fear of being labeled “illegal” when he first came here, and the Democratic platform seemed to promise a little more security for us. I also liked the candidate’s stance on climate resilience—living on the coast, I’ve seen a few nasty storms, and I worry about rising sea levels.  

That said, I’m not a policy wonk. I watch the news when I can, but most of the time I’m just trying to get through the day without my truck breaking down or the rent going up. For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls; I’m not thrilled about the whole process, but I think my vote still matters, especially in a swing district like ours. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of rent and the price of gasoline—both have jumped a lot in the past year and are squeezing my family’s budget.  

I’m pretty annoyed with the Republicans; they keep pushing for tighter immigration rules that feel like a slap in the face to my parents, and they talk a lot about “law and order” while ignoring the fact that a lot of the crime we see is tied to poverty. The Democrats, on the other hand, feel like they’re constantly fighting internal fights and don’t always deliver on promises, but at least they’re trying to address the issues that hit people like me. I’m not going to sit on the fence forever—I’ll keep voting for the side that seems more likely to keep my son’s future affordable.

## Media Diet  
I listen to NPR on my commute in the mornings, catch ABC News headlines in the evenings after work, and skim Newsweek and CBS News on the weekend to see what’s happening nationally. I clock about six hours a week across those outlets.
