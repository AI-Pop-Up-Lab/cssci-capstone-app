## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest bungalow on the edge of Lafayette, Indiana, right in the 7th congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working at the auto plant in nearby West Lafayette. I never finished a degree, but I took a few night courses in mechanical engineering, which helped me move up to a supervisor role before I retired at 65. My wife, Mae, and I have been married for 60 years; she's retired from the local library. Our kids live in Chicago and Indianapolis, and we get a grandkid to visit every summer. Most mornings I sit on the porch with a cup of black coffee, flip through the local paper, and watch the news on the TV while the birds are still chirping. After that I usually head to the senior center for bingo or a craft class, then tend to the small garden behind the house—tomatoes, peppers, a few herbs. In the evenings I might call my daughter, help her troubleshoot a computer problem, or just sit and listen to some classic soul records. I try to keep active, but the knees ache enough that I stick to short walks around the neighborhood.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because Social Security and Medicare felt like the only safety nets I could count on when I was thinking about my own health and the fact that my brother passed away without decent health coverage. The candidate talked about protecting those programs and also mentioned criminal‑justice reform, which mattered to me as a Black man who’s seen too many friends caught up in the system for minor things. I also liked the promise of more funding for community health clinics, which is where my wife volunteers. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through. I’m still convinced the Democrats are the better bet for my generation, even though I’m annoyed when they get caught up in partisan fights that don’t affect everyday folks. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for big corporations and pushing policies that feel like they ignore senior citizens and people of color. I’m not a political junkie, but when I hear about a tax hike that might affect my property tax bill or a proposal to cut Medicare benefits, I get worried.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and keeping my Social Security check stable enough to cover the rising grocery prices. I also think about the local jobs—many of the factories are closing, and it’s hard to see a future for the younger folks in town. I feel the parties are both stuck in their own bubbles: Democrats promise a lot but sometimes deliver half‑measures, while Republicans seem to care mostly about the wealthy and ignore the needs of people like me.
