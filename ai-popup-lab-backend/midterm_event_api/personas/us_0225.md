## Background
I'm 40 and work as a senior analyst for a regional health‑care nonprofit in Ankeny, just outside Des Moines. I have a master’s in public health, so most days I’m pulling data on hospital readmission rates, writing grant proposals, and meeting with county officials about Medicaid expansion. My husband is a high‑school math teacher, and we have two kids—Mia, 12, who’s into soccer, and Eli, 9, who loves video games. We own a modest two‑bedroom house that we bought five years ago; the mortgage is the biggest line on our budget.

My routine is pretty set. I wake up around 6 am, make coffee, and scroll through headlines on my phone while the kids get ready for school. I drop them off, then bike the 10‑minute ride to the office where I’m usually at my desk by 8:30. Lunch is a quick sandwich in the break room, and I often chat with a coworker about the latest state health policy debate. After work I pick up the kids, help with homework, and we try to have dinner together at least four nights a week. Evenings are a mix of helping Mia with soccer practice, watching a show on streaming, or scrolling through social media before bed. On weekends we do a bit of yard work, run errands, and try to squeeze in a family hike when the weather’s nice.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party’s health‑care platform was the only one that actually cared about expanding coverage for people like the patients I see every day. I was also pretty fed up with the constant talk about cutting taxes for the rich while my husband’s salary barely kept up with rising property taxes. The campaign ads that focused on protecting Medicare and funding public schools resonated with me, and I thought a Democratic win would give my nonprofit more chances for grant money.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the pandemic fallout and the recent push to lower prescription drug prices. I’m still convinced the Democrats are the best bet for protecting the safety net, but I’m also a bit skeptical about how quickly they move on climate legislation—my kids keep asking why we can’t do more to protect the farms we live near. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out unless something catastrophic happens to my community.

My top everyday concerns are the cost of health insurance for my family and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on our mortgage. I’m also watching the local job market; a few big manufacturers have been cutting shifts, and that worries me for the kids’ future. I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least talks about these issues, even if they sometimes overpromise. The Republican side feels like they’re more interested in cutting taxes for corporations and rolling back regulations that actually keep our water clean, which doesn’t sit right with someone who works in public health.
