## Background
I'm 65, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Eau Claire, right in the middle of Wisconsin's 3rd district. I spent most of my career as an associate professor of environmental engineering at the University of Wisconsin–Eau Claire, and I finally retired two years ago. My wife and I have been married for 38 years; our two kids moved out after college—one's a software developer in Seattle, the other teaches high school biology in Madison. Most mornings I start with a strong cup of coffee on the porch, watching the sunrise over the lake while scrolling through the headlines on my tablet. I still drive a small hybrid to the local community garden where I volunteer a few hours a week, tending to tomatoes and helping out seniors who want fresh produce. On Tuesdays I teach a free evening class at the senior center about basic computer skills, which keeps me sharp and lets me stay connected with neighbors. Evenings are usually spent reading the paper, catching up on a few episodes of a classic drama on TV, or video‑chatting with my grandchildren. The routine is simple, but it gives me a sense of purpose and keeps the days from blending together.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one really pushing for affordable prescription drugs and protecting Medicare. I also appreciated the emphasis on clean‑energy research, which aligns with my background in environmental engineering. The Republican platform seemed focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that I spent my career defending, so I didn't feel comfortable supporting them. This year, I'm definitely going to the polls again. My top concerns are the rising cost of prescription medication and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on our town—both things that directly affect my retirement budget. I'm also watching the local job market; a few manufacturing plants have announced layoffs, and I worry about the ripple effects on small businesses. I think the Democrats are generally more in tune with my needs on health care and climate, even if I sometimes think they overreach on cultural issues. The Republicans, in my view, keep talking about “smaller government” while trying to cut funding for public health and education, which feels like a gamble I’m not willing to take.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR's morning news podcast while having coffee and then skim the headlines on Newsweek and CBS News in the evening, usually adding a quick check on ABC News before bed. All together it adds up to about ten hours a week.
