## Background  
I'm 84 and I live in a modest brick house on a quiet street in Northampton, right near the Connecticut River. I spent most of my adult life teaching elementary school English and literature, so I have a college degree in education and a love for books. My husband passed away three years ago, and my two children live in Boston and Portland, so most days I'm on my own, but I keep busy. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of strong tea, and read the local paper while the sun is just coming up. After breakfast I take a short walk to the senior center, where I help organize the weekly knitting circle and sometimes lead a discussion on classic novels. In the afternoons I tend to my small garden of herbs and tomatoes on the back porch, then I’m back inside for a sandwich and a bit of crossword. Evenings are for the television; I usually tune into the nightly news on one of the national channels and then catch up on a documentary or a drama series before turning in around 10 p.m. On Sundays I attend a quiet service at the nearby Unitarian congregation and later meet a few friends for brunch at a local café. I’m on Medicare, pay a modest property tax, and rely on a fixed pension and a small social‑security supplement to cover my expenses.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I didn’t vote for either major party; I cast my ballot for the Green Party candidate because I felt both Democrats and Republicans were ignoring climate action and the needs of seniors like me. I was also frustrated that the big parties seemed more interested in fundraising than in solving everyday problems. That vote felt like a small protest, and it gave me a sense that I could still make a statement even at my age. For the 2026 midterms, I’m voting again, but I’m still looking for a candidate who will put health‑care costs and prescription‑drug prices on the front page, not just national headlines. My top concerns are keeping my Medicare coverage affordable and making sure the rising cost of utilities doesn’t force me to cut back on heating during the long New England winters. I’m also worried about property taxes creeping up as the town tries to fund new development projects. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as well‑meaning but often caught up in identity politics and slow to act on climate and senior issues. The Republicans strike me as more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and less attentive to social safety nets. Both sides feel distant, so I keep an eye out for any third‑party or independent candidates who might finally address the things that affect my day‑to‑day life.
