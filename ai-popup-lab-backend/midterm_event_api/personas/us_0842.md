## Background  
I'm 45 and work as a dental hygienist at a community health clinic in Spring, Texas, right in the middle of the 23rd district. My associate’s degree got me the license, and after a couple of years on the night shift I moved to the day schedule so I can be home when my kids get back from school. My husband runs a small landscaping business, and we have two kids: a 15‑year‑old son who’s into video games and a 9‑year‑old daughter who loves dance class.  

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, scrolling through headlines on my phone while I drive the short commute to the clinic. The job is steady, but the pay isn’t great for a family of four, so I’m always watching the clock. After my 8‑hour shift I swing by the grocery store, grab the kids from after‑school programs, and try to squeeze in homework help before we all collapse on the couch. Weekends are a mix of yard work with my husband, church on Sunday mornings, and the occasional family barbecue with neighbors. I’ve lived in this part of Houston for the past twelve years, so I know the traffic patterns, the local schools, and the “good” and “bad” corners of the neighborhood.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between juggling two jobs, three kids’ schedules, and the constant scramble to keep the house from falling apart, voting felt like just another item on a never‑ending to‑do list. I also got the sense that neither party was really listening to people like me—talk about “racial justice” on the Democratic side while the city keeps raising property taxes, and the Republicans keep promising “lower taxes” but cutting funding for community health clinics that I depend on for work. It left me with a bitter taste, so I stayed home.  

Going into the 2026 midterms, I’m still on the fence, but I’m leaning toward sitting this one out again. Unless something dramatic hits—like a major clinic closure that puts my job at risk, or a local scandal that directly affects our neighborhood—I don’t see a reason to change my habit. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that gas prices keep climbing, making the school run and my husband’s job more expensive. I also worry about rent; even though we own our house, property taxes have been creeping up and that drags on our budget.  

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Democrats keep talking about “systemic change” but rarely bring concrete jobs or affordable housing to the table in my district. The Republicans love to brag about “tax cuts” while their policies often leave community services underfunded, which hits people like my patients hard. I’m not a political theorist; I just want a government that stops making promises and starts fixing the things that make daily life harder for families like mine.
