## Background  
I'm 84 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Malone, a small town in the North Country that sits in NY‑7. I spent most of my working life as a postal carrier, delivering mail across the county until I retired at 65. Since then my days are a mix of routine and occasional surprises. I get up around 7 a.m., brew a cup of coffee, and read the printed edition of *The New York Times* that my daughter drops off once a week. After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood—mostly to keep the joints moving and to say hello to the few neighbors I still see.  

I’m widowed; my wife passed away ten years ago. I have two adult children who live in Albany and Rochester, and they visit me a few times a year. My grandson, who’s in college, calls me on Sundays and sometimes swings by for a home‑cooked dinner. I’m on a fixed income from Social Security and a modest pension, so I keep a close eye on my budget. Most of my money goes toward utilities, my prescription meds, and the property taxes that keep the town’s roads plowed in winter. I’m involved in the local senior center, where we play cards, watch classic movies, and occasionally bring in a speaker about health topics.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate. After the pandemic hit, I saw how hard it was to get my flu shot and then a COVID booster at the local clinic, and the Democratic platform’s emphasis on protecting Medicare and expanding prescription‑drug coverage felt like a lifeline for someone my age. I also liked the talk about investing in broadband for rural areas—my grandchildren need reliable internet to stay in touch, and the old dial‑up we have is a nightmare.  

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are more attuned to the issues that affect seniors, especially with the recent push to cap insulin prices and the proposal to raise the Social Security payroll tax cap. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about cutting taxes and deregulating energy, which sounds good for businesses but doesn’t solve my immediate worries about my medication bills or the cost of heating my house each winter.  

I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my Social Security checks intact and making sure my prescription costs don’t keep climbing. I also worry about property taxes; the town council has been debating a hike to fund a new community center, and I’m not sure if that’s worth the extra burden. Overall, I see the Democratic Party as the one that remembers folks like me, while the GOP feels more focused on younger voters and corporate interests. I’m not a political scholar, but I vote with my gut and what I see affecting my day‑to‑day life.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR’s morning news podcast while I’m having coffee, and I catch the evening broadcasts from ABC, CBS, and NBC on my TV after dinner. Between them it adds up to about twelve hours a week.
