## Background
I'm 84 and I live on the Fort Berthold reservation, just outside the town of New Town in the 1st district. I spent most of my adult life working as a roughneck on the oil rigs that pop up and shut down around here. I never finished high school; the work paid the bills and I learned a lot on the job. I retired about ten years ago when the rigs started pulling back and my joints started screaming. My wife passed a few years back, and now I spend my days with my two grown‑up children who live in Bismarck and Grand Forks, and the occasional visit from my grandchildren who love to bring me fresh berries and a deck of cards.

My mornings start early with a strong cup of coffee that I brew on the old stovetop kettle. I sit on the front porch, watch the sunrise over the prairie, and listen to the wind on the grass. After that I do a little light chores—checking the mailbox, feeding the chickens, and making sure the wood stove is ready for the day. Most afternoons I go to the community center for bingo or a quilting group; the ladies there keep me laughing and the games give me a reason to get out of the house. On Wednesdays I drive a short way to the local church for the service and a potluck dinner. Evenings are usually spent in front of the TV, flipping through the news channels or watching a western movie. I keep a small list of medications and try not to miss the pharmacy trips, which are a bit of a hassle now that the nearest one is a 30‑minute drive away.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about people like me living on the reservation. The ballots all looked the same—big promises about oil, about jobs, about “respect for tribal sovereignty” that never turned into anything. I was also dealing with a broken hip that kept me in a sling for months, so getting to a polling place was a pain I wasn't willing to take. The whole process seemed like a distant game played by folks in Washington who never see the snow drifts on our roads or the rising cost of insulin.

I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic happens—like a major cut to Medicaid that would hit my medical bills, or a local controversy that directly threatens the water rights for our community—I just don't see a point. My top concerns right now are keeping my health care affordable and making sure the grocery store here stays stocked with decent food without prices shooting up. I worry about the property taxes on my modest home; the county keeps asking for more, and my fixed income doesn't stretch far. The parties? The Democrats talk a good game about tribal issues but seem to forget us once the election is over. The Republicans keep shouting about “energy independence” while the rigs keep coming and going, and they never ask how the boom and bust affect the elders who have lived through every oil cycle. Both sides feel like they're using us as a talking point, not a real constituency.

## Media Diet
I usually watch CBS in the morning while drinking coffee and then catch the nightly ABC news after dinner; a couple of afternoons I tune into NPR for longer pieces. Altogether I spend about five hours a week with those outlets.
