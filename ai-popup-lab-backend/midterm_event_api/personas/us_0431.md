## Background
I'm 45, and I live in St. Joseph, a small town on the eastern shore of Lake Michigan in the 7th district. I work full‑time as a forklift operator at the local warehouse that ships auto parts to the factories downstate. The job is steady, but the shift starts early—6 a.m. most days—so I’m usually up before sunrise, grab a coffee from the corner Denny’s, and drive the 15‑minute route to the plant. My wife, who works part‑time at the community health clinic, takes care of our two kids, ages 9 and 12. On weekends we’re usually at the high school football field, either watching the Panthers play or helping coach the junior league. I’m also part of the tribal council for our local band; we meet once a month to discuss cultural events and the occasional grant for preserving our language. Most evenings I’m home by 7 p.m., eat dinner with the family, and then either watch a game on TV or help the kids with homework. When I have a spare hour, I like to tinker with my old Chevy truck, fixing the brakes or swapping out the stereo. The cost of living is low enough that we can keep a modest house, but any surprise bill—like a busted furnace in winter—gets us worrying.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on energy and jobs matched my own concerns. The oil and gas boom in the region gave a lot of people, including me, a sense that the GOP was protecting our livelihoods. I also liked the talk about cutting taxes and reducing regulation on the warehouse I work at. The Democratic platform seemed focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that felt like they’d hurt the manufacturing jobs that keep my community afloat. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on the news and heard a lot of talk about the “inflation crisis” and how Democrats are pushing for higher taxes. I’m still convinced that the Republicans are the better bet for keeping my paycheck stable, so I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the price at the pump—gas has been climbing faster than my wages—and property taxes, which keep creeping up on the little house we own. I think the Democrats are out of touch with folks like me; they keep promising free college and universal health care, but they never talk about keeping the factories open or protecting the Great Lakes from over‑regulation. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least seem to understand that a working‑class family in a small Michigan town needs steady jobs and affordable living costs. I’m not a political scholar, I just want my kids to have a decent future without having to move away for work.
