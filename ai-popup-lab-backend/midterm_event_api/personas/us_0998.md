## Background
I'm 84 and I live in Riverton, Wyoming, out on the edge of the Wind River Reservation where most of the houses are spaced out and the streets are just dirt. I spent most of my working life driving a semi‑truck for a regional hauling company, hauling feed for ranches and construction materials across the state. I retired about ten years ago when my knees started giving out, so now my days are a lot slower. My wife, Rosa, is still pretty spry; we’ve been married for fifty‑seven years and we both love the little garden we keep behind the house—tomatoes, beans, and a few peppers that remind us of our younger days in New Mexico. Our two kids live in Denver and Salt Lake City, and we get a call from the granddaughter once a week, usually when she’s home from college and wants to hear about the “old days.” I get up around 7 a.m., make a coffee, read the paper (the printed edition of a national newspaper) while the radio plays classic country. After that I either tend to the garden, walk the dog, or head down to the senior center for bingo and a potluck. In the evenings I sit on the porch, watch the news on TV, and sometimes scroll through a few articles on my tablet before turning in around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who depend on a steady Social Security check and want low taxes on my modest pension. The candidate promised to keep the federal government from reaching into my retirement money and to protect the right to own a gun, which matters out here where we have to protect our livestock from predators. I also liked the tough talk on the border; even though I’m Hispanic, I’ve seen the impact of illegal immigration on local jobs and felt the rhetoric resonated with my own experience of coming here legally and working hard.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top worries are health‑care costs—every time I go to the clinic the co‑pay seems to creep up—and the price of gasoline, which makes it harder for Rosa to drive to the doctor and for me to run errands. I also keep an eye on property taxes because the county has been talking about raising them to fund a new road project, and that would bite into my fixed income. When I think about the parties, the Republicans feel like they understand the need to keep government small and let people keep what they earn. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to want to raise taxes and push policies that don’t fit the rural, self‑reliant lifestyle we live. I’m not a political scholar, but I know which side seems to be looking out for my pocketbook and my way of life, so I’ll be casting my ballot for the Republican candidates this time around.
