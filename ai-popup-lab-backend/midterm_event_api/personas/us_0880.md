## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest ranch house on the outskirts of St. George, right near the foothills of the red rocks. I spent most of my working life as a carpenter, building homes and fixing up the old storefronts downtown. I never finished a degree, but I took a few night classes in drafting when I was in my 30s, so I can read a blueprint without squinting. My wife, Margaret, is 81 and still tends the garden; we grew up together in a farming community and moved here when the air got too dry up north. Our three kids are all grown and live in different states, so most evenings are just the two of us watching the news, feeding the chickens, and arguing over who left the lights on. I get up early, shuffle to the kitchen, brew a strong cup of coffee, and read the paper (the printed edition, not the online version) while the radio plays classic country. After breakfast I usually take a short walk around the block, checking on the neighbor’s fence and saying hello to anyone I see. In the afternoons I either work on a small woodworking project in the garage or sit on the porch with a crossword puzzle. On Sundays I attend the local Baptist church, where I’ve known most of the congregation for decades.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best protected the values I grew up with: limited government, strong national defense, and keeping taxes low for retirees like me. I liked the candidate’s promise to keep federal land management policies from getting too heavy-handed, which matters a lot when you own a piece of land out here. Over the past two years I’ve watched the headlines and heard the same old arguments—immigration, crime, the economy—repeat themselves, and I’m still convinced the GOP is the safer bet for keeping my property taxes from climbing. That said, I’m not a policy wonk; most of my opinions come from what I hear on the shows I watch and the conversations at the senior center. I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms, mainly to keep the Democrats from getting a foothold in my district. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the price of groceries; a sudden spike in either would force me to dip into my modest savings. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats; they seem more interested in identity politics than on fixing the potholes on Highway 91 or protecting the water rights we rely on for irrigation. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about “common‑sense” solutions that line up with what I’ve always believed.
