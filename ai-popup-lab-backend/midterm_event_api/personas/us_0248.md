## Background
I'm 49 and I live in Blackfoot, a small town on the Snake River in Idaho's 2nd congressional district. I grew up on a farm a few miles west of here, but after high school I moved to Pocatello for a couple of years to take some community‑college classes in business and hope for a better job. I never finished a degree, but the courses helped me land a full‑time gig as a stock clerk at the local grocery store. The job is steady, the hours are mostly mornings and early afternoons, and it lets me be home for dinner.

I'm married to Mike, a mechanic at the same store's auto service department. We have two kids: Jenna, 22, who just started her sophomore year at Idaho State, and Kyle, 17, a senior at the high school across the street. Most weekdays I drop Jenna off at the campus shuttle, make sure Kyle catches the school bus, then head to work. After my shift I pick up Kyle from practice, and we all eat dinner together around 6 p.m. On weekends we do chores around the house, mow the lawn, and sometimes go hunting or fishing with the guys from the community club. Money is tight enough that we keep a close eye on the grocery bill and the mortgage, but we manage.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus. The candidates in the Senate race kept talking about the same national stuff—climate policy, immigration—while I was worrying about whether the price of diesel would let me keep the tractor running for the next planting season. Between long shifts, kids' schedules, and trying to keep the house from falling apart, voting just didn't make the cut. I also thought my vote wouldn't change anything; the district leans heavily Republican, and the Democrats seemed like they were trying to push policies that would make my grocery bills higher.

I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic happens—like a major factory closing in the area or a local scandal that directly hits our community—I just don't see the point of carving out time to go to the polls. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax bill that's due next spring. Both hit my budget hard, and I keep thinking about how to stretch the paycheck a little further.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as a bunch of city folks who talk about student loans and green energy but don't understand what it's like to own a piece of land that you have to work on yourself. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep promising lower taxes and less regulation, which sounds good until they start talking about cutting funding for rural schools and roads. I get annoyed by both sides, but I lean a little toward the GOP because at least they talk about keeping taxes low, even if they forget about the potholes on our back roads.
