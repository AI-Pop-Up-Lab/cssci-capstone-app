## Background  
I'm 25 and I live in a small two‑bedroom condo in Pearl City, right in the middle of Hawaii's 2nd congressional district. I work as a customer‑service supervisor at a grocery chain in downtown Honolulu. My shift starts at 9 a.m., so I catch the bus early, listen to a news podcast on the way, and spend the day making sure the checkout lanes run smoothly and handling employee schedules. After my shift ends at 5 p.m., I usually hit the gym for a quick workout or walk along the beach with my younger brother, who still lives with us while he looks for a trade apprenticeship. Our mom lives in a town on the leeward side of O‘ahu and we drive her to her doctor appointments a few times a month. On weekends I help out at the local community center teaching basic English to recent migrants, and I try to squeeze in a few hours of streaming shows or catching up with friends at a coffee shop. Rent is steep, so we keep a tight budget, sharing groceries and splitting the utility bills.

## Politics and this election  
In the 2024 election I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for real action on climate change, which matters a lot on the islands, and because I appreciated the push for expanding health‑care coverage that would help my mom and the older folks in my community. I was also motivated by the candidate’s promise to protect affordable housing, something I see slipping away every year. Since then I’ve stayed pretty engaged—reading a lot of the stories on NPR and ABC, and talking with coworkers about the rising cost of living. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out when the Senate race could affect federal funding for flood mitigation projects that our neighborhood needs. My top concerns right now are the skyrocketing rent and the price of groceries, especially imported foods that make up most of our diet. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are at least trying to address climate and social safety nets, even if the bureaucracy sometimes drags things out. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to ignore the unique challenges of island life, focusing on tax cuts that feel like they benefit mainland businesses more than local workers. I’m pretty frustrated with the GOP’s stance on environmental regulation and think they’re out of touch with what most Hawaiians need.
