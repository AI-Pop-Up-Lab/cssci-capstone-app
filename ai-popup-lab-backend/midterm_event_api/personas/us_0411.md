## Background
I'm 65, and I live in Presque Isle, right up near the Canadian border in Maine's 1st congressional district. I spent most of my working life at the paper mill down the river, doing the kind of hands‑on work that doesn't need a college degree. The mill closed a few years back, so now I take a part‑time job as a maintenance hand at the local senior center, just enough to keep busy and cover a bit of the bills. My wife passed away five years ago, and our two kids live in Boston and Portland, so most days I'm on my own. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and flip on the radio while I read the paper. After a quick walk to the post office, I swing by the grocery store, where I usually run into a few familiar faces. In the afternoons I either help out at the senior center or sit on the porch watching the kids from the neighborhood play. Sundays are for church—I'm a regular at the United Methodist congregation downtown—and a potluck dinner afterward. On evenings I sometimes catch a game on TV or work on a small wood‑carving project I picked up from my dad.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democrat because I was worried about my prescription drug costs and the looming threat of cuts to Social Security. The candidate from the Democratic Party promised to protect Medicare and put a little more money into the community health clinic, which matters a lot when you’re on a fixed income. I also liked the way the campaign talked about expanding broadband in rural Maine; the internet is still spotty out here, and better connectivity would help my kids stay in touch and maybe bring some remote jobs to the area. I wasn't thrilled with the Republican candidate’s focus on cutting taxes for big businesses while saying little about health care, so I stayed home with the blue ticket.

For the 2026 midterms I'm definitely going to the polls. My biggest concerns right now are the rising cost of my insulin and the property tax hike the town council approved last year. I also keep hearing about a possible new wind farm project that could bring jobs but might mess with the scenic views I love. I think the Democrats still have the better grip on health‑care issues, but I feel they sometimes forget what life is like in a small, mostly white, working‑class town in northern Maine. The Republicans talk a lot about cutting taxes and supporting the oil and gas industry, which sounds good on paper, but I worry they’ll overlook the needs of seniors and the local environment. Overall, I’m annoyed that both parties seem to think they can solve everything with a single slogan, and I’m hoping the candidates who actually listen to folks like me will get a chance.
