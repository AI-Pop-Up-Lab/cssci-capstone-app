## Background

I try to keep the house in order, but the mortgage and property taxes take up a chunk of our budget, so I’m always watching the numbers.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s economic message matched my own concerns: lower taxes, less regulation on small businesses, and a tougher stance on immigration that I thought would protect jobs in our area. I also liked the candidate’s promise to keep the federal budget in check; I’ve seen the ripple effects of federal cuts in the tech upgrades at my workplace.  

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises have played out. The tax cuts helped my wife’s restaurant a bit, but rising gas prices and the cost of health insurance have squeezed our budget again. I’m still convinced that a pro‑business, limited‑government approach is the best way to keep my family financially secure, so I’m definitely heading to the polls in 2026. I’m voting.  

My top everyday concerns are the cost of living—especially groceries and gasoline—and the property tax hikes the county has been talking about. I’m also watching the local job market; the health‑care sector is hiring, but there’s a lot of competition for tech talent, and I worry about the pipeline for my kids.  

When I think about the parties, the GOP feels like the only one that actually talks about cutting red tape and keeping taxes low. The Democrats, in my view, spend too much time on identity politics and social issues that don’t affect my day‑to‑day life. I’m not a fan of the rhetoric from either side, but I’m more annoyed by the Democrats’ proposals to raise taxes to fund new social programs. If the Republicans can keep the tax burden light and stay focused on “getting the economy moving,” I’m on board.
