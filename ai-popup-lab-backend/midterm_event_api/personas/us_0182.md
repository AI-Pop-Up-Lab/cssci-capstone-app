## Background
I'm 70 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Lawrenceville, right in the middle of GA‑10. I spent most of my adult life working as a sales associate at a regional department store and later managed the floor for a few years before retiring five years ago. My husband and I own the place together; our kids are grown and live in Atlanta, and we get a handful of grandkids over the holidays. Most mornings I start with a cup of coffee on the porch while checking the mail, then I tend to my garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few citrus trees that remind me of my family's roots in Mexico. After lunch I usually take a walk around the neighborhood, stop by the local grocery for a few items, and sometimes swing by the community center to help organize the senior bingo night. On Sundays I attend the Spanish‑language mass at the nearby church, catch up with old friends, and later in the week I volunteer a few hours at the food pantry. I drive a reliable sedan, keep the radio on low, and try to stay active enough to manage the stairs without getting winded.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s focus on lower taxes and a tougher stance on illegal immigration lined up with what mattered to me at my age—keeping more of my retirement income and seeing stronger border enforcement. I also liked the candidate’s promises to protect Social Security from any cuts. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises have played out. The economy feels a bit shaky with rising prescription drug prices, and I’m not thrilled with how the other side talks about issues like climate change without offering concrete fixes for everyday folks like me. Still, I think the Democrats have been pushing a lot of cultural battles that distract from the real problems I face daily.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the cost of my prescription meds and the property taxes that keep going up on my modest home. I also worry about the potholes on the main road that make it harder for me to get to the pharmacy. When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones more likely to keep taxes low and stay firm on immigration, while the Democrats seem more caught up in identity politics and big‑government solutions that don’t translate into affordable health care for seniors. I’m not an activist, but I’ll cast my ballot for the candidate who promises to keep my paycheck steady and the roads fixed.
