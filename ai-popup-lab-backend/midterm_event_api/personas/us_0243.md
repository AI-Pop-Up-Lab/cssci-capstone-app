## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I heard that the GOP candidate was the only one who talked about keeping taxes low and protecting the right to own a gun. I don’t follow politics closely, but the talk at work and on the internet made it sound like the Democrats were trying to raise taxes and push stuff that didn’t matter to me. I also liked the candidate’s tough talk on immigration and felt that it matched what my friends were saying about “keeping our country for us.” Since then, I haven’t dug deep into any policy papers; I just keep hearing the same old arguments on the sites I watch. I’m planning to go to the polls again in the 2026 midterms because I don’t want the other side to take over the local offices that affect my paycheck and the cost of gas. My biggest worries right now are finding a stable job that pays enough to move out of my mom’s house and dealing with the rising price of groceries—everything seems to cost more than it did a few years ago. I also worry that if the Democrats win more seats they’ll push for things like higher taxes or stricter gun rules that would hit people like me harder. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about “getting the economy moving” and “protecting our freedoms,” even if I don’t understand every detail. I’m not a big fan of the political theater, but I feel more comfortable with the party that seems to promise me a chance to keep my low‑cost lifestyle.
