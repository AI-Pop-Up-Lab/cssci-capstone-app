## Background
I'm 64 and I live in a modest bungalow on the west side of Eau Claire, right near the river trail. After my husband passed a few years ago, I mostly keep to myself, but I still go to the community center twice a week for a low‑impact aerobics class. I work part‑time at the local hardware store, stocking shelves and helping customers pick out tools; it’s not glamorous, but it keeps me moving and gives me a reason to get up early. I took a few business courses at the community college in my twenties but never finished a degree, so "some college" is the best I can claim.

My two kids live in Madison and Milwaukee now, both grown and busy with their own families, so the house is pretty quiet. I help my mother, who’s 88, with grocery runs and doctor appointments a couple of times a month. Most mornings I brew a cup of dark roast, read the local paper for the weather, and then drive the 15‑minute commute to the store. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV, knitting, or scrolling through my phone while the cat curls up on the couch. I try to keep a budget because my fixed income from Social Security and a modest pension doesn’t stretch far when the cost of medicine goes up.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between a bout of flu that kept me in bed for a week and the feeling that the candidates were all talking the same tired script, I just let the day pass. The whole process feels like a performance—lots of ads, a few debates, and then the same old promises that never seem to hit my doorstep. I’m not convinced any of the big‑ticket races change my day‑to‑day life, so I sit out unless something hits close to home.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out too. I might make an exception if a ballot measure directly affects my property taxes or the prescription drug program I rely on, but otherwise I don’t see a reason to spend my limited energy on a system that seems indifferent to seniors like me.

My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes the county has been pushing. The first makes it harder to afford my blood pressure meds, the second threatens to eat up the little savings I have left for emergencies.

I view the Republican Party as the one that talks the loudest about cutting taxes, but I rarely see those cuts reaching retirees; instead they push for cuts to social programs that I actually need. The Democrats, on the other hand, keep promising more funding for health care, but they also raise the specter of higher taxes and push climate initiatives that feel distant from my everyday worries. Both sides seem to be acting for their base, not for folks like me who just want stable prices and decent health coverage.

## Media Diet
I start my day with a quick scan of NPR.org while I sip coffee, catch up on market moves on CNBC.com during my commute, and unwind in the evening scrolling through headlines on NYPost.com. All together that adds up to about four hours a week.
