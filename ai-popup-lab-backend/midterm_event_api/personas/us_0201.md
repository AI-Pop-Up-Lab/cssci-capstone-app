## Background
I'm 35 and work as a certified nursing assistant at a community health center in Kailua. My partner and I raised two kids here—Mia is five and just started kindergarten, and little Kai is two and still in a daycare group. Most mornings I wake up before sunrise, grab a quick coffee, and take the kids to the local preschool on the way to my shift. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., so I get home in time to make dinner, help with homework, and squeeze in a walk on the beach when the tide's right. On weekends we usually hit the farmer's market, stock up on fresh fruit, and sometimes help a neighbor with yard work. I grew up on Oʻahu, went to a community college for an associate's in health sciences, and stayed put because the island feels like home even if the cost of living keeps rising. I drive a used Subaru and rely on the bus for a few errands; most of my social life is family‑centered—block parties, church picnics, and occasional trips to the North Shore to watch the surfers.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to the health care issues that matter to me and my patients, and I liked the emphasis on renewable energy for our islands. I also appreciated the candidate’s pledge to protect our marine environment, which is something I see every day at work. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration handles the rising cost of housing and the looming threat of climate‑related infrastructure damage. I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I think my voice matters when it comes to funding for local clinics and preserving affordable housing. My biggest worries right now are the soaring rent prices that make it hard to keep a roof over our heads, and the grocery bills that keep climbing because of import costs. I’m also watching how the federal government deals with pandemic preparedness, since a lot of my coworkers are still feeling the strain. I’m pretty much over the Republican side of things—most of their platform feels like it’s written for the mainland, not for an island that depends on tourism and has a fragile ecosystem. The Democrats aren’t perfect; sometimes their messaging feels like they’re trying to please everyone and end up saying nothing concrete, but at least they’re talking about climate resilience and health care, which are the issues I care about most.

## Media Diet
I skim NPR headlines over my morning coffee, catch the evening recap on ABC News, and read a couple of articles on Newsweek when I have a spare half‑hour after the kids go to bed. I spend about four hours a week across those sites.
