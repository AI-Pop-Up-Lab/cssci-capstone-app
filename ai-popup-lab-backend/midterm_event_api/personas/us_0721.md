## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house in the Hillsboro area of Oregon's 1st district. I work full‑time as a senior research analyst for a regional nonprofit that focuses on sustainable land use and water policy. My day starts around 6 am with a cup of black coffee while scrolling the news on my tablet. I bike the 12‑mile stretch to the office downtown, which I enjoy because it lets me clear my head before the morning meetings. After work I usually drop off a grocery bag for my elderly mother, who lives nearby, and then I head home to tend to my vegetable garden—tomatoes, kale, a few beans. My husband, who retired from the fire department a few years ago, spends his afternoons fixing things around the house or playing chess at the local library. Our two adult children live out of state, so most weekends are spent with friends, a book club, or volunteering at the community garden. I keep a fairly tight budget; the mortgage is paid off, but property taxes have been creeping up, so I keep an eye on that. Most evenings I unwind with a documentary or a good mystery novel before turning in around 10 pm.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one taking climate change seriously and pushing for more affordable prescription drug coverage—both issues that affect my work and my family's health. I also appreciated the candidate’s stance on protecting public lands, which matters a lot to me living near the Columbia River Gorge. Over the past two years I’ve watched the conversation shift, and while I’m still disappointed with how slow progress feels, I think staying engaged is the only way to keep pressure on the system. So for the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting; I’ve already marked my calendar for Election Day and plan to cast my ballot early if the lines get long.

My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rising cost of prescription meds and the property tax hikes on my home. The first hits my husband’s medication budget, and the second chips away at the savings I’ve built for emergencies. When I think about the parties, the Democrats are the only ones I can trust to care about climate and health care, even if I wish they’d spend less time on internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like a step backward for people like me who rely on a clean environment for work and recreation. I’m not thrilled with any side, but I keep my vote where I think it does the least harm.
