## Background
I'm 30 and work as a data analyst for a regional health‑care provider in Sioux Falls. I grew up in a bilingual household; my parents came from Mexico and ran a small grocery store in Rapid City before we moved east when I was a kid. After finishing a master's in public health at the University of South Dakota, I got the analyst job and now spend most weekdays crunching numbers on patient outcomes and insurance trends. I rent a two‑bedroom apartment downtown with my partner, who teaches at a community college, and we share the space with a rescued pit bull named Luna. Mornings start with a quick espresso at the corner coffee shop, then a commute on the bus while scrolling through the news on my phone. I usually work from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., then hit the gym a few times a week or meet friends for a game of pickup basketball. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the big box store, helping my parents with paperwork over FaceTime, and catching a movie or a local music show when I can.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing for broader health‑care coverage and more realistic immigration reform—issues that hit close to home for my family and my job. The candidate's promise to expand Medicaid in South Dakota and invest in rural broadband also mattered; I saw those policies as directly tied to the data I work with. Since then I've kept an eye on how those promises are playing out, and I'm still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for the things I care about, even if I think they sometimes wobble on fiscal discipline. I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don't have any major reason to sit this one out. My top everyday worries are the rising rent prices that make it harder to stay in the city and the cost of prescription drugs that affect my partner's students and my own health plan. I see the Republican Party as largely dismissive of the health‑care safety net and hostile to immigration reform, while the Democrats, in my view, are at least trying to address those gaps, even if they sometimes overpromise. I’m not a political scholar, just someone who wants a stable paycheck and affordable living, so I gravitate toward the side that seems more likely to protect those basics.
