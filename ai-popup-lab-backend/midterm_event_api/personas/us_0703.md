## Background
I'm 20 and I live in Ponca City, right in the middle of Oklahoma's 3rd district. I left high school after sophomore year to help my mom cover the bills, so I haven't finished a diploma. Most days I clock in at the local grocery store for a 9‑to‑5 shift, bagging groceries and ringing up customers. After work I swing by my younger sister's apartment—she's 16 and still in school—to make dinner and do the laundry. On weekends I pick up extra hours at a nearby fast‑food joint, because the rent on our two‑bedroom place is creeping up and my mom's work schedule is erratic. I drive a beat‑up sedan that my dad gave me when I turned 18; it's not great on gas, but it gets me to the store and back. When I have a spare hour, I scroll TikTok or watch short clips on YouTube, mostly music videos and funny skits. I don't have a college plan right now; the idea of going back feels like a far‑off thing, especially with the money we’re trying to stretch each month.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing felt like a waste of time. Between working two jobs, taking care of my sister, and trying to keep up with rent, I couldn't fit a polling place into my schedule. I also get the sense that the candidates are all talking about stuff that doesn't touch my daily grind—big‑ticket issues that feel distant from a grocery clerk's reality. The ads were more about national drama than anything that would help my mom pay for her medication or keep my sister's school supplies stocked. That feeling of being ignored made me just stay home.

I'm not planning to head to the polls in the 2026 midterms either—I'm sitting this one out. The same problems that kept me away last time are still here, and unless something dramatic hits our neighborhood—like a factory closing or a sudden rent hike that forces us out—I don't see a reason to bother. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with the grocery bills and making sure the electricity stays on during the winter. I also worry about the local job market; a lot of the men I know are getting laid off from the oil fields, and the few openings at the store are barely enough to cover the basics.

When I think about the parties, I feel pretty sour about both. The Democrats seem to talk a lot about immigration and social programs, but when I walk into the store, I don't see any real help for Hispanic families like mine. The Republicans keep shouting about border security and tax cuts, but those cuts never trickle down to the people who are already struggling to pay for a decent apartment. It feels like they're both playing a game where the rules are written for someone else, and I'm just a pawn that gets moved around.
