## Background
I'm 79, and I live in Martinez, a small town along the Carquinez Strait in California's 13th congressional district. I grew up in Oakland and moved here after marrying my late husband, who worked at the local shipyard. I earned an associate's degree in medical assisting back in the 1970s and spent 30 years as a nursing aide at a nearby assisted‑living facility. I retired five years ago, but I still help out part‑time at the senior center, running the bingo games and teaching basic computer skills to fellow retirees.

My family consists of two adult children who live in the Bay Area and three grandchildren who visit every summer. Most mornings I start with a strong cup of coffee on the porch while watching the river traffic go by. I take a short walk to the nearby park, then swing by the grocery store to pick up fresh produce and a few canned goods. Afternoons are usually spent at the senior center or at church, where I’m part of the choir. Evenings are quiet—sometimes I catch up on a TV drama or read the newspaper before heading to bed around 10 p.m. The routine is simple, but it keeps me busy enough to feel useful.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about protecting Social Security and Medicare, which are lifelines for people my age. The Republican candidates kept talking about cutting taxes for big corporations, and that just didn’t sit right with me when I see my friends struggling to afford their prescriptions. I also liked the Democratic push for expanding broadband in rural areas; my sister lives out in the Central Valley and finally got decent internet after the 2024 elections.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the stability of my Social Security checks. I’ve also been hearing about property tax hikes in Contra Costa County, and that worries me because my fixed income doesn’t stretch as far as it used to. I tend to view the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to think about seniors, even if they sometimes miss the mark. The Republicans feel like they’re more interested in deregulating industries and cutting programs that help older folks, which makes me uneasy. I’m not a political analyst, but I know where my priorities lie, and that’s why I’m heading to the polls again.
