## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the east side of Tulsa, not far from the Arkansas River. I spent most of my working life as a clerk for the county clerk’s office, a job I held until I retired at 65 after taking a few night classes in business administration—nothing beyond some college credits. My husband passed away five years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live out of state. I get a weekly call from my daughter in Oklahoma City and a video chat with my grandson every other Sunday.

My days start early. I brew a cup of coffee, flip on the local radio, and read the printed newspaper while the dog, a rescued mutt named Scout, waits by the kitchen table. After a short walk around the neighborhood, I head to the senior center where I volunteer once a week helping with the bingo night setup. Most afternoons I’m at the grocery store picking up a few items—especially trying to find a good price on the brand of oatmeal I like. Evenings are usually spent watching the news on the TV, then knitting or doing a crossword before bed. I take my medication on schedule, and once a month I drive to the pharmacy in a small sedan that’s been my reliable ride for years.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate talked straight about keeping taxes low for retirees like me and promised to protect the oil and gas jobs that still keep a lot of people in our district employed. I also liked the tough talk on the border; I’ve seen a few friends from Mexico lose jobs when factories shut down, and I figured stricter immigration would help the local economy. I’m not a policy wonk, but the message felt like it was looking out for my pocketbook and the community I’ve lived in all my life.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. I’m not thrilled about the whole political circus, but I feel it’s important to keep a voice in the process, especially when the Democrats keep pushing for higher taxes on Social Security benefits. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that the local hospital is talking about cutting some services because of budget shortfalls. I also worry about property taxes creeping up; my fixed pension doesn’t stretch as far as it used to. I see the Republican Party as the one that understands seniors and small‑town folks, while I view the Democrats as more focused on big‑city issues and a “one‑size‑fits‑all” approach that never seems to consider the cost of living for people my age.
