## Background
I'm 55, and I live in Prattville, a small town just east of Montgomery in Alabama's 7th District. I work full‑time as the floor manager at a local grocery store. I never finished my associate's degree—life got in the way when I had my first child, so I took a few business classes and then went straight into work. My husband, who retired from the manufacturing plant a few years ago, helps out around the house and does a lot of the yard work. We have two kids: our daughter is in her senior year of college out in Atlanta, and our son just got a job as a mechanic at a nearby shop and still lives with us for the rent.

My days start early. I’m usually up by 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and catch a quick glance at the news on my phone while the kids get ready for school. I head to the store around 7 a.m., do the opening duties, and spend the next eight hours on stocking, customer service, and supervising a crew of part‑time workers. After my shift I pick up the kids from school, help with homework, and we all eat dinner together around 6 p.m. On Sundays I go to our local Catholic church, then we have a family lunch that drags into the afternoon. On the occasional weekend I’m at the community center helping with a neighborhood clean‑up or just running errands at the big box store. Money is tight enough that I keep a close eye on every bill, but we manage.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping my grocery store jobs stable and protecting my family’s finances. The talk about border security, lower taxes, and keeping the government from meddling in small businesses resonated with me. I also liked the candidate’s promise to keep the cost of gasoline down, which matters when I drive my kids around for school and work. I didn’t feel the Democrats were offering anything that would help a small‑town family like ours; the tax proposals seemed like they’d hit the middle class harder, and the talk about big‑government solutions just felt out of touch.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. I’m planning to cast my ballot for the Republican Senate and House candidates because I still think they’re the ones who understand what a working‑class family needs: cheaper groceries, lower property taxes, and a steady job market. My top concerns right now are the rising price of food and the creeping property tax bill that the county council has been discussing. I also worry about health‑care costs—my husband’s prescription meds have gone up a lot in the past year, and I can’t afford a big surprise.

My view of the parties is pretty clear. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about keeping things simple and not over‑regulating small businesses. The Democrats seem more interested in pushing a social agenda that doesn’t reflect the values of most people in my town. I get annoyed when the news talks about “progress” without mentioning how that progress will affect a family trying to make ends meet. I’m not a political scholar; I just want someone who will keep my grocery store open and my family fed.
