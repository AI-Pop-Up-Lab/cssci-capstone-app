## Background
I'm 35 and I work the night shift as a cashier at a grocery store in Mobile, Alabama, right in the heart of the 1st congressional district. I don't have a high school diploma; I left school after ninth grade to help my family when my mom got sick. My mom still lives in the same house, and I rent a small upstairs unit above a laundromat to keep the rent low. My days start early with a quick coffee from a corner stall, then I head to the store for the 2 p.m. opening. After the night shift ends around 9 a.m., I swing by the laundromat to drop off my mom's laundry, then I’m home for a quick shower and a bowl of instant noodles before I have to be up again for the next shift. On my days off I try to catch up on any paperwork for the food assistance program I’m on, and I spend a lot of the evening watching TV or scrolling on my phone while the house is quiet. I don’t have kids, but my older sister lives a few miles away and we text each other often about bills and groceries. My free time is mostly spent at the local community center where I help with a weekly bingo night for seniors.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t cast a ballot in the 2024 election. Between working two jobs, taking care of my mom, and trying to stretch every paycheck, voting felt like another thing I couldn’t fit into the schedule. I also feel like the candidates all promise the same thing—more “change” that never reaches my kitchen table. The whole process seems set up for people who have the time and the money to get to the polls, and I end up feeling like my voice doesn’t matter. Because of that, I’m planning to sit this one out too, unless something really hits close to home, like a local factory closing or a new law that makes my mom’s Medicaid harder to get.

My top everyday worries are keeping the rent paid and making sure there’s enough money for groceries. When I look at the two big parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Democrats; they keep talking about “green energy” and “student loan forgiveness” while I’m trying to afford a loaf of bread. The Republicans seem more aware of the cost‑of‑living squeeze, but I’m skeptical of their promises about tax cuts because I never see any of that cash in my paycheck. Overall, I just want someone to stop the bickering and focus on the things that actually affect people like me.
