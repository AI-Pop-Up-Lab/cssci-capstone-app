## Background  
I'm 75 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of La Crosse, right in the middle of Wisconsin's 3rd district. I never finished high school; after my teens I started working at the local grocery store and later helped out at the tribal community center doing maintenance and cleaning. The job never paid much, but it let me stay on my feet and meet folks from the reservation and the town. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my daughter moved to Madison with her own kids. I still get visits from my grandchildren a few times a month, and when they're here I cook the old family recipes—wild rice soup, frybread, that sort of thing.  

My days are simple. I get up around 6 am, make a strong cup of coffee, and sit at the kitchen table while the radio plays a mix of classic country and some news snippets. After breakfast I take a walk down the main street, stop at the post office to mail a few letters, and swing by the pharmacy to pick up my blood pressure meds. In the afternoons I either tend to the garden—tomatoes, beans, a few hardy greens—or I help the community center set up for the next powwow or cultural event. Evenings are mostly TV time; I flip through the channels, catch the local news, then settle in with a program on one of the national outlets I follow. I don’t drive much anymore; my daughter dropped me off at the senior center a few times a week for bingo and a knitting circle, which is where I spend a good chunk of my social time.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party spoke to the things that matter to me: keeping taxes low, protecting my right to hunt and fish on the reservation lands, and staying away from big‑government programs that seem to waste money. I also liked the candidate’s promise to support small businesses like the grocery where I used to work, and I trusted the promise to keep the military strong—my brother served, and that means a lot to my family.  

I'm definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. I'm not one to sit on the sidelines unless I think the stakes are low, and this time there are a few things that keep me interested: the price of my prescription drugs keeps creeping up, and I hear talk about new Medicare changes that could hit seniors hard. Property taxes in La Crosse have gone up too, and I'm already feeling the squeeze on my fixed income. Those two issues—healthcare costs and local taxes—are what I think about most when I hear about the campaigns.  

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward. The Democrats seem to want to raise taxes and push policies that ignore the traditions of people like me, especially when they talk about cutting hunting rights or imposing new regulations that feel like they come from far‑away bureaucrats. The Republicans, for the most part, talk about preserving the way of life we grew up with and keeping the government from overreaching. I know not every Republican is perfect, but at least they’re talking about the things I care about. I get annoyed when the news keeps shouting about “progress” without showing how it helps folks on the reservation or in small towns.

## Media Diet  
I usually start the day scrolling through Breitbart on my phone while I sip coffee, catch up on the headlines from the New York Post around lunch, and then settle in with the CBS Evening News after dinner. That adds up to about nine hours a week between the three sites.
