## Background  
I'm 79 and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Lakewood, right in the heart of Colorado's 4th district. I grew up in a farm town near Pueblo, moved to Denver for a couple of years to take night classes at a community college, and learned enough to land a job as a clerk‑type at the local grocery store. I stayed there for about thirty years, eventually moving up to the night shift supervisor slot before I retired at 65.  

My husband passed away ten years ago, and my only daughter lives about an hour away in Aurora with her own kids. We talk on the phone most evenings, and she visits a few times a month, bringing fresh tortillas and a sack of groceries. I have a small circle of neighbors—mostly other older folks—who I meet for coffee at the senior center on Tuesdays. We trade stories about the old days, complain about the rising cost of medicine, and sometimes play bingo.  

My daily routine is simple. I get up around 6 a.m., make a cup of coffee, and read the newspaper headlines on my tablet while the kettle whistles. After a quick walk around the block, I stop by the pharmacy to pick up my blood pressure meds, then head back to tend to my garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few cilantro plants. Lunch is usually a casserole I made last night, and in the afternoon I either watch a telenovela in Spanish or listen to a local radio talk show while knitting. I try to get to the senior center at least once a week for a light exercise class and a chance to be out of the house. When I’m not at home, I drive my old sedan to the nearby Walmart for groceries; the parking lot is my little social arena where I exchange a word with the cashier and the security guard.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping taxes low and protecting the border, things that mattered to me after watching prices climb and hearing about illegal immigration affecting my community. I also liked the candidate’s promises to cut back on federal regulations that make it harder for small businesses like the grocery store where I worked to stay open. I didn’t feel like the Democrats were listening to folks like me who have been working hard all their lives and just want a stable retirement.  

I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the cost of my prescription drugs and the property taxes on my house, which have jumped a few hundred dollars each year. I also worry about the local school district’s budget because my granddaughter will be heading there soon, and I want her to have decent resources.  

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward. The Republicans still seem the better choice for keeping my wallet from bleeding, even if I don’t agree with everything they say—especially the younger candidates who sound like they’re trying to be cool. The Democrats, in my opinion, push too many tax hikes and want the government to control everything, from schools to healthcare, which feels like a step backward. I’m not a political scholar; I just go with what feels right for my pocket and my family.
