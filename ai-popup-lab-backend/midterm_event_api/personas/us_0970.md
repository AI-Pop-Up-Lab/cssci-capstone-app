## Background  
I'm 45 and I live in the suburbs of Wheeling, right on the edge of the Ohio River. I work full‑time as a customer‑service manager at a regional health‑care provider; the job got me here after I finished a business degree at West Virginia University and a couple of years as a sales rep for a medical‑equipment company. My husband is a forklift operator at the nearby steel mill, and we have two kids—Jenna, 16, and Luis, 12. Mornings are a blur of coffee, a quick check of the family calendar on my phone, and a 30‑minute drive to the office where I spend most of the day fielding calls, sorting insurance paperwork, and trying to keep the team on schedule.

Evenings are family time: dinner on the porch, helping Jenna with her AP English essays and Luis with his math homework, then a little TV before bed. On weekends we hit the local farmers’ market, do a bit of yard work, and occasionally drive out to the mountains for a hike. I’m not a big fan of big‑city life, but I do enjoy the occasional concert in the downtown theater when the kids are away at camp. My parents live a couple of hours away in Huntington, and we talk on Sundays about everything from church to the price of diesel.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate talked about keeping taxes low for small businesses like the one my husband works at, and I felt the Democratic platform was pushing for higher regulation that could hurt the local manufacturing jobs. The campaign ads about “energy independence” and protecting the coal and gas industries also resonated; I’ve seen a lot of families in our town lose steady work when mines shut down. I wasn’t thrilled with any of the candidates, but the Republican message felt more in line with my day‑to‑day worries about the cost of living and keeping the mill open.

I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising price of gasoline and the uncertainty around health‑care costs for my family. If the current administration’s policies keep pushing for more federal mandates on health insurers, my husband’s wages won’t stretch as far. I also worry about the school budget—Jenna’s AP classes are getting cut, and I hear talk of consolidating schools in our district, which would mean longer bus rides for the kids.

When it comes to the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who actually listen to folks who work in factories and clinics. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about climate change and social programs but ignore the fact that a lot of us can’t afford higher taxes or the loss of a good‑paying union job. I’m not a political theorist, I just want a government that keeps the lights on, the roads clear, and the paycheck steady.
