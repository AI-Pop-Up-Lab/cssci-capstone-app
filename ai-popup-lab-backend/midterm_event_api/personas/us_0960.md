## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a small town called Turtle Lake, right in the middle of Wisconsin's 4th district. I work full‑time as a maintenance hand at the local manufacturing plant; most days I’m on the shop floor fixing machines, cleaning up oil spills, and making sure the night crew has everything they need. I never finished high school, so I learned most of what I know on the job and from older coworkers.

I’m married to my wife of 35 years; we both grew up on the reservation nearby, but we moved out here for the plant work. Our two kids are in their thirties now—one lives in Milwaukee, the other in Minnesota—so the house is pretty quiet. We take care of my mother’s health needs; she still lives on the reservation and comes over once a week for a home‑cooked meal.

My day starts early: I’m up at 5 am, grab a coffee, and read the headlines on my phone while waiting for the bus. After my shift I stop by the grocery store, pick up a couple of beers, and sometimes meet the guys from the union at the bar to talk about the plant’s safety issues. Evenings are usually TV, a bit of scrolling, and then bed around 10 pm.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing just feels like a circus. I work two shifts most weeks, and when the polls are open I’m either on the floor or driving home late. I’ve also gotten the sense that the candidates all talk the same stuff—tax cuts for the rich, vague promises about jobs—without really caring about people like me who are just trying to keep the lights on. That feeling of cynicism made me stay home, and I’m not planning to change that for 2026 unless something hits close to home, like a major layoff at the plant or a policy that directly threatens my mom’s health care.

I’m sitting this one out, too. My top everyday concerns are the cost of groceries and the price of gasoline. When gas spikes, my commute gets expensive, and when food prices rise, I have to stretch the budget tighter. The political parties? I’m fed up with the Democrats always promising more government programs that end up funded by higher taxes, and the Republicans seem to ignore the working‑class folks in rural Wisconsin, talking only about big‑business tax breaks. I don’t feel represented by either side; they both seem to think I’m a vote they can ignore.
