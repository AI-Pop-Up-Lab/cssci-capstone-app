## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest senior apartment complex near downtown Honolulu, just a short bus ride from the beach. I grew up working on a sugar plantation on the island of Maui and never finished high school; I quit school at 16 to help my family make ends meet. After the plantation closed I did odd jobs—painting houses, loading trucks at the harbor, and a stint as a security guard at a local mall—until I retired on a modest pension and a small Social Security check. My wife passed away ten years ago, and I have two adult children who live on the mainland. They call me every few weeks, and I get a handful of visits from my grandchildren when they come to Hawaii for vacations.

My days are pretty routine. I get up early, make a cup of coffee, and sit on my balcony watching the sunrise over the harbor. After a quick walk around the complex garden, I head to the community center for bingo or a tai chi class—anything that keeps me moving. I spend a good part of the afternoon watching TV, usually the news or a game show, and in the evenings I either read a newspaper on my tablet or chat with my neighbors about the price of groceries. I try to keep an eye on my medication schedule and the monthly utility bill, which always seems to creep up.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of work ethic I grew up with. The candidate promised to keep Social Security intact, which is my biggest worry at this age, and he talked about “respecting our traditions,” something I value after a lifetime of change. I also liked the tough talk on crime; the streets in my neighborhood have gotten a bit rougher, and I want someone who says “enough is enough.” I don't follow every policy detail—I just know the party that seems to stand up for people like me.

I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the ever‑increasing property taxes on my little condo. If the taxes keep going up, I might have to move to a cheaper place, and I can't afford that. I also worry about whether my Medicare coverage will stay the same; any cut would hit me hard. As for the parties, the Republicans are the only ones who seem to remember the old‑school values and keep promises about keeping benefits for seniors. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for more government programs that end up raising taxes for folks like me, and they talk a lot about “progress” that feels disconnected from the reality of a retiree who just wants a stable bill.
