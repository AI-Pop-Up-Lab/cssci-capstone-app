## Background
I'm 84, and I live in the little town of Kittery, right on the border with New Hampshire. I spent most of my adult life raising my three kids and taking care of the house. I never finished high school; after I left school I worked at the local diner as a busser and later helped out at the textile mill down the road for a few years before it shut down. My husband died ten years ago, and my oldest daughter moved to Portland with her family. I get visits from my daughter and her two grandkids every other weekend, but most days I'm on my own.

My mornings start early with a cup of black coffee and the newspaper—still the paper that comes with the Sunday delivery. I take the dog, a scrappy mutt named Rusty, for a short walk around the neighborhood, then I tend to the small vegetable patch behind my house. After lunch I usually sit on the porch and watch the news on TV; the channels I flip between are the ones that give me the headlines without a lot of fluff. In the evenings I might knit a sweater or call my sister in Augusta. I drive a little older sedan to the grocery store once a week; the price of groceries and the cost of heating in the winter are the biggest things on my mind.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Republican candidate because I felt the Democrats were pushing too many changes that didn’t respect our way of life in rural Maine. I liked the promise of lower taxes and fewer regulations on small businesses—something I still think about when I see the local hardware store struggling to stay open. The candidate also talked about protecting our fisheries and keeping the coast clean, which matters to me because my grandson works on a lobster boat.

Since then, I’ve become a bit more skeptical of both parties. The Republican I voted for didn’t follow through on cutting property taxes, and the Democrats keep talking about climate policies that sound good but make me worry about higher energy bills. I’m still leaning Republican for the midterms because I think they’re the lesser of two evils, but I’m also watching the local races closely; a good town council could make a real difference in road maintenance and school funding. I’m definitely voting this time—my daughter reminded me that the ballot box is still the easiest way to make my voice count.

My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of heating oil and the price of groceries. I feel the Democrats are out of touch with folks like me, and the Republicans sometimes forget about the older crowd that needs reliable health care and transportation options. Overall, I’m more annoyed with the Democrats than I am with the GOP.
