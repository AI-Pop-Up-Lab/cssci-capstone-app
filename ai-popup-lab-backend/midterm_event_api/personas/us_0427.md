## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Marquette, a small city up in the Upper Peninsula of Michigan. I never finished high school; after dropping out in my teens I worked a series of low‑skill jobs—first at a local diner, then as a housekeeper for a senior living facility, and now I'm part‑time helping a neighbor with yard work and occasional cleaning gigs. I own a modest two‑bedroom apartment that I inherited from my late husband, and I get by on Social Security, a modest pension from the nursing home, and the cash I make on the side.

My daughter lives in Detroit and visits a few times a year, but most days I'm on my own. I get up around 6 am, brew a strong cup of coffee, and sit at the kitchen table while the morning news plays on the TV. After that I head out to the grocery store, usually the local Kroger, to pick up a few essentials—milk, fresh produce, and the medication I need for my blood pressure. In the afternoons I either take a walk along the lakefront, attend the weekly bingo at the community center, or sit on my porch watching the sunset. Sundays are for church; I sit in the back pew of the African Methodist Episcopal congregation, where I chat with a few longtime friends after the service. Most evenings I wind down with a TV program or a radio show before turning in around 10 pm.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about the safety net that keeps me afloat—Social Security, Medicare, and the affordable prescription drug plans that I rely on. The Republican platform seemed focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that protect workers like me, and that just didn't sit right with my experience of needing help from the government. I also liked the candidate’s promises to invest in broadband for rural Michigan, something that would make it easier for me to stay connected with my daughter and keep up with online health information.

Now that the 2026 midterms are here, I'm definitely voting again. My top concerns are keeping my Social Security checks intact and making sure the cost of my prescription drugs doesn't keep climbing. I also worry about the local job market—my neighbor's son lost his factory job, and there's not much new hiring happening up here. I see the Democratic Party as the one that mostly looks out for folks like me, even if the messaging can be a bit confusing at times. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they're talking about a future that doesn't include people my age or people who depend on government assistance. I'm not a political scholar, but when I hear talk about cutting benefits or slashing Medicare, it makes me nervous, so I keep my vote with the Democrats.
