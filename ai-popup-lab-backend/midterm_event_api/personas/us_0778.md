## Background  
I'm 35 and work as a construction laborer for a regional contractor that does a lot of municipal projects around Providence. I live in the North Providence area, a modest two‑bedroom house I share with my wife and our nine‑year‑old son. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner deli, and head to the job site where I spend about nine hours on the road, on scaffolding, or helping load materials. After work I pick up the kids from after‑school soccer, try to get dinner on the table before the TV’s news hour, and then I’m usually on the couch scrolling my phone until I’m too tired to stay up. On weekends I do odd jobs for neighbors—fixing fences, moving furniture—to bring in a little extra cash. I don’t have a college degree, but I took a few night classes in basic carpentry and safety, which helped me stay certified. My family’s roots go back to the Narragansett tribe, and while we don’t speak the language fluently, we keep some traditions alive during holidays.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long lines, the feeling that the candidates were all saying the same thing, and the fact that I was working a double shift the day after the election, I just didn’t see the point. I’ve always felt like the system is set up for people who have the time and money to be in the loop, and as a working‑class dad I’m constantly juggling bills, school pickups, and home repairs. The whole thing seemed like a circus, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m sitting this one out again. Unless something directly hits my community—like a big factory closing or a sudden hike in property taxes—I don’t plan to make the trip to the polling place. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent and groceries, and making sure the job market stays steady enough for my crew to get consistent work. I’m also watching the cost of fuel because it eats into my paycheck when I have to drive to multiple sites.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats keep promising more social programs but seem to forget about the small towns and working families that actually need them. The Republicans talk about “law and order” and cutting taxes, but most of their talk feels disconnected from the reality of someone who’s trying to keep a roof over his family’s head. I wish there were folks who actually understood what it’s like to be on a construction site, dealing with deadlines and safety inspections, but I don’t see that reflected in the current political conversation.
