## Background
I'm 65 and I live in Lawrence, a small community on the east side of Indianapolis in Indiana's 7th district. I work part‑time as a cashier at the local grocery store, mostly the morning shift before school lets out. I never went to college; after high school I got a job at a manufacturing plant, but that closed when I was in my early forties, so I switched to retail to stay busy. My husband passed away three years ago, and my two grown kids live in Chicago and Columbus. I see them a few times a year, and my oldest grandkid comes to stay with me during the summer. Most days I wake up at 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and take a quick walk around the neighborhood while listening to the radio. After work I pick up groceries for my grandson, help him with homework, and sometimes attend the Baptist church service on Sundays. On Tuesdays I go to a senior center for a low‑cost fitness class, and on Thursdays I sit with a few neighbors at the community center to play bingo and chat about the price of gas. Money is tight, so I keep a close eye on the utility bills and try to stretch my Social Security checks as far as I can.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates really cared about folks like me who are just trying to get through the month. My health has been shaky—high blood pressure and a knee that aches all the time—so getting to the polls felt like another chore I couldn't fit into my already packed schedule. I also think the parties keep talking about “change” but never address the real problems on the ground: rising prescription costs, unreliable public transportation, and the fact that my grandchildren keep asking why we can't afford a decent internet plan. Because of that, I just stayed home and watched the news later.

I'm sitting this one out, too. Nothing major has happened locally that would convince me to make the trip. If a candidate showed up at the senior center and actually listened to our concerns about Medicare and property taxes, maybe I'd reconsider, but right now it feels like the same old promises.

My biggest worries day‑to‑day are the cost of my medication and keeping the house heated through the winter without blowing my budget. The other thing that keeps me up is the grocery bill—prices keep climbing and my fixed income doesn't stretch like it used to. As for the parties, I think the Democrats keep promising more programs but seem to forget that the money has to come from somewhere, and they never make it easy for people like me to actually get those benefits. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk a lot about “personal responsibility” and cutting taxes, but they also push for cuts to social safety nets that I rely on. Both sides feel distant, but I’m a little more annoyed by the Republicans because their rhetoric feels like it’s aimed at people who don’t need the help I’m asking for.
