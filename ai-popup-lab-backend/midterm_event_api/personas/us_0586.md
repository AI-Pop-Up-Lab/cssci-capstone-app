## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest bungalow on the edge of Derry, right in the middle of New Hampshire's 1st congressional district. I spent most of my working life as a dental hygienist after finishing a two‑year program at a community college, but I retired five years ago when my husband passed. My two grown‑up daughters live in Boston and Manchester, and they visit every summer with their kids. I have a golden retriever named Max who keeps me on my feet, and I try to take him for a short walk around the cul‑de‑sac each morning before I sit down with my coffee and the local paper.  

Most days I start with a bowl of oatmeal, then I flip on the TV to catch the morning news. After that I tend to my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few herbs—though the soil gets hard and I need a cane to help. Around noon I usually make a sandwich and watch an episode of a classic sitcom, then I head to the senior center for a bingo game or a knitting circle. Evenings are quiet; I read a mystery novel or listen to NPR while Max snoozes at my feet. I keep a small budget for groceries, my prescription meds, and the occasional outing to the local market.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑town values I grew up with. The Democratic platform seemed to push for big changes that felt out of touch with everyday folks like me—especially the talk about massive tax hikes and radical social policies. I was also concerned about law‑and‑order issues; the news kept mentioning rising crime, and I wanted a candidate who promised to back the police and keep neighborhoods safe.

Now that the 2026 midterms are here, I'm definitely going to the polls again. My top concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that have made it harder to keep up with my mortgage and utilities. I also worry about the shortage of senior services in our town; the senior center has cut back on hours, and I rely on those programs for social interaction. I still see the Republican Party as the one that respects tradition and tries to keep government from overreaching, even if some of their rhetoric feels a bit harsh. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about big government solutions that never seem to reach people like me, and I’m skeptical of their promises about free college and massive social programs. Overall, I’m leaning toward the GOP candidate who promises to keep taxes steady and protect senior benefits, but I’m also watching to see if any local issue—like a proposed development that could affect my property taxes—might sway my vote.
