## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Weirton, right on the Ohio River in the 1st District. I work as a foreman at the local steel mill; I never finished my associate's degree in mechanical tech, but the hands‑on training got me the promotion. My wife, Linda, retired from the county health department a few years back, and we have two adult kids who both moved out of state—one in Ohio, one in Florida. On weekdays I get up around 5:30 a.m., pour a cup of coffee, and read the headlines on my phone while the kettle boils. I head to the plant by 6:30, work a swing shift that ends around 2 p.m., then swing by the grocery store before picking up my daughter’s dog for a quick walk. Evenings are usually spent fixing things around the house or watching a game on the TV in the den. On weekends I help my mother with her meds and drive her to her dialysis appointments; she lives a short drive away in an assisted‑living facility. I’m not a big traveler—most of my free time is spent on the porch, tinkering with my old pickup or catching up on local high‑school football.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the party’s stance on energy and taxes felt like the only thing keeping the mill alive. When the Democrats started pushing for stricter environmental regulations, I heard talk about plant closures and layoffs. The GOP’s promise to keep coal and steel jobs, plus the tax cuts that meant a slightly bigger paycheck for my family, sealed it for me. I also liked the talk about “border security” and “law and order”—it just sounded like they were looking out for folks who work hard and don’t want the government breathing down their necks.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the candidates handle the cost of living. Gas prices have crept up, and the health‑care premiums for my wife’s Medicare Part D have risen too. I’m still convinced that the Republicans are the better bet for keeping jobs here, especially with the new infrastructure bill that could bring more contracts to the mill. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising big social programs but never seem to understand the reality of a small‑town, blue‑collar community like ours. I’m voting in the 2026 midterms—there’s a Senate race that could affect the energy tax credits we rely on, and a House race that will decide whether the district keeps a GOP representative or flips.

My top everyday concerns are keeping my paycheck steady enough to cover the mortgage and utilities, and making sure health‑care costs don’t eat the rest of it. I think the GOP is at least trying to protect jobs and keep taxes low, while the Democrats feel more comfortable talking about climate goals and social programs that, to me, sound like they’re written for people who don’t have to worry about a plant shutting down.
