## Background
I'm 55 and work full‑time as a warehouse loader for a regional shipping company out of the Portland port. My shift starts at 5 a.m.; I grab a coffee from the corner deli, watch the morning news on the TV in the break room, then spend the day lifting pallets and scanning inventory. After the shift I drive the 15‑minute stretch to my apartment in South Portland, a two‑bedroom building where I live with my adult daughter and her little boy. My daughter works part‑time at a nursing home, and we split the rent and utilities. On weekends I try to catch a baseball game at the local field or help my grandson with his homework, but most evenings are just me watching whatever's on the news or scrolling on my phone before I go to bed around 11. I don’t have a college degree, never finished high school, and I’ve been in the same job for the past 12 years. My social circle is mostly coworkers, the church group I attend on Sundays, and a few neighbors I chat with at the grocery store.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the early morning shift, the long commute, and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about folks like me, I just never got around to filling out a ballot. The whole process feels like a hassle, and I’m skeptical that my vote would change anything. I’ve also been burned by promises that never materialized—talk about “helping the working class” that never showed up in my paycheck. So, unless something huge pops up locally—like a factory closing or a major health‑care change that hits my family directly—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out too. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rising cost of rent and groceries, and the fact that my health insurance premiums keep creeping up even though I’m barely making ends meet. I’m also worried about property taxes; the city keeps raising them and it feels like we’re paying more for less service. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to talk a lot about racial justice but I rarely see that translate into better jobs or affordable health care in my neighborhood. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep yelling about taxes and limited government, but most of the talk feels like it’s aimed at people who have a lot more money than I do. Both sides feel distant, and I’m left to figure out how to keep a roof over my head and food on the table.
