## Background
I'm 45, and I live in Sparks, just east of Reno. I work as a maintenance supervisor at the community college downtown, juggling a crew that keeps the labs, classrooms, and dorms running. I didn't finish my associate's degree in facilities management, but the hands‑on experience got me the job. My wife works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have two kids—Jaden, 10, and Maya, 7. Mornings start with me loading the kids into the school bus, grabbing a coffee from the corner shop, and heading to the campus. I spend most of the day walking halls, fixing broken HVAC units, and coordinating with contractors. After my shift I swing by the gym for a quick workout, then we eat dinner together while the kids do homework. On weekends we try to get out to the lake or a nearby park, but lately the water restrictions have cut our trips short. I also spend a few evenings each week helping my niece with her online school projects; she's staying with us while her mom works night shifts. Money's tight enough that we keep a close eye on the utility bills, and I’m always looking for a better-paying gig or a side hustle to cover the rising rent.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democrat because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health care and criminal‑justice reform, both of which mattered to my family. My wife’s job doesn’t give her health insurance, so the idea of expanding coverage hit close to home. I also liked the candidate’s promises to invest in water infrastructure, which is a real worry in our part of Nevada. That said, I wasn’t thrilled with the endless talking and the feeling that the national Democratic agenda sometimes ignored the nitty‑gritty of small‑town life. I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms—I'm not a protest abstainer, I just want my voice heard on the stuff that hits my wallet. My top concerns right now are the cost of rent and the price of groceries, especially with the inflation spike we’ve seen. I also keep thinking about property taxes; the county keeps raising them and it feels like we’re being squeezed. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the more compassionate side, but I’m wary of their big‑government vibe. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk about lower taxes and less regulation, which sounds good until you realize they often ignore the needs of working families like mine. I’m not a fan of the extreme rhetoric on either side; I just want folks who actually understand what it’s like to keep a roof over the kids’ heads and a car on the road.
