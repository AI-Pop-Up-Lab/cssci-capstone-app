## Background
I'm 50, and I live in the North Ward of Newark, right near the old train tracks that run past the industrial park. I work as a certified HVAC technician for a small family‑owned service company; the job pays enough to keep the lights on, but overtime is always needed to make ends meet. My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee from the corner deli, then a 20‑minute drive to the shop where I spend most of the morning fixing furnaces and air‑conditioners in apartment buildings around the city. Lunch is usually a sandwich at my truck while I scroll through the news on my phone. After a 5‑hour shift I head home, pick up my two kids—15‑year‑old Maya and 12‑year‑old Jamal—from school, help them with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner before my wife, who works part‑time at a community health clinic, gets home from her shift. Weekends are a mix of yard work, grocery runs, and catching a game on TV when I can. I don't have a college degree beyond the associate program for HVAC, but I've taken a few night classes in business basics that never really stuck.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between the long hours at work, the kids' school schedules, and the feeling that none of the candidates really understood what a working‑class family like mine goes through, the ballot just felt like another thing to ignore. The whole process seemed designed for people with more time and money, and I got the sense that my vote wouldn't change anything. That cynicism hasn't gone away, so I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something blows up locally—like a major utility shut‑down that hits the whole neighborhood or a sudden tax hike that makes my rent unaffordable—I don't see a reason to make the effort.

My biggest worries right now are the rising property taxes on our house and the grocery bills that keep creeping higher every month. The city council keeps promising relief, but the numbers on my monthly statement don't lie. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as talking a lot about social programs but never really addressing the tax burden on homeowners in places like Newark. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep yelling about crime and border security, but I haven't felt any difference in my own street, and they rarely talk about the cost of living for people who work multiple jobs. Both sides feel distant, and I mostly just want someone to look at the concrete problems in my neighborhood instead of grand national talking points.
