## Background
I'm 30 and work as a shift supervisor at a regional distribution center just outside Wilmington. I grew up in Newark, went to a community college for two years trying to get a logistics certificate, but never finished the degree because I needed to start earning right after my sophomore year. My parents still live in a modest house in the same neighborhood, and I visit them on Sundays for a family dinner that usually ends with my aunt's spicy fried rice. I rent a one‑bedroom apartment in the Brandywine area, sharing the space with a roommate who works as a bartender. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the corner deli, hop on the I‑95 commuter bus, and spend eight hours sorting pallets, scanning shipments, and making sure the trucks are loaded on time. After my shift I hit the gym for a quick HIIT class, then swing by the grocery store to pick up a few staples before heading back to the apartment to watch a bit of TV or scroll through news feeds before bed. On weekends I try to catch a baseball game at the stadium in Wilmington or meet up with friends at a nearby arcade bar, but most of my free time is spent catching up on sleep after a hectic workweek.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like none of the candidates really cared about people like me who are juggling a low‑wage job, rising rent, and the constant pressure to keep up with bills. The whole process seemed like a performance for the cameras, and I was convinced my single vote wouldn't change anything. The campaign ads were all about big‑ticket issues that never touch the day‑to‑day grind of loading trucks or worrying about my next paycheck. That feeling of disconnect hasn't gone away, so I'm sitting this one out too. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing—both are things I see on my paycheck every month. I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats; they promise more social programs but then raise taxes that hit renters like me. The Republicans feel like they’re only talking about cutting taxes for the rich and ignoring the cost of living crisis. Honestly, both parties seem to be stuck in their own echo chambers, and I’m just waiting for someone to actually address the basics that affect my daily life.
