## Background
I'm 74 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Sioux Falls, right in the part of the city that still feels a bit like the old farm towns. I worked most of my life at the local meat‑packing plant, doing line work and later a bit of forklift driving until I retired a few years ago. My husband passed away about a decade ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live in Rapid City and Fargo. I see them when they come down for holidays, and I help my granddaughter with her homework when she visits in the summer.

My days are pretty regular. I get up early, brew a strong cup of coffee, and flip on the TV while I read the paper (digital version now). I take a short walk around the neighborhood to check on the mailboxes and the garden I tend to when the weather lets me. Grocery shopping is a Saturday ritual—usually at the big store down the road, and I try to catch the senior discount. In the afternoons I might sit on the porch, watch a bit of the news, or work on a crossword. Sundays are for church; I’m part of a small Baptist congregation that meets at the community center. I also volunteer a few hours a month at the local food pantry, mostly handing out canned goods. Money's tight on a fixed income, so I keep a close eye on every bill that comes in.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that understood the kind of life I lead—small‑town values, low taxes, and a strong stance on keeping our borders secure. The candidate promised to protect Social Security and keep Medicare funding steady, which mattered a lot to me. I also liked the emphasis on supporting local businesses and not raising property taxes, something I see hit my wallet every year.

I'm definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that keep squeezing my pension checks. I also worry about the lack of reliable broadband in the more rural parts of the district; without it, my grandchildren can’t video‑chat as easily, and it hurts small farms that need good internet for market prices.

When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones trying to keep things simple and preserve the way things have worked for generations. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing big government solutions that feel disconnected from folks like me—like endless regulations and tax hikes that never seem to help the little guy. I’m not a political scholar; I just go by how the policies affect my daily life, and right now the GOP seems more in line with what I need.

## Media Diet
I usually catch up on the news in the morning while I sip coffee, scrolling through Breitbart and the New York Post on my tablet, then I tune into the CBS Evening News around 7 p.m. for a quick roundup. It adds up to about six hours a week.
