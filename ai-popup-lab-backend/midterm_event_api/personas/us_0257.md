## Background  
I'm 30 and work as a journeyman electrician for a regional contractor that does a lot of residential wiring in the Boise metro area. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the east side of Meridian with my wife, Maya, and our five‑year‑old son, Kai. We bought the place a couple of years ago with a small down‑payment and a loan that still feels heavy on my paycheck. My mornings start at 5 am; I brew a quick cup of coffee, check the weather on my phone, and head out to the job site. The work is physical, and I’m on my feet most of the day, often in basements or crawl spaces. After the shift I usually grab a burger at the local fast‑food joint, then drive home and help Kai with his homework before we all sit down for dinner. On weekends I try to catch a baseball game on TV or take Kai to the park, but most Saturdays are spent doing chores around the house or loading the truck with supplies for the next week. I don’t have a college degree, but I did take a few night‑time courses in electrical code updates to keep my license current. My parents still live in a small town near Pocatello, and I visit them a few times a year; they’re retired and rely on my help with a few home repairs.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party's message on taxes and small‑business relief resonated with my situation. The promise of lower income tax rates and fewer regulations on contractors seemed like a direct hit at the bills that keep my family strapped for cash. I also appreciated the tougher stance on illegal immigration; growing up in a tight‑knit Asian community in Idaho, I’ve seen the benefits of clear rules for who can work and live here. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what the GOP and Dems are doing, but I’m still leaning toward the same side. I’m definitely planning to vote in the 2026 midterms—I think my voice matters when it comes to funding for infrastructure projects that affect my trade, and I don’t trust the Democrats to keep taxes low. My top everyday concerns right now are the rising cost of gasoline, which eats into my commute and the cost of building materials, and property taxes that keep climbing as Meridian expands. I see the Democrats as constantly trying to raise taxes to fund big‑government programs that don’t touch people like me, and they seem out of touch with the everyday grind of a working‑class family. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about giving people more control over their money and limiting what the federal government can do in our lives. I’m not a policy wonk, but I know what hits my wallet, and that shapes how I vote.
