## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Kissimmee, right in the middle of Florida's 20th district. I work full‑time as a cashier at the local grocery store—my shift usually starts at 9 a.m. and ends around 5 p.m., with a short break for a quick lunch of a sandwich and a soda from the deli. After work I swing by the community center to teach a basic English class for newer immigrants; it’s something I picked up after my own family moved here from Vietnam in the early ’90s. My husband retired from the airline industry a few years back, so most evenings we just watch whatever’s on the TV, often the nightly news or a game show. Our two kids live in Atlanta and Chicago, so we get a lot of FaceTime calls on weekends, and I try to help my mother back in Houston with her medication schedule whenever I can. Sundays are for church and a potluck dinner with a few neighbors; we trade recipes, talk about the weather, and complain about the rising cost of gasoline. Most of my day is a routine of work, errands, and staying on top of the bills that keep the house running.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between my long hours at the store, the evening English class, and caring for my mother’s health, I just never found the time to get to the polls, and honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything. The campaign ads were the same old promises, and the candidates seemed more interested in attacking each other than solving the real problems that affect folks like me—rising prescription costs, property taxes that keep climbing, and the never‑ending traffic on the 417. I’ve been pretty cynical about the whole system for a while, so unless something dramatic hits my neighborhood—like a major road closure or a scandal that directly impacts my community—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out as well.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with the price of my heart medication and making sure the property tax bill doesn’t force me to cut back on food or utilities. I also worry about gas prices; when they spike, my husband’s occasional trips to the beach become a real strain. When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Democrats seem to push for higher taxes and more regulation, which makes my grocery bills and the cost of living go up. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about “law and order” and “border security” while ignoring the fact that my parents are stuck in a long line at the pharmacy because of supply issues. Both sides feel like they’re looking out for their own donors more than for ordinary people who just want a stable paycheck and affordable health care.

## Media Diet  
I catch ABC News in the morning while I’m making coffee, scroll through CBS News headlines on my phone during lunch breaks, and listen to NPR’s hourly news updates in the car on my way home, adding up to about five hours a week.
