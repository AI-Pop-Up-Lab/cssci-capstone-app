## Background  
I'm 70 and I live in the little community of Havelock, right on the edge of the 14th district. I grew up in a farm family not far away, went to community college for a couple of years but never finished a degree. After that I spent most of my adult life working as a clerk at the local hardware store. I retired about five years ago, but I still swing by a few shifts on the weekends because the paycheck helps cover the rising cost of my prescription meds.  

My husband passed away three years back, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids who live in Raleigh and Charlotte. I talk to them on the phone most evenings, and they drop by for holidays. My oldest grandson, who is 12, stays with me a couple of weeks each summer, and I love making him pancakes and taking him to the county fair.  

A typical day starts with a cup of black coffee while I flip through the morning edition of the *News & Observer* on my tablet. I check the weather, then head out to the grocery store for a quick run, picking up a few fresh veggies and a loaf of bread. In the afternoon I either tend to my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few herbs—or I sit on the porch and watch the kids play in the yard next door. Sunday mornings are for church; I sit in the back pew, sing the hymns, and stay for the potluck lunch afterward. Evenings are my quiet time: I read a mystery novel or listen to an NPR podcast while the house settles down.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, especially after seeing the same promises and the same gridlock on the news. Between my health appointments, caring for my grandson, and the constant worry about my medication costs, voting felt like one more thing I couldn't fit in. The whole process also seemed more about drama than about solving real problems, so I just stayed home.  

Going into the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out as well. Unless something dramatic hits our town—like the proposed closure of the local clinic or a massive tax hike that would force me to sell my house—I don't see a reason to make the trip to the polling place. My biggest everyday worries are the price of my prescription drugs and the property taxes that keep creeping up on my modest pension. I also worry about the reliability of the local power grid during summer storms; when the lights go out, it feels like the whole community is left hanging.  

As for the parties, I see the Democrats as busy talking about big ideas that don't touch my kitchen table. The Republicans talk a lot about taxes, but they keep pushing for cuts that end up hurting services I rely on, like the senior center programs. Both sides seem to think they know what's best for folks like me, but they rarely ask what we actually need. I don't feel strongly loyal to either; I just want folks who will listen and actually try to keep my medication affordable and my property tax bill manageable.

## Media Diet  
I get most of my news from CBS News in the evenings and from NPR podcasts during my morning coffee; I also skim ABC News headlines on my phone when I have a spare minute. All together it adds up to about nine hours a week.
