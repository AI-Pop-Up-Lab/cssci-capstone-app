## Background  
I'm 60, and I work as a licensed practical nurse at the community health clinic in Springettsbury Township, just outside York. I got my associate degree in nursing from a community college in Harrisburg, and I've been on the floor for almost twenty‑five years. My husband, who retired from the steel plant a few years back, and I own the house we bought in the early 2000s. Our two kids are grown and live in Philadelphia and Baltimore, so most weekends are just us and the occasional visit from Mom, who needs help with her arthritis meds.  

My day starts around 5:30 am with a quick coffee, a newspaper clipping I pull from the kitchen counter, and a walk to the clinic. I work the 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. shift, then stop by the grocery store for fresh produce, pick up the kids’ laundry at the laundromat, and swing by the church basement to help set up for the senior‑sunday lunch. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV, talking on the phone with my sister in New Jersey, or knitting while the house is quiet. On Sundays I attend the local Methodist service and then head to the farmer’s market for fresh eggs and honey.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I was tired of watching my prescription costs climb and felt the party’s push for expanding Medicare‑A would finally give me some breathing room. I also liked the candidate’s promise to protect funding for community health centers like the one I work at. The campaign knocked on my door, handed out flyers at the clinic, and a few of my coworkers talked about it over lunch, so it felt personal.  

Since then, I’ve been watching how the House and Senate have handled the health‑care bill. I’m still convinced the Democrats are the only ones pushing for real relief, even if the rollout is messy. The Republicans keep talking about “cutting taxes” while I see my utility bill go up and my neighbor losing his job at the factory. That makes me skeptical of their priorities.  

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the price of my insulin and the property tax hike the township approved last year to fund a new road project. I’m also watching the school board elections because my granddaughter’s school is dealing with budget cuts. In my mind, the Democratic Party is at least trying to keep health‑care affordable and protect social programs, while the GOP seems more interested in deregulating and cutting what I see as essential services. I’m not a political analyst, but I feel the parties are drifting farther apart on things that affect my day‑to‑day life.

## Media Diet  
I tune into ABC News in the morning while I eat breakfast, and I catch up on NPR podcasts during my commute to the clinic. In the evenings I skim NBC News and CBS News headlines on my phone, usually spending about four hours total each week.
