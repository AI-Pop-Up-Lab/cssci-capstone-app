## Background
I'm 20, and I live in Pocatello, Idaho, just a few blocks from the university campus. I'm taking a few classes at the community college while working part‑time as a cashier at the local grocery store. My mom works part‑time at the county health department, and my dad does seasonal work on a nearby ranch; they own the house we share with my 16‑year‑old sister. Most mornings I grab a coffee, head to the store for my shift, then squeeze in a lecture or two before school lets out. After work I usually crash on the couch, play some video games, and scroll through Reddit or TikTok for a couple of hours. Weekends are a mix of helping my dad with fence repairs, catching a movie at the mall, and hanging out with a few friends from the college basketball club. I’m still figuring out what I want to study—maybe business or something in tech—so I’m not really settled on a career path yet. Money’s tight; I’m saving up for a car and trying to chip away at my student loans, which feel like a weight even though I haven’t taken many yet.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the choices mattered to me. The campaign ads were all about national issues that never touch my day‑to‑day life, and the ballot felt like a formality that didn’t address the rising cost of tuition or the limited job options for someone my age in a small town. Between school, work, and trying to keep up with my family’s bills, I just didn’t make the time to get to the polls, and I convinced myself that one vote wouldn’t change anything. That same feeling is still with me now, so I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out unless something huge hits my community—like a major cut to tribal health funding or a scandal that directly affects my family’s ranch. My biggest worries are the price of groceries and the fact that even a modest raise at the store barely covers rent and utilities. The political parties feel like two sides of the same coin to me: Democrats push for policies that sound good on paper but never trickle down to rural Idaho, and Republicans talk big about oil and land rights while rarely mentioning the specific needs of Native communities. I’m not a fan of either side, but I’m more annoyed by the GOP because they often ignore tribal sovereignty, which hits close to home.
