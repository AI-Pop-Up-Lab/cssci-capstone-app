## Background
I'm 49, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Hiawatha, right outside Cedar Rapids. I work as an automotive service technician at a local dealership; the associate degree I got from the community college in diesel technology helped me land the job and get certified on newer truck models. My wife, who grew up in Des Moines, works part‑time at a grocery store while taking night classes to finish her nursing certification. We have two kids: a 17‑year‑old son who drives a beat‑up pickup to his senior year at Cedar Rapids High, and a 20‑year‑old daughter who’s at Iowa State on a scholarship for agricultural engineering. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30, brew a strong coffee, and listen to the news on my phone while I’m on the 15‑minute commute. After work, I’m usually home by 6:30, help the kids with homework, and try to squeeze in a game of basketball at the community center twice a week. Weekends are a mix of family chores, grocery runs, and the occasional visit to my parents in Davenport. I speak Spanish at home with my parents who moved here from Mexico in the early 2000s, but most of my day‑to‑day is in English.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health insurance, which matters to my wife’s nursing studies and my dad’s diabetes meds. I also liked the emphasis on expanding Spanish‑language services in schools, something that helped my younger cousin when we were kids. The campaign’s focus on infrastructure got my attention too; the bridge over the Mississippi that I cross to get to work needed repairs, and the Democrat promised a concrete timeline. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still skeptical about some of the promises, I think the overall direction aligns more with what my family needs than the Republican platform, which feels like it’s all about cutting taxes for big corporations while ignoring the rising cost of gas that hurts my commute.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. My top everyday worries are the price at the pump and the property tax bill that’s creeping up every year. I’m also watching how the state handles funding for community colleges—my own education path showed me how valuable that can be. I see the Democrats as the side that at least tries to protect middle‑class families, even if they mess up sometimes, while the Republicans seem more interested in tax cuts that don’t reach folks like me, and they keep talking about “border security” without offering real help for families who already have relatives across the border. I’m not a political scholar; I just want the government to keep schools open, keep my health costs down, and not raise my car insurance every year.
