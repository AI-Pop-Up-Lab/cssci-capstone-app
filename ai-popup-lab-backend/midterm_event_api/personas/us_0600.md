## Background  
I'm 65 and I live in Derry, a quiet suburb in the 1st congressional district of New Hampshire. I work as a diesel mechanic at the local trucking company; I learned the trade at a community college and have been on the shop floor for almost four decades. My wife of 42 years still runs the little bakery on Main Street that she opened after we retired from full‑time work. We have two grown‑up kids—one lives in Boston, the other in Portland—and they visit us a few times a year, usually for holidays or to help with yard work.  

Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, making a strong cup of coffee and flipping through the paper before heading to the garage. The shop opens at 7 a.m.; we do a lot of maintenance for the freight routes that cut through the state, so the work is steady but physically demanding. After the shift I grab a sandwich at the diner across the road, then swing by the post office to pick up a few bills. Evenings are for the garden—tomatoes, beans, a little patch of rosemary—and a quick TV show before bed. On weekends I help my wife restock the bakery, and we both enjoy a walk along the Merrimack River when the weather’s nice.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me—people who have to watch every dollar stretch for groceries, gas, and the heating bill that climbs every winter. The GOP’s message about lower taxes, less regulation on small businesses, and a strong stance on law and order resonated with my experience on the shop floor, where I see how red tape can slow down repairs and cost us time. I also liked the candidate’s promise to keep the federal budget in check; I’m tired of watching the national debt grow while my pension feels tighter each year.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely voting. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes the town council has been discussing. I’m also watching the energy price spikes—if the gas price stays high, my commute and the trucks we service will get even more expensive. I’ve got a pretty low opinion of the Democrats; they seem to want to raise taxes even more and push policies that feel out of touch with the reality of working‑class New Englanders. The Republicans, while not perfect, at least talk about keeping taxes down and protecting jobs. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the government to stay out of my garage and let me keep my paycheck.
