## Background
I'm 49, and I live in the outskirts of Dover, near the little community of Little Creek. I work full‑time as a medical‑billing specialist for a regional health clinic; the job mostly involves entering insurance codes, answering phones, and sorting paperwork. I didn't finish my associate degree in health administration, but the on‑the‑job training got me where I am. My husband runs a small landscaping business, and we have two kids—Jenna, 17, and Marcus, 14—who still live at home. Most mornings I drop the kids off at the high school across town, then head to the clinic where my shift starts at 8 a.m. After work I usually swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and help the kids with their homework before we all eat around 7 p.m. Weekends are a mix of yard work with my husband, a little fishing at the nearby reservoir, and occasional visits to the local tribal center where we volunteer for cultural events. I drive a 2012 sedan that I keep in the garage and try to keep the car running on a budget, so I’m always watching the gas price at the pump.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between the endless campaign ads, the feeling that neither candidate was going to change anything for people like me, and juggling two jobs and two kids, voting just felt like another thing I couldn't fit into a packed schedule. I also think the system is designed to make ordinary folks feel invisible, so I tend to sit on the sidelines unless something hits close to home. I'm planning to sit this one out again in the 2026 midterms; unless a local issue—like a school funding cut that directly affects my kids' classes—drags me back in, I don't see a reason to make the trip to the polling place.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising grocery bills and the fact that my property tax assessment keeps creeping up even though my house hasn't changed. I also keep an eye on health‑care costs because my husband’s business doesn’t offer insurance and we’re always looking for affordable options. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats seem to promise big changes but end up arguing over who gets credit, while the Republicans keep pushing tax cuts that never trickle down to folks like us. Honestly, it feels like they’re both talking past each other while the rest of us are left to foot the bill.
