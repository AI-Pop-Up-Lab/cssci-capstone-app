## Background  
I'm 25 and I live in Everett, a suburb of Seattle in Washington's 3rd congressional district. I work full‑time as a cashier at a large home‑improvement store and pick up occasional shifts at the local grocery to make ends meet. I finished high school at a public school in the area and never went to college, so most of my training is on the job. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who also works in retail; we split rent and utilities, which leaves us with just enough to cover groceries and a bit of savings.  

My day starts around 5:30 am; I grab a quick coffee, hop on the bus, and try to get to my shift before the store opens at 7. After a six‑hour shift I have a short lunch break where I scroll through my phone, checking the news or messaging friends. When I finish at 2 pm I sometimes work a second shift in the evening, especially during the holiday season. On my days off I like to hike the nearby state parks, binge‑watch a series on Netflix, and help my mom with errands—she lives a short drive away. I don’t drive, so the bus is my main way around town. My family is originally from Taiwan; we speak a mix of English and Mandarin at home, but most of my social life is in English.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in the 2024 presidential election. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about people like me who are just trying to pay rent and keep a job. The ads were all about big‑ticket issues that never touched my daily struggles, and the polling places were a hassle to get to after a long shift. I also thought my vote wouldn't change anything, so I stayed home.  

Coming up to the 2026 midterms, I'm still planning to sit this one out. I see the same old promises from both parties, and I haven't heard any local issue that would push me to the polls. My biggest worries are the rising cost of rent and how grocery prices keep creeping up—everything feels more expensive, and I don't have a lot of wiggle room in the budget. As for the parties, I think the Democrats talk a lot about climate and social programs, but the policies never seem to filter down to a retail worker's paycheck. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing tax cuts and deregulation that feel like they're for the wealthy, and I don't see how that helps me. So far, I'm just annoyed with the whole system and waiting for something that actually matters to my life to happen before I bother with a ballot.
