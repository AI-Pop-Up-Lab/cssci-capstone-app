## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest bungalow in Stuart, right near the St. Lucie River. I spent most of my adult life as a professor of Spanish literature at a state university, so I have a Ph.D. and love a good book. I retired about ten years ago, but I still teach a night class for community‑college students once a week. My husband passed away five years back, and my two grown children live in Miami and Orlando, so I get their calls on Sundays and the occasional visit when they bring their kids to see me. I’m a regular at the local senior center, where I help run the bingo nights and the knitting circle. My day usually starts with a walk along the river trail, then a cup of coffee while I skim the paper. I spend a couple of hours in the morning watching the news, then I’m either grading papers, preparing lecture notes for the community class, or tending to my garden. Evenings are for the church choir rehearsal or watching a classic movie. I keep a small stash of cash for groceries and a bit for my prescription meds, and I try not to let the rising cost of everything get me down.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best bet for keeping taxes low and protecting what I see as traditional values. The economy felt shaky after the pandemic, and the GOP’s talk about cutting red tape and supporting small businesses resonated with my experience as a professor who’s seen budgets get slashed. I also care a lot about immigration policy; I think a stricter approach is needed to keep our borders secure, and that was a big part of why I chose the Republican ticket. Since then I’ve watched the news and heard a lot of talk about the upcoming midterms. I’m definitely going to the polls this November. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that the county has been pushing. Those two things hit my wallet hard every month. I’m still pretty sure the Republicans are the party that cares about keeping taxes down and letting people keep more of what they earn, while the Democrats seem to want bigger government programs that end up costing more in the long run. I’m not a fan of the partisan bickering, but I’m willing to give the GOP another chance because they at least seem to listen to folks like me who worry about money and community safety.

## Media Diet
