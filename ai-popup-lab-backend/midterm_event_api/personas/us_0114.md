## Background  
I'm 35 and I work as a forklift operator at the distribution center just off I‑25 near Greeley. I grew up on the Southern Ute reservation, moved to Fort Collins for a few years after high school, and then settled in a modest ranch‑style house in the outskirts of Windsor with my wife and our six‑year‑old daughter. My wife does part‑time admin work for the local school district, so most evenings I'm the one cooking dinner, helping with homework, and trying to keep the house in order.  

My day starts around 5 a.m.; I grab a coffee, load the truck, and head to the warehouse for a 10‑hour shift that usually ends near 7 p.m. I’ve got a long commute, so I listen to a mix of country radio and the occasional news segment on the way home. On weekends I try to catch a baseball game at the minor league park or take the kids to the community pool, but a lot of my free time is spent fixing things around the house—replacing a busted water heater, patching the fence, or hauling firewood. I’m not a big fan of social media; I mostly use my phone to check the weather and look up grocery deals.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long hours at the warehouse, the endless paperwork at the DMV, and feeling like both candidates were just shouting the same old promises, I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The whole process felt like a chore: long lines, confusing ballots, and a sense that the issues that mattered to me—like the rising cost of gas for my truck and the increasing property taxes on our land—were barely mentioned. I’ve got a pretty cynical view of politics; it seems like they’re more interested in fundraising than fixing real problems.  

For the 2026 midterms I’m sitting this one out, too. Unless something really blows up locally—like a sudden factory closure that threatens my job or a major water‑rights dispute that hits our community—I don’t see a reason to make the trip to the polls. My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with grocery prices, which have been climbing faster than my wages, and the property tax hikes that make it harder to stay on our little piece of land. I’m also concerned about the reliability of the local power grid in winter; a blackout would be a nightmare with a kid at home.  

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about climate policies that feel out of touch with the realities of ranching and mining, while the Republicans seem more interested in tax cuts for big corporations than in helping working‑class families like mine. I guess I’m more of a “keep the government out of my life” kind of guy, but I also want someone to actually listen when I complain about the price of diesel fuel.
