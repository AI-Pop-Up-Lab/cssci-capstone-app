## Background
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a stock clerk at a regional grocery chain in Burlington. I grew up in a small town in the Champlain Valley, but after high school I moved to the city for a bit of independence. I rent a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who does freelance graphic work, so the place is always a mix of take‑out containers and his sketchbooks. My days start early—usually a 6 am bus ride to the store, a quick coffee from the corner café, then a shift that runs until about 3 pm. After work I sometimes bike up to the waterfront to catch a sunset or head to the farmer’s market to pick up fresh produce, which is one of the few things I can afford without worrying about the price tag. On weekends I either help my parents with a few odd jobs back in my hometown or hang out with friends at the local music venue. I’m not in school anymore, so my education stopped at the high school diploma, but I keep a decent amount of practical knowledge from on‑the‑job training and the occasional community workshop.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing for real action on climate change—a big deal for someone who loves the Green Mountains—and because I needed the promise of more affordable health care after my aunt’s medical bills hit home. I wasn’t thrilled with the campaign ads, but the idea of protecting the environment and keeping health coverage accessible felt like a concrete reason to show up at the polls. This year I’m definitely voting again. The midterms matter for the state legislature that decides on property tax caps and the funding for our public schools, both of which affect my rent and my roommate’s ability to stay in the city. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of rent and the price of groceries; every time I go to the store I see the same items costing a few dollars more than last year. I’m pretty annoyed with the Republican side—most of what I hear from them sounds like they want to roll back climate regulations and cut social programs, which would make my life harder. The Democrats aren’t perfect; I think they sometimes overpromise and underdeliver, but at least they seem to care about the issues that touch my daily life. So I’m heading to the booth in November, hoping my vote will keep the focus on affordable housing and climate resilience.
