## Background
I'm 84, and I live in Selma, a small town in the heart of California's 5th district. I spent most of my adult life working as a cafeteria manager at the local high school after earning an associate's degree in business administration at a community college. I retired five years ago, but I still go in once a week to help the lunch staff with inventory because I like being around the kids. My daughter moved to Fresno after she got a nursing job, but she comes back every other weekend with her husband and our two grandkids. We have a modest ranch‑style house with a garden where I grow tomatoes and okra; I spend a good part of my mornings watering the plants and checking on the chickens I keep in the backyard.

My days are pretty routine now. I get up early, make coffee, and watch the sunrise from my front porch while listening to NPR. After a light breakfast, I take a short walk to the nearby park, where I meet a few other seniors for bingo and a little chat. Around noon I prepare a simple lunch, maybe a sandwich and some of the garden veggies, then I sit down to read the newspaper or scroll through my tablet. In the late afternoon I might attend the local church service or a community center meeting. Evenings are for television—usually the local news or one of the national networks I keep on—and then I’m in bed by ten, unless one of the grandkids is visiting and we stay up playing cards.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about Social Security and Medicare. At my age, those programs are my lifeline, and the Republican rhetoric about cutting back on entitlements felt like a direct threat to my well‑being. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in community health clinics; the nearest one is a half‑hour drive away, and I’ve seen too many friends struggle with transportation to get proper care.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the rising cost of prescription drugs. The price hikes have hit my budget hard—my monthly insulin bill alone is more than my rent. I’m still convinced that Democrats are more likely to push for price controls and protect seniors, even if the progress is slow. I’m also a bit annoyed by the constant partisan bickering; it makes me wonder why the politicians can’t just focus on fixing the basics.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are keeping my prescription costs down and making sure my property taxes don’t skyrocket, because the county has been looking at raising rates to fund new infrastructure projects. I’m not a big fan of the Republican Party; their talk about “lower taxes” feels like a code for cutting services I rely on. The Democrats, for all their flaws, at least talk about protecting seniors and funding health programs. So I’ll be casting my ballot for the Democratic Senate and House candidates, hoping they keep the pressure on the other side to address the issues that matter to folks like me.
