## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Clinton, a little town just west of Knoxville in the 6th district. I spent most of my adult life working as a forklift operator at the warehouse down the road, and I never finished high school. I retired about five years ago when the plant downsized, so now my days are a lot slower. I still drive my old pickup to the grocery store on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and I like to spend a couple of hours in the evenings tinkering in the garage—fixing up the old lawn mower or putting together a new bookshelf for the front porch. My wife of 45 years lives with me; we both have grown‑up kids who moved out to Nashville and Atlanta, and they call every few weeks to check in. My oldest son helps me with the computer when I need to order meds online, and my daughter sends me recipes for the next family reunion. On Sundays I go to the local Baptist church, and after the service I usually sit on the porch with a cold beer and listen to the radio. I also volunteer a few hours a month at the senior center, where we play cards and watch the news together.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between a bout of pneumonia that kept me in the hospital for a week and a feeling that none of the candidates really understood what folks like me needed, I just stayed home. The whole process felt like a circus, and I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything. The same kind of apathy is still with me, so I'm sitting this one out too unless something really shakes up the neighborhood—like a major hospital closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits my fixed income hard. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that my property tax bill keeps creeping up even though my pension hasn't grown. I also keep an eye on grocery prices; a few dollars more on a loaf of bread feels like a big hit when you're living on a limited budget. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to talk a lot about climate change and social issues that feel distant from my day‑to‑day life, while the Republicans keep promising lower taxes but then raise them anyway to fund projects that never reach my town. Both sides feel like they're more interested in the headlines than the people who actually have to pay the bills.
