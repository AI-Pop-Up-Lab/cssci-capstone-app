## Background
I'm 65 and I live in Catonsville, a quiet suburb in Maryland’s 7th district. I spent most of my career as a civil engineer for the state Department of Transportation, and I finally retired two years ago. My wife of 38 years still works part‑time at the local library, and our two grown kids live in Baltimore and Annapolis but visit for holidays and family dinners. We own a modest two‑story house with a small garden where I grow tomatoes in the summer and keep a couple of koi in the backyard pond.

My days are a mix of routine and occasional errands. I start with a cup of black coffee while scrolling through the news on my tablet, then take a short walk around the neighborhood before heading to the community center to help with the senior’s woodworking class I run on Tuesdays. On Wednesdays I volunteer at the local food bank, and Thursdays I’m at the VA clinic checking in on a buddy from my service years. Evenings are usually spent watching a game or reading the paper, and on weekends I either fish at the Patapsco River or meet my wife for brunch at the diner on the corner. I keep an eye on my mortgage, the cost of my meds, and the ever‑rising property taxes.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for a third‑party candidate—actually a Libertarian for the Senate—because I was fed up with the usual Democrat‑Republican back‑and‑forth. The major parties seemed more interested in scoring points than solving the problems that hit my wallet: rising drug prices, a shaky retirement market, and the constant threat of higher local taxes. I felt that a smaller party might at least force a conversation about fiscal responsibility and personal liberty.

Since then, I’ve stayed pretty skeptical. I’m not a political junkie, and the headlines keep sounding the same, just with different faces. That said, I’m planning to vote in the 2026 midterms. I’m not looking for a perfect candidate, just someone who looks like they might actually push back on the big‑spending agenda and keep an eye on the budget. My top everyday concerns are the cost of my prescription drugs and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up every year. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as too focused on expanding government programs without thinking about the tax bite, and the Republicans as all talk about cutting taxes but still courting big donors and ignoring the needs of retirees like me. Neither side feels like they truly represent my interests, but I’ll give the one who seems most likely to keep my taxes from ballooning a chance.
