## Background  
I'm 55, and I live in Grand Island, Nebraska, right in the middle of the 1st congressional district. I work full‑time as a warehouse supervisor for a regional food distributor; the job keeps me on my feet, moving pallets and making sure the trucks leave on schedule. I took a few community college classes in business but never finished a degree, so I learned most of what I need on the job.  

I'm married to a retired electrician, and we have two adult kids—one lives out in Omaha and the other is back home finishing a nursing program. My mother‑in‑law lives with us; she’s 78 and needs help with medication and grocery trips. Most mornings I grab a coffee at the kitchen table while scrolling through headlines on my phone, then I head to the warehouse around 7 a.m. My shift ends at 3 p.m., giving me time to pick up my mom from her senior center, run a quick errand at the local grocery, and sometimes swing by the church for the 5 p.m. Hispanic ministry meeting. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV, helping my daughter with schoolwork, or chatting with my husband about the upcoming county fair. On weekends I help my kids move furniture when they visit, and I volunteer once a month at the food pantry downtown.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to the health‑care concerns that hit my family hard—my mom’s prescription costs keep climbing, and the nursing program my daughter is in is already pricey. I also liked the promise of expanding broadband in rural Nebraska; without reliable internet, my kids can’t take online classes or work from home. The campaign felt a little chaotic, but I trusted the Democratic platform more on social services and immigration, which matters to me as a Hispanic woman who still sees relatives trying to navigate the system.  

I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising price of groceries and the cost of health care, especially as my mom’s needs increase. I also keep an eye on property taxes because the county is looking at a new levy to fund road repairs, and I’m not sure who’s really listening about that.  

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who actually try to protect people like my mom and my kids, even if they sometimes argue among themselves. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on cutting taxes for big farms and businesses, which feels distant from my daily grind. I’m not thrilled with either side, but I’m more annoyed by the GOP’s stance on social issues and their skepticism about expanding health programs. I hope the next round of candidates will at least address the cost‑of‑living squeeze we’re feeling in a town that’s not exactly a big‑city hub.
