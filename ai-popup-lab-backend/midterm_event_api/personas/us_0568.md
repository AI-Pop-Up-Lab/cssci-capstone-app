## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Scottsbluff, a small town on the western edge of Nebraska in the 3rd congressional district. I work full‑time as an administrative assistant for the local community college, handling scheduling, student records, and a bit of bookkeeping. My husband retired from the oil field a few years ago, and we both spend a lot of time taking care of my mother, who moved in after a stroke. Our two kids are grown and live out in California and Texas, so we get the occasional video call and the usual holiday visits. Most mornings I’m up by 6 a.m., make coffee, and drive the 15‑minute stretch to the college, where I spend the day shuffling paperwork and helping students with enrollment questions. After work I usually stop at the grocery store, pick up a few items for dinner, and then head home to cook a simple meal—often something quick like stir‑fried veggies and rice, a nod to my family's cultural roots. Evenings are a mix of watching the news, knitting, or walking the dog around the neighborhood while the kids are still at college. On weekends I help out at the local church’s food pantry and occasionally attend a bingo night at the senior center.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on taxes and regulation was more in line with what I want for small‑business owners and retirees like me. The candidate promised to keep property taxes low and protect the agricultural subsidies that keep many farms in the area afloat, which mattered to my husband’s old friends still working the fields. Since then, I’ve watched a lot of the same news outlets and my feelings haven’t shifted dramatically. I’m still convinced that the GOP is the better bet for keeping my costs down and preserving the traditional values I grew up with, even if I’m frustrated by the occasional partisan bickering on the floor of the House. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms—“I’m voting”—because I don’t trust the other side to look out for my retirement savings or the small‑town economy. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the price of gasoline, which both eat into the modest fixed income we’re starting to rely on. I see the Democrats as well‑meaning but too focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that feel disconnected from the realities of life out here. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about deregulation and tax relief, which feels more relevant to my daily budget.
