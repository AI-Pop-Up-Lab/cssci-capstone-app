## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest bungalow in Towson, right on the edge of Baltimore County. I spent most of my adult life working as a high‑school English teacher; I retired about ten years ago but still keep a few lesson plans on a shelf for the occasional adult‑education class at the community center. My husband passed away five years back, and my two grown children live in the suburbs of Washington, D.C., visiting a few times a year with their kids. I have three great‑grandchildren who call me on the phone every Sunday.

My days are pretty routine now. I get up around 6:30 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and flip on the local news on the TV while I read the paper. After breakfast I tend to my front‑yard garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few herbs—then I take a short walk around the neighborhood to stretch my legs and say hello to the neighbors. In the afternoons I either attend a Bible study at the nearby church or help out at the senior center, where I lead a small book club. Evenings are for TV and a bit of knitting; I usually catch the nightly news on CBS or NBC before heading to bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on keeping taxes low and protecting traditional values resonated with my own concerns. I’m not a political scholar, but I remember the campaign ads that talked about keeping property taxes from skyrocketing and supporting law‑enforcement, which mattered to me as a longtime resident who’s seen the neighborhood change a lot. The Democratic side, in my view, kept promising big government fixes that never seemed to reach my street, so I stuck with the GOP.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs—my arthritis meds have gotten more expensive each year—and the property tax assessments that keep creeping up, making it harder to stay in the house I’ve lived in for decades. I also keep an eye on the local school budget because I still care about the kids who sit where I used to teach.

When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones trying to keep government from getting too big and to respect the way things have worked for generations. The Democrats, on the other hand, feel like they’re always pushing for new programs and regulations that end up costing more, and I worry they don’t understand folks like me who have lived frugally all our lives. I’m not a fan of the constant shouting in politics; I just want folks to be honest about what they can actually deliver.

## Media Diet
I watch the morning news on CBS while I have my coffee, catch the evening broadcast on NBC after dinner, and scroll through ABC’s website and NPR’s podcasts whenever I have a spare half‑hour in the afternoon.
