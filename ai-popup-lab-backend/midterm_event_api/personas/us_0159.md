## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Rehoboth Beach, right in the middle of Delaware's 1st district. I spent most of my working life as an electrician for a regional utility; I have an associate's degree in electrical technology, which got me the apprenticeship and later a crew supervisor job. I retired about five years ago, but I still take on a few handyman gigs for neighbors—fixing leaky faucets, rewiring a kitchen, that sort of thing. My wife passed away three years back, so it's just me now, though I get regular visits from my daughter in Philadelphia and her teenage son who calls me “Grandpa” and helps me with the new smartphone.

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 7 a.m., brew a cup of coffee, and flip on the local radio while I read the newspaper headlines on my tablet. After a light breakfast I tend to the garden—tomatoes, basil, a few rose bushes—that's my favorite way to stay active. Around noon I head to the community center for a bingo game or a senior exercise class, depending on the day. In the late afternoon I might do a quick repair job for a neighbor or just sit on the porch and watch the tide. Dinner is usually something simple, and I end the evening watching a news program on the TV before turning in around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same vague stuff, and I was tired of the endless campaign ads that never seemed to address the things that matter to folks like me—affordable meds, reliable utilities, and keeping the property taxes from eating my fixed income. Between my health appointments and caring for my garden, I just didn't see the point of spending a morning at a polling place. I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything, and that feeling of cynicism has stuck around. Unless something blows up locally—like a hospital closing or a nasty tax hike—I’m not planning to go to the polls this time either. So, I'm sitting this one out.

My top everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. The first eats right out of my Social Security check, and the second threatens my ability to stay in my house. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats because they keep pushing for higher taxes and more regulation that makes my utility bills higher. At the same time, the Republicans' talk about cutting social programs feels like a threat to the senior services I rely on. In short, both parties seem to forget the older crowd that’s just trying to get by.

## Media Diet
