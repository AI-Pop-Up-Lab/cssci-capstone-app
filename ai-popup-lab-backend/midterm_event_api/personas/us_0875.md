## Background
I'm 65, and I live in St. George, right in the heart of Utah's 2nd congressional district. I work full‑time as a maintenance supervisor for a large senior‑living community; the job's mostly hands‑on, fixing HVAC, plumbing, and making sure the building stays safe. I didn't go to college—just finished high school back in the late '70s and picked up trades on the job. My wife, Rosa, helps run the kitchen at the same facility, and we have two grown kids who live out in Arizona and Nevada. Every morning I get up around 5:30 am, make coffee, and head to the site before the sun's too hot. After work I swing by the grocery store, check on my mother‑in‑law who lives nearby, and sometimes stop at the local church for the 6 pm service. On weekends I like to mow the yard, watch my grandkids play soccer at the park, and catch a game on the TV if there's anything decent on. I drive a beat‑up pickup that I've kept for years; it gets me to the hardware store, the VFW hall, and the occasional family barbecue.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old promises, and I was swamped with extra shifts when the filing deadline rolled around. The whole process seemed distant, and I didn't trust any candidate to actually care about folks like me who are just trying to pay the bills and keep the building running. Plus, the mail‑in ballot instructions were confusing, and I figured why bother if nothing changes? That feeling hasn't really shifted. I'm **sitting this one out** for the 2026 midterms unless something dramatic hits my community—like a sudden cut to Medicaid or a major hit to our senior‑living facility's funding. My biggest worries day‑to‑day are the rising cost of groceries and the ever‑growing health‑care premiums that cut into my limited retirement savings. I also keep an eye on property taxes; the county keeps trying to raise rates to fund new roads, and that hits my fixed income hard.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they're talking about big ideas but never showing up in our small towns. The Republicans talk loud about fixing the economy, but most of the time they're more interested in culture wars than fixing the water bill or the local school budget. Neither side seems to get what it's like to juggle a paycheck, a family, and a roof over my head. I get annoyed when the news spins a story into a political point instead of just telling me what I need to know to survive the next month.
