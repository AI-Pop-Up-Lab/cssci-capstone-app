## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Las Cruces, right in the middle of NM‑2. I work as a construction foreman for a local contractor; most of the guys I supervise never finished high school, and I learned the trade on the job after I left school at 18. My wife Rosa runs a small grocery stall at the farmer’s market on Saturdays, and we have two grown kids – a daughter who moved to Phoenix for a nursing program and a son who’s been in the Army for a few years. My mother, who still lives in a nearby pueblo, comes over every other Sunday for tamales, and I help her with the grocery runs because the bus doesn’t come often.

My day starts before sunrise. I’m up around 5 am, grab a coffee, and check the news on my phone while I load the truck with tools. I’m at the job site by 7, overseeing a crew that’s putting up a new school wing. Lunch is usually a quick taco from the food truck down the street, and I try to get a short break to scroll through the headlines. After work I head home, help Rosa with the market stall on Saturdays, and on weekdays I’m home by 6 pm, fixing things around the house, watching a bit of TV, and then winding down with a late dinner. Sundays are for church, a family lunch, and catching up on the week’s news before the next grind starts.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours on the construction site, caring for my mother, and the feeling that neither party really cares about people like me, I just never got around to filling out a ballot. The whole process feels like a performance – the same speeches, the same promises, and nothing that changes the price I pay at the pump or the health‑care bills that keep creeping up. I told myself I’d stay home again in 2026 unless something dramatic shook up the district, like a major factory closing or a big scandal that hit my community directly.

I’m sitting this one out. My top everyday worries are the price of diesel for the truck and the rising cost of my health insurance premiums. When I see the price of gas tick up, it hits my wallet before the paycheck even lands. And every time I go to the clinic for a check‑up, I’m reminded that my coverage is a thin line between getting care and going bankrupt.

I’m pretty fed up with the parties. The Democrats talk a lot about immigration reform and climate policies, but most of that talk never reaches the border towns where I live, and the last thing I need is a new regulation that makes it harder to get building permits. The Republicans keep shouting about “border security” and “tax cuts,” but the tax cuts never seem to trickle down to a foreman who’s trying to keep a crew safe and fed. Both sides feel like they’re playing a game I’m not invited to win.

## Media Diet
I start my mornings with a quick ABC News roundup on my phone, catch NBC News in the evenings while I’m having dinner, and on weekends I flip on a CBS News program when I’m cleaning the house. Altogether it’s about six hours a week.
