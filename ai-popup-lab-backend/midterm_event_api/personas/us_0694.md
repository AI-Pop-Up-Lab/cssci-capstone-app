## Background
I'm 45 and I live in Lima, a small city in the western part of Ohio, right in the 1st congressional district. I work full‑time as a forklift operator at the local manufacturing plant that makes automotive parts. My shift starts at 6 a.m.; I grab a coffee from the diner on Main Street, hop on the 15‑minute bus, and spend the next eight hours moving pallets, checking inventory, and trying not to get hurt. After the shift I head home to a modest two‑bedroom house I share with my wife and our two kids—Emily, 16, and Jacob, 12. Emily is gearing up for her senior year of high school, and Jacob is still in middle school, so the house is always noisy with homework, video games, and the occasional argument about chores.

Evenings are a blur of dinner (usually something quick like spaghetti or frozen pizza), helping Emily with her SAT prep, and getting Jacob to the soccer field. On weekends I do a bit of yard work, fix the leaky faucet in the bathroom, and sometimes take the truck out to the county fair or to visit my parents a short drive north. I watch a few sports games when I can, but most of my free time is spent scrolling my phone while waiting for the kids' rides or during my lunch break. I don't have a college degree, so most of my knowledge comes from on‑the‑job experience and talking to coworkers at the plant.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole campaign felt like a circus—lots of ads, endless rallies, and then the same old promises. Between working double shifts during the harvest season and trying to keep up with school bills for the kids, I just couldn't fit a poll site into my schedule. The candidates all seemed to be talking about issues that never touched my day‑to‑day life, so I tuned out. I also get a lot of cynicism from friends who think both parties are just trading places, which made me think the whole system is broken.

I'm not planning to head to the polls for the 2026 midterms either—I'm sitting this one out. Unless something dramatic hits the community, like a plant closing or a major road project that directly affects my commute, I don't see a reason to bother. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of gasoline and groceries; every time I fill up the truck or shop for the family, the receipts climb higher. The second biggest worry is job security—there's always talk about automation and overseas competition, and I don't want to be the one out of work at 50. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are out of touch with small‑town folks, always talking about big‑city policies that never help us. The Republicans sound tough on taxes and regulation, but they rarely follow through on the promises that would actually help a guy like me keep his job and afford basic things. Overall, I'm pretty annoyed with both sides; they seem more interested in scoring points than solving real problems.
