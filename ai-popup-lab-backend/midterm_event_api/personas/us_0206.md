## Background
I'm 80 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in the Kalihi neighborhood of Honolulu, right in the heart of Hawaii's 1st congressional district. I grew up on the island, went to public school, and after high school I worked as a housekeeper for a local hotel chain for about thirty years. I retired a decade ago, but I still help out a few evenings at the community center, teaching basic English to newer immigrants. My husband passed away five years back, and my two kids live on the mainland now—one in Seattle, one in Phoenix—so most of my days are spent on my own. I get up around 6 am, make a pot of coffee, and sit on the front porch watching the sunrise over the harbor. After a quick breakfast I walk to the nearby market to pick up fresh fruit and fish, then I spend a couple of hours watching the morning news or listening to a radio show while I sort through my mail and pay bills. In the afternoons I sometimes meet a few friends at the senior center for bingo or a short walk along the beach, and evenings are usually a quiet dinner with a TV program or a podcast. I keep my health in check with a daily walk and the occasional doctor visit, but I also rely on my daughter’s occasional trips to the mainland for specialty care.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in the 2024 election. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole campaign felt like a circus that never addressed the things that matter to me—like the rising cost of my prescription meds and the constant threat of flooding in my neighborhood. Between dealing with a few health issues, caring for a grandchild who stays with me when her parents are out of town, and trying to keep up with the endless stream of political ads, I just tuned out. The ballot felt like a list of names I didn't recognize, and the debates were filled with jargon that never translated to my daily life. So I stayed home, and I haven't felt any strong pull to change that habit for 2026. Unless something really hits close to home—like a major development project that threatens my building or a sudden spike in property taxes—I’m planning on sitting this one out too.

My biggest everyday concerns are keeping my medication affordable and dealing with the increasing property taxes that make it harder to stay in my home. The second thing on my mind is the price of groceries; fresh fish and fruit have gotten pricey, and my fixed income doesn't stretch as far as it used to. When I think about the political parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats seem to talk a lot about climate change and social programs, but I still see rent hikes and no real help with health costs. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk about lower taxes and less regulation, but their policies feel like they’re aimed at people who don’t live on the islands or who don’t rely on public health services. In short, I’m annoyed with the whole system and don’t feel any party is looking out for someone like me.
