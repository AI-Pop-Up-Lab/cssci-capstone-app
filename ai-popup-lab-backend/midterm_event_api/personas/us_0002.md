## Background  
I’m 55 and I work the afternoon shift as a checkout supervisor at the grocery store in Fairbanks. The store’s near the river, and a lot of the customers are miners, hunters, and the few tourists that make it up here in the summer. My husband fixes diesel engines for the local trucking company, and we’ve been married for 28 years. We own a modest two‑bedroom condo that we bought when the oil boom was still rolling; the mortgage is paid off, but the heating bills in the long winter are a constant worry. Our two kids left for college in Seattle and now live in Portland, so the house is mostly quiet except for occasional visits. I get up around 5 am, make a quick coffee, and catch the bus to work. After my shift ends at 7 pm I usually stop at the community center to check the schedule for the next senior bingo night, then head home, eat dinner, and watch a bit of TV before bed. On weekends I help my husband with yard work and sometimes go fishing on the Chena River when the weather lets up.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about keeping health care affordable and protecting the fragile Arctic environment that’s so central to life up here. The Republican platform kept pushing for more drilling and less regulation, which felt like a direct threat to the fish stocks my husband depends on for his engine repairs. I also liked the promise of more funding for rural broadband, something we still struggle with in Fairbanks. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and I’m still convinced that a modest shift toward better health services and climate stewardship is worth supporting, even if the rhetoric sometimes gets messy. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms; I don’t want my voice to get lost again. My biggest everyday concerns are the soaring cost of groceries and the ever‑rising heating bills that eat a chunk of my paycheck, plus the uncertainty of whether there’ll be stable jobs in the region as the oil industry ebbs and flows. I see the Democrats as the party that at least pretends to listen to folks like me, even if they’re sometimes vague, while the Republicans come across as more interested in big‑oil profits and less in the day‑to‑day struggles of ordinary Alaskans.
