## Background  
I'm 55 and have lived most of my adult life in Juneau, the little capital city tucked between the water and the mountains. I work full‑time as a customer‑service supervisor at the local grocery co‑op; the job doesn't need a college degree, but after a few years on the floor I learned how to handle schedules, inventory, and the occasional angry shopper. My husband, Luis, retired from the fishing fleet a few years back, and we have two grown‑up kids who live in Anchorage and Seattle. Most mornings I’m up before the sun to make a coffee, feed the dog, and check the weather—if it looks like a cold, windy day, I bundle up the kids' winter coats before heading out. After my shift I usually swing by the community center to help with the Spanish‑language tutoring program; it’s a way to stay connected to the small but vibrant Hispanic community we have up here. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a quick hike on the trail behind our house, and catching the ferry to visit relatives in Ketchikan. Money is tight enough that we watch the utility bills closely, especially when fuel prices spike in the winter.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping health care affordable for my husband’s lingering injuries and about protecting the salmon runs that support the local economy. I also liked the candidate’s promises on broadband expansion; having reliable internet is a lifeline for my kids who need to video‑call us and for my tutoring work. The 2026 midterms feel like another chance to push those issues, but I’m not as fired up as I was in 2020. I’m definitely going to the polls—my name’s on the voter list, I’ve got the ballot in my mailbox, and I’ll drop it off at the post office on the last day. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of heating oil and the fact that the state budget looks like it’s going to cut funding for the community college programs that give people a foothold in tourism jobs. I’m also annoyed that the Republicans keep talking about “energy independence” while ignoring how fragile our ecosystems are; they seem to think the answer is just more drilling, which I think will only make the climate problems worse for us up north. I’m not a big fan of the GOP; they feel out of touch with everyday Alaskans, especially people like me who rely on a mix of public services and small‑business wages. The Democrats, for all their flaws, at least talk about health care, climate, and infrastructure—things that matter to my family.

## Media Diet  
I spend about three hours a week catching up on the news. I usually watch a short CNN segment with my morning coffee, skim the NPR podcasts while I’m driving the ferry, and check the headlines on ABC News on my phone after dinner. It’s enough to keep me in the loop without feeling overwhelmed.

## Opinion Formation  
Constructor. I don’t sit down with a political philosophy handbook; I shape my opinions from what’s happening at home, what my neighbors are saying, and the headlines I see that week. If a storm hits or the price of fuel jumps, that’s what drives my political thinking more than any party platform.
