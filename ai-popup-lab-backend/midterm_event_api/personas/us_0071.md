## Background
I'm 35 and work as a junior accountant at a mid‑size firm in Glendale. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the edge of Peoria, just a fifteen‑minute drive from the office. My wife and I have been together for nine years, and we have a six‑year‑old daughter who is in first grade at the local public school. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30 a.m., making coffee, checking the kids’ backpacks, and scrolling through my phone for the latest headlines before the kids head off to school. I catch the 7:15 a.m. bus to work, listening to NPR podcasts on the way. The workday is a mix of crunching numbers, preparing tax filings, and occasional meetings with the senior staff. After work I usually swing by the grocery store, grab a few things for dinner, and then help my daughter with her homework. Evenings are low‑key: we eat together, watch a bit of TV, and try to get the kids to bed by 9 p.m. On weekends I like to take the family to the nearby desert trails for a hike or to the local community pool. I also spend a few hours each Sunday doing a bit of DIY around the house—fixing leaky faucets or painting the garage door. When I can, I meet up with a few college buddies at a bar downtown for a couple of beers and a game of pool.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about health‑care costs and the rising price of groceries, and the Democratic platform seemed more focused on expanding coverage and protecting Social Security. I also liked the candidate’s stance on water conservation, which matters a lot in Arizona. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the drought situation and the recent push for renewable energy projects in the state. I’m definitely planning to vote in the 2026 midterms—there’s a Senate race that could affect federal water policy, and I don’t want to sit this one out. My top everyday concerns right now are the mortgage payment creeping up as interest rates rise and the water bill that keeps getting higher every summer. I’m also watching the job market; even though my firm is stable, I hear a lot about layoffs in the tech sector and it makes me nervous about long‑term security. As for the parties, I feel the Democrats are generally more in touch with my day‑to‑day worries, but I’m frustrated by the internal fights and the occasional flip‑flopping on key issues. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to be pushing a lot of rhetoric that feels out of step with the realities of living in a desert state—especially on climate and immigration. I don’t trust them to handle water policy well, and their tax proposals look like they’d hit middle‑class families harder than they help anyone.
