## Background
I'm 30 and work as a licensed practical nurse at the community health clinic on the east side of Erie. I grew up in a small town in western PA, moved to the city for an associate’s degree in nursing, and have been living in a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate for the past three years. My days start early: I’m up by 5:30 am, make a quick coffee, and scroll through the news on my phone while getting dressed. My shift runs from 7 am to 3 pm, then I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the local laundromat. After work I usually do a short workout at the gym across the street, then head home to cook something simple—often pasta or a stir‑fry—while the TV plays background noise. On weekends I help my roommate with a small side hustle selling vintage clothing online, and I try to catch up with my parents on video calls. I don’t own a car; the city’s bus system gets me where I need to go, though the routes are a pain. I’m not married, no kids, and my social life mostly revolves around a few close friends I see at the local bar or the community garden on Saturday afternoons.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between juggling long shifts, two jobs, and the constant scramble to keep my rent paid, I felt like my ballot wouldn’t change anything. The candidates all sounded the same, and the campaign ads were more about attack lines than real solutions. I also got sick of the endless negative coverage and the feeling that both parties were only talking to their base, not people like me who are just trying to make ends meet. That cynicism stuck, and I told myself I’d sit this one out too unless something hit close to home—like a major hospital closure or a sudden spike in utility rates that directly affected my paycheck.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m sitting this one out as well. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and the rising grocery prices that make it hard to stretch a single paycheck. I’m also watching the local job market; the clinic’s budget cuts have made me nervous about future hours. As for the parties, I see Democrats as too focused on identity politics and not enough on the day‑to‑day struggles of working‑class folks, while Republicans feel like they’re pushing policies that would raise taxes on the middle class and cut social safety nets. Both seem out of touch, and I’m not convinced either side has a clear plan that addresses my immediate worries.
