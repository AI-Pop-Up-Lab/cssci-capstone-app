## Background
I'm 30 and work full‑time as a cashier at a grocery store in St. George, right in the heart of Utah's 4th district. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment with my boyfriend and our two‑year‑old daughter. My mornings start at 5:30 a.m. when I get the baby fed and dressed, then I drive the 15‑minute commute to the store. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., and after I finish I pick up my daughter from daycare, swing by the laundromat, and try to squeeze in a quick grocery run before we all collapse on the couch. On weekends I help my boyfriend with some yard work at his parents' house and we try to stretch the budget enough for a family outing at the local park. I didn’t go to college; after high school I took a few community‑college courses but never finished a degree, so most of my knowledge about the world comes from what I see on TV, at work, and from friends at the store.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in the 2024 election. Between juggling a toddler, a second job picking up extra shifts, and trying to keep the lights on, I just didn’t feel like I had the energy to get to the polls, and the whole process seemed like a far‑off thing that wouldn’t change my day‑to‑day grind. The candidates all sounded the same to me—big promises about the national economy that never touch a single paycheck at the register. I also felt a lot of cynicism toward both parties; the Republicans keep talking about “family values” while pushing policies that make it harder for a single‑parent household like mine to afford child care, and the Democrats seem to care more about big‑city issues that feel irrelevant in a place like St. George.

I’m sitting this one out too. Nothing major has happened locally that would pull me into the voting booth—no big scandal, no new school‑funding measure that directly affects my daughter’s daycare. I’ll probably stay home unless a candidate shows up at the community center with something concrete for low‑income families.

My biggest everyday worries are rent and grocery prices. The landlord raised the rent last year, and every time I go to the store the price tags seem to climb higher. I’m also watching the property tax bill because it eats into what little savings I have. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The GOP feels like they’re trying to force a one‑size‑fits‑all moral code on a community that’s already diverse, and the Democrats act like they’re only thinking about coastal voters. I wish there were more folks who actually understood what it’s like to stretch a paycheck for a family of three.
