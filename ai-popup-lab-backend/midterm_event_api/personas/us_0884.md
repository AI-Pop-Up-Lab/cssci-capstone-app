## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Charlottesville, right in the part of VA‑5 that still feels a little like a small town. I work full‑time as an administrative assistant at the local community college, handling enrollment paperwork and scheduling for the evening‑class programs. The job is steady, the hours are predictable, and it lets me keep a regular paycheck without the overtime grind that some of my coworkers complain about.

I'm married to a retired electrician who now spends most of his days tinkering in the garage and helping my parents with errands. We have two grown kids—one lives out of state, the other is back home with a toddler, which keeps the house a bit busy on weekends. My parents are both in their late 80s; I drive them to their doctor appointments and help with groceries when my husband can't make it. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the diner down the road, read the headlines on my phone while waiting for the traffic light, then head to the campus parking lot. After work I usually swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the church social hour on Thursday evenings. Weekends are a mix of yard work, a quick visit to the farmers market, and catching a late‑night movie with my husband when we can find a seat.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one that understood my worries about the cost of living. The rising price of gas and groceries hit my family hard, and I liked the candidate’s promise to keep taxes low and cut back on what I see as wasteful government programs. I also appreciated the tough stance on borders; my niece lives in Texas and she told me how much she misses having a clear plan for immigration. That year I felt like the Democrats were pushing too many new regulations that would hurt small businesses like the one my husband runs on the side.

Now that the 2026 midterms are here, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns are still my monthly grocery bill and the property tax hike the county council proposed last spring. If the tax increase goes through, it will squeeze our budget even more, and I’m not sure the state will do anything to help. I’m also watching the local job market; a couple of factories closed last year and I worry about what that means for my kids’ prospects. I think the Republican Party still has the best shot at keeping taxes down and protecting jobs, even if they sometimes sound a bit harsh. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free college and universal health care, but I hear about rising taxes and more government control everywhere they go. I’m not completely sold on any candidate, but I’ll stick with the party that seems to care about my wallet.
