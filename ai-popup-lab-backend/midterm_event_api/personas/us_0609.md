## Background
I'm 25 and I work full‑time as a cashier at a grocery store in Woodbridge, right in the heart of NJ‑9. I grew up in a small apartment with my parents and two younger siblings, and after dropping out of high school I started picking up shifts to help with the bills. My day usually starts at 5 a.m. with a quick shower, then I catch the bus to the store and work a 10‑hour shift that runs until mid‑afternoon. After work I swing by the corner bodega to grab a cheap dinner, then head back home where I help my mom with laundry and sometimes watch a bit of TV before I’m out for the night. On weekends I trade shifts with a coworker so I can help my sister with her college applications, and I try to squeeze in a few hours at the community center where I volunteer to tutor kids in basic math. Money is tight, so I’m always watching my rent, utility bills, and the price of groceries. I don’t have a car, so everything is on public transit, which makes my schedule pretty rigid.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates really cared about people like me who are just trying to make ends meet. Between long shifts, caring for my family, and the constant worry about rent going up, I didn’t have the energy to navigate the voting process, and the whole thing just seemed like a show where the big players get all the attention. I also think the system is rigged—every time I hear about campaign promises, they end up being empty. Because of that, I’m leaning toward sitting this 2026 midterm out as well, unless something really hits close to home, like a local school closing or a sudden rent hike that forces us out of our apartment.

My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and dealing with the rising cost of groceries. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats; they talk a lot about “helping the working class” but the taxes keep climbing and the cost of living is still soaring. The Republicans feel even further away from my reality—talking about tax cuts for the rich while ignoring the fact that I can’t even afford a decent meal most weeks. So I’m not enthusiastic about either side; I just want someone to notice the people who are stuck in low‑wage jobs and actually do something about rent control and food prices.
