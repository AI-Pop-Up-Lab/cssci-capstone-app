## Background  
I'm 74 and I live in St. Johnsbury, a little town up in the Northeast Kingdom of Vermont. I never finished high school; after I left school I worked a few years as a housekeeper for a local nursing home and later helped run the small grocery store my husband owned. He passed away five years ago, so now I'm mostly on my own, though I get regular visits from my daughter who lives in Boston and a teenage grandson who comes for the summer. My days start early—around 6 a.m. I take a short walk around the neighborhood, then I sit at the kitchen table with a cup of tea and listen to NPR while the kettle boils. After that I head to the senior center where I volunteer a few hours a week, sorting donations and helping folks with their paperwork. In the afternoons I tend to my garden, which is mostly herbs and a few tomatoes, and I try to keep the house warm with wood heat. Evenings are for a simple dinner, maybe a TV dinner, and then I flip on the evening news or catch up on the latest stories on ABC or CBS. I still drive my old sedan to the post office once a week, and I’m a regular at the local church on Sundays. Money is tight, so I keep a careful budget, but I manage to pay my mortgage and my prescription costs.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security and Medicare, which are my lifelines. The Republican ads kept talking about cutting taxes for big businesses, and I couldn't see how that would help a 74‑year‑old who lives on a fixed income. I also liked the candidate’s promises to protect rural broadband, which would let my grandson video‑call us more easily. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration handles the rising cost of prescription drugs—a big worry for me. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out because the stakes feel personal. My top concerns right now are keeping my health insurance affordable and making sure the property taxes don’t jump so high that I can’t stay in my house. I’m also watching the local job market because my daughter says the town’s younger people are leaving for better opportunities, and that hurts the community. When I think about the parties, I’m leaning heavily toward the Democrats. They seem to remember folks like me when they talk about “the elderly” or “rural America.” The Republicans feel like they’re more interested in cutting government programs that actually help seniors, and the talk about “personal responsibility” sometimes sounds like they’re blaming people for things they can’t control, like rising medical bills. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the folks in Washington to keep the safety nets that let me live with dignity.
