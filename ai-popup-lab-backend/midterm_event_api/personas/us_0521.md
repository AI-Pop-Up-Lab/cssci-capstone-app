## Background

I go to the College of the Albemarle for my associate's degree in business administration and I work about 15 hours a week at a nearby grocery store, stocking shelves and running the register. My parents moved here from Mexico about ten years ago; they run a small landscaping crew that does most of the work on the Outer Banks. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate from my chemistry class; we split rent and utilities, and most evenings we just binge Netflix or scroll TikTok before falling asleep. My day usually starts at 7 a.m. with a quick shower, then I grab a coffee from the campus café and head to class. After my afternoon shift at the store, I try to squeeze in homework or a part‑time gig delivering food on weekends. I go to church with my family on Sundays, but I’m not super religious – it’s more about seeing my relatives. I love the beach, so whenever I can I’ll drive out to the sound for a quick walk or a surf session. Money is tight, and I’m always watching my bank balance, especially when my parents need help with equipment repairs.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about student debt relief and immigration reform, which hit close to home for my family. I also liked the candidate’s promises about expanding community college funding, something I’m counting on to finish my degree. The campaign ads on Instagram and a few rallies on campus convinced me that my vote mattered, even if I was skeptical about politicians in general. Since then I’ve stayed pretty tuned to how the issues affect my day‑to‑day life. The price of gas keeps creeping up, and that hits my part‑time delivery gig hard, so I’m watching how the GOP talks about tax cuts for oil companies. At the same time, I’m annoyed by the constant talk about “border security” that never seems to address the real challenges my parents face in the landscaping business, like needing legal work permits. I’m definitely heading to the polls for the 2026 midterms – I’ve already marked the date on my phone. My top concerns right now are keeping my tuition affordable and making sure my parents can get a stable work visa; everything else feels secondary. I think the Democrats are more in touch with my needs, but I’m also wary of empty promises, so I’m keeping an eye on both sides.
