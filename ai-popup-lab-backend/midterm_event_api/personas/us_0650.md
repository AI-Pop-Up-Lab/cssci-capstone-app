## Background
I'm 25 and I work the night shift as a stock clerk at a big‑box store in Sparks, just outside Reno. I graduated high school in 2022 and never went to college; the job paid the bills and let me save a little for a studio apartment I rent with a roommate who’s in school. My mornings start around 7 am with a quick coffee from the corner diner, then a 15‑minute drive to the warehouse where I unload pallets and scan inventory until about 3 pm. After work I usually hit the gym for an hour, then swing by the grocery store to grab cheap staples—canned beans, ramen, frozen pizza—because rent and food costs are the biggest thing eating at me. Evenings are mostly streaming Netflix or scrolling TikTok while I try to keep my phone charged for the next shift. On weekends I sometimes help my aunt with yard work in a nearby suburb or meet up with a few friends at a local bar, but most of the time I’m just catching up on sleep. I don’t own a car; I rely on a shared bike program for short trips, which saves a bit on gas. My family lives a couple of hours away in a small town near Elko, and we talk on the phone once a week, mostly about my mom’s health and how the weather’s been.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything, and the whole campaign felt like a circus that never talked about the things that matter to me—affordable rent, decent wages, and reliable public transportation. Between working nights, trying to pay bills, and caring for my roommate who’s dealing with a chronic illness, I just didn’t have the energy to get out, read a ballot, or even follow the debates. The ads were either screaming about immigration or climate policy, and I couldn’t see how either would help me keep my apartment or get a raise. That cynicism stuck, and unless something blows up locally—like a major rent hike in Sparks or a utility shut‑off that hits my building—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out as well. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t skyrocket; everything else feels secondary. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to talk a lot about climate and social programs but ignore the cost‑of‑living crisis in places like mine, while the Republicans keep pushing tax cuts for the wealthy and downplay the need for decent public transit. Neither side seems to get the grind of a night‑shift worker trying to stay afloat, so I’m just waiting for a candidate who actually mentions “affordable housing” in a speech.
