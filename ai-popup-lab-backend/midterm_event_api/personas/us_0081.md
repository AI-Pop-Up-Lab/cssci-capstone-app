## Background  
I'm 50, and I live in Santa Maria, right in the middle of California’s 39th district. I run a small coffee‑roasting shop on Main Street that I opened about twelve years ago after getting a degree in business administration. My husband works as a mechanic at a nearby garage, and we have two kids – a 16‑year‑old daughter who’s into skateboarding and a 14‑year‑old son who plays baseball. Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, pulling the first batch of beans, checking inventory, and making sure the espresso machine is humming. By 7 a.m. I’m at the counter serving commuters and high‑schoolers, then I dash to drop the kids off at school. After the shop closes around 4 p.m., I handle the paperwork, post to social media, and sometimes meet with the local chamber of commerce about zoning issues. Evenings are family time: dinner, homework help, and the occasional Netflix binge. On weekends I’m at the farmer’s market selling beans, or I’m helping my husband with a home‑renovation project. Money is tight enough that I keep a close eye on the bottom line, but I love the freedom of being my own boss.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s tax policies were the only thing keeping my business from sliding into the red. The Democratic tax proposals seemed like they would raise my property taxes and cut back on the small‑business deductions I rely on. I also liked the talk about less regulation on food‑service establishments, which would let me experiment with new menu items without jumping through endless county hoops. The campaign ads about “cutting red tape” and “supporting family‑run businesses” resonated with my day‑to‑day worries.  

Since then I’ve watched the national conversation shift, and while I still think the GOP is the better home for my economic interests, I’m getting more annoyed by the party’s turn toward cultural battles that feel unrelated to my coffee shop. The Democrats keep pushing for higher minimum wages and stricter health‑code inspections, which sound good in theory but could increase my operating costs. I’m also wary of the climate‑policy talk that could bring new zoning restrictions on downtown parking.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent and property taxes manageable, and making sure gas prices don’t keep climbing so my delivery driver can afford to get to work. I’m less enthusiastic about national foreign policy and more focused on who will keep the local economy humming. I view the Republican Party as the one that at least talks about business‑friendly policies, even if some of their rhetoric feels overblown. The Democrats, in my mind, are well‑meaning but tend to overlook the practical impact of their proposals on small owners like me.  

## Media Diet  
I scroll through CBS News and CNBC in the morning with my coffee, catch NPR’s midday podcasts on the drive to the shop, and unwind with ABC News and NPR evening briefs after the kids are in bed. That adds up to about nine hours a week.
