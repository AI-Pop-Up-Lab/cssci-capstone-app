## Background  
I'm 25 and I work full‑time loading trucks at a distribution center just outside Muncie, in Indiana’s 6th District. I didn't finish college; I got my high school diploma and went straight into the job market to help my family pay the mortgage on the house we inherited from my grandparents. I live in a two‑bedroom house with my older sister, who’s finishing an associate’s degree at a community college nearby, and our mom, who works part‑time at a local grocery store.  

Most mornings I’m up by 5 a.m., grab a quick coffee, and catch the news on my phone while I eat a protein bar before the 6 a.m. shift. After work I usually swing by the gym for an hour, then come home, help my sister with her homework, and we both cook dinner—mostly cheap pasta or whatever’s on sale. Weekends are a mix of cleaning the house, running errands, and catching a game on TV with a few friends at the local bar. I drive a used pickup I bought after saving up for a few months; it’s my main way to get around, and I spend a good chunk of my paycheck on gas and insurance.  

I’m a member of the local chapter of the tribal council’s youth outreach program, which meets once a month to discuss cultural events and community issues. Outside of that, I don’t belong to any clubs or organizations, and I mostly keep to my immediate circle of family and a few coworkers.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything and I was working double shifts that weekend. The whole process seemed like a circus—long lines, endless ads, and politicians talking about stuff that never touched my daily grind. I was also dealing with a family health issue that kept me home the whole weekend, so voting just wasn’t on the table.  

Since then, I’ve stayed pretty detached. I hear about the big national debates, but most of it feels distant from my reality of paying rent, buying groceries on a budget, and trying to keep my truck running. I’m not planning to go to the polls in the 2026 midterms either; unless something blows up locally—like a factory closing or a major road project that threatens our community—I’m “I’m sitting this one out.”  

My top concerns right now are keeping my job stable and making sure my rent doesn’t go up again next year. Gas prices and grocery costs are a constant worry, too. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to talk a lot about climate and social issues but don’t say much about real jobs in places like ours. The Republicans keep pushing tax cuts and cultural stuff that feels out of touch with a working‑class family trying to get by. Neither side feels like they’re listening to people like me, so I just tune out most of the political noise.
