## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Grand Island, Nebraska, right near the Platte River. I work as a shift supervisor at the local meat‑packing plant, a job I've held since I was twenty‑four after I finished high school. My husband is retired from the highway department, and we have two adult kids—one lives a few counties away, the other is back in Grand Island working as a teacher. Most weekends I’m either helping my mom with her meds or picking up groceries for the house; we still own a modest ranch‑style home with a small garden out back. My day usually starts early: a quick coffee, a glance at the local weather on my phone, then a two‑hour commute to the plant. After my shift I swing by the hardware store for a few tools, stop at the grocery store, and sometimes meet friends at the diner for a pie. Sundays are for church and a potluck with the neighborhood association, which keeps me plugged into what’s happening locally, even if I don’t follow the national news obsessively.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at the plant, caring for my aging parents, and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about the everyday grind of folks like me, I just didn’t see the point. The campaign ads felt the same—lots of promises about “big changes” that never touch a small town in Nebraska. I also felt a bit skeptical after hearing about the endless recount drama in a few swing states; it seemed like a circus, not a solution to the issues that matter to me. So I stayed home, and I’m leaning toward sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something hits close to home—like a major factory shutdown or a serious hit to my property taxes. My biggest worries right now are the rising price of groceries and the looming increase in property taxes that could make it harder to keep the house we’ve paid off. Healthcare for my parents is also a constant worry; the local clinic’s hours are shrinking, and I’m not sure who’s going to fund better services. As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats talk a lot about climate and social programs, but they never talk about the cost of diesel fuel for trucks that run our farms. The Republicans keep shouting about lower taxes, yet they ignore the fact that we need more infrastructure on the rural highways. Neither side seems to get the straight‑talk I want from my representatives. So yeah, I’m not planning to cast a ballot this midterm.
