## Background
I'm 30, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Hays, Kansas, right in the 1st congressional district. I dropped out of high school when I was 16 to start working full‑time and help my mom pay the bills after she got sick. I’ve been a warehouse associate at the local grain elevator for the past eight years, loading and unloading trucks, running the forklift, and doing the kind of physical work that keeps me on my feet all day. My husband works part‑time at a nearby auto shop, and we have a five‑year‑old son who spends most of his day at the community preschool. On weekends we try to get out to the county fair or a fishing spot, but most evenings are just us watching TV, cooking cheap meals, and trying to stretch the paycheck until the next payday.

My day starts early. I’m usually up by 5 a.m., get a coffee from the gas station, and catch the 6 a.m. shift. After work I swing by the grocery store, grab a few basics, and head home to help my son with his homework before my husband gets back from his shift. We don’t have a lot of extra time or money, so most of my free moments are spent scrolling on my phone, looking at the news, or chatting with friends on social media. I’m not into fancy hobbies; I enjoy listening to country music on the radio while I work, and I watch a lot of the same news sites to keep up with what’s happening.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about the things that mattered to me: keeping gas prices low, protecting my job at the grain elevator, and making sure the border stayed secure. I’ve seen a lot of my neighbors lose work when the market got hit, and I’m skeptical of the Democrats’ promises about big government programs that never seem to reach us in rural Kansas. I also liked the candidate’s tough talk on immigration; even though I’m Hispanic, I’ve seen families struggle when undocumented folks come in without jobs, and I think the rules need to be stricter.

Since then, I’ve stayed pretty consistent. I’m still convinced that the GOP is better for small‑town economies, and I’m not buying the “they’ll fix everything” line from the Democrats. I do worry about the rising cost of healthcare and the fact that my son’s preschool fees keep going up, but I don’t think the left’s proposals will actually help us. I’m planning to go to the polls again for the 2026 midterms – “I’m voting.” My top everyday concerns are keeping my son’s school costs down and making sure my paycheck stretches far enough to cover rent and the rising price of diesel for the trucks we use at work. I see the Republican Party as the one that cares about hard work and personal responsibility, while the Democrats look like they’re more interested in big‑city issues that don’t translate to life out here.
