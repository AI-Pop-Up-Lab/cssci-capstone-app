## Background
I'm 55 and work as a forklift operator at a warehouse on the outskirts of Mansfield, Ohio, in the 13th Congressional District. I didn't finish high school; I left after ninth grade to help my dad on the family farm, and later I learned the job on the warehouse floor. My wife, who used to work at a diner, is now retired after a back injury. We have two grown kids—one lives in Ohio, the other moved to Florida for a construction gig. My mother, who lives a short drive away, needs help with her meds and groceries, so I swing by her house most evenings after my shift.

My day starts around 5 a.m. with a cup of cheap coffee and a quick look at the news on my phone while I wait for the truck to arrive. I work a 10‑hour shift, break for lunch at the cafeteria, then head home around 4 p.m. I try to get home before dark so I can help my mom with the grocery bag and then sit down to watch some TV before dinner. On weekends I do a bit of yard work for neighbors and sometimes help a buddy load a trailer for a hunting trip. I drive a used pickup, keep the radio low, and listen to the same few stations that keep the news short. I don't have a lot of free time, but when I do I like to shoot a few rounds at the range or watch a game of college football.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party cared about the things that matter to me: keeping taxes low, protecting the right to own a gun, and supporting the coal and manufacturing jobs that still exist in our area. I didn't like the way the Democrats talked about big government and higher taxes; it seemed like they'd take away the little wiggle room I have left after paying for my mom's meds and my own health bills. I also liked the candidate’s promises to push back against what I see as overreach from Washington.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. I think my ballot can still make a difference, especially for the Senate race that could affect what happens with health care costs and the federal budget. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of gasoline and the cost of prescription drugs. Every week I have to decide whether to fill the tank or skip a few meals for my mom. Property taxes are also a headache; the county keeps trying to raise them to fund schools and roads, but I feel like the money never gets used right.

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward: the Republicans are the ones who seem to understand folks like me who work hard and don't have a lot of extra cash. The Democrats, in my opinion, keep talking about big ideas but forget about people who are just trying to get by. I get annoyed when the media paints every Republican as a bigot and every Democrat as a savior—it's not that simple, but those are the narratives I see most often.
