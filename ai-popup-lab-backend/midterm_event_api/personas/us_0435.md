## Background
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a general laborer at the paper mill in Escanaba, right in the heart of Michigan's 8th district. I didn't finish high school; I left after sophomore year to help my mom cover the rent when my dad got laid off. The job is noisy, shift‑based, and pays just enough to keep a small apartment with my younger sister, who’s still in community college. Most mornings I’m up at 5 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner Denny's, and catch the 6 a.m. bus to the plant. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., then I head home, shower, and sometimes swing by the local bar for a cheap beer with a couple of coworkers. On weekends I help my aunt with yard work or take the truck out to the lake for a quick fish‑and‑chips dinner. I drive an old pickup that I bought used; it's a bit rusted but gets me where I need to go. My family’s pretty close‑knit – Mom still works part‑time at the nursing home, and we all chip in on groceries and the electric bill. I’m not big on politics, but I keep an eye on what’s happening because it affects my paycheck and the cost of gas when I drive to the next town for supplies.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because the candidate promised to keep taxes low for working folks like me and said he’d protect the plants from new environmental regulations that could cut hours. I also liked the tough talk on crime; the town’s crime rate has crept up a bit, and I felt the Democrats weren’t doing enough. Since then I’ve stayed mostly the same – the mill’s still running, but I’ve seen a few price hikes on diesel and groceries that make every paycheck feel tighter. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’m not planning to sit this one out because the Senate race has a candidate who’s talking about a “jobs-first” agenda that sounds like it could help keep the mill open. My biggest everyday worries are keeping enough cash for rent and groceries, and making sure the cost of gas doesn’t eat up the little extra I try to save for a new truck. I think the Democratic Party is more focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that feel disconnected from my reality, while the Republican side, even if a bit rough around the edges, seems to care more about people who work hands‑on jobs and want to keep their families afloat.
