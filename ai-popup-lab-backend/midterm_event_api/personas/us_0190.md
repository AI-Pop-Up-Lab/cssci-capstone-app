## Background
I'm 55 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Jefferson, a small town that falls in GA‑5. I work the morning shift at the local warehouse, loading and unloading trucks. The job is physically tough, but it’s steady, and I’ve been there for about twelve years. My husband retired from the county sheriff’s office a few years ago, so most of the household income comes from my paycheck and his modest pension. We have two grown‑up kids—one lives in Atlanta, the other is back home taking night classes at the community college. My mother, who came over from Taiwan when I was a teen, still lives with us; I help her with medicine and grocery runs.

My day starts around 5 a.m. with a quick coffee, a sandwich, and a drive to the warehouse. After a six‑hour shift I come home, drop off the kids when they visit, and then head to the local Asian market for fresh produce. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV, catching up on the news, or helping my mother with her phone. Sundays are for church and a potluck with neighbors—mostly a mix of Southern and Asian dishes. Money is tight; we’re careful with bills, especially the electric bill in the summer, and we keep an eye on the price of gas for the truck.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates really cared about people like me—warehouse workers, older immigrants, and folks trying to make ends meet. The whole campaign seemed like a circus, and I was exhausted from work and caring for my mom. I also thought my vote wouldn’t change anything, so I stayed home and watched the news later on my phone. That feeling hasn’t shifted much. I’m still skeptical about the whole system, and unless something dramatic hits my community—like a factory closing or a local scandal—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out too. My top everyday worries are the rent (well, mortgage) and grocery prices; every time the price of rice or fresh vegetables goes up, it squeezes our budget. I also worry about health care costs, especially with my mom’s prescriptions.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re talking about big‑picture stuff—climate, student debt—that doesn’t touch my daily grind. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to promise lower taxes but then push policies that hurt labor rights. Both sides feel distant, and I’m more annoyed by the noise than the platforms. So yeah, I’m sitting this one out.
