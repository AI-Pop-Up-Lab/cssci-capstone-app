## Background  
I'm 34 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Owens Cross Roads, just outside Huntsville. My husband works as an electrician for the power company, and we have two kids—Mia, 8, and Eli, 5. Most mornings I’m up before the alarm just to get the kids dressed, make breakfast, and drop them off at the elementary school that’s a ten‑minute drive away. After that I head to the logistics center where I’ve been a warehouse supervisor for the past six years. The job is mostly shift work—loading trucks, making sure the inventory system is up to date, and dealing with the occasional angry driver who missed a deadline. It’s not glamorous, but it pays the bills and lets me take a lunch break to catch a quick video on my phone.

When I’m not at work, the day usually ends with a quick grocery run at the big box store down the road, then helping the kids with homework while my husband watches the news on the TV. We try to get to church on Sunday mornings; the community there feels like an extension of the family, and the potluck after service is the one time a month I really look forward to. On weekends I sometimes help a neighbor with yard work or drive the kids to their soccer games. Money is tight enough that we keep an eye on every expense, but we manage to put a little aside for the kids’ birthday parties and the occasional family road trip.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me—people who work hard, pay their taxes, and just want to keep a roof over their heads without the government telling us how to run our lives. The candidate’s promises to cut taxes, protect the Second Amendment, and push for more jobs in the manufacturing sector resonated with my daily grind at the warehouse. I also appreciated the talk about “border security” and “law and order,” which felt like a response to the headlines about crime that I see on the news.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening in Washington, but most of my concerns are right here at home. Gas prices have been climbing, and that hits my commute and the cost of getting the kids to school and activities. The mortgage payment is the biggest line item in our budget, and any hint of a property‑tax hike makes my stomach drop. I’ve heard a lot of talk from friends about the Democrats wanting to raise taxes to fund big‑government programs, which I see as a direct threat to the little savings we’ve managed to build.

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. I’m not a political junkie, but when a ballot comes my way I’ll mark the box for the candidate who says they’ll keep taxes low and protect what I see as our freedoms. My top concerns are keeping the mortgage affordable and getting gas prices to stay reasonable. As for the parties, the Republicans feel like the ones who understand the everyday worker—though sometimes they sound like they’re yelling too loudly. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more interested in big ideas that don’t trickle down to folks who are just trying to pay the bills. I’m not thrilled with any of the rhetoric, but I lean toward the side that promises less government interference in my life.
