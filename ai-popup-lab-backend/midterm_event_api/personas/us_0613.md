## Background  
I'm 75, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Mays Landing, a small community in the 2nd district of New Jersey. I spent most of my working life as an electrician for a regional utility, doing on‑the‑job training rather than going to college. I retired about ten years ago, but I still help out with odd jobs for neighbors—fixing a busted outlet or hanging a new light fixture. My wife passed away five years back, and I have two grown‑up children who live in Philadelphia and Atlantic City. I see my grandchildren every other weekend, and they keep me up late playing cards on the kitchen table.  

My days start early; I make a cup of coffee, flip on the radio, and read the local paper while the house is still quiet. After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood, waving to the other retirees who sit on their porches. In the afternoons I volunteer at the senior center, helping run bingo nights and teaching a basic computer class. Evenings are for the TV—usually the news and a bit of classic western reruns—before I head to bed around 10 p.m. The routine is simple, and I like having a predictable schedule.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping taxes low and protecting what I see as traditional values. The economy had been shaky, and the talk about raising property taxes hit close to home—my property tax bill keeps climbing every year, and on a fixed retirement income that’s a real strain. I also liked the emphasis on law‑and‑order and the promise to keep Social Security intact, even if the details were vague.  

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. The latest debates about the state budget and a proposal to cut funding for senior services have me concerned. I’m not a political junkie, but I think the Republican candidates are still more aligned with my priorities—lower taxes, less regulation on small businesses, and a tougher stance on immigration. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for higher taxes and more government control, which feels like a threat to my hard‑earned savings.  

My top everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the ever‑increasing property taxes. If the next round of taxes goes up again, I’ll have to dip into my emergency fund, and that’s not something I want to do. I also worry about the quality of local healthcare; the nearest clinic has long wait times, and I’m not sure the state will invest enough to fix that. As far as the parties go, I see the GOP as the side that remembers the struggles of working folks, while the Democrats seem more interested in big‑government solutions that don’t translate into real help for seniors like me.
