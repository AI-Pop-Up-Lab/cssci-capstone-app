## Background  
I'm 70 and I live in Canton, a small town in the 3rd district of Mississippi. I work part‑time as an administrative assistant at the community health clinic down the road; the job mostly involves scheduling appointments, handling paperwork, and greeting patients. I got my associate degree in office administration at a local community college when I was in my early twenties, and I’ve been doing similar clerical work ever since I left the factory where I started my career.  

My husband passed away five years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live in Baton Rouge and Dallas. We talk on the phone almost every weekend, and they visit me a few times a year. I also look after my 12‑year‑old granddaughter when her mom works night shifts; we bake biscuits together and she helps me tend the garden.  

My day starts around 6 a.m. with a cup of black coffee and a quick walk around the neighborhood with my old Labrador. After that I head to the clinic, usually getting in by 8 a.m. I work until about 2 p.m., then I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from church youth group, and spend the late afternoon either crocheting, watching TV, or helping my granddaughter with school projects. Evenings are usually quiet; I watch the news, read the local paper, and try to get to bed before 10 p.m. because I’m not as good at sleeping through the night as I used to be.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best bet for keeping taxes low and protecting the way of life we grew up with. The economy seemed to be moving in the right direction, and I liked the emphasis on personal responsibility and strong borders. I also appreciated the candidate’s promises to keep Medicare Advantage plans stable, which matters a lot to me and my friends who rely on those benefits.  

Since then I’ve watched the news and talked with folks at the church kitchen, and I still think the GOP is the safer choice for people like me who want to see the cost of living stay manageable. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t plan on sitting this one out. My top everyday worries are the rising price of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes the county has been proposing to fund road repairs. I’m also keeping an eye on the state’s plan to expand Medicaid – I’m not sure if that will help or hurt my budget, but I want to know how it plays out.  

When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who understand small‑town America, who keep the government from getting too big, and who talk straight about crime and immigration. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to be focused on big ideas that don’t translate into anything useful for folks who are already retired and just trying to make ends meet. I get annoyed when the media keeps talking about climate change or “social justice” without mentioning how those policies could raise my electric bill or force me to pay more for my home.  

Overall, I’m leaning toward the Republican Senate and House candidates who promise to keep taxes low, protect Social Security, and not waste money on programs that feel like they’re for someone else’s benefit.
