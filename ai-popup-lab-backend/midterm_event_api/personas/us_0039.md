## Background
I'm 55, and I live in a modest house on the east side of Montgomery, not far from the downtown historic district. I work as a high‑school math teacher at a public school in the district, a job I’ve held for about 20 years after getting my bachelor's in mathematics from Alabama State. My wife, who used to work as a nurse, is now retired and helps me with the yard and the kids. We have two grown children: a daughter who’s finishing a nursing program at a community college and a son who’s in the Army, stationed overseas. My days start early— I’m usually up by 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and drive the 15‑minute route to school. After school I grade papers, coach the chess club, and sometimes stay late for faculty meetings. Evenings are for dinner with the family, catching the nightly news, and when the kids are home for the weekend I help them with chores and watch a bit of sports. On Sundays we attend a Baptist church service and then head to a family brunch at a local soul‑food spot. Most of my spare time is spent on my old pickup, fixing things around the house, or sitting on the front porch with a cold sweet tea.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about the issues that hit my community directly—affordable health care, better funding for public schools, and protecting voting rights. The Republican candidates kept pushing for cuts to education and seemed indifferent to the legacy of segregation that still shapes Montgomery. I was also motivated by the memory of my parents’ civil‑rights activism; casting a ballot felt like a small way to keep that fight alive. Since then, I’ve stayed pretty engaged, though I’m not glued to every campaign ad. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; the school board race and a congressional primary are both on the ballot, and the state legislature is debating a teacher‑pay raise that could affect my paycheck next year. My top everyday concerns are keeping my school funded so my students get decent resources, and the rising cost of groceries that makes budgeting tight for a family on a single teacher’s salary. I think the Democratic Party is at least trying to address those concerns, even if they sometimes talk in vague promises, while the GOP feels like it’s more interested in cultural battles than in fixing the potholes on our streets or making health care affordable. I’m pretty annoyed when politicians use big‑talk without delivering anything concrete, but I still lean toward the side that seems more willing to listen to Black communities in the South.

## Media Diet
I usually scroll through NPR’s morning podcasts on my phone while I’m driving, then catch the evening headlines on ABC News and read a couple of articles on Newsweek before bed. It adds up to about four hours a week.
