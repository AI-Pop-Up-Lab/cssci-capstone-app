## Background
I'm 55, and I live in a small neighborhood on the north side of Rapid City, just a short drive from the Black Hills. I work full‑time as a medical assistant at the community health clinic downtown. The job mostly involves taking vitals, prepping patients for exams, and handling the paperwork for insurance claims—things I learned in the two‑year program at a local community college. My husband, who’s been a construction foreman for the past 30 years, and I have been married for 28 years. We own a modest ranch‑style house with a garden out back where I grow potatoes and beans in the summer. Our two kids are both in their thirties; the son lives in Minneapolis and the daughter is a teacher in Arizona, so most evenings the house is just the two of us.

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. I make coffee, check the weather on my phone, and skim the headlines while the kettle is heating. I’m on the road by 6:15, and the clinic opens at 7 a.m. Lunch is usually a quick sandwich in the break room, where we trade stories about the weather or the latest sports scores. After my shift ends at 3 p.m., I head home, water the garden, and run errands—usually a grocery run or a stop at the local pharmacy. Evenings are low‑key: I cook dinner, watch a bit of TV, and sometimes video‑chat with my daughter. On weekends I help the church with the potluck and do a few extra hours at the clinic if we’re short‑staffed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was fighting for the things that matter to me: keeping energy jobs alive in the Black Hills, protecting the Second Amendment, and keeping federal taxes low. I liked that the candidate talked about supporting small businesses like the family‑run hardware store on Main Street, and I trusted the promises about tougher immigration enforcement, which I thought would keep my community safer. Over the past two years I’ve seen the price of gasoline stay relatively stable compared to the big spikes elsewhere, and I think the policies that encouraged local oil and gas development helped with that.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely voting. My biggest worries right now are the rising property taxes on my house and the cost of prescription meds for my husband’s back pain. I also keep an eye on the local school budget because my nephew is in the district’s elementary school, and any cuts would hit families like ours hard. I still feel the Republican Party is the one that understands life in a place like the Black Hills—hard work, personal responsibility, and keeping government from getting too big. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more focused on big‑city issues and keep pushing for higher taxes and more regulation, which feels out of touch with my daily reality. I’m not a political scholar; I just go with the side that seems to protect my family’s bottom line.
