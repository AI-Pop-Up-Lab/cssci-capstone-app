## Background
I'm 30 and work the night shift loading trucks at a distribution center just outside Fergus Falls. I didn't finish high school, so after a few odd jobs I learned the ropes on the floor—lifting pallets, scanning pallets, and making sure the trucks leave on time. My girlfriend and I rent a two‑bedroom house a few miles from town; we split the rent and the grocery bill, and most evenings we cook something cheap and quick, usually pasta or frozen pizza. I get up around 3 a.m., grab a coffee from the 24‑hour gas station, and drive an hour to the warehouse. After my shift ends at noon, I sometimes swing by the local bar for a beer, catch up with the guys, or help my girlfriend with errands. On weekends I like to fish at the nearby lake, though the gear is old and the fish aren't always biting. I don't have a car beyond the one I use for work; most of my trips are short, and I rely on the occasional Uber when I need to go into town for a haircut or a doctor’s appointment. I keep my phone on a basic plan, mostly for texting and checking the news in the evenings.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was hoping the promised expansion of Medicaid would finally cover my girlfriend’s chronic asthma medication and because the campaign talked about keeping the local factory open. I was tired of hearing about tax cuts that never seemed to reach folks like me, and the idea of more affordable health care felt like a lifeline. Since then, I’ve stayed pretty skeptical about both parties. The Democrats have pushed a few health‑care measures, but I still see a lot of talk and not enough action on wages or affordable housing. The Republicans keep talking about “working‑class values,” yet most of their policies feel like they’re aimed at people who own big farms or corporations, not the warehouse crew. I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out when my paycheck is already stretched thin. My biggest worries are the rising grocery prices and whether my job will stay stable if the warehouse gets automated. I also keep an eye on property taxes because we’re hoping to buy a house soon, and any hike would push that dream farther away. Overall, I’m annoyed with the political circus, but I still think casting a ballot is the only way to try to nudge things in the right direction.
