## Background
I'm 65, and I live in the suburbs of Cary, just outside Raleigh in North Carolina's 2nd congressional district. I work part‑time as an administrative assistant at the local community college, handling enrollment paperwork and scheduling for a few dozen faculty members. I got my bachelor's degree in business administration back in the early ’80s, which helped me land this job after a stint as a clerk in a small family‑owned retail store. My husband, a retired electrician, and I have been married for 38 years. Our only child, a daughter, moved to Charlotte after college but comes home for the holidays and visits us most weekends. Most mornings I brew a cup of dark roast, read the paper headlines on my tablet while the house is still quiet, then drive the 15‑minute commute to campus. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the tribal cultural center where I volunteer to help with language classes for kids. Sundays are for church—our small Baptist congregation is a big part of the community—and a potluck afterward. I enjoy gardening in the backyard and tend to a few native plants that remind me of my Cherokee roots.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party's focus on keeping taxes low and supporting the military resonated with my own concerns about paying for my husband's medical bills and preserving a sense of security for our community. I also appreciated the candidate's promises to protect religious freedoms and to keep the government from getting too involved in small businesses like the one where I used to work. Since then, I've kept an eye on how those promises have turned out. I'm still inclined to back the GOP, but I'm a bit wary of some of the more extreme rhetoric I've seen on social media. Still, I think the party is the better bet for keeping my property taxes from shooting up and for making sure the local school district gets enough funding without raising fees. So for the 2026 midterms, I'm voting. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the way property taxes keep creeping higher each year, squeezing our fixed retirement income. I see the Democrats as well‑meaning but often out of touch with folks like me who live in the suburbs and value personal responsibility; they push for big‑government solutions that feel like they could raise my taxes even more. The Republicans, in my view, are more in line with my desire for low taxes and limited regulation, even if I don't agree with every single stance they take.
