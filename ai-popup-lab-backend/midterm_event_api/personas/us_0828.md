## Background
I'm 20 and I work full‑time as a cashier at a grocery store in the south side of Knoxville. My parents live in Nashville, so I only see them on holidays and when they can drive up for a weekend. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with two other college kids who are also working part‑time jobs; we split rent, utilities, and the occasional pizza order. My days start early—around 5 a.m. I grab a cheap coffee, scroll through the NPR morning news podcast while getting ready, then catch the bus to the store. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., and after work I either study a bit for a community‑college class I’m taking part‑time or help my roommates with chores. Dinner is usually something quick from the freezer aisle, and we spend the evenings watching whatever’s on the TV or scrolling TikTok. On weekends I sometimes work a second shift or help my aunt with a family catering gig. I don’t own a car, so most of my errands are done by walking or the city bus. My family is originally from Taiwan, and we still speak Mandarin at home on Sundays, but most of my friends are fellow students and coworkers who grew up in Tennessee.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between juggling two jobs, school, and trying to keep a roof over my head, politics felt like another thing I couldn’t fit into a packed schedule. The campaigns all seemed the same—big promises, endless ads, and a feeling that none of the candidates really cared about someone like me who’s just trying to make rent and put food on the table. I also felt pretty cynical after seeing the same partisan fights on the news; it just seemed like they were arguing over who gets credit for the same old policies. Because of that, I’m sitting this one out again. I’ll probably go to the polls only if something local—like a proposed rent hike on my building or a new grocery store closure—gets me fired up.

My biggest everyday worries are paying the rent that keeps creeping up and the price of groceries, especially because I’m on a modest wage and my family doesn’t send money regularly. I also worry about the cost of healthcare; a simple cold can turn into a big bill if I have to go to urgent care. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about “green new deals” and identity politics, but I don’t see any concrete help for low‑income workers. The Republicans brag about tax cuts, yet they push for cuts to social programs that I rely on, like Medicaid expansions. It feels like they’re both playing a game that leaves folks like me stuck in the middle.
