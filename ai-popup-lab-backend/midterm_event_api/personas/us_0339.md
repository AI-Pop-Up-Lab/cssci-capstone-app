## Background
I'm 25 and I work the night shift loading trucks at the distribution center just outside Hazard, Kentucky, in the 5th District. I grew up in a two‑bedroom house on the edge of town with my mom, who works part‑time at the local grocery store, and my 19‑year‑old sister who just started community college. After I got my high school diploma I took a job as a forklift operator at the same plant; the pay's decent enough to keep the lights on, but I still have to chip in for the rent and the car loan. My days start early— I’m usually up by 5 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner Denny’s, and catch the 6 a.m. bus to the plant. After my 10‑hour shift I come home, cook something quick, and spend a couple of hours on the internet before I’m out for a few hours of video games or hanging out with friends at the local skate park. On Sundays I help my mom with chores and sometimes we drive my sister to her classes in Ashland. I don’t have a college degree, but I’ve taken a couple of online certifications for forklift safety and basic computer skills to stay employable.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in the 2024 election. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates were speaking to people like me. The campaign ads were all about abstract policy talk while I was trying to make rent and keep my truck running. I also work nights, so the voting hours never line up with my schedule, and I never got a reminder to vote early or by mail. It just seemed easier to skip it than to waste a night figuring out where to go. That feeling of being ignored hasn’t changed much, so I’m planning to sit this one out too unless something huge happens locally—like a plant closure or a major road project that directly affects my job. My biggest worries right now are keeping my job stable when the company talks about automation, and the rising cost of gasoline and groceries that eat up my paycheck. I’m also nervous about the health‑care costs if I ever need a serious check‑up; my mom’s insurance is on a shaky plan, and I don’t have any of my own.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats talk a lot about racial justice, but the promises feel far away from the factory floor. The Republicans keep saying “lower taxes” and “less regulation,” but I don’t see any real help for someone working a night shift in a small town. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the day‑to‑day problems that keep me up at night. I’m skeptical of the whole system, which is why I’m not motivated to go to the polls unless something forces my hand.
