## Background
I'm 40, and I work as a dental hygienist at the community health clinic downtown. I live in Decatur, Illinois, in a modest two‑bedroom house a few blocks from the elementary school my kids attend. My husband works the night shift at the local factory, so most mornings I'm up early getting the kids ready, making coffee, and packing lunches before I head to the clinic at 7 a.m. My day is a mix of cleaning teeth, filling out paperwork, and chatting with patients about their health. After my shift ends around 3 p.m., I pick the kids up from school, help with homework, and we swing by the grocery store before dinner. On weekends we usually go to church, visit my parents who live an hour away, or do some yard work. I’m fluent in Spanish and English, which comes in handy at work and when I help my neighbor’s kids with their Spanish homework. When I have a spare hour, I binge true‑crime podcasts or scroll through TikTok, but most of my free time is spent with family.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was more likely to protect my kids’ health care and keep tuition affordable at the community college where I took my associate’s degree. The candidate’s promise to expand Medicaid and fund school nurses resonated with me, and I was tired of hearing about tax cuts that seemed to benefit the wealthy while my grocery bills kept climbing. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are being followed up. I’m still leaning Democratic, but I’m a bit wary after hearing about some internal party squabbles and the lack of progress on immigration reform, which matters to my extended family back in Mexico.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax increase the city council pushed last year, which makes it harder to keep up with the mortgage. I also worry about the local hospital’s funding because several of my patients rely on it for basic care. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the more compassionate side, even if they’re sometimes sloppy, while Republicans feel like they’re shouting about “law and order” without really listening to folks like me who are just trying to make ends meet. I don’t expect miracles, but I’m hoping the next round of candidates will actually follow through on the promises that affect my daily life.
