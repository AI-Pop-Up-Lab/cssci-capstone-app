## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Marquette, the biggest town in Michigan's 1st district. I work as a shift supervisor at the local paper mill; the job's mostly about keeping the line moving, making sure the crew gets their safety gear, and reporting any downtime to the plant manager. I went to community college for a couple of years, took some business classes, but never finished a degree. My husband runs a small landscaping business that does a lot of work for the city and the university, and we have two kids—Jenna, 22, who just started community college, and Max, 16, who rides his bike to the high school football games.

My days start early. I’m usually up by 5 a.m., making coffee and scrolling through the news on my phone while the kettle boils. The morning shift at the mill starts at 6 a.m., runs until 2 p.m., then I head home for a quick lunch, often a sandwich I packed the night before. After work I pick up Max from practice, drop Jenna at the campus library, and we all eat dinner together around 6 p.m. Evenings are for catching a few episodes of a crime drama, checking the news, and sometimes helping my husband with invoices. On weekends I’m at the farmers market, fixing up the lawn, or helping a neighbor shovel the driveway after a snowstorm. I’m a regular at our local United Methodist church, where I volunteer for the food pantry once a month.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about keeping the mill running and protecting the jobs that keep our town afloat. The Democratic candidates kept pushing for higher taxes and stricter environmental rules that, to me, sounded like they’d shut down the plant. I also liked the talk about securing the border and keeping fuel prices down—things that affect my commute and the cost of heating the house in our long, cold winters.

Since then, I’ve watched the news and heard a lot of talk about inflation and the national debt. I’m still pretty sure the GOP is the better side for my family’s bottom line, but I’m also a bit annoyed that some of the party’s rhetoric feels like it’s aimed at big‑city voters instead of folks out here in the Upper Peninsula. The Democrats still seem out of touch with the reality of a town that depends on manufacturing and tourism.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax hikes the county board is proposing. I also worry about whether the state will keep funding for the local hospital and the highway repairs that keep trucks moving in and out of town. As far as the parties go, I think the Republicans have the right ideas about the economy but need to remember the small‑town voters they rely on. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about climate change and social issues without offering concrete plans for keeping our factories open or making housing affordable for working families.

## Media Diet
I start my day with NBC News headlines on my phone, then in the evening I watch a few Breitbart videos while I’m winding down, and I skim CNBC during my lunch break at the plant. All together it adds up to about eight hours a week.
