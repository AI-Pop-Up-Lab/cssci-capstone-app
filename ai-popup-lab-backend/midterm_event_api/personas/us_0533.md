## Background
I'm 35 and I live in Garner, a suburb just east of Raleigh that falls in NC‑13. I work full‑time as a logistics coordinator for a regional distribution company; the job mostly has me juggling shipping schedules, talking to truck drivers, and making sure pallets get moved on time. I got my bachelor's in business administration from UNC‑Charlotte, so the office work feels like a step up from the warehouse floor I used to be on.  

My wife, Maria, works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have a nine‑year‑old daughter, Sofia, who goes to the public elementary school down the road. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 am, grab a coffee, and listen to NPR while I drive Sofia to school and then head to the warehouse. After work I usually pick her up, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner before we all watch whatever’s on the TV. Weekends are a mix of family soccer games, grocery runs, and catching up on a few episodes of a crime drama on Netflix. I don’t have a lot of extra cash, so I keep a close eye on the rent and the utility bills.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long shifts at the warehouse, the kids’ after‑school activities, and feeling like none of the candidates really understood the day‑to‑day grind, I just didn’t make it to the polls. I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything, especially when the campaign ads seemed more about national drama than the issues that hit my paycheck. That feeling of cynicism didn’t go away, so I’m sitting this one out again for the 2026 midterms unless something hits close to home—like a local school funding fight or a sudden rent hike that forces a move.

My top everyday worries are the rising grocery prices and the property taxes that keep creeping up on our house. Every time I go to the store, the price tags seem higher than last month, and the county keeps sending notices about higher tax assessments. I’m also worried about immigration policies that affect my extended family back in Mexico; the headlines make it feel like we’re always on the edge of being separated.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats. They talk a lot about climate and social programs, but I don’t see any concrete help for families like mine who are just trying to keep the lights on. The Republicans, on the other hand, push for lower taxes and tougher immigration rules, which sounds good on paper, but their rhetoric sometimes feels hostile toward people like me and my community. Overall, I’m fed up with the noise and just want policies that make rent affordable and groceries cheaper.

## Media Diet
I catch NPR’s morning news on the drive, then scroll ABC News and CBS News in the evenings after Sofia’s bedtime. On weekends I binge a few CNBC segments when I have the TV on while doing chores. All together it’s about seven hours a week.
