## Background
I'm 65 and I live in a modest house in the Kakaʻako neighborhood of Honolulu, right in the middle of the HI‑1 district. After I finished a couple of years of community college, I landed a job as an administrative assistant at the local community college, where I handle enrollment paperwork and help students with registration. I work part‑time now, about three days a week, because my husband passed away a few years back and I’m the primary caregiver for my mother, who has early‑stage dementia. My two grown‑up kids live on the mainland, so I talk to them on FaceTime most weekends. My day usually starts with a strong cup of Kona coffee, a quick scan of the headlines on my phone while I’m waiting for the bus, then a shift at the college. After work I pick up groceries at the market, swing by the church for the Wednesday Bible study, and try to fit in a walk along the waterfront before dinner. On Sundays I help out at the senior center, serving meals and listening to folks share stories. The routine is simple, but it keeps me busy and connected to the community.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health care and protecting our islands from the worst impacts of climate change. I’m still pretty convinced that the Democrats are the ones paying attention to rising utility bills and the need for more robust public transportation on Oʻahu. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in small‑business grants, which matters to the few local shops that still survive in my area. I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms—I'm heading to the polls in early November. My biggest worries right now are the cost of living, especially rent and grocery prices that keep climbing, and the strain on the health‑care system for seniors like my mother. I’m also watching the housing market; new condos keep popping up, but they’re out of reach for many longtime residents. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty sure the Democrats are at least trying to address those issues, even if they’re not perfect. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and less on the everyday struggles of people like me. I don’t trust them to prioritize climate action or protect Medicare. So, while I’m not a fan of every Democratic policy, I feel my vote matters more there than on the other side.

## Media Diet
