## Background
I'm 65, and I work as a maintenance supervisor at the petrochemical plant just outside McAllen, right in the heart of Texas's 18th district. I got my associate's degree in industrial mechanics from a community college, and that got me the hands‑on job I’ve been doing for the last 30 years. My wife, Linda, retired from the local school district a few years back, and we both take care of my mother who lives with us; she needs help with medication and a little mobility assistance. Our two kids live in Austin and Dallas, so most weekends I'm either fixing things around the house or watching the grandkids when they visit for a day or two.

My day starts around 5 a.m. with a quick coffee, a newspaper—mostly the print edition of the *Houston Chronicle*—and then I’m at the plant by 6 a.m. The shift runs until 3 p.m., with a short lunch where we swap stories about the latest equipment issues. After work I usually swing by the local grocery store, pick up a few things, and then head home to help my mother with dinner. In the evenings I sit on the porch, flip on the TV for the news, and sometimes listen to an NPR podcast while I read a mystery novel before bed. Sundays are for church, a potluck with neighbors, and a bit of yard work if the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate promised to keep taxes low, protect the border, and keep the energy jobs that keep my plant running. I felt the Democratic platform was pushing too much regulation that could hurt the kind of work I do. The economy felt steady that year, and I thought a solid Republican would keep the cost of living from climbing too fast for folks like me on a fixed retirement income.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising gas prices that make my commute and the cost of my mother's medication, plus property taxes that keep creeping up on our home. I’m not thrilled about any party, but I still trust the GOP more on keeping business-friendly policies and staying tough on immigration, which I think affects our community directly. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about big social programs but never seem to address the real‑world costs that hit retirees and small‑business owners. I’m not going to sit this one out unless something crazy happens, like a major scandal that makes the whole system feel broken.

## Media Diet
I catch the morning headlines on CBS News while having my coffee, then tune into ABC News in the evening after dinner. On weekends I listen to a couple of NPR podcasts while doing chores, which adds up to about nine hours a week total.
