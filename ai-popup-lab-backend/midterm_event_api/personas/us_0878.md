## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Brigham City, right on the edge of Cache Valley. I spent most of my working life as an electrician for the local utility, a job I got after finishing a two‑year program at a community college. I retired three years ago, but I still do a few part‑time gigs fixing wiring for older homes that need updates for newer appliances. My wife, Marlene, and I have been married for 45 years; our two kids live out in Salt Lake City and Reno, so most evenings the house is pretty quiet. We have a golden retriever named Scout who needs a walk every morning before I sit down with a cup of black coffee and the local paper. After that I usually head to the senior center where I volunteer to teach basic computer skills. My days are a mix of that, a little gardening in the backyard, and catching up on the news while I eat lunch. On weekends I like to drive out to the mountains for a short hike, then meet a few friends at the diner for a burger and a cold beer.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because Medicare was a big issue for me. The campaign promised to protect and even expand coverage, and I was convinced that the Republican platform was flirting with cuts that could affect my prescription drug costs. I also liked the emphasis on renewable energy projects that would bring jobs to our district without hurting the air we breathe. Since then, I've kept an eye on how the administration follows through. Some of the promises have stalled, but I still feel the Democrats are the safer bet for my health care and for protecting the little things that matter to seniors like me. I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms; I think my voice matters, especially when local issues like water rights and property tax hikes keep popping up. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription meds and the property tax assessments that keep going up on our older home. I get annoyed with the Republicans—they seem more interested in cultural fights than in fixing the pipelines or broadband gaps we need out here. The Democrats aren't perfect either; sometimes they talk about big national programs that feel distant from our small‑town reality, but at least they're more willing to talk about Medicare and social safety nets.
