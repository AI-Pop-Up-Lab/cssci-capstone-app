## Background
I'm 20 and I’m in my third semester of an associate’s program at a community college in Minneapolis, taking a bunch of general education courses while I figure out if I want to transfer to the University of Minnesota. I live with a roommate in a two‑bedroom apartment in Bloomington, just a short bus ride from the mall and a grocery store that stays open late. My parents own a small hardware shop back in St. Paul, so I fly home for holidays and help out a few times a year when they need an extra pair of hands.

Most weekdays I work a 20‑hour shift at a coffee shop downtown, pulling espresso shots in the morning and cleaning tables in the afternoon. After my shift I head to class or hit the library to finish assignments. On weekends I either binge‑watch a new series with my roommate, go to the local farmers market, or volunteer at the community garden behind the apartment complex. I’m on a tight budget, so I keep an eye on my rent, utilities, and the occasional car‑share expense when I need to get to a job interview or a concert.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the presidential candidate’s stance on protecting reproductive rights and expanding tuition assistance felt like the only thing that mattered to me at the time. I also liked the climate plan that promised to invest in public transit, which would make my bus rides easier. I wasn’t thrilled with any of the Republican candidates—they seemed more interested in cutting social programs than helping people my age who are just trying to finish school and find stable work.

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. The Senate race has a candidate who’s been pushing for more funding for community colleges, and that’s something I can’t ignore. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of rent and the price of groceries; every month I have to decide whether to skip a night out or cut back on food. I also worry about student loan debt, even though I haven’t taken a loan yet, because my friends are already feeling the pressure.

My view of the parties is pretty blunt: I think the Democrats are at least trying to address the issues that affect younger folks, even if they sometimes move too slowly. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem disconnected from the realities of people like me—talking about tax cuts while I’m trying to make ends meet feels tone‑deaf. I’m not a political theorist; I just want policies that keep my rent affordable and my future job prospects decent.
