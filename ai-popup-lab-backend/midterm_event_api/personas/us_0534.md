## Background

I dropped out of high school when I was 16 to start working full‑time at the local lumber mill, and I've been a warehouse operative there ever since. My wife works part‑time at the grocery store, and we have two kids—Javier, 12, and Maya, 9—who both go to the public middle school a few miles away. Most mornings I wake up at 5:30 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner deli, and catch the early news on the TV in the kitchen while the kids get ready. ## Politics and this election
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between working double shifts, the kids’ school schedules, and trying to keep the house from falling apart, I just never found the time or motivation. The whole process feels like a show where the same faces keep popping up, promising change that never seems to stick. I’m pretty sure my vote wouldn’t have made a dent, so I stayed home. That feeling hasn’t really changed; unless something blows up locally—like a factory closure or a huge road project that hits our community—I’m going to sit this 2026 midterm out too. My biggest worries are keeping up with rent and mortgage payments and making sure the grocery bill doesn’t explode. The local job market feels stagnant; the mill’s hours are cut whenever demand dips, and there aren’t many other options nearby. As for the parties, I see both of them as the same old talk, each promising tax cuts or more social programs, but mostly arguing over who gets credit for the same old policies. I’m more annoyed by the Democrats for trying to push new regulations that could hurt the mill, and the Republicans for talking about “big government” while cutting services we actually rely on, like the local health clinic. It all feels like a tug‑of‑war that leaves folks like me stuck in the middle, watching the same headlines and hoping something finally changes for the better.
