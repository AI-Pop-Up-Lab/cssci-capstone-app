## Background

I'm 40, and I live in Oak Ridge, a small town that feels more like a suburb of Knoxville than a separate city. I work as a civil engineer for a regional construction firm; most days I'm on site checking blueprints, making sure the crews follow safety rules, and coordinating with city planners. My husband runs a landscaping business out of our garage, and we have two kids—13 and 9—who both go to the same public middle school a few miles away. My mom still lives in a nearby reservation, so I drive up to her on weekends to help with groceries and errands. Mornings are a blur of coffee, school drop‑offs, and a quick scan of the news on my phone before I hit the road. Evenings are usually dinner with the family, homework help, then a couple of hours of TV or scrolling before I crawl into bed. On Sundays we attend the local Baptist church, and after service I usually meet a few neighbors for coffee at the diner downtown. The cost of keeping the house in good shape and the kids' extracurriculars eats up a good chunk of our budget, so I pay close attention to any changes in property taxes or utility rates.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on keeping taxes low and protecting gun rights matched what mattered to me and my family. My husband runs a small business, and the idea of fewer regulations sounded like a lifeline. I also liked the candidate’s emphasis on supporting the energy sector, which is a big employer in our area. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration handles the economy and the border. I’m still pretty convinced that the GOP is the better choice for keeping my household costs down, even if I sometimes think some of the rhetoric gets a little over the top. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t see any reason to sit this one out unless something really shakes up the local scene.

My top everyday concerns right now are grocery prices and property taxes. Every time I go to the store I notice the milk and meat costing a few dollars more than last year, and the county just sent out a notice that property taxes are slated to rise again to fund road repairs. I’m frustrated with the Democrats because most of their proposals feel like they’re aimed at big cities and don’t consider the ripple effects on places like Oak Ridge. The Republicans, for all their noise, at least talk about cutting taxes and giving small businesses a break, which is what we need. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the next few years to be steady enough that I can keep the lights on and the kids in school without constantly worrying about the next bill.
