## Background
I'm 45, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Anderson, South Carolina. I work full‑time as a floor supervisor at a regional home‑improvement store; the job mostly involves scheduling crews, handling customer complaints, and making sure the inventory stays straight. I took a few courses at a community college but never finished a degree, so most of what I know about the trade comes from on‑the‑job training. My husband is a mechanic at the same plant that’s been the town’s biggest employer for decades, and we have two kids—16‑year‑old Maya, who’s gearing up for college applications, and 12‑year‑old Eli, who is always on his phone. Our mornings start around 5:30 a.m. with coffee and a quick glance at the news on my phone before I head out to the store. After work I pick up the kids from soccer or band practice, help Maya with her essays, and try to get dinner on the table before the house gets too noisy. Weekends are a blur of grocery runs, yard work, and the occasional visit to my sister’s house in Greenville. Money’s tight enough that we keep a close eye on the budget, but we manage to put a little aside for emergencies.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. I felt like the whole system was a circus and none of the candidates really cared about folks like me who are just trying to keep the lights on and get the kids through school. Between long shifts, the kids’ schedules, and a feeling that my vote wouldn’t change anything, I just stayed home and watched the news instead. The whole thing left me with a sour taste, and honestly, I’m not convinced anything’s different now. I’m sitting this one out, too, unless something hits close to home—like a major factory shutdown or a sudden hike in property taxes that would knock us out of the budget. My biggest worries day‑to‑day are the rising grocery prices and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. I’m also watching health‑care costs; a single unexpected visit can throw the whole budget off balance. As for the parties, I see Democrats as talking a lot about change but never delivering anything that helps regular people in a place like ours. The Republicans seem to promise a return to “the good old days,” but most of the time it feels like the same old talk that never trickles down to the small towns. Both sides feel distant, and I’m tired of the endless back‑and‑forth that never ends up fixing the things that matter to me.

## Media Diet
