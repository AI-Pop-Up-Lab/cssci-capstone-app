## Background  
I'm 74, and I live in a modest ranch-style house on the outskirts of Paragould, right in the middle of Arkansas's 2nd district. I grew up on a farm near Jonesboro, went to a two‑year community college where I got a certificate in bookkeeping, and spent most of my adult life working as a clerk at the local grocery store. I retired five years ago, but I still pull a few shifts during the busy holiday season because the extra cash helps with the rising cost of my prescription meds.  

I'm a widow; my husband passed away three years ago. We raised two kids who are both married now and live in Little Rock and Memphis. I see my grandchildren a few times a year, and when they're in town I take them fishing at the nearby lake or bake cookies for school events. My days usually start with a strong cup of black coffee while I flip through the local paper, then a walk around the neighborhood to keep the joints loose. I attend the Methodist church twice a week, and I volunteer once a month at the senior center, helping with bingo and meals. In the evenings I usually sit in the living room, watch a bit of TV news, and sometimes call my sister in Missouri to chat about family and the weather.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one standing up for folks like me—people who worked hard all their lives and now just want to keep a roof over their heads and afford their medicine. The Democrats kept talking about big government solutions that never seemed to trickle down to the small towns. I liked that the Republican candidate promised to cut taxes and keep the military strong, and I trusted the promise that the Federal Reserve would finally tame inflation.  

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to the polls. I'm voting for the Republican Senate candidate who says he'll protect Social Security and keep the VA hospitals funded. My top everyday concerns are the price of my blood pressure pills and the property tax hike the county just approved to fund a new road project that seems to bypass my street. I also worry about the local factory closing down; there aren't many jobs left for the younger folks who want to stay in town.  

When I think about the parties, the Republicans feel like the ones who remember what it's like to be a working‑class senior. They talk about personal responsibility and keeping government from getting too big. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to care more about climate conferences and identity politics than fixing the potholes on Main Street or making sure my Medicare checks come on time. I get annoyed when they talk down to older voters like me, assuming we don't understand modern issues. I'm not a political scholar, but I know I want someone who respects the values I grew up with and who won't raise my taxes just to fund a program I don't use.
