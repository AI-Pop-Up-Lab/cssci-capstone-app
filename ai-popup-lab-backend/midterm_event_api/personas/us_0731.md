## Background

I work as a foreman for a regional construction firm; we do a lot of residential projects and a few small commercial jobs around the Portland metro area. I didn't finish my associate's degree in civil engineering, but I learned a lot on the job and now I'm responsible for a crew of about a dozen guys.  

I'm married, and we have two kids—12‑year‑old Maya and 9‑year‑old Luis. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30, pour a coffee, and get the kids ready for school. I drop them off at the elementary school on the way to the job site, where I spend the bulk of my day on the road, checking plans, making sure safety rules are followed, and talking to the foremen of other crews. After work I pick up the kids, help with homework when I can, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner before we all watch a bit of TV. On weekends I do yard work, take the family to the local park, and sometimes help a neighbor fix a leaky faucet. I speak English and some Spanish at home, mostly with my parents who still visit from Mexico a few times a year.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about the things that keep my family afloat—steady wages for construction work, lower taxes on my paycheck, and a tougher stance on illegal immigration that I think has been hurting jobs in the region. I also liked the promise of cutting red tape for small contractors like the one I work for. Since then, I've kept an eye on how the administration has handled the economy and the border. The gas prices have been a pain, and the cost of building materials keeps creeping up, so I'm still leaning toward the GOP, but I'm also watching what the Democrats are saying about infrastructure spending and climate incentives that could bring more projects our way.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the price at the pump and my property tax bill—both have been rising faster than my raise. I also worry about whether there will be enough local construction jobs next year as the big developers shift to greener projects. I see the Republicans as the party that gets the job market moving, even if some of their rhetoric feels a little over the top. The Democrats, in my view, are good at talking about climate and social issues, but they keep pushing higher taxes and more regulations that make it harder for people like me to keep a steady income.
