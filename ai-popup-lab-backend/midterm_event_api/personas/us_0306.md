## Background

I'm 20 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the east side of Wichita, right near the river trail. My roommate and I split rent and utilities, which keeps the monthly cost just under $800. I’m in my second year at Wichita Area Technical College, taking an associate’s degree in business administration, but I’m also working part‑time at the local grocery store. My shift usually starts at 7 a.m., I’m on the floor until about noon, then I head to class for a couple of afternoons a week. When I’m not working or in class I’m either scrolling TikTok on the bus ride home or doing a quick grocery run for us. On the weekends I sometimes pick up extra shifts, or I go to the downtown farmers market with my roommate, grab a coffee, and hang out at the library to catch up on readings. I don’t drive a car; the bus system gets me where I need to go, and I’m trying to save up for a used car after I finish school. My parents live a few hours away in a small town near Salina, and I talk to them on Sundays, but most of my daily life is here in Wichita.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Between juggling a morning shift, afternoon classes, and trying to keep up with rent, I felt like there was no time to even figure out the ballot. More than that, I was fed up with the whole circus—both parties seemed to promise the same thing about “making life better” while ignoring the real pressures on people like me: rising tuition, a tight rental market, and a job that barely covers the bills. I thought my single vote wouldn’t change anything, so I stayed home. That feeling hasn’t really shifted. I’m still skeptical that the system will listen to a 20‑year‑old college student working a part‑time job. Unless something blows up locally—like a big price hike at the grocery store or a scandal that hits the community directly—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out as well. My top everyday concerns are paying rent on time and keeping my tuition from spiraling higher; everything else feels secondary. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as good at talking about student debt but rarely delivering concrete relief, and the Republicans as all about cutting taxes for big businesses while ignoring the squeeze on low‑wage workers. Both sides feel like they’re more interested in scoring points than solving the problems that affect my day‑to‑day life.
