## Background
I'm 55, and I live in a small rental house on the edge of Spokane, Washington, in the 2nd congressional district. I never finished high school; I left after ninth grade to start working full‑time at the local lumber mill. The job is physically demanding and the pay is just enough to keep the lights on and cover the rent. My wife, who also works part‑time at a grocery store, and I have two grown‑up kids who moved out a few years ago. My mother lives a couple of hours away in a rural town, and we help her with groceries and a little cash when she needs it.

My days start early. I’m up around 5 am, grab a coffee from the diner down the street, and catch the first shift on the mill. After a six‑hour stint I head home for a quick lunch—usually a sandwich and whatever’s in the fridge—then back for the afternoon shift. When I’m not at work I’m either fixing things around the house, watching a game on TV, or scrolling through my phone while waiting for the next break. Weekends are a mix of yard work, a few trips to the hardware store, and the occasional visit to my sister’s house for a barbecue. I don’t drive far unless it’s for work or to pick up my mom; most of my life revolves around the mill, the house, and the local community center where I occasionally volunteer to help with the senior‑citizen basketball league.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about the kind of jobs people like me have. The candidate promised to keep regulations on the timber industry low and said something about protecting “hard‑working families” from too much government meddling. I was also fed up with the rising cost of gasoline and the way the media kept talking about climate policies that seemed to ignore the reality of people who need to drive trucks to make a living. The campaign ads I saw on the radio and the flyers I got at the mill made it feel like the GOP was the only one listening.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m still leaning Republican; I’m not thrilled with any candidate, but I think the Democrats keep pushing for higher taxes and more regulations that could hurt the mill. My top everyday concerns are keeping my paycheck steady—especially with the price of diesel going up—and making sure my mother can afford her medication and a warm place to live. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats; they talk a lot about climate change but seem to forget about the people who actually have to put wood on a truck and bring it to market. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about “energy independence” and “border security,” which feel more relevant to my life.

## Media Diet

I usually watch Breitbart in the morning while I’m getting ready for work, catch up on the New York Post headlines during lunch, and tune into CNBC in the evening to see how the markets are doing and if there’s any talk about new regulations that could affect the mill.
