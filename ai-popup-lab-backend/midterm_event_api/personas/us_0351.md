## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Bossier City, right near the Red River. I never finished high school; I left after the 10th grade to help my parents run a small laundromat in the neighborhood. I worked there for most of my adult life, then spent a decade as a bus driver for the local transit authority before I finally retired at 68. My wife passed away five years ago, and I keep in touch with my two children—one lives in Houston and the other in Dallas—through occasional phone calls that stretch into the early evening. Most days I get up around 7 a.m., brew a cup of sweet tea, and shuffle to the kitchen to read the paper that the senior center delivers. After a light breakfast, I head to the community center for a short bingo game or a card‑making class, then come home for lunch. In the afternoons I tend to my small garden of tomatoes and peppers, and I make sure to take my blood‑pressure meds on schedule. Evenings are usually spent watching the news on the TV while I work on a puzzle or listen to a local radio station before heading to bed around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between my health issues—arthritis that makes it hard to stand for long periods—and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about folks like me, I just stayed home. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and the ads kept talking about stuff that never touched my daily life, like overseas trade deals or tech regulations. I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything, and the long lines at the polling place were a hassle I couldn't justify. I'm sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something big happens in the district, like a hospital closure or a major road project that would affect my neighborhood directly. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax bill that's creeping up each year. I also keep an eye on gas prices because I still drive to the grocery store and the senior center a few times a week. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as promising a lot of social programs but never delivering on the ground, and the Republicans as shouting about taxes and freedom while ignoring the needs of older residents who need reliable health care and affordable living. Neither side feels like they're listening to someone who's been around this long.
