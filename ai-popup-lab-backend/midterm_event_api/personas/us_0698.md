## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the west side of Lorain, Ohio, inside the 8th congressional district. I went to a community college for two years and got an associate's in automotive technology, so I've been working as a diesel mechanic at the local trucking company for the past 35 years. My day starts early; I’m usually at the shop by 5 a.m., because the night shift runs till dawn. After a quick coffee and a donut from the corner store, I spend the morning fixing engines, swapping parts, and sometimes training the younger guys who just finished high school.

I'm married to my wife of 38 years; we both grew up in the area and have three adult kids who moved out after college. My oldest daughter lives in Cleveland and visits a few times a year, while my son is stationed overseas with the Army. My parents are both in their late 80s; I help my wife drive them to their appointments at the clinic on Tuesdays and Fridays. On weekends I like to take the truck out to the lake, fish a little, and catch up with a few neighbors at the community center. Grocery shopping is a chore I share with my wife, and we keep an eye on the utility bills because the heating costs climb every winter.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at the shop, caring for my aging parents, and the feeling that the big‑ticket races never really change anything for folks like me, I just didn’t bother to get to the polls. The candidates all sounded the same, and the campaign ads felt more like background noise than anything that mattered to my daily life. I’ve been pretty skeptical of the whole system for a while now, and unless something hits close to home—like a factory closing down in town or a sudden hike in property taxes—I’m likely to sit this one out again in 2026.

I’m sitting this one out. My biggest concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the ever‑increasing property tax bill on our house. The price of fresh produce keeps creeping up, and every time the county reassesses, my wife and I have to scramble to figure out how to make the mortgage and taxes fit into the same budget. I’m also watching the local job market; a few of the younger mechanics have left for better pay elsewhere, and I worry about who will keep the trucks running when I’m gone.

When it comes to the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats seem to promise more social programs but never deliver on the streets where I see the need, and the Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes but end up favoring the big corporations that already dominate the region. I don’t have a strong allegiance to either side; I just want someone who will keep the roads maintained, the utilities affordable, and the local factories open.

## Media Diet
I catch the morning headlines on CBS and NPR while I’m driving to work, then scroll through ABC and NBC news articles during my lunch break. All told I’m around seven hours a week glued to those sites.
