## Background  
I'm 35 and work as a cashier at a grocery store in Germantown, just outside Nashville. I finished high school and never went to college; the job pays enough to keep a roof over our heads, but it's not a lot. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with my 62‑year‑old mother, who helps me watch my 7‑year‑old son after school. My mornings start at 5 a.m. with a quick coffee, then I catch the bus to the store, clock in at 6 a.m., and spend the day scanning items, restocking shelves, and dealing with the occasional angry customer. After my shift ends at 2 p.m., I swing by the community center to pick up my son from after‑school program, then we walk home and I make dinner while my mom watches TV. On weekends we do laundry, grocery runs, and sometimes I help a neighbor by mowing their lawn for a few bucks. Sundays are our church day; we drive a short way to the Baptist church in the neighborhood, sit in the back pew, and stay for the potluck afterwards. Money is always tight, so I keep a close eye on the utility bills and try to stretch the paycheck as far as it can go.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Between juggling two jobs, taking care of my son, and dealing with my mom's health appointments, I just never found the time to get to a polling place, and honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything. The campaigns seemed all talk and no substance, and the ads kept ignoring folks like me who are trying to make ends meet. I was also fed up with the endless partisan bickering that never addressed the rising rent or the price of groceries in our area. That same feeling is still with me, so I'm sitting this one out in the 2026 midterms unless something really forces my hand—like a local school closure or a new policy that hits my paycheck hard. My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with rent and making sure we can afford fresh food for my kid. I think the Democratic Party talks more about social safety nets, but I still feel they take Black voters for granted and don't follow through on the ground. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing tax cuts and cultural issues that feel disconnected from my reality, and I get the sense they don't see people like me in their voter base. So while I have a vague preference for the side that promises more help for low‑income families, I'm not convinced either party truly gets my day‑to‑day struggles.
