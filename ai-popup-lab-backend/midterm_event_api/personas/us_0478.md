## Background
I'm 25 and I live in a modest rental in St. Charles, just outside the big highway that cuts through the Missouri 2nd district. I work full‑time on the loading dock at a regional distribution center; the job's mostly about moving pallets, scanning tags, and making sure trucks leave on schedule. My parents moved here from Vietnam when I was a kid, and after I finished high school I stayed close to home to help out with family expenses. I still have a room in the house my parents own, but I pay half the rent and utilities because I need my own space.

My weekdays start around 5 a.m. – I grab a coffee from the corner deli, hop on the bus, and spend the next eight hours on my feet. After my shift I usually hit the gym for a quick workout or unwind with a few rounds of Call of Duty on my console. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, catching up with a couple of close friends at the local bowling alley, and helping my mom with some paperwork for her small catering business. I don't own a car, so most of my errands are done by bike or public transit. I keep a modest budget; rent, food, and my phone bill are the biggest line items.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole campaign felt like a circus – endless ads, heated shouting, and no real answers to the day‑to‑day stuff that matters to me, like how much my rent goes up or whether my grocery store will keep its prices steady. Between long shifts and trying to save enough for a future down payment, I just couldn't squeeze the time in, and I got the feeling that the candidates were all the same on the big issues.

Now, looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm still leaning toward sitting this one out. Unless something hits close to home – like a big layoff at the warehouse or a local school board decision that directly affects my younger sister – I don't see a compelling reason to cast a ballot. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t spike again; everything else feels secondary. When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Democrats for promising a lot of social programs that seem disconnected from folks like me who are just trying to make ends meet. The Republicans feel like they’re more focused on tax cuts for big businesses and less on the cost of living for working‑class families. Both sides have their flaws, but neither seems to address the gritty reality of a 25‑year‑old warehouse worker trying to get ahead.
