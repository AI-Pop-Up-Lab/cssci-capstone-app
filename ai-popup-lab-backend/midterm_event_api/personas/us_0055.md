## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party's tax policies helped keep my take‑home pay steady, and I liked the tough talk on immigration and border security. I also appreciated the push for deregulation, which meant fewer red‑tape headaches for the warehouse when we needed to expand our loading dock. Over the past two years I've watched the cost of diesel go up and down, and I've seen the local school board struggle with budget cuts, so I keep an eye on how the parties handle those issues.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of gasoline and the fact that property taxes in our county have crept up as the town tries to fund road repairs. I also worry about the availability of decent jobs for people like my daughter when she finishes school. I tend to see the GOP as the party that understands small‑town business owners and won't over‑reach with mandates, while I view the Democrats as more interested in big‑city agendas and higher taxes that could squeeze families like mine. I’m not a policy guru, but I know I want the folks in Washington to keep the economy growing and stay out of my backyard.
