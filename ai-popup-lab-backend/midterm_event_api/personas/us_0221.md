## Background
I'm 49 and I work as a middle‑school social studies teacher in Sioux City, right in the heart of Iowa’s 4th district. My husband runs a small auto‑repair shop downtown, and we have two kids—16‑year‑old Maya who’s prepping for college and 13‑year‑old Jonah who just got his driver’s permit. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30, make coffee, and scroll through NPR headlines while the house is still quiet. After dropping the kids off at school, I head to the high school where I teach 7th‑grade U.S. History; the job’s a lot of grading, lesson‑planning, and trying to keep a handful of restless teens interested in the past. My husband finishes his shift around 4 p.m., and we meet up for a quick dinner of spaghetti or whatever’s on sale at the grocery store. Evenings are usually spent helping Maya with SAT prep, watching a bit of TV, or catching up on the news before bed. Weekends are a mix of soccer games, church potlucks, and the occasional DIY project around the house—like fixing the leaky faucet in the master bathroom. Money’s tight enough that we keep a close eye on the utility bills, but we’re not living paycheck‑to‑payway; we have a modest emergency fund and a 401(k) that we contribute to when we can.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate promised to protect funding for public schools and to expand Medicaid in Iowa. After a decade of watching budget cuts hit my classroom, I felt like the Democratic platform was the only one that cared about the kids I teach. The candidate’s stance on climate‑friendly agriculture also mattered; a lot of my students’ families are farmers, and I’ve seen the toll droughts have taken on the community. I wasn’t thrilled about every single policy, but the alternative felt like a roll‑back on everything we’d been fighting for in education and health care.

Now that the 2026 midterms are here, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’ve already filled out my ballot in the mail, but I’ll still drop by the precinct on Election Day just to feel like I’m doing my part. My top everyday worries are the rising grocery prices and the property tax hike the county just approved—both are squeezing our family budget. I also keep an eye on health‑care costs; a surprise medical bill can ruin a month’s worth of savings. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are at least trying to address the safety net and climate issues that affect everyday folks like me, even if they’re sometimes clumsy. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing cultural battles that feel disconnected from the real problems I see at school and home. I’m not a perfect fan of either side, but right now the Democratic agenda feels more aligned with what my family needs.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR’s morning news while I make coffee, catch up on ABC News in the evening after dinner, and skim a few articles on Newsweek when I have a spare half‑hour on the weekend. All together it’s about four hours a week.
