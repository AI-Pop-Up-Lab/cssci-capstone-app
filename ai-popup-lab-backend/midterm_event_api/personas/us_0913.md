## Background  
I’m 45 and I work as a policy analyst for a regional health nonprofit in Burlington. Most days I’m at the office by eight, sifting through grant applications and drafting reports on how to keep rural clinics afloat. After work I usually swing by the farmer’s market, grab a bag of carrots for the kids, and head home to the little house on the outskirts of the city where my wife and our two teenagers are. My wife teaches elementary school, so the mornings are a blur of school‑run prep and me trying to get the kids to eat something other than cereal. On weekends we hike the Green Mountains, take the dog to the lake, or I’ll help my neighbor fix a broken fence. I’m still paying off the mortgage on the house, so every extra dollar matters. When I’m not at the office or on the trail, I’m on my laptop reading research papers or catching up on the news while I’m cooking dinner. I don’t have a lot of spare cash, but I try to put a little into a retirement account each month. My parents live in a small town up north, and I visit them a few times a year, bringing back fresh maple syrup and a few bottles of local cider.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one taking climate change seriously enough for a state like Vermont, and the candidate’s health‑care plan promised to protect the rural clinics I work with. I also liked the emphasis on expanding broadband in underserved towns – that’s something my kids need for school. At the time I was a little skeptical about the whole process, but the candidate’s town‑hall meetings in Burlington made me feel like my voice mattered, even if it was just a handful of questions after the speech.  

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m not a political junkie, but the last couple of years have reminded me that local races can change how much money my nonprofit gets and whether the state will keep funding for mental‑health services. My top everyday concerns are the rising property taxes on my house and the cost of prescription drugs for my wife’s asthma. I also keep an eye on the state’s plan to improve internet access in the more isolated parts of the district because without that, my kids can’t do their homework and the local businesses suffer.  

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward: Democrats are messy and sometimes too focused on identity politics, but at least they’re trying to address climate, health care, and economic inequality. Republicans, on the other hand, seem to care mostly about tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like a step backward for a place that relies on tourism and clean water. I’m not a fan of either side’s rhetoric, but I’ll keep voting for the one that at least tries to tackle the issues that hit my family and community.
