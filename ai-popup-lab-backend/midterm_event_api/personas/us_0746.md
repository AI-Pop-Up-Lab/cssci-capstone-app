## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a small senior community on the outskirts of West Chester, the heart of my congressional district. I was born in a farming village in Taiwan and came to the U.S. in my twenties to work as a housekeeper for a family in Philadelphia. I never finished high school, but I learned a lot on the job and by watching the news on the little TV in the kitchen. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two children live in the suburbs of Pittsburgh and Baltimore, so most of my days are spent here with a few close friends from the community center.

My routine is simple. I get up around 7 a.m., make a bowl of instant oatmeal, and turn on the local channel for the morning news—usually a quick glance at the headlines before I head out for a short walk around the garden. I attend a weekly Mandarin class at the senior center on Wednesdays, which helps me keep my language sharp and stay connected to other Asian seniors. Thursdays are my grocery day; I take the bus to the nearby market and bargain for fresh produce and the occasional frozen dumplings. In the evenings I sit in the common room, watch a couple of programs on CBS or ABC, and sometimes listen to NPR on my old radio while knitting. Sundays I go to the Methodist church down the road; the pastor’s sermons about caring for the vulnerable resonate with me.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security and Medicare, which are the biggest things on my mind at my age. The Democratic candidate promised to protect prescription‑drug pricing and to fund community health clinics, and that sounded like a lifeline for me. I also liked the fact that the candidate visited my senior center and talked about senior safety and transportation, which made me think they actually listened.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to the polls again. I may be frail, but I can still get to the polling place with a little help from my neighbor, and I don't want to see any cuts to the programs that keep me healthy. My top everyday concerns are the cost of my prescription medicines and the rising property taxes on my modest condo. If the tax bill goes up too much, I might have to dip into my savings, which is scary. I also worry about the quality of the home‑care services that my daughter relies on when she visits. As far as the parties go, I think the Democrats are more reliable when it comes to seniors' issues, while the Republicans seem more focused on cuts and business tax breaks that don't help people my age. I get annoyed when I hear talk about "balancing the budget" if it means slashing health benefits, so I tend to lean toward the side that promises to keep those programs alive.

## Media Diet
I watch CBS and ABC news in the mornings and evenings, and I tune into NPR for longer stories when I have a quiet afternoon; altogether it adds up to about seven hours a week.
