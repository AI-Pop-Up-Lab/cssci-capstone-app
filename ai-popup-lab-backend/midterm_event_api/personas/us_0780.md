## Background
I'm 35, and I live in East Providence, right near the waterfront where the old factories have been turned into lofts and tiny shops. I work as a warehouse supervisor for a regional shipping company; most days I'm on my feet, moving pallets, making sure the trucks are loaded on schedule, and dealing with the occasional angry driver. I didn't finish my associate's degree in logistics because the job paid enough to support my family, so I have a few college credits but no diploma.

My wife works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have two kids—Mia, 7, and Eli, 4. Mornings are a blur of school drop‑offs, breakfast cereal, and me checking the news on my phone while I sip coffee. After my shift ends around 5 p.m., I pick the kids up from after‑school programs, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner before we all crash on the couch. Weekends are mostly errands: grocery runs, a trip to the farmers market, and the occasional visit to my parents in Warwick. I drive a beat‑up sedan that’s seen better days, and I’m always looking for a decent deal on car insurance or a cheaper phone plan. I watch a lot of sports on the TV—mostly baseball and a bit of hockey—because it’s the easiest way to unwind after a long week.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about people like me who are just trying to keep a roof over their heads and put food on the table. My shift ended right when the polls opened, and I couldn't get time off, so I just stayed home and watched the news flicker past on the TV. The endless ads and the same old talking points made me think my vote wouldn't change anything, so I stayed home. That feeling of being stuck between a rock and a hard place—high rent, rising grocery prices, and a job that barely keeps up with inflation—made the whole process feel pointless.

Going into 2026, I still feel the same way, so I'm sitting this one out. My top everyday concerns are the cost of rent and the price of groceries; everything else is just noise. I see the Democrats as promising big ideas but never delivering for working‑class families, and the Republicans as shouting about taxes and culture wars while ignoring the fact that my paycheck is already stretched thin. Both parties seem more interested in scoring points than solving the problems that keep me up at night, like whether I can afford a new set of tires next month or if the school will have enough teachers for my kids. I’m not looking for a perfect candidate—I just want someone who’ll listen to the folks who have to work two jobs to make ends meet.
