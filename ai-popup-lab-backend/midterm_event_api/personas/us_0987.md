## Background  
I'm 55, and I live in Casper, Wyoming, right near the North Platte River. I work full‑time as a dental hygienist at the local clinic; the two‑year program at the community college got me the license I needed. My shift starts at 7 a.m., so I’m usually out the door before sunrise, coffee in hand, and I’m back home around 4 p.m. I’m a single mother of two grown kids—one lives in Denver, the other just moved back to Cheyenne with a job in construction. On weekends I take the kids’ dog to the park, help my neighbor with yard work, and sometimes volunteer at the church food pantry. My rent is modest, but I’m still paying off a car loan and a small mortgage on a duplex I bought a few years ago to rent out one unit. I watch the sunrise over the river when I can, and I’m a big fan of the local high school football team, even though I’ve never been much of a sports fanatic.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long drives to the polling place, the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about people like me, and the constant juggling of work, bills, and caring for my kids, I just left the ballot empty. I’ve always thought the system was tilted toward the big oil interests that dominate Wyoming, and I didn’t see any real reason to waste a day at the polls. The whole thing feels like a performance—candidates say they’ll fix things, but after the election the same issues keep coming back. I’m not planning to vote in the 2026 midterms either, unless something huge happens locally—like the clinic I work at being threatened with closure or a major scandal that directly affects my community. My day‑to‑day worries are paying the rent and keeping the mortgage current, and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing so high that I have to skip meals. I’m also worried about health care costs; my mother’s arthritis meds keep getting more expensive and the insurance options here are limited.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Republicans. They’ve been in power for ages in Wyoming, and I feel like they ignore the concerns of Black residents and low‑income families. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to show up on the national stage with nice sound bites, but I don’t see much concrete help coming down to my town. I guess I’m stuck in a place where I’m annoyed by both sides, but the GOP gets the bigger part of my frustration because they control the state legislature that decides on things like Medicaid expansion and property tax caps that directly hit my wallet.

## Media Diet  
I usually listen to NPR in the mornings while I’m getting ready for work, and I catch up on ABC News and CBS News in the evenings after dinner. In total I spend about seven hours a week scrolling through their websites or watching clips on my phone.
