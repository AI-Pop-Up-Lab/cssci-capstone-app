## Background

Most mornings I grab a coffee, check the news on my phone while the kids are still sleeping, then drive the 20‑minute commute to the office where I spend my day crunching data, writing reports, and meeting with local officials.

My wife, Maria, runs a small catering business from our kitchen, and we have two grown‑up children who live on the mainland but come home for holidays. I try to surf at the beach whenever the swell’s decent, but more often I’m at the community center teaching basic computer skills to seniors. ## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on tax relief and small‑business support resonated with my own financial worries. My wife’s catering business was hit hard by rising labor costs, and I was frustrated with what I saw as a Democratic focus on climate regulations that didn’t consider the real‑world impact on local contractors like me. The Republican candidate promised to cut unnecessary red tape and protect property rights, which felt like the most practical option for my situation.

Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the new administration’s policies are playing out. The tax cuts helped a bit, but the federal budget talks have been a mess, and I’m still uneasy about the lack of a clear plan for affordable housing on the islands. I’m also annoyed by the constant partisan bickering on TV; it makes me wonder if any side is actually listening to everyday Hawaiians.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the soaring cost of groceries and the shortage of affordable housing that’s pushing many families out of the island. I’m also watching the local job market—my wife’s business is still struggling to hire reliable staff. As for the parties, I think Democrats are out of touch with the economic realities of middle‑class families, while Republicans sometimes swing too far to the right on social issues that don’t affect my daily life. I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I just want whoever is in office to keep the cost of living from spiraling further and to make it easier for small businesses to stay afloat.
