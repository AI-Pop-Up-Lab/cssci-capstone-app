## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a modest bungalow on the outskirts of Santa Cruz, just a few blocks from the beach. I spent most of my career as a professor of electrical engineering at the University of California, Santa Cruz, and before that I worked a decade in R&D at a Silicon Valley hardware startup. I retired ten years ago, but I still teach occasional night classes and mentor a few graduate students. My wife of 55 years has arthritis, so I help her with her meds and the grocery trips. Our two kids live in the Bay Area and Seattle; they call every Sunday, and the grandkids visit in the summer. My days usually start with a strong cup of coffee, a quick scan of the news on my tablet, then a walk along the boardwalk with the dog. Afternoons are for gardening, checking my email, and sometimes volunteering at the local senior center where I run a basic computer class. Evenings are quiet—TV, a bowl of soup, and a crossword puzzle before bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate promised to keep taxes low and cut red tape for small businesses, which felt like the right move for someone who spent a lifetime watching the tech sector grow. I also liked the emphasis on personal responsibility and the promise to protect Social Security from cuts. Over the past two years I've watched the Democrats push for higher taxes and more regulation, and that still feels like a threat to the savings I built up for retirement. I’m planning to vote again in the 2026 midterms; I’ll probably stick with the Republican ticket, though I’m not thrilled about any of the choices. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that my wife's medical bills keep creeping up, plus the property tax hike the county approved last year. I think the GOP is better at keeping the government lean, even if some of their rhetoric can be over the top. The Democrats, in my view, are more interested in identity politics and big government programs that will end up raising my taxes and limiting what I can do with my own money. I’m not a political scholar, I just want my retirement to stay secure and my wife to get the care she needs without my checking the bank account every night.
