## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Council Bluffs, right near the Missouri River. I spent most of my working life loading and unloading trucks at the meat‑packing plant downtown; I never finished high school, but the job paid the bills and gave me a steady routine. My wife passed away five years ago, so I’m mostly on my own now, though my adult daughter visits on Sundays and brings over a casserole. I get up early, make a cup of black coffee, and sit at the kitchen table while the morning news runs on the TV. After that I take a short walk to the post office, stop by the grocery store for a few essentials, and then head back to tend to my small garden—some tomatoes, a few beans, and a couple of herb pots. In the afternoons I usually watch a bit of TV, do crossword puzzles, or sit on the porch and watch the river traffic. Once a week I go to the local senior center for a bingo night and to catch up with a few old friends. I still drive my old pickup to the church on Saturday mornings, where I sit in the back and listen to the sermon while the choir sings.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate promised to protect Medicare and to keep the prescription‑drug prices from climbing any higher. At my age, reliable health care feels like a lifeline, and I was tired of hearing talk about cutting benefits. The Republican opponent seemed more focused on cutting taxes for businesses, which didn’t speak to my daily worries about paying for insulin and my own medical appointments. I also liked the way the Democrat talked about funding rural broadband, something I need to keep in touch with my daughter and to watch the news online.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are keeping my Medicare coverage intact and making sure property taxes don’t keep rising on my modest home. I also worry about the price of groceries; the last few trips to the store have left my wallet lighter than it should be. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least pretend to care about seniors and health care, even if they’re a bit messy on the budget. The Republicans, in my view, keep shouting about cutting taxes and deregulating, but that never seems to trickle down to the people who are already on fixed incomes. I’m not a political analyst, I just know which side has been more attentive to my needs lately.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I drink coffee, catch the ABC News evening wrap‑up after dinner, and skim CNBC’s market updates a couple of times a week when I’m on the porch. All together it adds up to about ten hours a week.
