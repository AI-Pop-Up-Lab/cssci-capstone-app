## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in Darlington, a small town that sits right in the middle of South Carolina's 7th district. I worked most of my adult life as a dental hygienist at the local clinic; the associate degree I got from a community college in the '80s was enough to get me the certification I needed. I retired five years ago, but I still go in a few mornings to help the clinic with patient intake when they're short‑staffed.  

My husband passed away three years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids who live in Charlotte and Atlanta. They call me every Sunday, and I drop by my granddaughter's house once a month to watch her do homework and bake cookies. I belong to the First Baptist Church downtown, and most Saturdays I’m either at the church potluck or volunteering at the food pantry that serves a lot of seniors.  

A typical day starts with a strong cup of coffee on the porch while I read the headlines on my tablet. After that I walk my dog, Buster, down to the grocery store, pick up a few canned goods, and then head to the clinic or the pantry. Evenings are usually spent watching the news, catching up on a mystery novel, or chatting with my sister on the phone. I drive a used sedan that’s been reliable for decades, and I’m careful with my money because my fixed retirement income doesn’t stretch as far as it used to.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus. The candidates all sounded the same, and the ads kept promising things that never seemed to happen in my town—like big factories or fancy highways that would never touch Darlington. Between caring for my sister, juggling the volunteer schedule, and trying to keep my health in check, voting just felt like another thing I’d have to fit into an already cramped calendar. Plus, I’ve been pretty skeptical about the whole system for years; it seems like the folks in Washington keep arguing while the price of my prescription meds keeps creeping up.  

I’m sitting this one out again in 2026. Nothing dramatic has happened locally that would pull me back into the booth, and I’m still convinced that my single vote won’t change anything. My biggest worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that make it harder to stay in my house. I also worry about whether there’ll be enough decent jobs for the younger folks who want to stay in the area instead of moving to the bigger cities.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats. They seem more interested in identity politics than fixing the real problems that hit people like me—like health care and taxes. The Republicans, at least on paper, talk more about keeping taxes low and supporting small businesses, which sounds better for my pocketbook, but I’m not blind to the scandals and the occasional extreme rhetoric that makes me uneasy. Overall, I’m just tired of the noise and would rather see folks actually work on the issues that affect everyday life.

## Media Diet
