## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Pine Bluff, the part of Arkansas that falls in the 3rd district. I never finished high school; I left school at fifteen to start working in the local timber yard, hauling logs and loading trucks. I stuck with that kind of manual labor for most of my life, later moving to a nearby warehouse where I helped unload shipments of farm equipment. I retired about ten years ago when my back finally gave out, but I still like to keep busy. My wife passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in Little Rock and Dallas, so most days I'm on my own. I get up early, make a cup of black coffee, and sit on the porch watching the sunrise while the neighborhood kids ride their bikes past. Afterward I head to the senior center for bingo on Tuesdays and a weekly church service on Sundays. In the afternoons I tend to my small garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few herbs—just enough to have fresh veggies for the week. When I'm not at the garden, I'm either watching the news on TV or taking a short walk down the street to the local D‑L‑C store to pick up groceries and a newspaper.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. By the time the primaries rolled around I was still recovering from a knee surgery and felt too weak to make the trip to the polling place, and honestly, I was tired of hearing the same promises over and over. The candidates all seemed to be talking about big‑picture stuff that never touched my daily grind—nothing about fixing the cracked sidewalks in my neighborhood or keeping the senior center funded. I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything, so I just stayed home. That feeling hasn't really shifted. I'm sitting this one out, too. Unless something dramatic happens—like a sudden cut to the senior center budget or a local scandal that hits close to home—I don't see a reason to stand in line again. My biggest worries right now are the cost of my prescription drugs and the rising property taxes that make it harder to keep my house. I also worry about the reliability of the local power grid; a few blackouts this winter left my house freezing and my heating system struggling. When I think about the parties, I feel like the Democrats are focused on big cities and don't understand rural Arkansas, while the Republicans keep shouting about culture wars and tax cuts that never seem to trickle down to folks like me. Both sides feel distant, and I end up just trying to get by day by day.
