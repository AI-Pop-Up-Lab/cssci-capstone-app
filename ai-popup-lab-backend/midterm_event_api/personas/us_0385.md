## Background
I'm 25 and work as a research analyst for a biotech startup in Columbia, Maryland. I finished my master’s in molecular biology a year ago and spent a few months in a post‑doc fellowship before the job came up. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who’s in grad school for computer science, so rent is the biggest line item on our budget. My days start early: I grab a coffee from the corner café, hop on the MARC train to the office in Laurel, and spend most of the morning crunching data on new drug candidates. Lunch is usually a quick sandwich at my desk while I scroll through a few headlines on my phone. After work I either hit the gym at the local community center or meet up with friends for a casual dinner at a nearby food truck park. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the big box store, a weekly yoga class, and occasional trips to the Mall in Columbia to browse for clothes I can’t really afford. I’m not married, no kids, and my parents live about an hour away in Frederick, so I’m pretty independent but still call home for holidays.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on climate research funding and student loan relief mattered to my career and my roommate’s tuition situation. The Democratic candidate also promised to protect Medicaid expansion in Maryland, which is important to a lot of folks I know who rely on it. I was a bit skeptical about the Republican platform, especially on issues like reproductive rights and science funding, so I wasn’t excited to cast a ballot for them. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I think my voice still matters, especially with the Senate race shaping up and the House seats that could swing. My top everyday concerns right now are the rising cost of rent and the price of groceries—both have been creeping up faster than my paycheck. I also worry about childcare costs because my roommate might need a daycare soon. As for the parties, I see Democrats as the more reasonable option on most issues that affect me, even if they sometimes feel a bit out of touch with working‑class folks. Republicans, in my view, seem more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that actually protect public health, which feels like a step backward for someone in biotech.
