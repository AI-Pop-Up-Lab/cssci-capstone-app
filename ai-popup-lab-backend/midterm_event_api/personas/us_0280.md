## Background
I'm 35 and work as a data analyst for a regional health insurer in Champaign, Illinois, right in the middle of the 14th district. My day starts around 6 a.m. with a quick coffee and a scan of the headlines on my phone before I hop on the commuter bus to the office downtown. The job is mostly spreadsheet‑heavy, pulling together claims data to spot trends that help set premium rates. I have a master's in public health, which is how I landed this gig after a stint as a research assistant at the university.

I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment with my partner and our nine‑month‑old daughter. We share chores, but most evenings I'm on the couch watching a sitcom or scrolling through social media while the baby naps. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the local market, a quick trip to the county fair with the family, and occasional visits to the tribal community center where I volunteer to teach kids about our heritage. I drive a used hybrid to save on gas, and I try to keep the car clean because it’s my main way to get to my parents’ house a few hours north where we gather for pow‑wows every summer.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health care, something that hits close to home given my job. The campaign ads about expanding Medicaid and protecting pre‑existing conditions resonated with me, and I appreciated the candidate’s promise to invest more in rural broadband, which would benefit the tribal lands I care about. Since then I’ve stayed pretty engaged—reading policy briefs, chatting with coworkers about the upcoming midterms, and keeping an eye on how the current Congress handles the budget.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the looming property tax hike the county board is debating. I’m also watching the job market; even though my position feels stable, I hear friends in manufacturing getting laid off. I like the Democrats because they’re more likely to protect social programs and push for infrastructure that reaches my community, but I’m frustrated by what I see as endless talk and not enough action. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental protections that affect tribal lands. I’m not a fan of the partisan bickering; I just want policies that keep my family fed and safe.
