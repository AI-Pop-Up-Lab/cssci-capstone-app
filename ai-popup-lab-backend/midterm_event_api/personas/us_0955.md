## Background
I'm 40, and I work as an HVAC technician for a small family‑run service company out of Baraboo, Wisconsin, in the 7th congressional district. I graduated from a community college with an associate’s degree in mechanical systems, which is how I got into the trade. My wife and I bought a modest three‑bedroom house about five years ago; we have two kids, ages 8 and 11, who are in the local public schools. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 am, grab a quick coffee, and drive the 15‑minute stretch to the depot where we load trucks and get our routes for the day. The work is hands‑on and often hot, but I like knowing I keep people comfortable in the dead of winter.

After a typical eight‑hour shift, I come home around 5 pm, help the kids with homework, and try to squeeze in a little dinner prep before we all eat together. My wife works part‑time at a nearby grocery store, so we juggle schedules to make sure someone is always home. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV or scrolling on my phone while the kids finish up chores. Weekends I’m either doing house projects, like fixing the leaky faucet in the bathroom, or helping my brother with his landscaping business. We’re a fairly tight‑knit family, and most of my free time is spent around home or at the junior high’s soccer games.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to the issues that matter to my family—things like health care costs, worker safety regulations, and immigration policies that affect my extended family back in Mexico. I also appreciated the candidate’s promises to invest in job training programs for tradespeople like me, which seemed like a direct benefit to my community. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still uneasy about some of the higher‑up rhetoric, I think the overall direction is better for folks who rely on steady wages and affordable health coverage.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising grocery prices that are eating into our budget and the property taxes that keep going up every year, making it harder to stay in the house we worked so hard to buy. I’m also watching the local job market; if the manufacturing plants in the area start cutting back, that could hit a lot of families like mine. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who generally push for stronger labor protections and more inclusive immigration reform, even if they sometimes get tangled in internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about tax cuts and smaller government, but I feel they don’t really understand the day‑to‑day grind of a trade worker who needs steady work, health benefits, and decent wages. I’m not a fan of the extreme rhetoric on either side, but I’m leaning toward the party that seems more willing to address the cost‑of‑living squeeze we’re feeling.
