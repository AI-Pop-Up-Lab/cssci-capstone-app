## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Warwick, Rhode Island, just a short drive from the Narragansett Bay bridge. I work as a shift supervisor at a regional warehouse that ships furniture and appliances to stores across New England. I never finished my associate degree in business; I took a few classes and then went straight to the floor when the job opened up. My wife and I have been married 35 years, and our two kids are grown and live out of state, but we still get a call every Sunday. My mother, who moved here from Puerto Rico after retiring from nursing, lives with us now, so I’m the one who handles her medication and doctor appointments. My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a cup of coffee and the radio, then a 30‑minute drive to the loading dock. I spend the morning checking inventory, the afternoon coordinating drivers, and the evening—if I’m not doing overtime—checking the mail, cooking dinner, and watching the news before bed. On weekends I help my grandson with his baseball practice and try to keep up with the garden in the small backyard behind our house.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to the rising cost of healthcare and the need for better retirement security—things that hit me hard as I think about my own future and my mom’s health. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in broadband for rural parts of the state, which would help small businesses like the one my brother runs in nearby Pawtucket. Since then, I’ve been watching how the administration handles the budget and the new infrastructure projects. I’m still convinced the Democrats are the better bet on those issues, but I’m also annoyed by the constant partisan bickering on TV, especially when it seems to stall any real progress. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out because my wife’s Social Security check is getting squeezed by the rising cost of prescription drugs, and I’m worried about property tax hikes on our old house. My top concerns right now are keeping my mom’s medical expenses manageable and making sure my paycheck stretches far enough to cover grocery prices that keep climbing. I view the Republicans as mostly focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations that affect the coastal areas we live near. The Democrats, for all their flaws, at least talk about expanding Medicare and protecting the shoreline, which matters to me.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m driving, catch the CBS Evening News after work, and scroll through ABC’s website at night when I’m winding down. I log about eight hours total each week.
