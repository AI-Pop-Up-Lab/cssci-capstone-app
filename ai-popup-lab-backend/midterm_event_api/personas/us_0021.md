## Background
I'm 40, and I live in the Madison area of Alabama's 5th District, just outside Huntsville. I work full‑time as a dental hygienist at a busy clinic downtown; the associate degree I earned at a community college got me the credential I needed. My husband works as a forklift operator at a nearby warehouse, and we have two kids—Jaden, 12, and Maya, 8—who both go to the public middle school a few miles away. Most mornings I’m up before the kids, making coffee and packing lunches while the house still smells of the overnight rain. I drive the kids to school, then head to the clinic where I spend my day cleaning, taking x‑rays, and chatting with patients about flossing. After my shift I pick Maya up from soccer practice, then we all eat dinner together, usually something quick like rotisserie chicken and frozen veggies because we’re short on time and money. Weekends are a mix of church on Sunday morning, grocery runs at the local market, and trying to catch up on house chores. When I can, I sit on the porch with a cup of tea and listen to the radio while the kids do homework. I’m not a big traveler; most of my life revolves around work, the kids, and the community center where I volunteer once a month to help with after‑school tutoring.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at the clinic, two kids’ schedules, and the feeling that none of the candidates really understood the daily grind of folks like me, I just didn’t make it to the polls. The whole process felt like a circus—ads promising change, then the same old promises after the election. I’m sitting this one out too, unless something blows up locally, like a school closure or a sudden hike in property taxes that hits my family directly. My biggest worries right now are the rising grocery prices and the uncertainty about stable jobs in the area; the warehouse where my husband works has been cutting shifts, and the clinic’s hours have been trimmed a few times. I’m also watching the property tax bill the county is debating—if it goes up, it could squeeze our budget hard. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about systemic change but rarely address the immediate cost of living issues in my town. The Republicans, on the other hand, push for lower taxes but seem to ignore the need for better public services that my kids rely on, like school funding. I’m not a political theorist; I just want a government that stops making promises and starts fixing the things that affect my family’s day‑to‑day life.
