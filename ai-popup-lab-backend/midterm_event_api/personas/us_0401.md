## Background
I'm 20, and I live in a modest apartment on the outskirts of Bangor, right in the heart of Maine's 2nd district. I finished high school three years ago and now split my time between a part‑time job at the local grocery store and night classes at the community college where I'm working toward an associate degree in business. My mom works two shifts at the hospital, and my little brother is still in middle school, so I help with meals and sometimes watch him after school. Most mornings I’m up before 6 a.m., grabbing a coffee from the corner diner before my 7‑to‑3 shift stocking shelves and checking out customers. After work I head to campus for a couple of hours of classes, then come home, do laundry, and scroll through TikTok or listen to a podcast while I finish homework. On weekends I hang out with a few friends from school, sometimes going to the local skate park or helping out at the community center where we run a youth mentorship program. Money’s tight—rent, utilities, and school fees eat most of my paycheck, so I’m always watching my budget.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate talked about expanding health care coverage and investing in renewable energy, which felt like the only way to protect my mom’s job at the hospital and the future of my coastal state. I also liked the emphasis on student loan relief; even though I’m only in community college, the idea of not being buried in debt resonated. Since then I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening locally—rising grocery prices and the lack of affordable housing have hit me hard, and I’m still hoping the party will push harder on those issues. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out when the Senate race could affect health‑care policies that matter to my family. My top everyday concerns right now are the cost of rent and the price of groceries; a sudden jump in either would force me to cut back on school supplies or skip meals. I see the Democrats as the side that at least pretends to care about working‑class folks like me, even if they sometimes get tangled up in internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re talking about a world that doesn’t include people like me—tax cuts for big corporations, social issues that seem disconnected from my daily grind, and a general vibe that they’d rather keep the status quo than address rising living costs.
