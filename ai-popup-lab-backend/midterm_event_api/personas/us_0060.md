## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s tax policies helped small‑business owners like the shop I work for, and I liked the emphasis on “law and order” after the wave of protests I saw on the news. I also liked the candidate’s promise to keep regulation light for tradespeople; a lot of my coworkers complained about new licensing rules that would have made our job harder. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s trade policies affect the price of parts we need, and I’m still convinced that a pro‑business stance is the best way to keep my family’s paycheck stable. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’m not planning to sit this one out. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax hikes the county has been talking about—both hit the family budget hard. I also worry about the local school funding, but I’m not sure the Democrats have a clear plan for that. I tend to see the Republicans as the party that respects hard work and personal responsibility, while I view the Democrats as more focused on big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with folks who run a wrench for a living. That’s why I’m sticking with the GOP this cycle.
