## Background
I'm 19 and I live in a small town on the reservation near Norton, right in the middle of Kansas's 1st congressional district. I didn't finish high school; after dropping out at 16 I started working full‑time on a nearby cattle ranch. I pull feed, fix fences, and sometimes drive the pickup to haul hay for the farm owner. My mom lives with me; she's getting her Social Security and does a bit of quilting to bring in extra cash. My little sister is still in middle school, and we all share a cramped two‑bedroom house that doubles as a place for family gatherings and powwow prep when the season comes around.

My days start before sunrise. I’m up by 5 am, eat a quick bowl of oatmeal, and head out to the barn. After a twelve‑hour shift I come home, take a quick shower, and help my mom with dinner—usually beans and cornbread. In the evenings I either watch whatever's on the TV or scroll on my phone while I eat. Weekends are the only time I get to hang out with friends; we mostly play video games at the local arcade or drive out to the nearest Walmart to pick up cheap snacks. I don't have a driver’s license yet, so I rely on a cousin to give me rides when I need to go farther.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything. The campaigns never talked about the issues that matter to us on the reservation—like water rights, tribal health clinics, or the lack of good jobs for young people. Most of the ads were about national stuff I couldn't relate to, and the polling places were a half‑hour drive away, which is a pain when you work 12‑hour days and can't get time off. I also got the vibe that both parties just use us as a talking point and then move on.

I'm sitting this one out again for the 2026 midterms unless something dramatic happens locally, like a major cut to our tribal health funding or a big scandal that hits the district directly. My top everyday concerns are keeping a steady paycheck on the ranch and dealing with the rising price of gasoline—if gas goes up, I can't get to work or to the town for supplies. I also worry about the lack of affordable health care; the nearest clinic is far, and the insurance options are confusing.

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Democrats keep promising more social programs, but when I read the fine print it feels like they’re talking about urban voters, not folks like me. The Republicans shout about lower taxes and border security, but none of that helps with the water contamination on our land or the fact that most of our jobs are in agriculture, not the tech sector they brag about. Both sides seem to think they can win my vote with a generic slogan, but I just see a lot of noise and not much substance.
