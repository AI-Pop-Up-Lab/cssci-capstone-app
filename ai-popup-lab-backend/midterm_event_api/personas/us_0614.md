## Background
I'm 20, and I live in Paterson, New Jersey, right in the heart of the 5th congressional district. My parents run a small grocery store downtown, and I help out after school most evenings. I'm enrolled part‑time at a community college, taking a mix of general education courses and a few business classes because I want to eventually help the family business expand. Most days I wake up at 6:30 a.m., grab a coffee, and hop on the bus to the college campus in Newark. After a handful of lectures, I head back to the store around 2 p.m., stocking shelves and ringing up customers until it closes at 9 p.m. My little brother is 15, and we both share a cramped two‑bedroom apartment with our parents. On weekends I sometimes work a shift at the local fast‑food joint to earn extra cash for rent and my own phone bill. When I get a break, I like to play basketball at the community center and catch up with friends on Instagram, scrolling through memes and short videos. I don’t drive yet, so the bus is my main way around, and I’ve learned to navigate the whole county on my own.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attuned to the struggles my family faces—rising rent, the cost of groceries, and the uncertainty around student loans. My dad talked a lot about how the incumbent’s platform promised more support for small businesses and a push for higher minimum wages, which sounded like something that could ease the pressure on our store. I also liked the candidate’s stance on immigration reform; as a Hispanic household, we’ve seen friends and relatives hit roadblocks that make daily life stressful. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out. I’m a bit disappointed that the rent‑control bill stalled in the state legislature, and the grocery price spikes have not eased. Still, I think the Democratic side is trying to address those issues, even if progress is slow.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—I'm going to the polling place in my neighborhood on Election Day. My top everyday worries are keeping a roof over our heads and putting food on the table; rent keeps creeping up and the price of fresh produce feels like it’s on a treadmill. As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Republicans; their rhetoric feels out of touch with folks like me who are just trying to get by, and I don’t trust their promises about the economy or immigration. The Democrats aren’t perfect, but at least they seem more willing to talk about student debt relief and social safety nets. I’m not a political junkie, so I mostly vote based on who I think will make my life a little less stressful.
