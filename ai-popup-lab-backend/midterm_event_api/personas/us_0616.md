## Background
I'm 79, and I live in Marlton, a suburb of the Pine Barrens in Burlington County. I never finished high school; I dropped out in my teens to help my parents with a small landscaping business. I worked as a day‑laborer and later ran a modest lawn‑care side gig that kept me busy until I retired a few years ago. My wife passed away five years back, and my two kids live in Pennsylvania and Florida now, so most days I'm on my own. I still go to the local senior center twice a week for bingo and a cheap lunch, and I spend the mornings walking the neighborhood, checking on my garden, and making sure the fence is still upright. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I swing by the grocery store in the strip mall, grabbing a bag of oranges and a loaf of bread before heading home to watch the news on my old TV. I get my meals mostly from the pantry and a little help from the senior nutrition program, and I try to keep the house warm with a space heater because the heating bill always hurts my limited pension. I drive a beat‑up Chevy that I've kept running for years; the engine coughs, but it gets me to church on Sundays and the senior center on weekdays.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping my taxes low and protecting my pension from cuts. The Democratic candidates kept talking about big government programs that sounded like they'd raise my property taxes even more, and I didn't trust a lot of the promises about climate regulations—those just seemed like more red tape for a small‑time guy like me. I also liked the tough‑on‑crime rhetoric; I want my neighborhood safe, and the Republican message sounded straightforward.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries are the rising cost of gasoline and the fact that my prescription meds keep getting pricier. I also keep an eye on property taxes—if they go up again, I might have to cut back on heating and that would be a nightmare this winter. I don't have much patience for the Democrats; they seem to think they can solve everything with big programs that end up costing me more. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about keeping government small and letting folks keep what they earn. I'm not thrilled with any politician, but I lean toward the party that promises to keep my pocketbook from getting any thinner.
