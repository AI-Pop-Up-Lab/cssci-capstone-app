## Background  
I'm 55 and I work as a dental hygienist at a busy practice in Mesa, right in the heart of AZ‑6. I earned an associate’s degree in dental hygiene at a community college in Phoenix, and after a few years on the job I settled into a schedule that lets me start my day at 7 a.m. I drive a ten‑year‑old Honda that I keep in the garage next to the house I bought with my husband back in the late‑90s.  

We have two kids; the oldest moved to Denver for a tech job and the younger is finishing a nursing program in Tucson. They both come home for holidays, but most weeks the house is just the two of us and my mother, who lives with us after her husband passed away. I spend my evenings cooking, watching a bit of TV, and sometimes helping my mom with her Medicare paperwork. On Saturdays I do a bit of gardening—tomatoes, peppers, a few herbs—because fresh produce helps keep the grocery bill down. Sundays are for church and a potluck with neighbors; I’m on the volunteer committee that runs the coffee table and the bake‑sale.  

After work I usually stop at the grocery store, grab a coffee, and head home. I try to get out for a walk around the neighborhood a few times a week, mostly to clear my head before I settle in for a night of streaming shows or scrolling through the news on my phone.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old story: both parties promising change while the headlines kept showing the same rising costs for gas, electricity, and my health insurance premiums. I was swamped with extra shifts at the clinic when the primaries hit, and the whole process just seemed like a circus I didn’t want to be part of. The feeling of being talked at rather than listened to made me think my vote wouldn’t matter anyway.  

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I’m still planning to sit this one out. Unless something dramatic happens—like a major water‑rights fight that hits our community directly, or a scandal that forces a clear choice—I don’t see a reason to spend the time filling out a ballot. My top everyday worries are the ever‑increasing utility bills and the cost of healthcare. Every month I have to decide whether to pay the electric bill or stretch the grocery budget a little thinner. Property taxes are creeping up too, and with the house getting older, I’m already thinking about repairs.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to want to raise taxes and push more regulations that feel like they’ll hurt small businesses like the one my husband runs—a landscaping company. The Republicans keep shouting about the border and “law and order,” but they forget that water is our real border issue here in Arizona; the drought is getting worse and the state’s water policies feel like a joke. I wish someone would actually listen to folks who just want the lights on and a prescription that doesn’t break the bank.
