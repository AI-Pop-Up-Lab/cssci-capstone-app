## Background
I'm 30 and work as a registered nurse at the regional medical center in Anchorage. My shift starts at 7 a.m., so I usually grab a coffee from the corner store on the way, then drive the ten‑minute commute through the snow‑slick streets. After my 12‑hour stretch I come home to a small two‑bedroom apartment in Eagle River, where I share the space with my roommate, a fellow nurse who’s also from out of state. We split the rent, which is a constant worry because housing prices in Anchorage keep climbing. I’m the one who does most of the cooking, often whipping up quick pasta or canned chili while the dishwasher hums. Weekends I try to get out on the trail, either snowshoeing or fishing on the nearby lake, because the cold air clears my head after long weeks in the ER. My parents live in a suburb of Seattle; we video‑chat on Sunday evenings, and I send them a care package of Alaskan smoked salmon once a year. I don’t have kids, but I’m thinking about adoption down the line. When I’m not at work or on the trail, I’m scrolling my phone, checking the weather, and occasionally helping a neighbor carry groceries up the stairs.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between back‑to‑back night shifts, the long drive, and the constant scramble to pay my rent, the ballot felt like another thing I didn’t have time for, and honestly I’m pretty fed up with the whole circus. The candidates all seemed to promise the same thing—more funding for the hospital, better infrastructure—while ignoring the day‑to‑day mess of high heating bills and the scarcity of affordable housing in Anchorage. I’ve been told my vote doesn’t matter anyway, and after watching the same partisan bickering on the news, I just tuned out. I’m sitting this one out too, unless something truly blows up locally, like a major hospital funding cut or a sudden spike in fuel prices that forces me to choose a side.

My biggest worries right now are the cost of heating my apartment during the long winter and the price of groceries, especially fresh produce that has to travel thousands of miles. I also keep an eye on the state’s approach to oil development because a lot of my colleagues’ families depend on that income, but I’m skeptical of both parties. The Democrats, in my view, talk a lot about climate but forget that we still need reliable energy to keep our homes warm. The Republicans push for more drilling and fewer regulations, but that just feels like a gamble with our environment and health. Both sides seem to think they can solve Alaska’s problems with a single policy, and I’m not buying it.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR’s morning news on my commute and skim ABC News headlines in the evening while I’m winding down after my shift, adding up to about two hours a week.
