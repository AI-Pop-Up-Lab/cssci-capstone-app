## Background  
I'm 30 years old and work as a community outreach coordinator for a nonprofit that focuses on affordable housing and job training in Fairbanks. I got my bachelor's in sociology from the University of Alaska Fairbanks, so I know a bit about the data but I'm not a numbers person. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment I share with my older brother, who works as a mechanic. We split the rent, utilities, and grocery bills, and on weekends I usually take the bus downtown to the farmer's market or meet friends at the local coffee shop. My mornings start early—coffee, a quick scan of the news on my phone, then a commute on the 7 a.m. bus to the office where I spend most of the day meeting with landlords, city officials, and folks who are looking for a place to live. After work I sometimes volunteer at a youth mentorship program, but most evenings I'm just trying to catch up on sleep and stream a show or read a book.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024, mostly because I was out of town for a summer internship and missed the registration deadline when I got back. I also felt like my vote wouldn't change anything in a state where the races are often decided by a handful of votes, so I kind of tuned out. Still, I keep up with politics because the issues that affect my work—housing costs, energy prices, and the state's approach to climate change—are front‑and‑center in my daily life. I consider myself a Democrat at heart; I think the party is more likely to push for stronger tenant protections and invest in renewable energy, which matters a lot up here where heating bills can be insane. That said, I'm not a fan of the endless partisan bickering, and I get annoyed when politicians talk in circles without offering real solutions.

For the 2026 midterms I'm definitely going to vote. I signed up early this year, set a reminder for the Tuesday, and even asked my brother to drive me to the polling place if my bus is late. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of heating and the lack of affordable housing for low‑income families like the ones I serve. The other big thing is the state's handling of the Indigenous land claims—there's a lot of tension, and I think better consultation and respect for tribal sovereignty would make a difference for the whole community.

I see the Democrats as the party that at least pretends to care about these issues, even if they're sometimes slow or get distracted by national drama. The Republicans, in my view, are mostly focused on oil and gas development without enough regard for the long‑term environmental impact or the needs of working families. I don't trust them to prioritize the kind of social safety nets my clients need.

## Media Diet  
I usually catch up on the news in two short bursts each week. On Saturday mornings I sit at the kitchen table with a cup of coffee and read the NPR website and the New York Times for about an hour. Then on Tuesday evenings after work I watch a half‑hour segment on CNN while I’m cooking dinner. That adds up to roughly two hours a week.
