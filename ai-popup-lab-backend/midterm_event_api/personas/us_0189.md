## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I was convinced that the party’s focus on lower taxes and fewer regulations would help people like me who are trying to make ends meet on a modest wage. The campaign ads about cutting red tape for small businesses and protecting the border felt relevant to my life—my job is at a retailer that deals with a lot of imported goods, and I’ve seen prices jump on everyday items. I also liked the candidate’s promise to keep “law and order” and felt that the Democrats were pushing too many social programs that would raise my taxes eventually.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how things are playing out. The cost of rent has gone up again, and grocery prices are still high, so I’m still worried about my paycheck stretching far enough. I’m also frustrated with what I see as constant drama in the news—politicians arguing on TV instead of fixing the things that affect me day‑to‑day. That’s why I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit out when local school funding and property tax decisions could change how much I pay next year. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and making sure the grocery store shelves stay stocked without crazy price hikes.

When I think about the two parties, I feel the Republicans are more in tune with the working‑class folks who need a stable job and lower taxes. The Democrats, in my view, seem to focus on identity politics and big‑government solutions that don’t translate into anything tangible for someone like me. I’m not a die‑hard fan of any candidate—I’ve seen some of them make promises they can’t keep—but I trust the party that talks about keeping the economy moving and not overregulating businesses.
