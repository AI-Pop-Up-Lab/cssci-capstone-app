## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest senior apartment building on the east side of Stockton, right in the heart of California's 29th district. My wife passed away a few years back, and my two grown kids moved to the Bay Area for work, so most of my days are spent here. I never finished high school; after leaving school I spent a decade working in a warehouse loading trucks, then picked up a part‑time gig as a bus driver for the local transit agency until I finally retired at 68. These days I get my Social Security and a small pension, and I rely on the senior center for meals and a bit of activity. I usually get up around 7 a.m., take my blood pressure meds, and head down to the community room for the morning bingo. After lunch I might go to the nearby grocery store, which is a bit of a trek because the bus schedule is spotty, or I sit on the porch and watch the kids play in the park across the street. My sister lives a few blocks away and drops by on Sundays with a casserole. I try to keep a routine because it helps me manage my arthritis and the occasional memory lapses.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Medicare, Social Security, and the kind of community health clinics that keep me from having to travel far for a check‑up. I also remember the civil‑rights rallies my parents talked about, and the Democratic candidate seemed more respectful of that history. The campaign ads on the news talked about protecting seniors’ benefits, and that mattered to me. I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms – I’m voting. My biggest worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that my property tax bill keeps creeping up even though my income is fixed. I also worry about safety in the neighborhood; there’s been a few break‑ins lately and the police response feels slow. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who keep the safety net alive, while the Republicans seem more interested in cutting taxes for the rich and rolling back regulations that protect renters like me. I’m not a political scholar, I just know which side has helped me stay on my feet.

## Media Diet
