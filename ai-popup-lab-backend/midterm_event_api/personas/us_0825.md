## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Clinton, a small town just north of Knoxville in Tennessee's 2nd district. I work full‑time as a shift supervisor at the local warehouse that ships goods for a regional retailer. I didn't finish my associate's degree in business because I needed to start working to help my parents pay the mortgage when I was 22, but I still take a community‑college class in Spanish once a week to stay sharp. My husband, Luis, is retired from the county sheriff's office, and we have two grown kids—one lives in Nashville, the other is still in college down in Atlanta. We also take care of my mother, who has arthritis and needs help getting around, so most evenings are spent cooking, doing laundry, and making sure her meds are organized. My days start early: I grab a coffee from the corner deli, drive the kids to school, then head to the warehouse for a 7‑a.m. shift. After work I usually stop by the grocery store, then come home to a quiet house where I watch the news while Luis reads the paper. On weekends I help out at the church food pantry and sometimes go fishing with a few neighbors.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole campaign just seemed like a circus of ads and fights that didn't touch my everyday worries—rent, gas prices, and making sure my mom can get to her appointments. My husband and I talked about it once, and we both decided to stay home. The ballot felt like a long list of promises that never materialized, and I was too exhausted after my shift to bother with the morning rallies or the endless TV spots. I also didn't get any mailers from the parties; the few I did get were all the same generic stuff.

Going into the 2026 midterms, I'm still sitting this one out. Unless something major hits our community—like a factory closing or a new road that would cut off our access—I don't see a reason to drag myself to the polls. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and making sure my mother's health care stays covered. The Democrats feel like they're always promising more social programs but never delivering on the ground, while the Republicans keep talking about "lower taxes" but that just means cutting services we actually need, like the senior center program. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the potholes on Main Street or keeping the local school funded.
