On my days off I run errands at the local grocery store, check the mailbox, and try to squeeze in a walk along the Penobscot River. ## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything and I was exhausted from working two jobs to keep the lights on. The whole process seemed like a circus, and the candidates all sounded the same—just promises that never reach my kitchen. I also missed the early voting window while I was covering a shift, and by the time I could get off work the polls were already closed. That experience left me pretty cynical, so I'm planning to sit this one out too unless something really hits home, like a local factory closing or a sudden rise in property taxes that pushes me over the edge. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent and making sure there's enough money for groceries; I can't afford any surprise bills. I also worry about the lack of decent jobs in our area—most of the good-paying positions have moved out of town. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as talking a lot about climate and social programs but not caring about the real struggles of people like me in rural Maine. Republicans seem to promise lower taxes and less regulation, but I haven't seen those promises translate into better wages or more stable jobs for folks in my community. So far, I feel stuck between two sides that both seem out of touch with my day‑to‑day reality.
