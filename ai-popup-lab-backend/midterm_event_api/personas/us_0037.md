## Background
I'm 75 and live in a modest house on the east side of Montgomery, just a few blocks from the historic downtown. I spent most of my working life as an HVAC technician after getting my associate degree from a community college in Birmingham. I retired five years ago, but I still do occasional maintenance work for a couple of local churches and the senior center; it keeps my hands busy and brings a little extra cash for the grocery bill. My wife passed away three years back, so it's mostly me and my two grown‑up kids who live in Atlanta and Mobile. I see them on holidays and they call almost every Sunday. I have a handful of grandkids who visit in the summer; I try to take them fishing on the river or to the local park when they’re in town. My days start early with a cup of sweet tea and the paper, then a short walk around the neighborhood to check on the mailbox and say hi to neighbors. I attend the Baptist church on Sundays, help out with the coffee hour, and sometimes drive the choir van. Evenings are usually spent watching the news or reading a few articles on my tablet before I head to bed around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Democratic because the candidate promised to protect Medicare, push for stronger voting‑rights legislation, and invest in broadband for rural parts of the district—things that matter to a senior like me who still relies on the internet for telehealth appointments. I also felt the party was more attentive to criminal‑justice reforms that affect my community. The campaign rallies in Montgomery gave me a sense that someone actually cared about the issues we face every day, so I turned out at the polling place despite the heat.

I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the crumbling roads that make it hard to get to the clinic or the church. I’m also uneasy about the state’s property‑tax hikes that are squeezing retirees on fixed incomes. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more willing to keep Social Security and Medicare strong, while the Republicans keep talking about tax cuts that feel like they’re aimed at the wealthy and don’t address my day‑to‑day needs. I’m not a political scholar, but I get frustrated when I hear talk of “small government” that just means fewer services for people like me.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR’s morning news while I’m having tea, catch the ABC News evening broadcast after dinner, and flip through Newsweek articles during my lunch break. All together it’s about six hours a week.
