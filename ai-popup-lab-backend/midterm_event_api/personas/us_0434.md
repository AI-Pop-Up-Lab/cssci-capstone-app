## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one talking about the rising cost of gasoline and the need to keep taxes low for working families like mine. The candidate promised to protect manufacturing jobs and to push back against what I see as over‑regulation that makes it harder for small businesses to stay afloat. I was also drawn to the tough stance on immigration; I think the border needs to be secured so that wages don’t get driven down by cheap labor. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have played out. The economy feels a bit steadier now, but I’m still worried about the price of diesel for my truck and the property tax hikes the county approved last year. I’m definitely planning to vote in the 2026 midterms—there’s a Senate race that could affect how much money goes to infrastructure projects in my area, and a House candidate who’s promising to fight the new state income‑tax proposal that’s circulating.

My top everyday concerns are keeping the utility bills manageable and making sure my kids can afford college without drowning in debt. I’m also fed up with the way the news keeps blowing up every little scandal; it feels like none of it really changes anything for people on the ground. I tend to see the Republican Party as the one that at least pretends to understand what folks like me need—steady jobs, lower taxes, and a little respect for traditional values. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more focused on identity politics and big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with the realities of a middle‑class family in a rust‑belt town. I’m not a political scholar, just a HVAC tech who wants a decent paycheck and a safe neighborhood.
