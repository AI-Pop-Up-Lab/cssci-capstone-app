## Background  
I'm 40, and I work as a senior data analyst for a health‑tech startup in St. Louis Park, just outside Minneapolis. I spent four years getting a master's in statistics, then another year on a research fellowship before landing the job. My wife and I bought a modest two‑bedroom condo downtown a few years ago; we have a three‑year‑old daughter who goes to a public preschool nearby. Most mornings I’m up by 6 a.m., make coffee, and scroll through the news on my phone while the coffee brews. I drive the kids to preschool, then hop on the commuter rail to the office, where I spend the day cleaning up data sets and building predictive models for patient outcomes. After work I usually pick up my daughter, swing by the grocery store, and try to squeeze in a quick workout at the gym before we all eat dinner together. Weekends are a mix of errands, helping my sister with her immigration paperwork, and occasional hikes around the lakes when the weather’s nice. I try to keep up with my parents back in California through video calls every other Sunday.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt the whole process was a circus and my vote wouldn’t change anything. Between the endless campaign ads, the confusing ballot, and the feeling that both parties were more interested in scoring points than solving real problems, I just stayed home. Work was crazy that November—our product launch got delayed, and I was pulling all‑nighters to meet the deadline, so voting fell to the bottom of my list. I’m still pretty fed up with the system, so I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms unless something dramatic hits close to home, like a major layoff at my company or a local school funding crisis that directly affects my daughter. My biggest worries right now are the rising rent in the metro area and the price of groceries, which keep climbing faster than my paycheck. I’m also anxious about the cost of health insurance for my family. When I think about the parties, I’m more annoyed with the Republicans—they seem obsessed with tax cuts for the rich while ignoring everyday costs. The Democrats feel like they’re stuck in endless infighting and can’t get anything done, but at least they talk about expanding health care and protecting immigrant families, which matters to me. Overall I’m cynical, but I still keep an eye on what’s happening because it eventually trickles down to my pocket.
