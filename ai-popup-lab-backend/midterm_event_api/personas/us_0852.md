## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Bastrop, a small town just east of Austin in Texas' 15th Congressional District. I spent most of my adult life working as a maintenance supervisor at a local manufacturing plant; I retired five years ago when the plant downsized. My wife passed away three years back, so now I mostly keep company with my two grown children—one lives a few counties away and the other is retired in San Antonio—and the occasional visits from my grandchildren when school lets out. My days start early; I get up around 5 a.m., make coffee, and take a short walk around the neighborhood to check on the garden and the fence my son helped me repair last summer. After that I usually watch the morning news while I eat toast and read the paper. I still do some odd‑job work for neighbors—painting a porch, fixing a leaky faucet—just to stay busy and earn a little extra cash for groceries. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I go to the local Catholic church for mass, and on Saturdays I meet a few friends at the community center for a card game. Most evenings I wind down watching a mix of sports reruns and the local radio station that plays classic hits.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑business environment that let me work a decent wage for decades. I also liked the talk about border security; growing up near the border, I saw a lot of illegal crossings and thought stricter enforcement would help my community. Since then, I've been watching the headlines and hearing folks at the church talk about how the economy is still shaky—prices at the grocery store keep creeping up, and my pension checks don’t stretch as far as they used to. That makes me nervous, but I still trust the GOP more than the other side. I think the Democrats keep promising more social programs, but I worry they'll raise taxes to pay for them, which would hit retirees like me hardest. I'm definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I don't feel like sitting this one out. My top concerns right now are my fixed income keeping up with rising rent on my apartment and the cost of gas for my truck, which I still need for the odd jobs. I see the Republican Party as the one that understands the need for personal responsibility and limited government, while I view the Democrats as too eager to expand the state’s role in everyday life.
