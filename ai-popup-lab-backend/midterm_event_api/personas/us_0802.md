## Background
I'm 30 and work the night shift as a cashier at the local grocery store in a small community on the Pine Ridge Reservation, which falls in the western part of the state. My mom still lives in the same house; we share the kitchen and the porch swing where I sometimes smoke after work. My younger brother just finished high school and is looking for a construction job, so I help him with his résumé and drive him to the job fair on Tuesdays. I don’t have a high school diploma, so most of my training has been on the job—learning the register, handling inventory, and dealing with the occasional angry customer. I get up around 5 a.m., grab a cup of instant coffee, and catch the sunrise over the Black Hills before heading to the store. My evenings are spent watching a bit of TV, scrolling through my phone, and sometimes helping my mom with the garden. On weekends I help with the tribal powwow prep, which is a big social event for the whole community, and I also pick up extra shifts when the store needs extra hands.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at work, caring for my mom, and the feeling that none of the candidates ever really talked about the water contamination on the reservation or the lack of decent jobs, I just didn’t see a point. The whole process felt like a far‑away thing that never touched my daily life, so I stayed home. The campaign ads were all about national issues that didn’t reflect what we deal with here, and I was tired of the same old promises. I’m sitting this one out too, unless something dramatic happens locally—like a major change to the tribal health clinic funding or a scandal that directly affects our community. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that the nearest clinic is still a two‑hour drive for many families. I also worry about the lack of stable jobs on the reservation; most of the work is seasonal and low‑pay. When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Democrats for talking a good game about tribal sovereignty but never delivering concrete help, and the Republicans seem to care more about tax cuts for big corporations than about clean water or health services for people like me. So far, I’m not feeling optimistic about either side.
