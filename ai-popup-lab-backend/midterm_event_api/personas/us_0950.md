## Background  
I'm 30 and work as a registered nurse at the community hospital in Portage, Wisconsin, which sits in the 4th congressional district. I earned an associate’s degree in nursing from a local technical college, and the job pays enough to rent a two‑bedroom apartment a few blocks from the campus. My roommate, also a nurse, and I split the rent and utilities, and we keep a small terrier named Scout in the living room.  

My days start early; I’m usually up by 5 a.m., make a quick coffee, and catch the 6 a.m. news on my phone while scrolling through the local weather. I bike to the hospital, work 12‑hour shifts three times a week, and on my off days I do grocery runs at the nearby grocery store, drop Scout at the dog park, and binge‑watch a few episodes of a crime drama on the couch. Weekends are a mix of catching up on sleep, meeting my sister for brunch at a diner in Madison, and volunteering at the free clinic once a month. I’m not married, don’t have kids, and I’m saving up for a down‑payment on a house someday.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about protecting health care access and safeguarding reproductive rights—issues that hit close to home in my line of work. The campaign also talked about investing in community hospitals, which made the promise feel personal. Since then I’ve watched the cost of my health insurance premiums creep upward, and I’ve heard a lot of talk about cutting funding for public health programs. Those concerns have kept me leaning Democratic, but I’m also skeptical about the party’s handling of the national debt and some of the more extreme progressive proposals that seem out of touch with my everyday worries.

I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms (“I’m voting”). My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of rent and the steady increase in my health‑care premiums that make budgeting a nightmare. I’m also watching the local job market; the hospital’s staffing cuts have made us all work harder for less. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are better at protecting social services, but they sometimes feel like they’re fighting internal battles that distract from real results. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about tax cuts and “small government,” but I worry they’ll roll back the health‑care protections that keep people like me and my patients safe. Both sides have their flaws, but right now I feel the Democrats align more with my immediate needs.
