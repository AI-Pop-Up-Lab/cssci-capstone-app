## Background
I'm 70 and I live in Rochester, right in the heart of New Hampshire's 2nd District. I spent most of my working life as an HVAC technician after getting an associate's degree from a community college. I retired five years ago, but I still swing by the local hardware store now and then to help a younger guy figure out a tricky installation. My wife passed away three years back, so it's just me and my old golden retriever, Max. My two kids live out in Boston and Portland; they visit around the holidays and call every Sunday. My days start early with a cup of black coffee and the local paper, then a short walk with Max around the neighborhood. After that I usually check my email, do a bit of gardening, and spend an hour at the senior center helping run the bingo night. In the evenings I cook a simple dinner, watch a bit of TV, and read a paperback before heading to bed around 10.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I was worried about my Social Security check and the rising cost of my prescription meds. The candidate promised to protect Medicare and push for lower drug prices, which hit close to home. I also liked the emphasis on expanding broadband in rural areas—my nephew needed a reliable connection for his remote work. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the policies are playing out, and I’m still convinced the Democrats are the better bet for seniors like me. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I feel my voice matters when it comes to funding for the senior services I rely on.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping my Social Security check intact and making sure my prescription costs don’t keep climbing. The other thing that keeps me up at night is the property tax bill on my house—every year it seems to creep up a bit more, and I’m on a fixed income. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who actually listen to older folks and care about health care. The Republicans, in my view, are mostly talking about cutting taxes for the wealthy and don’t seem to understand that my biggest expense is my medication, not a fancy car. I get annoyed when the GOP tries to downplay climate change, because the extra humidity this summer is already making my asthma act up.

## Media Diet
I start my mornings with NPR’s news hour while I’m drinking coffee, then catch ABC News at night after dinner. I sprinkle in a couple of stories from CBS News and Newsweek when I have a spare moment, adding up to about seven hours a week.
