## Background  
I'm 44 and I live in the outskirts of Wheeling, right on the Ohio River. I work the afternoon shift at the local manufacturing plant that makes steel components for the automotive industry. The job's physically demanding, but it pays the bills and lets me take my kids home before dark. My husband is a part‑time truck driver, and we have two kids—one in high school and a younger teenager who just started at the community college nearby. Most weekends we’re either at my sister’s house in Weirton or at the church potluck down the road. I drive the 30‑minute commute to the plant, grab a coffee at the diner on the way, and spend my evenings doing laundry, helping with homework, or scrolling through my phone while the kids watch TV. On Sundays I’m usually at the Baptist service, then we have a family dinner that stretches into late afternoon. The cost of heating the house in winter and the price of gasoline are the biggest line items on our budget.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between long shifts, kids’ schedules, and a feeling that the candidates were all the same—talking about “change” while the mines keep closing—I just didn’t see a point. The whole process felt like a chore, and the polling place was a half‑hour drive away, which made it easier to skip. That cynicism hasn’t really gone away. I’m sitting this one out again for the 2026 midterms unless something dramatic happens locally, like a major plant shutdown or a flood that forces the state to act. My top everyday worries are the rising price of groceries and the uncertainty around local jobs—especially as the coal industry keeps shrinking and the plant I work at is flirting with layoffs. I also worry about property taxes; the county’s trying to raise them to fund road repairs, and every extra dollar feels like a squeeze.  

When I think about the two main parties, I’m pretty skeptical of the Democrats. I feel they push for policies that don’t understand life in Appalachia—like green energy mandates that ignore our dependence on coal and manufacturing. The Republicans, at least, talk about protecting jobs and keeping taxes low, which lines up more with what I need. Still, I’m not a die‑hard fan; I’ve seen some GOP folks make promises they don’t keep, and the partisan fights just add to the noise. Overall, I’m fed up with both sides acting like they’re in a circus, and I’m only interested in whoever actually delivers on keeping my family’s paycheck steady and the cost of living manageable.
