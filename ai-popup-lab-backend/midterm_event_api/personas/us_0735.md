## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I just never felt like any of the choices mattered to me. The whole process feels like a show put on for the media, and I was working two jobs and trying to keep up with rent, so getting to a polling place seemed like a waste of time. The candidates all sounded the same, and the ads were just noise. I also get the sense that most politicians don't understand what it's like to be stuck in a low‑pay job without a diploma, so I stay away. I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something hits close to home—like a big factory closing in the district or a sudden spike in my rent—I don't see myself lining up at a booth.

My top concerns right now are making sure I can afford my rent next month and keeping grocery prices from climbing higher. I try not to think too much about the big picture, but when I do, I see the Democratic Party as a bunch of city‑slickers pushing policies that raise taxes and make regulations that hurt small businesses. The Republicans, while louder and sometimes a bit extreme, at least talk about jobs and cutting red tape, which feels a little more relevant to my daily grind. Still, I'm not enthusiastic about either side; I'm just hoping someone will actually listen to folks like me.
