## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Casper, Wyoming, in the 1st congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working as a diesel mechanic for the local oil‑field service company, a job that only required a two‑year technical degree and a lot of hands‑on know‑how. I retired last year, but I still do occasional repairs for the crew and help my neighbor with his tractor when he needs a hand. My wife, who used to teach at the community college, now spends her days gardening and volunteering at the senior center. We have two adult children—one lives in Denver, the other in Jackson—so the house is mostly quiet except for the occasional phone call or video chat.

My days are pretty routine. I wake up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and read the news on my tablet while the kettle boils. After a quick breakfast, I either head out to the garage to finish a few repair jobs or go for a walk along the North Platte River, where I like to watch the elk and think about the weather. Lunch is usually a sandwich and a can of Mountain Dew, and I try to catch up on a couple of shows on the TV before dinner. Evenings are the best part: my wife cooks, we eat together, and we often sit on the porch watching the sunset over the foothills. On Sundays we go to the local church, then drive out to the flea market for a few hours before heading home.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best represented my views on energy, land use, and limited government. Working in the oil industry, I’ve seen how federal regulations can slow down projects and cost jobs, so I gravitated toward candidates who promised to loosen those rules. The Republican ticket also talked about protecting the Second Amendment and keeping taxes low, which lined up with what I’ve always believed. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies are affecting my community—higher fuel prices at the pump and some new restrictions on drilling have been frustrating, but I still think the GOP is the better option for Wyoming.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the price of gasoline and diesel, which directly hit my wallet and the work I still do, and the way the federal government is handling public lands—there’s a lot of debate about grazing rights and mining permits that feels like it’s being decided far away from us. I’m also watching property taxes; the county has been looking at raising them to fund schools, and I’m not sure I can afford another increase.

When I think about the parties, the Republicans feel like the ones who understand rural life and the importance of the energy sector. I get annoyed with the Democrats; they seem to focus on urban issues and push climate policies that, in my view, ignore the realities of a state that relies on natural resources. I don’t think either side is perfect, but I’m more comfortable with the GOP’s approach to economic freedom and personal responsibility.
