## Background
I'm 24 and work full‑time as a sales associate at a big‑box home improvement store in Grand Rapids. The job means I’m on my feet most of the day, stocking shelves, helping customers pick out paint, and juggling the register during busy weekends. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the east side of the city with a roommate who’s studying nursing. We split rent, utilities, and the occasional pizza night. My parents live about an hour north in Grand Haven and I visit them on holidays; they still help me out when my car needs a cheap repair. On weekdays I get up around 6 am, grab a coffee from the corner diner, and catch a quick scroll on my phone while the coffee brews. After work I usually hit the gym for an hour or run errands, then unwind by watching a sitcom or scrolling TikTok before bed. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, catching up with friends at the local brewery, and sometimes volunteering at the community garden where I help plant tomatoes. I don’t have a college degree, so most of my knowledge about politics comes from what I hear at work, online videos, and the news sites I check.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to affordable health care and climate initiatives that affect my city. I also liked the promise of expanding broadband in rural parts of the district, which would help my parents back home. Since then I’ve stayed pretty consistent—my friends and coworkers mostly lean left, and the local Democratic office does a decent job of texting reminders, so I’ve kept the habit. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to hand the Senate over to a party that keeps trying to cut funding for community programs I rely on. My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with rent as prices creep higher and the cost of groceries, especially fresh produce. I also worry about the local manufacturing jobs that used to be steady but are now shifting to automation. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Republicans—they seem more interested in tax cuts for the wealthy than fixing the potholes on my street. The Democrats aren’t perfect; I think they sometimes overpromise and underdeliver, but they’re at least trying to address the issues that touch my daily life.
