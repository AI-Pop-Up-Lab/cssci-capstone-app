## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of St. Joseph, right in Missouri's 4th district. I grew up in a tight‑knit neighborhood where everybody knew each other's name, and after I left school at 16 I took a series of jobs that kept me busy – first as a dishwasher in a local diner, then as a housekeeper for a few families, and finally as a home‑health aide for seniors. I’ve been doing that for the last 25 years, and the work is hard but it lets me stay active and meet folks who need a hand.

My husband passed away five years ago, so now it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live a few towns over. I see my daughter once a month, and my grandson visits in the summer; they bring fresh fruit and a lot of stories about college. Most mornings I start with a cup of strong coffee, check the local bus schedule, and head out to the senior center where I volunteer a few hours helping with paperwork for other seniors. After my shift I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few essentials, and then settle in front of the TV or my tablet to catch the news before bed. On weekends I tend to my small garden, grow tomatoes and beans, and sometimes meet friends at the church bake sale. Money is tight; my Social Security check covers most of it, but I always watch the utility bills.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Medicare, prescription‑drug costs, and protecting Social Security – the three things that keep my life steady. I also liked the way the candidate talked about criminal‑justice reform and investing in community health clinics, which matter in my neighborhood. The campaign knocked on my door, handed me a flyer at the senior center, and reminded me that every vote counts for folks like me.  

Going into the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. My top everyday concerns are keeping my Social Security check intact and making sure the price of my prescription insulin doesn't jump again. I also worry about the rising property tax on my house; the county keeps asking for more money to fund road repairs, and I’m already stretching every dollar. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re at least trying to listen to seniors and people of color, even if they mess up sometimes. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to push policies that would cut benefits or raise taxes on the working poor, and that makes me nervous. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the government to stay steady on the things that keep me alive and healthy.

## Media Diet
