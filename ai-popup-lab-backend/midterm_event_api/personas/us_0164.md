## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a quiet cul‑de‑sac just outside Gainesville, in the part of the county that falls in the 3rd district. I spent most of my adult life as a middle‑school English teacher, and I retired about ten years ago after the school district cut the budget. My husband passed away five years back, and my two grown kids live in Orlando and Tampa; they visit a few times a year with their kids, which keeps the house feeling busy. I own a small house with a front porch that I tend to every morning, and I have a rescued Labrador named Marley who gets his walk at sunrise. My days usually start with a cup of coffee and the local paper, then a quick check of my phone for headlines, followed by a walk with Marley, a bit of gardening, and an hour at the senior community where I lead a book club. Afternoons are for grocery shopping, sometimes a trip to the library, and evenings are spent watching the news or knitting while listening to classic radio shows. I try to keep active, but the heat in the summer makes me stay inside more often, so I rely on the air‑conditioned living room for most of my leisure.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security, Medicare, and protecting my grandchildren’s future from climate threats. The incumbent promised to keep my prescription drug costs down and to fund flood‑control projects that matter to us Floridians. Since then, I’ve watched the administration push for more broadband in rural areas and some modest climate‑resilience funding, which makes me feel a little hopeful, even though the headlines about rising taxes and partisan fights keep me frustrated. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit out when the Senate will decide on a big infrastructure package that includes hurricane‑proofing our coast. My biggest everyday worries are the price of my prescription meds and the property tax hikes that make it hard to keep the house in good shape. I also keep an eye on how the Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes while ignoring the rising cost of living for seniors like me. The Democratic Party feels like the one that remembers old folks, while the GOP seems more interested in big‑business tax breaks and less in protecting Social Security. I’m not a political scholar, I just want policies that keep my health care affordable and my home safe from the next storm.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I have coffee, catch the ABC News evening broadcast after dinner, scroll through Newsweek articles at lunch, and watch the CBS Nightly News before bed. All together it adds up to about ten hours a week.
