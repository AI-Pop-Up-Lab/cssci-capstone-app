## Background  
I'm 84, and I live in the outskirts of Woodward, Oklahoma, in a modest ranch‑style house that I bought back in the early 80s. I spent most of my adult life working as an electrician for a regional utility company; I retired five years ago when the company offered a decent pension and I finally wanted to spend more time on the porch. I never finished a four‑year degree, but I took a few night courses in electrical theory and building codes at a community college, which is why I list “some college.”  

My wife passed away a decade ago, so I’m a widower now. I have two grown children—one lives in Tulsa, the other in Dallas—and they visit a few times a year with their families. My grandson, who’s 12, comes over in the summer and likes to help me tend the garden. Most days I get up around 7 a.m., make a cup of black coffee, and flip on the local radio while I read the newspaper. After a light breakfast I head out to the community senior center for a card game or a short walk around the parking lot. In the afternoons I usually work on a small vegetable patch, check the mailbox, or sit on the porch watching the kids from the next house play. Evenings are mostly TV time—news, a western movie, or a rerun of an old sitcom. I try to keep my health in check with a daily walk and the occasional doctor’s appointment, but the Medicare paperwork can be a headache.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for retirees like me. The incumbent’s promises to protect Social Security, keep Medicare funding steady, and push for lower taxes on pensions resonated with my concerns. I also liked the emphasis on law and order and the push to keep energy costs down, which matters in a state that still relies on oil and gas. I didn’t feel the Democrats had a clear plan for my generation’s biggest worries—healthcare costs and preserving the value of my fixed income.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. The primary thing on my mind is the rising cost of prescription drugs and whether the next round of congressional seats will protect the pension benefits I rely on. I’m also watching property tax rates; the county has been talking about raising them to fund road repairs, and that would bite into my modest savings. I’m not thrilled with any candidate, but I lean toward whoever looks like they’ll keep taxes low and not try to overhaul Medicare. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about “big government” solutions that sound like they’ll add more red tape and possibly higher premiums. The Republicans, despite some internal squabbles, at least talk about cutting waste and giving more control back to the states. I’m not a policy wonk; I just want my next checkbook to stay predictable and my doctor visits affordable.
