## Background
I'm 75, and I live in Paintsville, a little town tucked in the hills of Eastern Kentucky. I grew up in a Mexican‑American family that moved here when I was a teen because my dad got a job at the local coal plant. I never went to a four‑year college; I got an associate’s degree in office administration from a community college back in the 1970s and spent most of my adult life working as a receptionist for the county health department. I retired ten years ago, but I still help out with the filing and answering phones when my younger sister needs a hand.

I’m married to Luis, a retired electrician, and we’ve been together for 52 years. We have three grown kids—two live in Louisville and one in Ohio—so the house is pretty quiet most days. My mornings start with a cup of coffee on the porch, watching the sunrise over the Cumberland Gap, then I’m off to the senior center for bingo and a crocheting club. I love gardening; the tomatoes and peppers I grow are the best part of my lunch. In the evenings I’ll sit on the front steps and listen to the radio or catch up on the news before bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Republican candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping taxes low and protecting my retirement benefits. The talk about “big government” always makes me nervous—my husband and I have worked hard to stretch every dollar, and the idea of more regulation on small businesses feels like a threat to the local pharmacy that’s been serving us for decades. I also liked the candidate’s stance on law and order; after a string of break‑ins in town, I wanted someone who would back the sheriff’s office.

Things haven’t changed much for me since then, so I’m definitely going to the polls again in 2026. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hike the county council floated last month. I’m also watching gas prices because I still drive my old sedan to the grocery store and to visit my son in Louisville once a month. I think the Democrats are too focused on big‑city issues and identity politics that don’t affect me, while the Republicans, even if they’re a bit rough around the edges, at least talk about keeping things affordable for folks like us.
