## Background  
I'm 30 and work as a marketing coordinator for a midsize manufacturing company in Fort Wayne. My day starts around 6:30 a.m. with a quick coffee from the corner deli, then a 20‑minute drive to the plant office where I juggle social‑media posts, email newsletters, and a few small‑scale ad campaigns. After lunch—usually a sandwich I bring from home—I spend a couple of hours in meetings with the sales team, trying to translate their numbers into something that looks good on a slide deck. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with my roommate, Maya, who’s a graduate student in nursing. We split rent and utilities, and on weekends we either hit the local farmers market or binge‑watch shows on the couch. I go to the gym twice a week, mostly for the treadmill because I hate the cold Indiana winters. When I’m not working, I’m either scrolling through Instagram or helping Maya study for her next exam. I don’t have kids, and my parents live a few hours away in Indianapolis, so I visit them only on holidays.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates seemed to care about people like me—someone juggling a full‑time job, rising rent, and the cost of groceries. I was swamped with a big product launch, and the early voting days fell right in the middle of a hectic week at work. By the time I got home, the news was just another background noise, and I convinced myself that my single vote wouldn’t change anything. That feeling of cynicism hasn’t really shifted; the political ads keep promising change but end up sounding the same. I’m planning to sit this one out too, unless something dramatic hits my neighborhood—like a factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes—that forces me to pay attention.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with rent and making sure the grocery bill doesn’t blow past my paycheck. The other thing that keeps me up at night is whether there will be stable, decent‑pay jobs in Fort Wayne after the current wave of automation hits the manufacturing sector. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about student debt and climate stuff, but they never address the cost of living for people in the Rust Belt. The Republicans brag about tax cuts, yet they ignore the fact that many of us can’t afford basic necessities. It feels like they’re both shouting at each other while we’re stuck in the middle trying to make ends meet.
