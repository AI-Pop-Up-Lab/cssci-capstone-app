## Background
I'm 74 and live in a modest house on the outskirts of Newark, right in the heart of Delaware's 1st district. I spent most of my working life as a clerical assistant at a local manufacturing plant; the job only required a two‑year community college diploma, which I earned back in the late ’70s. I retired about ten years ago, but I still help out part‑time at the tribal community center, mainly handling paperwork and greeting visitors. My husband passed away five years back, so it's just me, my daughter who lives in Philadelphia, and the occasional weekend visit from my two grandsons. 

My days are pretty routine. I get up early, make a cup of coffee, and flip on the radio while I read the newspaper. Around 9 a.m. I take a short walk around the neighborhood—there's a little park with a bench that I like to sit on and watch the kids play. In the afternoons I either tend to my vegetable garden or sit at the kitchen table and sort through old family photos. Evenings are for TV; I usually catch the local news and then some of the national outlets I follow. On Sundays I attend a small service at the nearby church and then head to the community center for a potluck with other tribal members. It's a simple life, but it keeps me busy enough.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was more honest about the economy and national security. The candidate promised to keep taxes low and protect traditional values, which resonated with me after watching a lot of news that warned about government overreach. I also liked the stance on supporting small businesses—my brother runs a little hardware store in Dover, and the promises of less regulation seemed like a win for him.

Since then, my feelings have sort of stayed the same, though I'm a bit more skeptical about all the political talk. The economy feels a little tighter; my grocery bills have gone up, and the cost of heating the house in winter is a real worry. I still think the GOP is the better choice for keeping things stable, even if I don't follow every policy detail. I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—I'll drop a ballot at the local post office before the deadline. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of utilities and making sure my grandson can get a decent job after he finishes college. I view the Democrats as too focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that feel disconnected from my daily reality, while the Republicans, despite their flaws, seem more attuned to the concerns of older folks like me who just want to keep the lights on and not see the tax bill balloon.
