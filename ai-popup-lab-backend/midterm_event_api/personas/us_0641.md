## Background
I'm 55 and I live in Sparks, right on the edge of the Reno‑Tahoe corridor in Nevada's 2nd district. I work full‑time as a floor supervisor at a distribution warehouse that ships building supplies to the growing suburbs. My mornings start around 5 am with a strong coffee while the kids are still sleeping; I grab a quick toast, check the weather on my phone, and head out to the lot where the trucks are waiting. The job is physically demanding, but I like the routine and the fact that I get to see the same crew every day.  

I'm married to a retired Army veteran who now does odd jobs fixing things around our house and helping out at the tribal community center. We have two grown children—one lives in Phoenix, the other in Seattle—so the house is mostly quiet except when the grandchildren visit for the holidays. On weekends I help out with the tribal powwow committee, setting up stalls and coordinating food trucks. When I'm not working or helping the tribe, I like to watch a bit of TV, tend to my garden, and drive out to the nearby lake for fishing when the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same old things, and none of them seemed to understand the unique challenges our tribe faces—water rights, health care access, and the constant pressure from developers on our land. Between long shifts, caring for my husband’s health issues, and the kids being far away, voting just felt like another task I could skip without any real impact. I also get pretty fed up with the endless ads and the way the parties talk over each other, never really listening to the reservations we have about federal policies that affect our reservation.

Going into the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic happens—like a major water‑rights lawsuit that directly hits my community or a candidate who actually shows up at the tribal council and talks to us—I don't see a reason to spend another Saturday at the polls. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are keeping my health insurance affordable and making sure the water we rely on for our farms and homes isn’t siphoned off by big projects. I also worry about property taxes creeping up as the Reno area expands; the county keeps raising rates and it feels like we’re getting squeezed from both sides.  

When I think about the parties, I find myself irritated by both. The Democrats keep pushing big climate regulations that could shut down the small mining operations that provide jobs for a lot of our people, while the Republicans talk tough on crime and border security but never address tribal sovereignty or the specific water‑allocation issues that hit us hard. It feels like they're both playing checkers while we're trying to move a whole board.
