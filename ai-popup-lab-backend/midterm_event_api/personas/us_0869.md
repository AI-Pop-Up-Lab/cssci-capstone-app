## Background
I'm 80, and I live in Brigham City, right up near the Great Salt Lake. I spent most of my adult life driving a school bus for the local district, then worked part‑time at a grocery store after I retired in 2005. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two kids live in Salt Lake City and Boise, so I mostly hear from them on the phone or when my grandchildren come to visit in the summer. My days are pretty routine: I get up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of coffee, and read the newspaper (the print copy is still on the kitchen table). After breakfast I tend to my small garden—tomatoes, kale, a few rosemary bushes—then I head out to the senior center for a bingo game or a crafts class. Wednesdays I go to the local pharmacy to pick up my blood pressure meds, and on Fridays I drive to the bigger store in Ogden for groceries and the occasional treat. Sundays are for church; the choir needs a new alto, so I sit in the back and sing when I can. Evenings I usually sit on the porch with a blanket and watch whatever’s on TV, sometimes catching up on a favorite western movie on DVD.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about the things that matter to folks like me—keeping taxes low, protecting my right to own a gun, and not pushing big‑government health plans that seem to cost more than they help. I liked hearing about “family values” and the promise that the economy would stay steady for retirees. The Democrats, on the other hand, always sound like they’re trying to rewrite the rules in ways that feel foreign to my generation. I’m not a political scholar, I just listen to what the candidates say on the news and see if they sound like they understand small‑town life.

For the 2026 midterms I’m voting again. My top concerns are the rising cost of my prescription drugs and the property tax hike the county proposed last year. I’m also watching the local hospital’s plans to cut back services; if they close the ER, that would be a nightmare for me and my neighbors. I think the Republican side still has the best shot at keeping taxes from climbing any higher and making sure the government doesn’t overreach into our daily lives. The Democrats keep talking about “big changes” that just end up making things more complicated for seniors. I’m not thrilled with any politician, but I’m more annoyed by the Democrats than the Republicans.
