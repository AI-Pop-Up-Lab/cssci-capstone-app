## Background
I'm 25 and I live in a third‑floor walk‑up in Burlington, right near the waterfront. I’m finishing a master’s in environmental policy at the University of Vermont, so most of my days are a mix of research papers, a few lab hours, and a part‑time shift at the coffee shop on Church Street. My roommate is a fellow grad student, and we split rent, utilities, and the occasional grocery run for fresh produce at the farmer’s market. Mornings start with a quick jog along the lake, then I bike to campus for a couple of classes, grab a latte at the shop, and head back to the apartment to write. Evenings are usually a mix of reading policy briefs, watching a documentary, or scrolling through my phone while the kettle boils. On weekends I volunteer at a local climate‑action group, help with community garden projects, and sometimes meet friends at a bar in the downtown area. I’ve been in Vermont since I was a kid, but I moved back for school after a stint in Boston doing data analysis for a nonprofit.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party’s platform on climate action and affordable health care matched what mattered to me. The candidate’s stance on protecting the Green Mountains and expanding Medicaid resonated with my own concerns about the rising cost of living and the vulnerability of our health system. I also liked the emphasis on funding public education, which directly affects my fellow students. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for the issues I care about, even if I’m frustrated by the occasional partisan bickering in Congress.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. My top everyday worries are rent and grocery prices – the rent on my apartment has gone up a few dollars each year, and the price of fresh produce keeps climbing, which hits my budget hard. I’m also watching the student‑loan situation; even though I’m in a repayment pause, I’m nervous about what comes next. I see the Democratic Party as the one pushing for climate legislation, protecting social programs, and generally being more in tune with younger folks like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like a step backward for the state I love. I’m not a hardcore activist, but I vote because I want my voice to count on the issues that affect my rent, my food, and the future of the planet.
