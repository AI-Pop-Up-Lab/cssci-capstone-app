## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in the suburb of Greer, just outside Spartanburg. I work as a senior data analyst for a regional health‑care system; I got my Ph.D. in statistics from a university in North Carolina and have been in the field for over thirty years. My wife, who retired from teaching a few years back, and I own the house on a cul‑de‑sac where we keep a small garden of tomatoes and herbs. Our two kids are out of state—one’s a software engineer in Austin, the other is finishing a master’s in public policy in Boston—so most weekends I’m either mowing the lawn, fixing a leaky faucet, or meeting my parents at the local Chinese restaurant for dim sum. I drive a midsize sedan to the office, but most days I’m on the train for the commute, listening to podcasts and the news on my phone. After work I usually stop by the gym for a quick weight session, then have dinner with my wife while we watch the evening news. On Sundays we attend a non‑denominational service at a nearby church and later help out at the community center’s senior‑tech class.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at the hospital’s analytics department, caring for my aging parents, and the feeling that the two parties keep talking past each other, I just never got around to filling out a ballot. The whole process feels like a circus—campaign ads that never address the real cost of my prescription drugs, and debates that end up on Twitter fights. I’m not convinced any candidate will make a dent in the issues that matter to me, so I’m sitting this one out too, unless something dramatic hits my neighborhood, like a major hospital closure or a sudden tax hike that hits my property bill.

My top everyday worries are the rising cost of health‑care and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. Grocery prices have been a pain too, but I can stretch a little by buying in bulk at the warehouse club. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as too focused on identity politics and not enough on practical solutions for seniors, while Republicans seem to ignore the needs of minority families and push for tax cuts that don’t help people on fixed incomes. Both sides just keep shouting at each other, and I’m left watching from the sidelines.
