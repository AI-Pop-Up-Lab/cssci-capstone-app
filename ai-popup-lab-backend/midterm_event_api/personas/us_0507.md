## Background  
I'm 55, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Missoula, right where the river bends around the foothills. I work as a public health researcher at the University of Montana, focusing on rural health disparities. My day starts around 6 am with a mug of black coffee while scrolling through the morning headlines on my tablet. I usually bike the 15‑minute ride to campus, because the mountain air wakes me up better than any alarm.  

I share the house with my husband, who retired last year from a job as a civil engineer. We have two grown kids—one lives in Seattle, the other is finishing med school in Boston—so the house is mostly quiet except for when we host occasional family dinners. On weekends I either tend to the garden, take the kids' dog to the trailhead, or meet up with a small group of friends at the local farmer’s market. We try to keep our expenses modest; the biggest line item is the mortgage, which we’re still paying off, and the cost of health insurance for both of us.  

When I'm not at work, I volunteer a few hours a month at a community center that offers English classes for recent immigrants, something I feel connected to given my own family's background. I watch a lot of the same news sites I list below, and I listen to NPR's morning news while I bike.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party's platform on healthcare and climate change was the only one that addressed the concrete problems I see in my community. The rising cost of prescription drugs hit my husband hard—his heart medication went up by 20 % last year—so I was looking for a candidate who would push for price controls and expand Medicare. I also liked the emphasis on protecting the public lands that surround Missoula; I spend most of my free time outdoors, and I can't stand the idea of loosening those protections.  

Since then, I've kept an eye on how the administration has handled the pandemic fallout and the recent push for broadband expansion in rural Montana. The Democratic senators have been pushing a bill that would finally bring reliable internet to places like my hometown, which would help my research and the community center's online classes. The Republicans, on the other hand, have been pushing for more drilling on the Western Slope, which feels like a direct threat to the water quality in the Clark Fork River.  

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are: keeping health‑care costs from spiraling out of control, and making sure we get decent broadband service out here. I also worry about property taxes—my mortgage is still up, and the county has been talking about raising rates to fund the new road projects. I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least tries to address those worries, even if they move slowly. The Republican side feels more like they're interested in short‑term profits for the energy sector, and I don't trust that they'll prioritize the health of the environment or the affordability of basic services for folks like me.  

## Media Diet  
I usually watch the morning brief on ABC News while I bike, then catch up on NPR podcasts during lunch, and I read the evening roundup on CBS News before bed, adding up to about five hours a week.
