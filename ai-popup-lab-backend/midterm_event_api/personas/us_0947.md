## Background
I'm 55 and I work as a shift supervisor at the regional distribution center on the outskirts of Janesville. I didn't finish my associate's degree in business after two years, but the job lets me manage a crew of about twelve people and keep the trucks rolling on time. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on a quiet cul‑de‑sac with my husband, who retired from the local factory a few years ago. Our oldest son lives in Milwaukee and sends money now and then; our younger daughter just started college in Madison, so we get a mix of calls about tuition and rent.

My day starts at 5 a.m. with a quick coffee and the news on my phone before I head out to the warehouse. The shift runs until about 2 p.m., then I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids' textbooks, and sometimes stop at the community center where I volunteer to teach basic computer skills to seniors. Evenings are usually spent cooking dinner, helping my daughter with her assignments over FaceTime, and scrolling through the news before I go to bed around 10 p.m. I drive a used sedan, keep an eye on the oil change schedule, and try to stay on top of the rising cost of utilities.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything and the whole process just seemed like a big, messy circus. Between the long hours at work, caring for my aging parents who live nearby, and the constant juggling of bills, voting felt like another thing I couldn't fit in. I also got the impression that both parties were more interested in scoring points than actually solving the problems that hit my wallet—like the grocery prices that keep creeping up and the health‑insurance premiums that never seem to drop.

I'm sitting this one out in 2026, too, unless something really blows up locally, like a proposed plant closure that would put a lot of my coworkers out of work. My top everyday concerns are the cost of food and the price of health insurance; if either of those spikes, I feel the pinch right away. I see the Democrats as the ones pushing higher taxes and more regulation that makes small businesses like the warehouse harder to run, while the Republicans keep talking about "lower taxes" but rarely address the day‑to‑day grind of someone who has to stretch a paycheck to cover rent, meds, and school fees. Neither side feels like it truly gets what a middle‑aged Asian American family is dealing with in a small Wisconsin town.
