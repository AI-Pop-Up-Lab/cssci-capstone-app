## Background
I'm 35 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in West Columbia, just across the river from the state capital. I work the afternoon shift at the local grocery store, ringing up groceries, bagging, and sometimes helping stock the aisles when we’re short‑handed. I’m a single mom with two kids, ages 7 and 10, so my day starts before sunrise. I get them ready, drop the younger one off at the elementary school across the street, and the older one at the middle school a few blocks away. After that I head to the store, usually clocking in around 1 p.m. and staying until the store closes at 9 p.m. When I get home I make dinner, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick scroll through my phone before I’m out of energy. Weekends are a scramble of laundry, grocery runs, and the occasional visit to my sister who lives a short drive away in a more rural part of the district. I don’t have a college degree; I left high school after sophomore year to start working and support my family. My biggest worry is keeping the lease up to date and making sure there’s enough money for the kids’ school supplies and a reliable car.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two jobs, taking care of the kids, and feeling like the candidates were all the same—talking about “change” while promising the same tax breaks and school funding I’ve heard about for years—I just didn’t see a point. The ballot felt like a performance, and I was exhausted by the endless ads and arguments that never seemed to touch my day‑to‑day reality. I also thought my vote wouldn’t make a difference in a district that leans heavily toward one party; why bother? That cynicism has stuck around, and unless something blows up locally—like a sudden spike in property taxes or a school closure I’m forced to deal with—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out too. My top everyday concerns are rent and grocery prices; when the price of milk goes up, that’s the headline I care about. As for the parties, I’m fed up with the Democrats acting like they’re the only ones caring about the working class, while the Republicans keep promising tax cuts that never trickle down to folks like me. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than actually fixing the problems I face on the ground.
