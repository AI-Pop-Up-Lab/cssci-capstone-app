## Background
I'm 35 and work as an administrative assistant at the regional health clinic in Rapid City. My parents moved here from Minnesota when I was a kid, and I grew up shuffling between the library and the grocery store down the street. After high school I took a few community‑college classes in business admin, but life got busy—first a part‑time job at a local coffee shop, then a full‑time gig at the clinic, and now I'm pulling double shifts when the clinic is short‑staffed. I rent a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who works as a mechanic; we split the rent, utilities, and the occasional pizza night. My days start early: I’m up by 5:30 am, make a quick oatmeal, then drive the 15 minutes to the clinic where I spend most of my day answering phones, scheduling appointments, and handling insurance paperwork. After work I either hit the gym for an hour or walk the downtown river trail, then come home, cook something cheap—usually beans and rice—and catch a show or scroll through social media before bed around 11. On weekends I help my mom with grocery runs and sometimes volunteer at the local community center teaching basic computer skills to seniors.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote didn’t matter; the campaigns were all the same noise, and I was juggling extra shifts at the clinic when the primaries rolled around. The ballot felt like a headache I couldn’t fit into my already packed schedule, and the candidates all seemed to be talking past each other instead of addressing the real stuff that hits my wallet—rising grocery prices and the cost of health care. I’m still leaning toward sitting this one out in 2026 unless something shakes me up, like a major cut to clinic funding that would directly affect my job or a local scandal that forces a special election. My top everyday concerns are keeping up with rent and making sure my health insurance premiums don’t skyrocket. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to promise more social programs but also talk about higher taxes that would pinch my paycheck, while the Republicans keep pushing deregulation that feels like it benefits big ranches and oil firms more than people like me trying to get by. I’m pretty annoyed with both sides; they both act like they’re listening, but the policies that actually matter to folks on the ground get lost in the sound‑bites.

## Media Diet
I tune into CNBC for the market updates during my morning coffee, catch NPR’s midday news roundup on my lunch break, and skim ABC News headlines in the evening while I’m winding down. I clock about four hours a week across those outlets.
