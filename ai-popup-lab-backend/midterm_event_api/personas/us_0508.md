## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Hamilton, a small town tucked near the Flathead River in Montana's 1st district. I never finished high school; after my teen years I started working at the local grocery store and have been a part‑time stock clerk ever since. My husband retired from the copper mine a few years back, and we both get up early to catch the sunrise over the mountains before the day gets busy. Most mornings I pour a cup of strong coffee, read the headlines on my phone while waiting for the bus, then head to the store for a shift that runs from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. After work I swing by the post office to mail a few bills, drop off groceries for my sister who lives in Missoula, and sometimes stop at the community center for the weekly bingo night. My two adult children live out of state—one in Seattle, one in Denver—so most evenings are just us, the TV, and the occasional call to check in. I also help my aging mother who lives a couple of hours away; I drive her to her doctor's appointments when I can. The routine is simple, a lot of manual labor, and a steady paycheck that keeps the house roof over our heads.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing feels like a circus, and between long shifts, caring for my mom, and trying to stretch every dollar, I just didn't see the point. The candidates all sound the same on the TV, and the promises never seem to reach places like Hamilton. I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything, so I stayed home. That feeling hasn't really shifted; I'm still pretty skeptical about the system. I'm sitting this one out too—unless something blows up locally, like a big factory closing or a sudden hike in property taxes, I won't bother with the polls. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that my health insurance premiums keep creeping up, even though I'm still working part‑time. I also worry about the road conditions in winter; when the snow comes, getting to the store or the doctor gets a lot harder. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as a bunch of city‑slickers who talk a lot about climate and social programs but don't understand the real cost of living in a rural town. The Republicans? They keep yelling about “freedom” and “lower taxes,” but when the tax cuts don't trickle down to folks like me, it feels like empty noise. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than actually fixing the problems that keep me up at night.
