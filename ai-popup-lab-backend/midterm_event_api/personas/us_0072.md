## Background
I'm 75 and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the outskirts of Maricopa, just outside Phoenix in Arizona's 5th district. I spent most of my working life as an HVAC technician; the trade school gave me a two‑year certificate, and I loved the hands‑on work fixing air conditioners for both homes and small businesses. I retired five years ago, but I still take occasional call‑outs when a neighbor's unit blows hot air in the summer. My wife, Rosa, helps me tend a small garden where we grow chiles and tomatoes; we eat most of what we grow, which keeps grocery bills a bit lower. Our two adult children live in Tucson and Phoenix, and we get video calls from my grandson every weekend. My days are pretty routine: I start with a cup of coffee, read the morning news on my tablet while the radio plays classic rock, then I walk the dog, check on the garden, and sometimes help out at the local senior center where I teach basic home‑repair skills. In the evenings I watch the news, and on Sundays I go to mass at our local parish. The community is a mix of long‑time residents and newer retirees, and we look out for each other, especially when the desert heat spikes.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one that really cared about keeping taxes low and protecting my pension. I liked the talk about deregulating the energy sector—after all, my trade depended on stable electricity prices—and I was wary of the Democrats' push for higher government spending, which I feared would mean bigger deficits and higher taxes down the line. The candidate promised to keep the border secure, and that mattered to me as a Hispanic who grew up seeing the challenges of illegal immigration in our community. I also appreciated the emphasis on law‑and‑order, especially after hearing about rising crime in some parts of the state.

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns right now are the cost of prescription drugs and the rising property taxes that are starting to bite into our fixed income. The health‑care bills that keep getting tossed around make me nervous; I just want to be able to afford my medication without having to choose between it and the electric bill. The other big worry is the local job market—my grandson is trying to find a stable gig after college, and it seems harder for young people to get decent wages here.

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward: I trust the Republicans to keep government small and let people keep more of what they earn, while I see the Democrats as too eager to raise taxes and push policies that feel out of touch with folks like me who have worked hard all their lives. I get occasional frustration with the GOP's rhetoric sometimes being too blunt, but overall I think they're the better choice for my situation.
