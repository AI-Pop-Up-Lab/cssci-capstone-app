## Background
I'm 70, and I live in the Jamaica section of Queens, right in the 24th district. I work part‑time as a building‑maintenance aide for a low‑income senior complex; the job’s mostly fixing leaky faucets, sweeping hallways, and helping residents with the mail. I’m widowed, and my two kids live in upstate New York, so most weekends I get visits from my granddaughter who brings the latest iPad games and a bag of candy. My day starts early with a cup of coffee and the radio, then I head to the complex around 7 a.m. After a morning shift I usually stop by the local bodega for a bagel and a cheap coffee, then swing by the church for the 10 a.m. service on Sundays. Evenings are my time to sit on the front porch, watch TV, and catch up on the news before I go to bed around 10 p.m. I don’t drive much; the subway is my main way to get around, and I rely on the city’s senior discount for the bus when I need to run errands farther away.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my ballot didn’t matter—every election seems the same, and the politicians keep promising things that never happen in my neighborhood. Between long hours at the building, caring for my health (I’m on a few meds for blood pressure and arthritis), and trying to stretch my Social Security check, voting just felt like another task I could skip. The polling place was a few subway stops away, and the line was always long; I figured I’d just stay home and watch the news instead. That feeling of cynicism hasn’t faded, so I’m sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something huge happens, like a local hospital closing or a scandal that directly hits my community.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with the rising cost of groceries and making sure my rent stays affordable. The price of fresh produce keeps creeping up, and when the landlord talks about a rent hike, I start counting how many meals I can still afford. I also worry about my health care—prescriptions are getting more expensive, and I’m nervous about any changes to Medicare.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats talk a lot about helping people like me, but the money never seems to reach the streets of Jamaica, and the city’s services feel stretched thin. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to care more about tax cuts for the rich and don’t talk about rent control or senior health care. I don’t feel like either side really gets my daily grind.
