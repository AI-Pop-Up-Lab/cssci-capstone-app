## Background

I'm 50, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Lake Charles, in Louisiana's 3rd district. I work the night shift as a stockroom associate at the local Walmart; the job's mostly lifting boxes, scanning inventory, and making sure the aisles are stocked when the morning crew shows up. I didn't finish high school—got a GED later just to be able to apply for a few better-paying gigs, but most of my work has been hands‑on. My husband passed away five years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids. My daughter lives in Houston with her own kids, and my son is back in Lake Charles trying to get a trade certification. Most evenings I’m home by 9 p.m., cooking a cheap dinner of beans and rice, watching a couple of TV shows, and then scrolling on my phone before bed. On weekends I go to church on Sunday morning, then swing by the grocery store, run errands, and sometimes help my neighbor with yard work. I don’t drive much beyond the 30‑minute radius around my home because gas is pricey and the bus schedule is unreliable, so most of my life revolves around the corner store, the church parking lot, and the Walmart break room.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing feels like a circus that never changes. I work nights, I’m exhausted, and the polling places are only open during the day when I’m at work. I also feel like none of the candidates ever talk about the stuff that matters to me—rising fuel prices, the cost of my prescription meds, and the fact that the local factory is cutting back on shifts. I’ve been told voting is a civic duty, but when the choices look the same and the promises never stick, I just tune out. I’m not planning to go to the polls in the 2026 midterms either—“I’m sitting this one out.” The only thing that might pull me in would be a real crisis in our community, like a plant closing or a hurricane disaster response that directly affects my neighbors.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with the rising cost of gasoline and making sure I can afford my monthly meds. The other thing that keeps me up is the local job market—if the refinery slows down, a lot of folks in my town could lose their paychecks. As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats; they talk a lot about climate change and social programs, but I never see any real help on the ground in my town. The Republicans? They promise lower taxes and more jobs, but the last time they pushed a tax cut, my paycheck didn’t change, and the promised jobs never materialized. Both sides feel like they’re speaking to someone else, not folks like me who are just trying to get by.
