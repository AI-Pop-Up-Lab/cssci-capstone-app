## Background
I'm 35, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Tupelo, right in Mississippi's 1st district. I work full‑time as a warehouse supervisor for a regional distributor that ships furniture across the Gulf Coast. Most days I get there at 7 a.m., grab a coffee from the truck stop, and spend the next eight hours moving pallets, checking inventory, and dealing with a crew that’s a mix of older retirees and younger guys fresh out of high school. I went to community college for a year, took a few business classes, but never finished a degree because I needed to start earning right away to help my parents back in the small town of Amory where my older sister lives with her kids.

I’m married to Rosa, who works part‑time at a local grocery store and takes care of our two kids, ages 4 and 7. On weekends we try to squeeze in a little family time—usually a drive to the nearby state park, a quick barbecue in the backyard, or a trip to the church potluck. My dad still lives in a trailer near the river, and I visit him at least once a month, bringing a box of groceries and a few beers. After work I often watch a couple of news clips on my phone while I eat dinner, then help the kids with homework before we all crash around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote didn’t matter, and the whole campaign felt like a circus that kept promising change but never delivered anything that touched my paycheck or my kids’ future. Between long shifts, the kids’ school schedule, and Rosa’s erratic hours, there was never a good window to get to a polling place, and the mail‑in process seemed confusing. I’m still pretty disillusioned, so unless something huge blows up locally—like a factory closing or a sudden spike in gas prices that makes my commute impossible—I’m planning on sitting this 2026 midterm out too.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping enough money on the table to cover rent and utilities, and watching grocery prices creep higher every month. I also worry about the cost of health insurance for Rosa and the kids, especially since we don’t have a big safety net. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as talking a lot about climate and social issues that feel removed from the daily grind of a warehouse floor, while Republicans keep shouting about culture wars and taxes but rarely address the rising cost of living that hits families like mine. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the problems that keep me up at night.

## Media Diet
