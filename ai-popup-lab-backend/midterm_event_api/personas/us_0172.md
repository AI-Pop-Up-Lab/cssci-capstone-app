## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s tax policies and focus on deregulation were better for my family’s financial situation. The economy felt shaky after the pandemic, and the promise of lower corporate taxes and fewer “red‑tape” hurdles sounded like a way to keep my company growing, which meant more job security for me. I also appreciated the Republican stance on immigration reform—something that hit close to home given my own family’s story. Over the past two years I’ve watched the headlines and felt the Democrats were pushing for higher taxes and more government control, which I worry could choke small businesses like my wife’s boutique.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely voting. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on our condo fees. I’m also watching the local job market; if the insurance company starts cutting staff, that would hit me hard. I think the Republican Party still aligns better with my priorities on the economy and limited government, even though I’m annoyed by some of the more extreme rhetoric you hear on the news. The Democrats, in my view, seem more focused on identity politics and big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with everyday people trying to make ends meet. I’m not a political scholar, just someone trying to keep a roof over our heads and food on the table.
