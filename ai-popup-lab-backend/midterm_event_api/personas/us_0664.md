## Background
I'm 60, and I live in the Binghamton area of NY‑21. I work as a bilingual Spanish teacher at the local public high school, where I spend most of my day in a classroom of teens who are either trying to keep up with English or figuring out how to stay connected to their families back in Latin America. My husband, Luis, is a retired auto mechanic; we’ve been married for 35 years. Our only child, a daughter, moved to New York City after college and visits us a few times a year. I also help take care of my mother, who has arthritis and lives nearby. My day starts around 5:30 am with a quick coffee and the news on my phone, then I’m out the door to drop Luis at his part‑time job at the hardware store and pick up my daughter’s brother from his after‑school job. After school I stay for a couple of periods, then I run a small after‑school Spanish tutoring program for kids who need extra help. Evenings are usually a mix of cooking, watching a bit of TV, and helping my mother with her medication. On weekends I go to church, shop at the farmer’s market, and sometimes help out at the community center with adult ESL classes. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan and try to keep the house in shape while watching my utility bills climb.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about the things that matter to me—affordable health care, a realistic immigration policy that would let families like mine stay together, and funding for public schools. I was especially motivated by the candidate’s promise to protect Medicare and to increase school funding for bilingual programs. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are being handled, and while I’m disappointed that progress on prescription‑drug pricing has been slower than I hoped, I still think the Democrats are the better bet for my priorities. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out because the stakes feel real for my mom’s health care and for the budget my school gets next year. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of my mom’s prescription meds and the property tax hikes that make it harder to keep our house. I’m also worried about the lack of decent jobs in the area—most of the factories have closed, and the few openings that exist don’t pay much. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least talk about social safety nets, even if they sometimes get tangled in internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing immigration rules that would make it harder for families like mine to bring relatives over. I don’t trust them to protect the programs that keep my mom alive or to invest in the kind of job training my community needs.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR’s morning podcasts while I drive, skim ABC News during my lunch break, and read a few articles on Newsweek in the evening; it adds up to about seven hours a week.
