## Background
I'm 30 and work as a data analyst for a midsize manufacturing company in Peoria. I have a master’s in applied statistics, so most of my day is pulling production numbers into dashboards and trying to spot trends that can save the plant a few hundred thousand dollars a year. I rent a two‑bedroom apartment on the west side with my boyfriend and our little terrier, Milo. Mornings start with a quick coffee from the kiosk across the street, then a 20‑minute drive to the plant where I’m usually at my desk by 8:30 a.m. I grab lunch at the cafeteria, but I’m trying to eat healthier, so I bring a salad from home a few times a week. After work I hit the gym for a spin class or, if it’s raining, I binge‑watch a true‑crime series while scrolling through the news feeds on my phone. Weekends are a mix of grocery shopping at the local market, mowing the lawn in the backyard, and meeting friends at a nearby bar for a cheap drink. I don’t own a car beyond the one I commute in, so most errands are done on foot or by bike.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because the candidate promised to cut corporate taxes and roll back regulations that I felt were hurting the manufacturing sector where I work. I also liked the emphasis on “law and order” and the promise to protect the border, which seemed to line up with the concerns I hear from my coworkers about job security. Since then I’ve stayed pretty much where I was; the economy feels a little tighter with gas prices creeping up, but I still think the GOP’s focus on business-friendly policies is the best bet for my paycheck and for the plant’s future. I’m not thrilled with every Republican on the national ticket, but I’m more annoyed by the Democrats’ push for higher taxes and what I see as over‑reaching climate mandates that could cost my industry more money.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax hike that the county board just approved. I feel the Republican Party, even with its flaws, is more in tune with keeping taxes low and keeping regulations from choking small‑town jobs. The Democrats, in my view, seem more interested in big‑picture idealism than in the day‑to‑day grind that people like me have to deal with.
