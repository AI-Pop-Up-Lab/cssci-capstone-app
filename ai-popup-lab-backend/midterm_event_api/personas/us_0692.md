## Background
I'm 60 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Grove City, just outside Columbus. I never finished high school; I left after sophomore year to help my dad with the family’s landscaping business. When the business folded in the early 2000s I got a job with the county road crew, shoveling snow in winter and fixing potholes in the summer. I work a regular 8‑to‑5 shift, Monday through Friday, and I’m still on the payroll because they value the experience I’ve built over the years. My wife, Linda, retired from the local supermarket a few years ago, so she’s mostly at home now, gardening and watching daytime talk shows. We have two grown kids – a son in Detroit who’s married with three kids, and a daughter who lives in Cincinnati and works as a nurse. On weekends I usually take the truck to the hardware store, pick up firewood, and sometimes meet a few buddies at the bar for a beer and a game of darts. We’re also looking after my mother, who’s in her late 80s and needs help with medication and meals. Money’s tight enough that we watch the utility bills and try not to splurge on anything unnecessary.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote didn’t matter anyway. The candidates all sounded the same, the ads were just noise, and I was exhausted from working extra hours that year to cover a sudden car repair. I also got a call from a friend who said the whole system is rigged, and that stuck with me. The whole process feels like a hassle – waiting in line, filling out the ballot, and then nothing changes. So I’m sitting this one out too. I’m not planning to go to the polls in 2026 unless something dramatic happens, like a major factory shutting down in the district or a scandal that directly hits my community. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and keeping up with the property tax bill that seems to go up every year. I also worry about the road crew budget; if they cut back, I could lose overtime, and that’s my main source of extra cash. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats seem to promise a lot of social programs but never deliver anything that reaches people like me. The Republicans talk about “lower taxes” and “big government” but then push policies that hurt small‑business owners and raise fuel prices, which hurts my job on the road crew. I just want someone to stop the endless bickering and actually fix the basics: affordable gas, stable jobs, and a tax system that doesn’t eat up my paycheck.
