## Background
I'm 35 and work as a program analyst for a regional health nonprofit in Paducah, Kentucky. I have a master's in public health, which lets me dig into data on Medicaid enrollment and community health outreach. I live in a small rented duplex with my partner and our two kids, ages 7 and 4. Mornings are a rush: I drop the kids off at the elementary school across the street, then drive ten minutes to the office where I spend most of the day pulling reports and meeting with local clinics. After work I pick the kids up, swing by the grocery store for fresh produce—prices have been creeping up, so I try to stretch the budget by buying in bulk when I can. Evenings are a mix of homework help, a quick scroll through news on my phone, and sometimes a video call with my parents in Texas. Weekends we usually hit the riverfront park, or my partner works a shift at the warehouse while I volunteer at the community health fair. I’m not a big party person, but I do enjoy the occasional salsa night at the cultural center downtown.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attuned to the health and education issues that affect my family. The candidate’s promise to expand Medicaid in Kentucky and invest in early childhood programs resonated with the challenges I see at work and at home. I also appreciated the emphasis on immigrant rights, which matters to me as a Hispanic mother trying to set a solid example for my kids. Since then, I’ve stayed fairly engaged—my job forces me to keep an eye on policy changes, and I’ve talked with neighbors about the rising cost of gasoline and the lack of decent broadband in rural parts of the district. 

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. My top everyday concerns are the spiraling cost of health insurance premiums and the fact that property taxes keep climbing even though my landlord says he can’t afford any upgrades. I’m frustrated with the Republicans because they keep pushing cuts to social programs that my community relies on, and their rhetoric about immigration feels hostile to families like mine. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to talk a lot about big‑city issues that don’t always translate to western Kentucky, but at least they’re not actively trying to roll back the safety nets we need. I’m hoping the next congressional candidate will actually listen to folks on the ground instead of just spouting party lines.

## Media Diet
