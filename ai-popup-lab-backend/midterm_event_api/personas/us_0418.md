## Background  
I'm 35 and I work as a foreman at a plywood mill just outside Brewer, Maine. I grew up in a Spanish‑speaking household; my parents came here for the shipyard jobs and taught me to work hard. After high school I took a few night classes at a community college, tried a bit of logistics and welding, but never finished a degree. I married my high school sweetheart five years ago, and we have two kids—an eight‑year‑old daughter and a five‑year‑old son. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30, make coffee, and drive the kids to the public school in Bangor while I grab a quick bagel and scan the headlines on my phone. The shift at the mill starts at 7, runs till 3, and I’m usually on my feet the whole time, moving pallets and checking quality. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from soccer, and try to squeeze in a little fishing on the river over the weekend when the weather’s decent. We live in a modest ranch house we bought with a small mortgage, and the kids’ school activities keep the evenings busy. When I have a spare hour, I’m either watching a game on TV or scrolling through the news feeds on my phone before bed.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about keeping the mill running and protecting the jobs that keep my family afloat. The candidate promised to cut regulations that were slowing down production and to push for lower taxes on small businesses like ours. I was also tired of the constant talk about “green policies” that, in my view, threatened the timber industry that’s been the backbone of our community for generations. Since then, I’ve watched the new administration’s trade talks and the occasional push for higher carbon standards. It’s made me a bit nervous, but I still think a Republican approach—lower taxes, less red tape, a strong stance on immigration to keep wages from being driven down—makes more sense for someone in my situation. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I don’t have the patience for another year of “talk” without results. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of gasoline for the truck I use to haul lumber and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on our mortgage. I also keep an eye on the local school funding because my kids’ education matters a lot. When I think about the parties, the GOP feels like the side that actually understands the grind of a working‑class family in a small town. The Democrats seem more focused on big‑city issues and cultural debates that feel far removed from the reality of a mill town. I’m not a political scholar, but I’ve got a gut feeling that the Republican platform is the one that will keep my family’s paycheck steady.
