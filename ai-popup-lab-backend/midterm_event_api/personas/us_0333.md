## Background
I'm 25 and work as an automotive service technician at a small garage on the outskirts of Paducah, Kentucky. I got my associate degree in Automotive Technology from a community college in Henderson, so I can read the computer diagnostics but I’m still on the shop floor most days. My shift starts at 7 a.m., I’m out the door by 6 a.m. to catch the bus that runs past the river, and I’m back around 4 p.m. most weekdays. After work I usually swing by the local taquería for a cheap taco and a soda, then help my younger sister with her homework when she comes over. She’s finishing high school and wants to be a nurse, so I try to keep the house quiet enough for her to study. On weekends I do a bit of extra gig work—delivering pallets for a warehouse that hires on call—just to chip away at the rent and help my parents send money back to Mexico. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with my mom and my little brother; the rent is $800 a month, and the landlord just raised the water bill last winter. My day usually ends with me scrolling through my phone while I wait for the bus home, checking the news for a quick heads‑up before I fall asleep.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same stuff, and I was working two jobs to keep up with rent and groceries. The polls and the ads just blended together, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t shift anything anyway. I also missed the early voting window because I was stuck on a delivery shift and the polling place was too far away. That left me with a sour taste toward the whole system—nothing felt personal, and the candidates kept talking about “the middle class” while I was juggling three paychecks just to keep the lights on. I’m not planning to go to the polls for the 2026 midterms either; unless there’s a major local issue—like a factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes—I’m just going to sit this one out. My biggest everyday worries are making sure I can keep up with rent and afford enough groceries for the month; when gas prices jump even a few cents, I have to cut back on eating out, which hurts my sister’s morale when she sees me skip meals. As for the parties, I think both of them are out of touch with people like me. The Democrats keep pushing fancy green initiatives that sound good on TV but don’t help me pay the electric bill, while the Republicans talk about “law and order” and tax cuts but ignore the fact that my mom works two low‑wage jobs and still can’t make ends meet. If I had to pick, I’m more annoyed with the Democrats because they seem to think their policies will magically fix everything, but the Republicans have at least talked about trade deals that could bring more jobs to western Kentucky. Still, I’m not a fan of either side; I just want someone who’ll listen to a guy trying to keep a roof over his head.
