## Background
I'm 20 and I work the night shift stacking pallets at the distribution center on the edge of Spokane. I grew up in a modest house a few miles south of the city, and after graduating high school I decided not to go to college right away because I needed to help my parents pay the mortgage. I share a small two‑bedroom apartment with my older sister, who’s in her late‑20s and works as a dental assistant. Most evenings I’m home around 7 a.m. after my 11‑pm shift, so I spend a lot of time on my phone scrolling through social feeds while I wait for my coffee to brew. On weekends I try to squeeze in a pickup basketball game at the community center and sometimes I help my dad with odd jobs around the house—yard work, fixing the fence, that sort of thing. My free time is mostly video games, listening to a couple of podcasts about tech and pop culture, and catching up with friends at the local skate park. I don’t drive a car; I rely on the city bus and rides from my sister when I need to go farther.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between my night shifts, school‑type classes at the community college, and trying to keep my budget from falling apart, the whole campaign felt like background noise. I’m skeptical that any candidate really cares about a 20‑year‑old who’s working two jobs just to stay afloat. The ads were all about big‑ticket issues that don’t touch my daily grind—foreign policy, climate treaties, and the usual partisan bickering. I figured my single vote wouldn’t move the needle, so I stayed home. I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms unless something hits close to home, like a sudden rent hike or a local school closure that forces my sister to quit her job. Right now my top concerns are keeping my apartment, which is already “just barely” affordable, and the rising price of groceries; a bad month at work can mean skipping a few meals. I see the Democratic Party as a bunch of career politicians who talk about student debt and climate but never understand the cash‑flow crunch of a young worker. The Republicans look like they’re more interested in culture wars and tax cuts for the super‑rich than in fixing the minimum‑wage gap. Both sides feel distant, and I’m more annoyed by the constant shouting than by any specific policy.
