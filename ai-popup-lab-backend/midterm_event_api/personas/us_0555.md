## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Republican candidate because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑business environment that lets me keep my part‑time job without too many regulations. I also liked the candidate’s tough talk on immigration and the promise to keep the military strong—things that felt relevant after hearing a lot of talk about border issues on the sites I read. Since then, I’ve been watching the midterms more closely because a few of the Senate seats could shift the balance of power, and that might affect the federal funding we get for the college tuition cuts the state is pushing. I’m definitely going to the polls this November; I already have a ballot in my wallet and plan to vote early at the campus voting center. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are paying rent when my paycheck is stretched thin and the rising price of gas for the occasional road trips to my parents’ farm. I’m pretty sure the Democrats are out of touch with folks like me—too focused on big‑city issues and “green” policies that feel like they’ll raise my utility bill. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least seem to understand that I want a job, affordable housing, and less government meddling in my life.
