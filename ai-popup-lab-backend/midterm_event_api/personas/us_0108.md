## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 mostly because the candidate talked about protecting the energy jobs that keep a lot of families in our area afloat. Growing up near the oil fields and seeing a lot of folks at the grocery store work in that sector, I felt the party’s message about fewer regulations and lower taxes would help keep those jobs here. I also liked the candidate’s tough stance on illegal immigration; it felt like a direct response to the headlines I saw about border issues. Since then, I’ve stayed pretty quiet about politics—most of my friends are more focused on school and part‑time gigs than on campaign rallies. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms; I think the Senate race could affect how much funding our local schools get, and I’m worried about the rising cost of rent in Greeley. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure groceries stay cheap enough for my family. I see the Democrats as a lot of talk about climate change and social programs that sound good on paper but end up raising taxes and hurting small businesses. The Republicans, for the most part, seem more in touch with the “real world” of work and money, even if some of their rhetoric feels a bit over the top. I’m not a political scholar, I just want policies that let my mom keep her job, keep my brother’s tuition low, and keep the gas pumps from getting too pricey.
