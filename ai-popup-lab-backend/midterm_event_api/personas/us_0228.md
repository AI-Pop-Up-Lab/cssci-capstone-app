## Background  
I'm 35 and work as a dental hygienist at a clinic just outside Council Bluffs. My associate's degree in dental hygiene got me the license, and I’ve been on the job about eight years now. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house with my partner and our seven‑year‑old daughter. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and get the kid ready for school while my partner drops off his own kids at the same elementary. I drive the 15‑minute stretch to the clinic, work a 9‑to‑5 schedule with a short lunch break where I usually eat a sandwich and scroll through my phone. After work I pick up my daughter, swing by the grocery store, and try to squeeze in a quick visit to the community center where I volunteer to help kids with homework once a week. Weekends are a mix of church services, family barbecues with neighbors, and the occasional DIY project around the house—usually fixing a leaky faucet or painting the front porch. Money is tight enough that I keep a close eye on utility bills and try to stretch every paycheck, but I’m proud of the stable routine we’ve built despite the challenges.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between long hours at the clinic, caring for my daughter, and feeling like the candidates all promised the same vague “change” without any real plan for working families, I just shrugged it off. The ballot felt like a formality, and the whole process seemed more about party theatrics than solving the everyday problems I face—like rising grocery prices and the cost of health insurance for my family. I’m not thrilled about the political circus, so I’m sitting this one out too, unless something hits close to home, like a major hospital closure in our district or a policy that directly threatens my child’s school funding.

My biggest worries right now are the price of food and the looming cost of health care. Every time I get the grocery bill, it feels like I’m paying more for the same items, and the idea of a sudden increase in my health insurance premium makes me lose sleep. As for the parties, I think the Democrats talk a lot about helping working‑class folks, but they haven’t delivered on the ground where I live. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem out of touch with city‑side concerns and focus on issues that don’t affect my day‑to‑day life. Both sides feel like they’re playing a game while I’m just trying to keep my family afloat.

## Media Diet  
I usually listen to NPR in the car on my way to work and catch the ABC News evening roundup while I’m cooking dinner. It adds up to about three hours a week.
