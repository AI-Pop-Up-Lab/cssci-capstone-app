## Background  
I’m 75 and have lived most of my life in Emporia, Kansas, right in the middle of the 2nd congressional district. I never finished high school; after dropping out at sixteen I worked a series of low‑wage jobs—cleaning houses, washing dishes at the local diner, and later helping out on a neighbor’s farm picking vegetables. I retired about ten years ago when my knees gave out, and now I get a modest Social Security check plus a little pension from the diner where I used to work.  

I’m widowed; my husband passed away five years ago. Our two children moved to California and Washington state when they were in their twenties, so I mostly hear from them on the phone once a month. My grandson visits us for a weekend every summer, and I love cooking his favorite fried rice.  

My days are simple. I get up around 7 a.m., take a short walk around the neighborhood, then head to the senior center for bingo and the occasional craft class. I shop for groceries at the big chain a few blocks away, and I go to church on Sundays. I spend a lot of time on the porch watching the kids play in the park and listening to the radio when I’m not watching TV.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I didn’t vote. My doctor told me to avoid long waits at the polling place because of a heart condition, and I felt like the choices on the ballot didn’t really affect my day‑to‑day life. I also didn’t feel informed enough; the campaign ads just blended together and I wasn’t sure who was who.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m voting. My niece reminded me that the state legislature is voting on a property‑tax freeze that could lower the amount I have to pay on my modest home, and my primary doctor warned that Medicare cuts are being discussed at the federal level. Those are the two things that keep me up at night: keeping my house affordable and making sure I can keep getting the medicines I need.  

I’m not a big fan of either party. The Republicans keep talking about lowering taxes, but when I look at the local news I hear about cuts to rural health clinics that I rely on. The Democrats promise to protect Medicare, but they also push for higher taxes that would make my grocery bill go up. I’m leaning a little toward the Republican candidate who says he’ll protect the property‑tax freeze, but I’m still skeptical and will probably swing whichever side seems more likely to keep my rent low and my doctor’s office open.

## Media Diet  
I usually catch the morning headlines on abcnews.com while I’m having breakfast, and I settle in for the evening news on nbcnews.com after dinner. Between those two slots I scroll through cbsnews.com on my tablet a few times a week, adding up to about six hours total.

## Opinion Formation  
Constructor. I don’t follow politics closely; I form my opinions from what I hear in the moment. For example, last week my neighbor at the senior center mentioned a new road project that would bring more traffic near our homes, and I instantly decided I’d support the candidate who promised to keep speed limits low, even though I hadn’t read any detailed policy papers.
