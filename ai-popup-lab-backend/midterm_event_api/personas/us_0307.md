## Background
I'm 75 and I live in Pittsburg, a small town in the 2nd congressional district of Kansas. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I worked at a local garment factory for most of my adult life. I retired about ten years ago when the factory closed, and now I spend a lot of my time at the senior center where I volunteer to knit blankets for the homeless. My husband, who used to be a mechanic, passed away three years ago, so I live with my daughter who moved back in after her kids grew up. We have a modest house with a garden out back where I grow tomatoes and beans; it helps keep the grocery bills down. My days usually start with a cup of tea and the morning news on TV, then a walk to the post office, a quick stop at the grocery store, and sometime in the afternoon I either bake something for the senior center or sit on the porch reading the local paper. Sundays are for church, and after the service we have a potluck with neighbors. I drive a used sedan that I keep on a strict maintenance schedule because I can't afford any big surprises.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in the 2024 election. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything. The candidates all sounded the same, and I was dealing with a busted hip that kept me in physical therapy most of the week. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I was too exhausted to bother with the endless ads and rallies. I also get a lot of mail from both parties promising help for seniors, but I never see any of it actually happen, so I just tuned it out. Coming up to the 2026 midterms, I'm still leaning toward sitting this one out. Unless there’s a sudden scandal that hits my town directly—like a hospital closing or a major utility rate hike—I don't see a reason to drag myself to the polls again.

My biggest everyday worries are the cost of my prescription drugs and the property taxes on my fixed retirement income. Every time the tax bill comes, I have to cut back on something, whether that's the amount of food I buy or the heating I can afford in winter. The other thing that keeps me up is how pricey my blood pressure meds have become; the pharmacy keeps telling me the price will go up again next year. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are always promising more programs but then end up raising taxes to pay for them, which hurts folks like me on a limited budget. The Republicans talk a lot about cutting taxes and securing the border, but they never seem to address the real health care and rural infrastructure problems we face here. Both sides feel like they're playing a game, and I'm just the pawn that gets shuffled around.
