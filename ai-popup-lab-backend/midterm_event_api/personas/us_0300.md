## Background

I work as a senior data analyst for a regional health insurance company; the job lets me stay in the office most days, crunching numbers for Medicare reimbursement models. I earned a master's in statistics and later took a few Ph.D.-level courses, but never finished a dissertation. My wife, who used to teach high school English, retired two years ago, so now I'm the one handling most of the household chores and the grocery runs. We have two grown kids—one lives in Chicago with a tech job, the other is back home trying to finish a law degree. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the diner down the street, read the headlines on my phone while watching the news on the TV in the kitchen, and then head to the office by 8 a.m. After work I usually stop by the gym for a quick weight session, then come home, make dinner, and spend the evening either watching a game, tinkering with a woodworking project in the garage, or scrolling through the news feeds before bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party's tax proposals would finally give me some breathing room after paying for my wife's medical bills and my own retirement contributions. I also liked the emphasis on border security and the promise of less regulation for small businesses like the one my brother runs downtown. Since then, I've been watching how the administration has handled inflation—prices at the grocery store are still climbing, and my wife's fixed pension hasn't stretched as far as we hoped. The midterms feel like a chance to either double‑down on those promises or push back if the economy keeps slipping. I'm definitely voting this time; I don't want to sit out again unless something catastrophic hits our community. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax hike the county approved last year. I keep an eye on the local job market too, since a few of the manufacturing plants in the district have been cutting shifts. When I think about the parties, the GOP still seems the better bet for keeping taxes low and supporting law‑and‑order policies, even if I get annoyed by some of the more extreme rhetoric on social media. The Democrats, in my view, talk a lot about climate and social programs, but I worry those ideas end up raising my taxes or costing me more at the pump. Still, I do listen to a few centrist outlets, so I'm not completely shut off from their arguments.
