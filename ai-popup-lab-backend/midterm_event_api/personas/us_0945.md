## Background
I'm 45 and I work as a public policy analyst for a nonprofit research institute that partners with the state university system. My office is in downtown Madison, but I live in Middleton, a quiet suburb with good schools and a decent bike path that I use most mornings. My husband runs a small landscaping business, and we have two kids: a 13‑year‑old daughter who just started high school and a 9‑year‑old son who goes to the local elementary school. Most weekdays I drop the kids off at school, grab a coffee at the corner cafe, and ride my bike to the office. The job is mostly spreadsheets and policy briefs, but I get to attend a few town‑hall meetings when the institute is looking at housing affordability or climate‑resilient infrastructure. After work I usually pick up the kids from after‑school programs, help them with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner before we all watch a show together. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a little yard work with my husband, and trying to keep the house in order while the kids play soccer or go to their piano lessons. I also volunteer a few hours a month at the local community center, teaching basic computer skills to older adults who speak Spanish at home.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing hard enough on affordable health care and climate action—issues that hit home for me with my kids' future and my husband's small business dealing with rising utility costs. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding Medicaid in Wisconsin, which helped a neighbor who works at the same landscaping company. Since then I've kept an eye on how the administration has handled the federal pandemic relief money and the recent push for a clean‑energy tax credit; the rollout has been uneven, but I still think the Democrats are more likely to protect the social safety net I rely on.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the skyrocketing cost of childcare and the ever‑increasing property taxes that make it harder for families like mine to stay in the suburbs we love. I'm also watching the local job market—my husband’s crew is getting fewer contracts because some developers are delaying projects, and I’m concerned about what that means for wages and stability. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least try to address these concerns, even if they mess up on the details. The Republicans, in my view, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthiest and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like a gamble I can't afford to take with my family’s future. I get annoyed when the GOP talks about “small government” while ignoring how that translates to fewer social services for people who need them. Still, I don’t have a perfect picture of every policy; I just know which side feels more aligned with the day‑to‑day grind I’m in.
