## Background
I'm 35 and work as a maintenance handyman for the public school system in Great Falls. My day starts before sunrise; I grab a coffee from the truck stop, put on my work boots, and head to the school to fix leaking roofs, replace broken lockers, and keep the hallways clean. I live in a modest ranch house on the edge of town with my wife and our two kids, ages 7 and 4. My wife picks up some part‑time shifts at the local grocery store, so we both hustle to keep the bills paid. On weekends I help my older sister with her small ranch, moving hay bales and fixing fences, because family ties are strong in our community. When I have a spare hour, I sit on the porch with a cold beer and watch the game on the old TV, or I scroll through the news on my phone while waiting for the kids’ school bus. I don't have a college degree or even a high‑school diploma; most of what I know comes from on‑the‑job learning and the stories my grandparents told me about the reservation and the land.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like none of the choices mattered to me. The ballot looked the same as every other year—big promises from folks I never met, and I was working two shifts that weekend. I get the sense that politicians talk about the big cities while we’re just trying to keep the lights on and get food on the table. The whole process feels like a circus, and I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything. That feeling hasn't gone away, so I'm sitting this one out again for the 2026 midterms unless something hits close to home—like a major layoff at the school district or a big change to our water rights that forces us to protest. My biggest worries are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that property taxes keep creeping up, squeezing us tighter each year. I also worry about reliable jobs; the school budget cuts make my hours feel insecure.

When I think about the parties, I'm fed up with the Democrats. They push for policies that feel like they ignore rural Montana—more taxes, stricter regulations that make it harder to run a small farm or keep a school building in shape. The Republicans seem to promise the opposite, but they also talk about big national issues and ignore the day‑to‑day struggles of people like me. Both sides feel distant, and I end up just trying to survive rather than get caught up in the partisan noise.
