## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Coffeyville, a small town in the southeast corner of Kansas. I work the night shift as a forklift operator at the local meat‑packing plant; the job's tough, the hours are odd, but it puts food on the table. My wife, Linda, retired from the school district a few years ago, and we both take care of my mom who lives with us and needs help with meds and getting around. Our two kids are grown and live out in Texas and Oklahoma, so most evenings are just us and the TV.

My day starts around 4 a.m. I grab a coffee from the corner diner, drive ten minutes to the plant, and spend eight hours loading and unloading crates. When I get home around 2 p.m., I try to catch up on the news on my phone while Linda cooks. After dinner we usually sit on the porch and watch the local high‑school football game on the big screen TV if there's a match. On Sundays I go to the Baptist church, then head back to the house to work on the garden and fix up the old pickup. The routine is simple, and I don't have much time for anything beyond work, family, and the news.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about the kind of life I lead—steady jobs, low taxes, and keeping the government out of my backyard. The candidate promised to protect the meat‑packing industry from new regulations and to keep fuel prices from shooting up, which mattered to me since my commute is long and the plant runs around the clock. I also liked the talk about “border security” and “traditional values,” which line up with what I hear at church and from folks around town.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising price of gasoline and the cost of prescription meds for my mom. I also keep an eye on property taxes; the county has been talking about raising them to fund a new road, and that would squeeze our budget. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as out of touch with rural Kansas—always talking about big‑city issues and green policies that feel like a threat to my job. The Republicans, while not perfect, still seem the best bet for keeping things familiar, even if sometimes the leadership feels a bit too focused on national drama instead of local concerns.
