## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Yakima, right in the middle of Washington's 8th district. I grew up on the reservation not far from here and went straight into the workforce after high school, spending most of my adult life as a cashier at the local grocery store. I retired five years ago, but I still swing by the store a few times a week to pick up fresh produce and chat with the regulars. My husband passed away three years back, and my two grown kids live in Portland and Spokane, so most of my days are spent with my granddaughter, Maya, who comes over every other weekend for pancakes and board games.

My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee on the porch while the sun climbs over the hills. I like to read the local paper—though I mostly skim the headlines on my tablet—then I head to the community center for a low‑impact exercise class. After that I take a short drive to the senior center, where I help organize the bingo night and sometimes volunteer at the food pantry. Evenings are for watching the news, knitting, and calling Maya. I keep a small garden in the backyard; pulling weeds and checking on the tomatoes feels like a good excuse to get outside.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about Medicare, Social Security, and protecting my grandchildren's future. The pandemic hit our community hard, and the Democrats' push for vaccine outreach and expanded health coverage made a real difference for my sister, who was on dialysis. I also liked the candidate’s stance on tribal sovereignty and protecting water rights, which matters a lot where I come from.

Since then, I've kept an eye on how things are playing out. The price of prescription drugs has kept creeping up, and I've noticed my utility bill nudging higher every winter. The Republican candidates this cycle have been talking about cutting taxes, but most of that talk sounds like it would hurt funding for the senior services my community relies on. I'm not thrilled with either side; the Democrats seem a bit complacent on housing costs, and the Republicans keep bringing up “family values” while ignoring the reality of rising medical bills for folks like me.

I'm definitely going to the polls this November. My top concerns are keeping my Medicare benefits intact and making sure the cost of my prescription meds doesn't spiral out of control. I also worry about property taxes—my house is modest, but the rates have been creeping up, and I don't have a big paycheck to cushion the hit. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least try to look out for seniors and Native communities, even if they stumble sometimes. The Republicans, in my view, are more focused on cutting government spending, which feels like a gamble that could leave people like me without the safety nets we need. I’m not a political scholar, just someone who wants to make sure the next few years aren’t a scramble for basic care.
