## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest ranch house on the outskirts of Midland, right in the middle of Texas – 36th. I work part‑time as a senior analyst for an oil‑field services company; the job lets me sit at a desk most days, crunch numbers on production forecasts, and occasionally hop on a rig site for a quick look‑over. I have a husband who retired from the local school district a few years back, and we have two grown‑up kids—one lives in Austin, the other in Dallas—who call when they can. My mother, who’s in her late 80s, still lives a few counties over, so I drive out to check on her every couple of weeks. Most mornings I wake up early, brew a strong cup of coffee, read the headlines on my tablet while the house is still quiet, then head to the home office. After a lunch of a sandwich and a side salad, I usually take a short walk around the neighborhood to stretch my legs before diving back into spreadsheets. Evenings are for the church potluck on Wednesdays, a quick TV show, and sometimes a video call with my sister in Houston. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, yard work, and the occasional trip to the local rodeo with friends.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the party’s stance on keeping taxes low and supporting the energy sector felt like the only realistic way to protect the jobs that keep my community afloat. The oil and gas industry is the backbone of Midland, and I’ve seen families—my own included—depend on it for steady paychecks. I also liked the talk about “border security” and felt the Democrats were more focused on city issues that don’t matter to us out here. That said, I’m not a die‑hard ideologue; I get annoyed when the GOP drifts into petty infighting or when they start promising things that sound like they belong in a campaign ad but never materialize. The same goes for the Democrats—I see them as out‑of‑touch with real Texas life, more interested in “green” policies that could hurt the local economy.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the price of gasoline and the stability of local jobs in the energy field. If the price spikes again, I’ll be watching my budget tighter than ever. The other thing that keeps me up at night is property taxes; the county keeps trying to raise rates to fund schools, and I’m not sure the extra money is being spent wisely. I think the Republican Party still has the best shot at keeping taxes low and jobs secure, even if I wish they’d be a bit more honest about the trade‑offs. The Democrats, in my view, are more interested in pushing a national agenda that doesn’t line up with what people like me need day‑to‑day.
