## Background
I'm 35 and I live in a modest rental house on the edge of Sioux City, right near the Missouri River. I work as an administrative assistant for a regional insurance agency; the job mostly means answering phones, filing claims, and juggling a few spreadsheets, which I can handle with the two years of college I took before I started working full‑time. My mom still lives on the reservation up north, so I drive up to visit her once a month, bringing groceries and a few extra blankets. I’m married to a guy I met at a community center basketball league; we don’t have kids yet, but we’re saving up for a down‑payment on a house. Most weekdays I’m up by 6 a.m., make coffee, and catch a quick news clip on my phone before the kids—well, my roommate’s kids—are asleep. I work a 9‑to‑5 schedule, then head to the gym a few times a week, and on weekends I either help my mom with errands or tend to my small garden of tomatoes and herbs on the back porch. I’m pretty comfortable with the routine; the biggest hassle is the rising cost of utilities and the occasional pothole on my commute.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the candidate’s stance on taxes and small‑business support matched what I needed to keep my paycheck from shrinking. I also liked the promise to cut down on federal regulations that I thought were choking the local manufacturing plants that employ a lot of folks in our district. I wasn’t thrilled about every single thing the party said—some of the social rhetoric feels out of touch—but the economic angle won me over. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have affected my grocery bill and my landlord’s rent hikes; the price of milk and gasoline still feels too high, and I’m hearing from neighbors that property taxes are creeping up. I’m definitely planning to vote in the 2026 midterms; I’m leaning toward the Republican Senate hopeful because I think they’ll keep pushing for lower taxes. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats; they seem to focus on identity politics and climate talks that don’t help me pay the electric bill. The GOP’s talk about “energy independence” and “fewer regulations” feels more relevant to my day‑to‑day life, even if I don’t agree with everything they say.
