## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a small house on the outskirts of Bismarck, right in the heart of the 1st district. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I went straight into a series of low‑skill jobs—first as a meat‑packing line worker, then as a cashier at the local grocery, and now I’m a home‑health aide for a handful of seniors in the area. My husband and I have been married for 35 years; our two kids left for college in the early 2000s and now live in Fargo and Minneapolis. My mother, who’s 84, still lives with us and needs help with meds and meals, so a lot of my day revolves around her and the clients I visit.

My mornings start before sunrise. I feed Mom, make coffee, and head out around 6 a.m. to the senior’s house across town. The shift is usually 8 hours, with a short lunch break where I’ll grab a sandwich from the deli down the road. After work I swing by the grocery to pick up a few things, then drive home to cook dinner and help Mom with her blood‑pressure checks. On Tuesdays and Thursdays we go to the local church’s senior lunch, which is also where I catch up with old friends. Weekends are mostly chores, yard work, and the occasional trip to the nearby mall to get cheap clothes or a new TV. I don’t have a car that’s anything fancy—just a beat‑up pickup that gets me where I need to go.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party cared more about keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of jobs that people like me rely on. The Democratic platform seemed focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that would hurt the oil and agriculture sectors that keep North Dakota afloat. I also liked the candidate’s tough talk on immigration—something that felt like a direct hit at the “flood” of people I hear about on the news. I was pretty confident that the GOP would keep the federal government from over‑reaching into our lives.

I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising price of gasoline and the cost of my health‑care premiums. When I’m on a tight budget, a few extra bucks at the pump can mean cutting back on groceries. And with Mom’s health getting worse, any increase in my own insurance rates feels like a punch in the gut. I also keep an eye on property taxes; the county has talked about raising them to fund a new school building, and that would hit our fixed income hard.

My view of the parties hasn’t changed much. The Republicans still seem the only ones willing to stand up for small‑town folks, keep the government lean, and support the energy industry that pays a lot of my coworkers’ wages. The Democrats, in my mind, are more interested in imposing regulations that make life harder for people who can’t afford to move to a big city. I don’t trust a lot of the talk about “green new deals” because it sounds like more taxes and less freedom. So I’m sticking with the GOP, even if I’m not thrilled with every single candidate they put forward.

I tune into CBS News in the mornings while I’m making coffee and scrolling through the site, then I catch up on Breitbart articles in the evening after dinner, and I skim the New York Post headlines when I’m on my break at work. All together it’s about seven hours a week.
