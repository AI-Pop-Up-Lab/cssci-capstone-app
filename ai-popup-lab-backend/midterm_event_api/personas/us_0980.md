## Background
I'm 75, and I live in Fairmont, West Virginia, right in the middle of the 2nd congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working at the local coal plant; after the mine shut down in the early 2000s I took a job as a maintenance hand at the county hospital and finally retired five years ago. My wife passed away three years back, and my two grown‑up kids live in Charleston and Pittsburgh, so most days I’m on my own. I get up around 6 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and sit on the porch watching the sunrise while the old radio plays gospel and the news. After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood, sometimes stopping to chat with Mrs. Greene next door about the garden or the church potluck. I still volunteer a few hours a month at the community center, helping with the senior bingo nights. Sundays are a ritual: I drive to the African Methodist Episcopal church downtown, sit in the back pew, and listen to the choir. In the evenings I usually watch a bit of TV, read the paper, or work on a small woodworking project in the garage. My biggest bill is the heating oil that keeps the house warm through the long winters.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for seniors like me. The promise to protect Medicare, keep prescription drugs affordable, and invest in broadband for rural areas mattered more than anything else. I also appreciated the candidate’s visits to the coal towns and the pledge to fund retraining programs for former miners. Over the last two years I’ve watched the same issues play out, and I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for my health care and for keeping the social safety net intact. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to leave my voice out of the conversation about the upcoming Senate race and the state legislature’s budget battles.

My top everyday concerns are keeping my Medicare coverage stable and paying the heating bills when the price of oil spikes. The other thing that keeps me up at night is the lack of decent jobs for the younger folks in town—when the plant closed, a lot of families left, and the town never really recovered. I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to care about ordinary people, especially seniors, while the Republicans seem more interested in cutting taxes for the rich and pushing policies that ignore the realities of a former coal community. I’m not a political scholar, but the way the news talks about “energy independence” feels like a code word for more drilling, which I’m not comfortable with.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I have coffee, and I catch the evening updates on ABC News and CBS News after dinner; that adds up to about seven hours a week.
