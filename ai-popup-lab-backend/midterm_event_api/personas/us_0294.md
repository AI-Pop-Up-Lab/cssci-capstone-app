## Background
I'm 65 and I live in Hammond, right on the edge of Chicago. I work as a diesel mechanic at the local freight yard; I learned the trade at the community college down the road and have been at it for almost four decades. My wife, Mary, retired from the school district a few years back, and we spend most evenings on the porch watching the sunset over the steel mills. Our two kids live in Ohio and Florida now, but we get a handful of grandkids over the holidays and sometimes on the weekend. I start my day at 5 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner diner, and head to the shop where I’m usually on my feet all day, fixing engines and sometimes training the younger guys. Lunch is usually a quick sandwich from the lunchroom, and after work I swing by the hardware store to pick up a few things for projects around the house. In the evenings I watch a bit of TV, check the news on my tablet, and sometimes play a round of golf at the senior league on Saturdays. When the weather’s bad I stay home, work on my classic car in the garage, and call my sister up in Michigan.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party understood folks like me who rely on steady wages and low taxes. The candidate promised to keep regulations from choking the freight business and said he’d protect the pensions that many of us depend on. I also liked the tough talk on law‑and‑order; after a string of break‑ins at the yard, I needed to feel someone was taking crime seriously. Since then I’ve stayed pretty consistent—my concerns haven’t changed much. I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms; I’ll be at the polls next month. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on my home. I also keep an eye on the local job market; if the yard cuts shifts, that’s a direct hit for my paycheck. As for the parties, I think the GOP still has the best shot at keeping taxes low and not over‑regulating the kinds of jobs I do. The Democrats seem more focused on big‑government solutions that end up raising my bills, and I don’t trust a lot of their promises about “green” projects that could hurt the freight industry. I’m not a huge political junkie, but when I do think about it, the Republicans feel like the safer bet for my pocketbook and my community.
