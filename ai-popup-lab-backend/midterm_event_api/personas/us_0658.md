## Background  
I'm 80, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Sparks, right in Nevada's 1st district. I spent thirty‑five years teaching Spanish and civics at a community college after getting my bachelor's in education. I retired five years ago, but I still help out with the night‑school program once a week, mostly grading papers and tutoring a few students who need extra help with English. My wife passed away three years back, so it's just me and my two adult kids who live in Reno and Carson City. They visit a lot, especially on holidays, and my granddaughter comes over every other weekend for pancakes and a game of checkers.

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., put on a pot of coffee, and read the local paper while the dog, a tired old beagle named Chico, snoozes at my feet. After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood, which is mostly flat and easy on my knees. In the afternoons I either tend to the garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few herbs—or I sit on the porch watching the traffic on Interstate 80. Twice a week I drive to the community center to volunteer with the senior outreach program, handing out flyers about free flu shots and helping folks fill out paperwork for Medicaid. Evenings are for television or the radio, and I try to keep my phone off after 9 p.m. so I can get a decent night's sleep.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security and Medicare, which are the two things I worry about most now that I'm on a fixed income. The Republican candidate seemed more interested in cutting taxes for corporations and didn't say much about protecting prescription drug prices, something I need because I take several meds for my blood pressure and arthritis. I also liked the Democratic stance on expanding broadband in rural Nevada; my grandson needed a reliable connection for his online classes, and the promise of better service hit home.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are making sure my Social Security check keeps up with rising living costs and the price of my prescription drugs. I'm also watching the water situation around the Truckee River; droughts have made my garden less productive, and I hear talk about new water restrictions. I think the Democratic Party generally looks out for seniors and invests in public services, even if they sometimes get tangled up in bureaucracy. The Republicans, in my view, keep talking about "small government" but that usually translates to cuts in programs that people like me rely on. I get annoyed when the GOP blames everything on "big business" without offering concrete solutions for everyday folks. Still, I don't follow every campaign ad; I just try to pick the candidate who seems least likely to make my paycheck shrink.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I have coffee and catch up on the headlines, then I skim ABC News and CBS News in the evenings after dinner. All together it adds up to about four hours a week.
