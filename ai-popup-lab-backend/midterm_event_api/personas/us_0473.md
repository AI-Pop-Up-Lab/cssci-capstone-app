## Background  
I’m 55 and live in a small cul‑de‑sac on the west side of Columbia, right near the university but far enough that the streetlights are still on when I get home. I work as the maintenance supervisor at the regional hospital, fixing HVAC systems and making sure the emergency‑room generators run when they’re needed. I took a couple of courses at the community college after high school, tried my hand at an associate’s degree in engineering, but never finished because the job paid enough to support the family.  

My wife, who used to teach elementary school, now does part‑time tutoring from our kitchen table. We have two kids: a son in his late twenties who moved to St. Louis for a tech job, and a daughter who lives nearby and works at a nonprofit. My parents are both in their late seventies; my dad still tends a garden out back, and my mom helps with the grandchildren when they visit. Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, making coffee, checking the news on my phone while the house is still quiet, then heading to the hospital for a 7 a.m. shift. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and try to squeeze in a walk with the dog before the kids come over for dinner on weekends. On Sundays I usually watch the football game and catch up on a few home‑improvement projects around the house.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one standing up for the things that matter to me: keeping taxes low, protecting our borders, and making sure the government doesn’t overreach into everyday life. The Democratic platform seemed focused on big‑city issues that never touch my town, and I was skeptical of their climate policies that sounded like they would raise my electric bill. I also liked the candidate’s promise to back small businesses and keep the highway funding steady for places like the one I commute through each day.  

Since then I’ve watched the news and seen the cost of groceries inch up, and the local school district struggling with budget cuts. It makes me wonder if the promises about “small‑government” really translate into lower costs for families like mine. Still, I think the GOP is more likely to push for policies that keep my paycheck from shrinking and keep the hospital’s budget in check. I’m planning to vote in the 2026 midterms because I want a say in who controls the state legislature and the Senate seats that will affect Medicaid funding and property tax rules.  

My top everyday concerns right now are the rising price of food and the property tax bill that’s creeping up each year. I’m also watching the job market; the hospital’s hiring freezes make me nervous about my own job security. When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who understand that working‑class folks need steady wages and predictable taxes, while the Democrats strike me as more interested in cultural battles and big‑government solutions that feel distant from my daily grind. I don’t pretend to have all the answers, but I’m leaning toward the side that promises to keep my community’s costs down.
