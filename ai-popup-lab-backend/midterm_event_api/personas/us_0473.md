## Background  
I'm 55 and I've been working as a maintenance supervisor at a regional meat‑packing plant just outside St. Joseph for the past 18 years. I grew up in a small town near Kansas City, dropped out of community college after two years to help my dad with the family’s plumbing business, and never went back. My wife, Karen, runs a part‑time bookkeeping gig from home, and we have two grown kids—Mike, 28, who lives in Kansas City and visits a few weekends a year, and Lisa, 25, who just moved back to help with the kids at the local elementary school where she teaches. Most mornings I’m up by 5 a.m., grab a coffee, and drive the half‑hour to the plant. My shift runs 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., then I swing by the hardware store to pick up a few things for the house, stop at the church coffee hour, and head home to watch some TV before bedtime. On weekends I like to fish at the Missouri River, fire up the grill, and catch the game with the guys at the bar down the road.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I’ve always felt the GOP’s tax policy is the only thing that keeps my paycheck from shrinking every year. The Democrats keep talking about raising the minimum wage, but in my line of work a higher wage means my plant cuts overtime or brings in cheaper labor from out of state. I also like the way the party talks about the border and law‑and‑order—things that feel real to a guy who deals with trucks and freight crossing state lines.  

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. I’m going to the polling place in St. Joseph on Election Day; I already have the ballot in my mailbox and I’m planning to drop it off at the county clerk’s office if the line’s too long. My biggest worries right now are property taxes—our home’s assessed value jumped last year and the bill looks like it’ll keep climbing—and the rising cost of health insurance for my wife’s freelance work. I’m also watching the local job market; the plant has been talking about automation and I’m nervous about what that means for guys like me. As for the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re trying to rewrite the rulebook on everything—taxes, education, even what we call “family values.” The Republicans, despite their flaws, at least seem to understand that a lot of folks are trying to keep a roof over their heads and a steady paycheck. I’m not a fan of the whole “culture war” stuff, but I’m tired of hearing politicians talk about it while ignoring the bills that actually hit my wallet.
