## Background
I’m 60 and I work as a registered nurse at Twin Falls Regional Medical Center. I grew up in a farming community near Pocatello, but after I earned my BSN at Boise State I moved to Twin Falls for a stable job and have been here ever since. My husband, Carlos, is a mechanic at a local garage, and we’ve been married for 35 years. We have two grown‑up kids—María, who lives in Salt Lake City with her own kids, and Jorge, who’s back in Idaho doing construction. On weekdays I’m up around 5 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and catch a quick glance at the news on my phone before my 7 a.m. shift. My days are a mix of patient rounds, charting, and the occasional emergency that keeps the adrenaline up. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up some fresh produce, and try to fit in a walk around the foothills when the weather’s nice. Evenings are usually spent watching a TV show or scrolling through the news feeds while Carlos fixes something around the house. Weekends I help my grandchildren with school projects and sometimes volunteer at the local food pantry.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I didn’t feel any of the two major parties represented what I cared about, so I cast a write‑in for the Libertarian candidate. I was mainly fed up with the endless talk about culture wars and the lack of real solutions for rising healthcare costs, which hit me hard as a nurse watching patients struggle to afford prescriptions. The Libertarian platform’s emphasis on reducing government interference in personal choices felt like a middle ground, even if I wasn’t convinced they could actually pull it off. I also liked the idea of a smaller federal footprint because it seemed like it might let states like Idaho experiment with more locally‑tailored health policies.

I’m definitely going to the polls this November. My top everyday worries are the cost of health insurance premiums for my husband and me, and the property tax hikes that keep squeezing our budget now that we’re nearing retirement. I’m still pretty skeptical of both parties. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising universal health care but never seem to fund it without raising taxes or expanding bureaucracy. The Republicans talk a lot about cutting taxes, but they also push for cuts to social programs that many seniors rely on, and they keep turning the conversation into a shouting match about cultural issues instead of focusing on things like affordable meds. I’m not a fan of the political theater; I just want policies that keep my family healthy and our home affordable.
