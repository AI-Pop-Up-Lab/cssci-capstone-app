## Background
I'm 25 and work the afternoon shift as a cashier at the grocery store in Wheeling, right near the Ohio River. I never finished high school; I left after sophomore year to help my mom pay the rent when my younger brother was born. These days I live in a small two‑bedroom apartment with my mom and my brother, who’s still in high school. We split the utilities and take turns cooking; most nights I’m on the couch watching a show after a long day of stocking shelves and ringing up groceries.

My mornings start early with a cheap coffee from the corner deli, then a quick walk to the bus stop. The bus takes me past the old steel mill that’s been idle for years, a reminder that jobs in this part of the district are scarce. After work I often swing by the local church for the weekly potluck, because it’s a place where the neighborhood catches up and the food is free. On weekends I try to make extra cash by doing odd jobs—yard work, moving furniture—for folks in the community. I don’t have a car, so everything hinges on the bus schedule. When I have a spare hour, I scroll through my phone, looking at the news sites I trust, and occasionally text my friends about what’s going on.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I felt the Republican candidate didn’t care about the struggling families in our district. The Democratic candidate talked about expanding broadband in rural areas and protecting the few remaining manufacturing jobs, which hit close to home for me. I also appreciated the promise of more funding for public schools, even though I didn’t finish high school myself; I want my brother to have a better shot.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the promises are turning out. The broadband rollout is moving slowly, and the factory that was supposed to reopen is still in talks. That makes me a bit skeptical, but I still think the Democrats are more likely to push for the kind of social safety net my family needs—like affordable health care and rent assistance. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out again. My top concerns right now are keeping the rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing. I feel the Republican Party is mostly focused on tax cuts for big corporations and doesn’t really understand what people like me deal with day to day. The Democrats, for all their flaws, seem more willing to talk about worker training and health coverage, even if they sometimes overpromise.
