## Background
I'm 40 and I live in Yukon, a small town on the edge of the Oklahoma City metro in the 4th congressional district. I work full‑time as a department supervisor at the local grocery store, handling the produce aisle and scheduling the part‑time crew. My husband is a mechanic at a nearby auto shop, and we have two kids – a 15‑year‑old daughter who drives a beat‑up sedan to school and a 9‑year‑old son who still needs a car seat on the way to soccer practice. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30 a.m., making coffee, getting the kids dressed, and loading the minivan with backpacks and a lunch bag. After dropping them off at school, I head to the store for a 9‑to‑5 shift, which usually ends around 6 p.m. I grab dinner with the family at about 7, then help the kids with homework while the house fills with the sound of the TV news in the background. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a bit of yard work, and trying to keep the car running smooth for my husband’s long shifts. I’m involved in the local PTA a little, but most of my free time is spent on the couch or scrolling on my phone.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Between long work hours, my kids’ schedules, and feeling like none of the candidates really cared about the everyday grind of a small‑town family, I just didn’t make the effort to get to a polling place. The whole process felt like a distant drama that didn’t affect my rent or the price of gas. I’ve been pretty cynical about politics for a while; the endless ads and the way the parties flip‑flop on issues just makes me think it’s all the same show. I’m not planning to go to the polls for the 2026 midterms either – “I’m sitting this one out.” Unless something huge hits our community, like a factory closing or a major road project that forces us to drive farther for work, I’ll probably stay home.

My biggest everyday worries are the grocery bill creeping up and the price of gasoline that eats into the paycheck. The other big thing is keeping the house in good shape – the roof needs a repair and property taxes keep going up. When I think about the two major parties, the Democrats feel like they’re talking about big‑city problems and taxes that would hit a family like mine harder. The Republicans, on the surface, promise lower taxes and “protecting our way of life,” but lately I hear more talk about extreme positions that don’t match the practical concerns of a working‑class family. So I’m stuck feeling annoyed by both sides, but I’m more inclined to blame the Democrats for over‑regulation and the Republicans for being too combative and not delivering on the promised tax relief.
