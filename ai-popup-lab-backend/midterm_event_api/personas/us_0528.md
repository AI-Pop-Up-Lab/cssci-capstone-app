## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a small house on the outskirts of Angier, right in the middle of NC‑13. I spent most of my working life as a custodial aide at the local high school; I retired about ten years ago when the building got a new contractor and I finally qualified for full Social Security benefits. My husband passed away five years back, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live a couple of hours away in Raleigh and Charlotte. They visit when they can, and the grandkids swing by for a few weeks in the summer. Most days I get up around 7 a.m., pour a cup of coffee, and flip on the radio while I read the newspaper in the kitchen. After that I tend to my garden—mostly tomatoes and some herbs—because fresh food keeps the doctor away, or at least that's what the nurse at the clinic says. In the afternoons I take a short walk down the driveway to the community center where we have bingo on Tuesdays and a church choir rehearsal on Thursdays. Evenings are for a little TV, usually the nightly news, and then I head to bed early; my knees aren't what they used to be, and I need my rest.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security and Medicare, which are the two things that keep me afloat. The Republican candidates kept talking about cutting taxes for big businesses, and I just couldn't see how that would help an 84‑year‑old who lives on a fixed income. The Democratic candidate also promised to protect Medicaid funding for the community clinic where I get my check‑ups, and that mattered to me. Since then, I've kept an eye on what the parties are doing, but I still think the Democrats are the safer bet for seniors like me. I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—I'm not going to sit this one out. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the possibility that my Social Security check might get squeezed by future budget cuts. I also worry a bit about property taxes; the county has been looking at raising them to fund new road projects, and that would make it harder to keep the house heated in winter. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least pretend to listen to folks on my street, while the Republicans seem more interested in deregulating stuff that ends up hurting the little guy. I don't follow every policy detail; I just know which side feels more like they'll keep my rent low and my meds affordable.

## Media Diet
