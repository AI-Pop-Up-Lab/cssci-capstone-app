## Background  
I'm 74 and I live in a little place called Blackville, right in the heart of South Carolina's 2nd district. I never finished high school; I dropped out when I was 16 to start working on my uncle's tobacco farm. I spent most of my adult life doing manual labor—first as a farmhand, then later as a maintenance guy for the local high school until I retired at 62. My wife passed away five years ago, so it's just me now, but I get visits from my two kids every other weekend and a handful of grandkids who come by in the summer.  

My days are simple. I get up early, make a cup of coffee, and sit on the porch watching the sunrise while the rooster from the neighbor's yard crows. After that I head to the community center for the 8 a.m. bingo game; it's a good way to stay sharp and catch up with folks. In the afternoon I tend to my garden—tomatoes, collard greens, a few peach trees—and I try to keep the old pickup running for occasional trips to the grocery store in Aiken. Evenings are mostly TV: the news, some classic country music shows, and occasionally a rerun of "The Andy Griffith Show." I drive a beat‑up Chevrolet, and I still keep a toolbox in the garage for any small repairs around the house.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me—people who work hard, pay their taxes, and just want a stable community. I liked the talk about protecting the Second Amendment, keeping taxes low, and not pushing for big government programs that I think end up wasting money. I also trusted the candidate’s promises to keep the border secure and to support the military; those issues matter to me and my grandkids who have friends in the service.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that our roads are getting worse—potholes that make my old truck shake every few miles. I also worry about the lack of reliable internet in our area; without decent broadband, it's hard for my kids to stay connected with their own families and for anyone to get decent information.  

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as out of touch with rural life. They keep talking about climate policies and big city tax hikes that feel like they don't apply to me. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to understand that we need practical solutions—lower taxes, more support for small businesses, and a focus on law and order. I'm not blind to their flaws; sometimes I think they talk too much about culture wars and not enough about fixing the local school system, but overall I feel they’re the better bet for keeping my community afloat.
