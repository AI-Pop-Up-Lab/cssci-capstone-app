## Background
I'm 45 and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the outskirts of Rapid City, right near the Black Hills. I work full‑time as a research analyst for the regional medical center, pulling data on patient outcomes and helping the administration plan community health programs. My day starts early; I’m usually up by 5:30 am, make coffee, and scroll through my phone while the kids—16‑year‑old Maya and 13‑year‑old Jonah—get ready for school. I drop Maya off at the public high school, then drive Jonah to the middle school across town before heading to the hospital at 8 am.

After work I often stop at the grocery store on Main Street, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes swing by the local farmers’ market if the weather’s nice. Weekends are a mix of soccer games for Jonah, a book club meeting at the community library, and occasional trips to the mountains for hiking. My husband, who runs a small construction business, and I have been married fifteen years; we own the house together and are trying to save enough for Maya’s college fund. I’m a member of the local United Methodist church, but I don’t attend every Sunday—sometimes I’m just too tired after a long shift.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one seriously talking about expanding Medicaid in South Dakota and protecting funding for the community college where I teach a few evening courses. I also liked the emphasis on renewable energy, which matters to me since the Black Hills tourism economy depends on clean air and water. My vote felt like a small way to push back against the state legislature’s repeated attempts to cut education budgets.

Since then my view has become a bit more nuanced. I still think the Democrats are the better option for health‑care and climate, but I’m frustrated by the endless primary battles and the way some national figures seem more interested in partisan drama than concrete solutions. The Republican side, on the other hand, feels like they’re mostly focused on cutting taxes for big businesses while ignoring the rising cost of living that hits families like mine hard.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the cost of prescription drugs and the property tax assessment on my home, which has been creeping up as the county tries to fund road repairs. I’m also watching the local job market; my husband’s construction business is feeling the squeeze from rising material costs. When I think about the parties, I see the GOP as largely out of touch with everyday folks in the western part of the state, while the Democrats, despite their flaws, at least seem to care about the issues that affect my family’s health and financial stability.
