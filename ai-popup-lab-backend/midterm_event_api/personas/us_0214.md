## Background
I'm 40, and I live in a modest bungalow in the Kalihi neighborhood of Honolulu, inside the 1st congressional district. I work as a front‑desk supervisor at a mid‑size resort on the island, a job I got after completing an associate’s program in hospitality management at a community college on Oʻahu. My wife runs a small online craft shop from our kitchen, and we have two kids—an 11‑year‑old in middle school and a 7‑year‑old in elementary. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m. to catch the bus to the hotel, grab a coffee, and skim the news on my phone before the shift starts. After work around 3 p.m. I pick up the younger kid from after‑school care, help with homework, and we all eat dinner together by 6. The evenings are mostly for chores, a little TV, and trying to squeeze in a quick jog along the Ala Moana beach path before bed. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the local market, helping the kids with soccer practice, and occasional trips to the beach with the family. My paycheck covers the mortgage, utilities, and the pricey island groceries, but there’s never much left over for anything extra.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on climate resilience and affordable housing mattered most for Hawaiʻi. The hurricane relief plan they pushed and the push for more funding for public schools resonated with my concerns about my kids’ future. I also liked the candidate’s promises to protect the tourism industry while investing in local workers, which directly affects my job at the resort. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and I’m still leaning Democratic, though I get annoyed when the same old promises get recycled without real results. I’m voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t plan to sit this one out. My top everyday worries are the soaring rent and grocery prices that make it hard to stretch a single paycheck, and the uncertainty of tourism‑linked jobs when the world’s travel patterns shift. I see the Democratic Party as the one more likely to address those cost‑of‑living issues, even if they’re not perfect, while I view the Republicans as largely out of touch with island life—focused on policies that make sense on the mainland but feel irrelevant or even harmful here. That said, I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I’ll back a Republican if they actually propose something concrete for housing or job security, but that’s a rare scenario.
