## Background
I'm 65, and I live in Brigham City, right up near the foothills of the Wasatch. I never finished high school; after I married at 18 I started working full‑time at the local grocery store, stacking shelves, bagging groceries, and later helping with the deli counter. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two kids moved to California and Texas when they were in their twenties. I still get occasional calls from my grandson, who lives in Salt Lake City and visits for the holidays. 

Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, making a pot of coffee and getting ready for my shift at the store, which starts around 7 a.m. I work the morning and early afternoon, then come home to do a load of laundry, cook a simple lunch, and sometimes run errands at the pharmacy. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I volunteer a few hours at the community center, helping with the senior bingo night. Sundays are the only day I’m not working; I go to church, sit with a few neighbors after the service, and enjoy a modest potluck. Evenings I usually sit on the porch with a blanket, watching the local news or one of the national channels on my tablet while the kids’ voices echo from the next room where they’re watching their own shows.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between caring for my aging mother, dealing with a bout of flu that left me bedridden for weeks, and feeling like the candidates were all the same—talking about “change” but never addressing the real cost of groceries or the rising price of my prescription meds—I just didn’t see the point. The ballot felt like a circus, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t move the needle anyway. I also missed the early voting window because my schedule at the store was packed, and by the time I got home I was too exhausted to even think about driving to the polls.

For the 2026 midterms I’m sitting this one out, too. Unless something dramatic hits our town—like a major hospital closure or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces me to sell my house—I’m not planning to make the trip. My everyday worries are the price of groceries, the cost of my insulin, and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. I’m also worried about the local jobs market; a lot of the younger folks are leaving for the Wasatch County because there aren’t enough decent wages here.

I see the Democratic Party as being all about big‑city issues, talking loudly about climate change and immigration while ignoring the fact that my community’s water supply is getting tighter and my kids can’t find decent work locally. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep promising tax cuts but never deliver on affordable health care, which is what I need the most. Both sides feel like they’re looking at a different country, and honestly, I’m just tired of the endless promises that never reach a kitchen like mine.
