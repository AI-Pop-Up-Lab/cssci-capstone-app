## Background
I'm 79 and I live in a modest bungalow in Cranston, right on the edge of the Providence Metro area. I spent most of my working life as a high‑school English teacher, but I never finished my bachelor’s degree because family responsibilities pulled me away; I stopped at a few college courses and a teaching credential. I retired about ten years ago, and my husband passed away five years back, so now I mostly keep company with my two grown‑up children who live a few towns over and visit on weekends. My days start with a strong cup of coffee on the porch, a quick scan of the headlines on my tablet, then a short walk around the neighborhood to stretch my legs and say hello to the other retirees out front. Most afternoons I’m at the senior center volunteering to run a book club, or I’m in my garden tending to tomatoes and roses. Evenings are usually quiet: a bit of TV, maybe a crossword, and a phone call to my daughter who checks in about my blood pressure meds. I drive a reliable old sedan to the grocery store once a week, and I’m careful about my budget because my fixed Social Security check is the main income.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really paying attention to Medicare, prescription‑drug prices, and protecting Social Security. The Republican platform seemed to push for cuts that would have hit retirees like me hard, and the talk about “fiscal responsibility” felt like a code word for slashing benefits. I also liked the candidate’s stance on climate‑related infrastructure, since the rising sea levels around our coastline worry me. Over the past two years I’ve stayed pretty engaged; I still think the Democrats are the safer bet for my generation, even if I’m frustrated by the constant bickering in Congress. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—my local Senate race has a candidate promising to protect the prescription‑drug discount program, which is my top everyday concern. The other big worry is the rising cost of property taxes on my modest home; the town council’s budget talks keep creeping up. I’m not a fan of the GOP; their talk about “lower taxes” feels like a joke when I’m already paying more than I can comfortably afford. The Democrats, for all their flaws, at least talk about expanding health coverage and funding senior services, which is what keeps me leaning their way.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I have coffee, and I catch the evening updates on ABC News and CBS News after dinner; it adds up to about six hours a week.
