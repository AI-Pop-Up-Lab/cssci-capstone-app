## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best represented my concerns about taxes, gun rights, and keeping regulations low for small businesses like the family‑run hardware store my dad owns down the road. I liked the promise of keeping fuel prices stable and not raising the property tax that would hit my mortgage. The campaign ads about “protecting our way of life” resonated with the everyday grind I go through on the road. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening in Washington, but I still think the GOP is the better bet for my pocket and my values. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve already marked the date on the calendar and filled out the absentee ballot because my schedule is hectic. My top everyday concerns right now are the rising cost of diesel and the way the state is handling school funding—our kids’ schools are getting tighter budgets, and that worries me. I’m also watching property tax hikes in our county; any increase would squeeze our household budget. As for the parties, I feel the Democrats are too eager to raise taxes and push policies that feel out of touch with folks like me who work hard and want to keep more of what we earn. The Republicans I support seem more in tune with my need for economic freedom, but I’m annoyed by the internal squabbles and some of the more extreme rhetoric that can turn voters off. Still, I think the GOP is the lesser of two evils for my day‑to‑day life.
