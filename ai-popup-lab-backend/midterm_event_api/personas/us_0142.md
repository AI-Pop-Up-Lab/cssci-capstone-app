## Background  
I'm 55 and work full‑time as a front‑desk manager at a community health clinic in Wilmington. My shift starts at 7 a.m.; I grab a coffee from the corner deli, catch the bus, and spend the morning checking in patients, handling paperwork, and making sure the waiting room runs smoothly. After my shift ends around 3 p.m., I head home to a modest two‑bedroom house I share with my sister, who lives here to help take care of our aging mother. My sister and I split the rent and utilities, and we both take turns grocery shopping at the local supermarket, hunting for the best deals on fresh produce and meat.  

I have two adult kids—one lives in Maryland and the other serves in the Coast Guard—so I get occasional video calls, but most of my evenings are spent watching a bit of TV, knitting, or helping my mom with medication reminders. On Sundays I attend a small Native American cultural gathering at the community center, which keeps me connected to my heritage and gives me a chance to catch up with friends. I drive a used sedan that I’ve had for six years; it’s reliable enough for the occasional trip to the beach in the summer or a quick drive to the state fair in the fall.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Between my long work hours, caring for my mother, and feeling that the candidates were all the same—promising change but delivering more of the same—I just didn’t see a point in lining up at the polls. The whole process felt like a circus, and the ads were so negative that I stopped paying attention altogether. I’m pretty sure I’ll sit this one out too, unless something dramatic hits my neighborhood—like a major hospital closure or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces me to move.  

My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the uncertainty around my health insurance coverage. My mom’s medical bills are already a strain, and any change to Medicaid or Medicare feels like a personal crisis. I also worry about the local job market; the clinic’s funding has been shaky, and I’ve heard rumors about possible layoffs.  

When I think about the two major parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Republicans, in my view, talk a lot about “family values” but vote for policies that make health care less affordable for people like my mom. The Democrats claim to care about working families, yet the bureaucracy at the clinic feels just as tangled and inefficient as before. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than actually solving the problems that affect my day‑to‑day life.

## Media Diet
