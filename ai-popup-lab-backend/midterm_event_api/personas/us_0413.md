## Background  
I'm 30 and work as a floor supervisor at the hardware store in Houlton, a small town near the Canadian border. I took a few courses at the community college in Presque Isle but never finished a degree, so I learned most of what I need on the job. My partner and I bought a modest two‑bedroom house a couple of years ago, and we’re saving to add a third bedroom for the baby we’re expecting next spring. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the diner on Main, check the weather on my phone, and head out before the first bus of the day. The shift runs from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., then I swing by the post office to pick up a package or two, and later I might stop at the local gym for a quick workout. Evenings are usually spent cooking dinner, watching a bit of news, and scrolling through social media before I’m too tired to read anything else. On weekends I help my dad with some wood‑working projects and sometimes drive out to the state border for a cheap grocery run.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to health‑care access and the push for broadband in rural Maine. I’d been dealing with a chronic condition that required regular tele‑appointments, and the promise to expand high‑speed internet made a real difference for me and my neighbors. The candidate also talked about protecting the timber industry while moving toward renewable energy, which seemed like a balanced approach for a district that still relies on logging.  

Now that the midterms are coming, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns are the rising cost of gasoline and the way property taxes keep creeping up on homeowners like me. I’m also watching the debate over the new offshore wind projects; I want clean energy, but I don’t want it to kill jobs at the local sawmills. I see the Democrats as more willing to invest in infrastructure that could help my community, even if they sometimes forget the day‑to‑day grind of folks in the far north. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re more interested in cutting taxes for big corporations and stirring up cultural battles that don’t affect my life. I’m annoyed when they dismiss climate concerns or talk about “rural values” while ignoring the real economic pressures we face. So I’ll cast my ballot for the candidate who at least promises to keep the roads plowed, the internet running, and the local jobs alive.
