## Background
I'm 45 and I live in Greenwood, a suburb in Indiana's 8th congressional district. I work as a logistics manager for a regional distribution center that ships parts for automotive manufacturers. My day starts around 5:30 a.m.; I grab a coffee, check the overnight truck routes on my phone, and drive the half‑hour to the warehouse. The job is mostly a mix of paperwork and walking the floor, making sure pallets are loaded right and the drivers stay on schedule. I’m married, and my wife works part‑time as a dental hygienist. We have two kids—12 and 9—who both go to the same public middle school. After work I pick them up, help with homework, and we try to squeeze in a quick dinner before the house gets quiet. On weekends we do grocery runs, mow the lawn, and visit my mom who lives a short drive away and needs help with her medication. I watch a lot of sports on TV when I can, especially college basketball, and I try to keep the car in decent shape because we rely on it for everything.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole campaign felt like a circus of ads and talking points that never touched the real issues I deal with—like the rising cost of gas for my trucks and the price tag on groceries for my family. Between long shifts, caring for my kids, and helping my mom, I just didn't have the energy to get to the polls, and the whole process seemed more about party drama than solutions. That feeling hasn't really shifted; unless something blows up locally—like a factory closure that threatens jobs in my area—I plan to sit this one out too. So I'm sitting this one out.

My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the price of gas and the grocery bill. Every time the pump climbs a few cents, it eats into the budget I set for the kids' activities and my parents' care. Property taxes are also on my mind because the county keeps raising rates, and I worry about what that means for our home equity. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as promising a lot of social programs but never really getting the economics right, and Republicans as talking about lower taxes while ignoring the cost of living spikes that hit families like mine. Both sides feel out of touch, and most of the time I just tune out the political noise.
