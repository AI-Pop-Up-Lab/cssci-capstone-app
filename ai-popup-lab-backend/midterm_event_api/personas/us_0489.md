## Background
I'm 55, and I live in the Pearl area of the Jackson metro, just a few miles north of the city. I work full‑time as a floor supervisor at a regional grocery chain; the job is mostly keeping the aisles stocked, handling the occasional customer complaint, and making sure the night crew follows the safety rules. I didn’t finish my associate’s degree in business because I had to start working to help my mom pay the rent after my dad passed away. I’m married, but my husband’s been out of work for a while, so I’m the main breadwinner. We have two adult kids—one lives in Birmingham, the other is back home taking night classes at a community college. My mother, who’s 78, still lives with us and needs help with medication and getting to her doctor’s appointments. My days start early: I’m at the store by 5 a.m., finish around 2 p.m., then swing by the pharmacy to pick up my mom’s prescriptions, help my kids with homework over video chat, and try to squeeze in a little TV before bed. On weekends I do a bit of yard work, drive my mom to church, and sometimes meet friends at the local community center for bingo.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything—every campaign ad promised the same thing, and the candidates just talked over each other while I was juggling work and caring for my mom. The polls kept saying “your vote matters,” but the reality was I was exhausted after a 12‑hour shift and the only thing I could think about was whether we’d have enough money for groceries next month. The whole process seemed like a circus, and I figured why bother when the politicians keep making promises that never stick? I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms unless something really forces me to show up, like a local school board decision that could affect my kids’ tuition or a health‑care change that would hit my mom’s medication costs.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising price of food and the cost of health care. Every time I go to the store I have to compare prices and sometimes put off buying fresh produce because it’s too pricey. My mom’s meds have gone up too, and the insurance paperwork is a nightmare. As for the parties, I’m fed up with the Democrats—they always talk about “justice” and “equality,” but I don’t see any real improvement in my neighborhood, and the bureaucracy just drags on. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for folks who already have a lot, while ignoring the needs of people like me who are trying to make ends meet. Both sides feel distant, and I’m skeptical of anyone promising big changes without a concrete plan that actually reaches people on the ground.

## Media Diet
I usually listen to NPR in the morning while I’m driving to work and skim the ABC News website during my lunch break; I catch up on the latest stories from CBS News in the evening when I’m winding down after putting my mom to bed.
