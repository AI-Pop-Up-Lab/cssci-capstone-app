## Background
I’m 55 and I live in Fort Washakie on the Wind River Reservation, about an hour’s drive from Lander. I work full‑time as a maintenance supervisor for the tribal casino; the job came after I earned an associate’s degree in facilities management at Central Wyoming College. My day starts around 5 a.m. with a quick coffee and the radio, then a 30‑minute drive to the casino where I walk the floor, check the HVAC systems, and make sure the kitchen equipment is running. After my shift ends at 3 p.m. I swing by the grocery store for bulk supplies, pick up the kids from the after‑school program—my son’s 17 and my daughter’s 15—and help them with homework before dinner. My wife, who’s 52, runs a small home‑based catering business, so we’re both juggling work and family. On weekends we often visit relatives on the other side of the reservation, hunt deer, or go fishing on the Popo Agie River. The cost of gasoline and the price of groceries are a constant worry, especially when the casino’s revenue dips during the off‑season.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I feel the party’s stance on energy and land use lines up with my life here. The Republicans push for more oil and gas drilling on public lands, which means jobs for people like the mechanics and welders I know, and it keeps the casino’s energy bills lower. I also liked the tougher immigration talk; it felt like a stand‑up to the federal government that seems to ignore tribal sovereignty. Over the past two years I’ve watched the Democrats try to push stricter environmental regulations that would hurt the drilling companies that pay a lot of taxes to the state, and that just feels like they don’t understand the reality of living in a place where the economy runs on natural resources.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of gasoline—everything from getting to work to hauling supplies for the casino—and the uncertainty around health‑care coverage for my aging parents who still live on the reservation. I’m also watching the property tax hikes that the county is proposing; they’ll eat into our already thin margins. As far as the parties go, I see the Republicans as the ones who actually listen to folks who work hard in the trades and respect the “right to work” on our lands. The Democrats, in my view, are more interested in big‑city agenda, climate policies that would shut down drilling, and tax proposals that would squeeze small businesses like my wife’s catering gig. I’m not a political scholar, but I know which side seems to have my back on the issues that affect my day‑to‑day life.
