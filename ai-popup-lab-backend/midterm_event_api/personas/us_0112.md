## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I just felt like the whole thing was a circus—both parties spouting the same promises while the real problems—like my kids' school funding or my wife's erratic schedule—went unnoticed. Between my long shifts, picking up extra gigs, and juggling the kids' needs, I never found the time or the energy to get to a polling place, and I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything. The news I do watch mostly repeats the same talking points, so I got even more convinced that the system is set up for the same old faces. I'm sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms unless something blows up locally—like a sudden closure of the warehouse or a big price hike on basic groceries—that forces me to take a stand. My everyday worries are the rent that keeps creeping up and the grocery bills that never seem to shrink, plus the uncertainty about whether my kids will have stable jobs after high school. When I think about the parties, the Democrats come across as high‑falutin, always talking about climate goals and student debt while ignoring the day‑to‑day grind of people like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to focus on tax cuts and law‑and‑order, which sounds good on paper, but I don't trust the rhetoric about “big government” not caring about small‑town folks. Bottom line: I'm fed up with the noise, and unless a candidate actually talks about keeping my rent affordable or making groceries cheaper, I'm not going to bother voting.
