## Background  
I'm 40, and I live in Lithonia, a suburb on the east side of Atlanta in Georgia’s 6th district. I work full‑time as a medical assistant at a community health clinic about 15 minutes away. My associate’s degree in health sciences helped me get the job after I finished a two‑year program at a technical college in Decatur. I’m married to a truck driver, and we have two kids—Jaden, 10, and Maya, 7. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m. to make breakfast, get the kids ready for school, and drop them off at the elementary school a few miles north. I commute to the clinic, sometimes taking the MARTA bus when traffic is bad. My husband works nights, so I’m usually home by 6 p.m., help with homework, and we all eat dinner together around 7. On weekends I’m either grocery shopping at the local Walmart, attending the Baptist church service on Sunday, or helping Jaden with his baseball practice. I keep a small garden in the backyard—tomatoes and okra—that gives us a little fresh produce and a reason to get outside. Money is tight; we’re still paying off the kids’ school supplies and the house’s roof repair from a few years back.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to the health‑care issues that affect my patients and my own family. The clinic was understaffed, and the Democratic platform promised to expand Medicaid and fund community health centers, which mattered to me on a daily basis. I also liked the candidate’s stance on criminal‑justice reform; a friend of mine was recently released from prison after a wrongful conviction, and I saw the Democrats pushing for better legal aid. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for protecting the social safety net.  

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—“I’m voting.” My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the uncertainty around my husband’s job as fuel prices swing. I also worry about the quality of the public schools; the district’s budget cuts have meant larger class sizes, and I’m nervous about what that does to Maya’s education. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like the ones trying to keep health care affordable and protect workers like my husband, even if they’re sometimes sloppy on the details. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that keep clinics like mine funded. I’m not a political scholar, but I get frustrated when I hear talk of “trickle‑down economics” that never seems to reach people like us. Overall, I’m leaning heavily toward supporting candidates who will keep Medicaid strong and fight for better school funding.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR’s morning news roundup while I’m making coffee, catch the ABC News evening update after dinner, and skim CNBC’s market brief on the weekend to see how the economy is doing. All together that’s about six hours a week.
