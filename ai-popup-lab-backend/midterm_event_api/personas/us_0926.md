## Background  
I'm 45 and work as a middle‑school Spanish teacher in Bellevue, Washington. I grew up in a bilingual household in Spokane, so I still switch between English and Spanish at home with my husband and our two kids—15‑year‑old Maya and 12‑year‑old Luis. My husband drives a delivery route for a local grocery chain, and we both take the light‑rail to the school district office a few times a week for meetings. My days start early: I drop the kids off at a charter school, sip a coffee while scrolling through the morning news feed on my phone, then head to the classroom where I teach language arts and coordinate the after‑school tutoring program. After school I stay for a few hours to grade papers, then we all eat dinner together—usually tacos or something quick from the nearby food court. Weekends are a mix of soccer games for Luis, Maya's piano recitals, and grocery trips at the big box store where I try to catch the weekly sales. I’m active in the local PTA and occasionally volunteer at the community center teaching adult ESL classes. I don’t own a car, so I rely on the commuter rail and rideshares when we need to go farther.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party cared most about public schools, health care access, and climate action—issues that hit close to home both at work and in my neighborhood where rents keep climbing. I was also motivated by the candidate’s promise to protect funding for bilingual education, which directly affects my students. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still frustrated by the slow pace on climate legislation, I think the Democrats are at least trying to move the needle. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out because the Senate race could tip the balance on a few key education bills I’m lobbying for. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of housing—rent has jumped a lot in the last two years—and the price of groceries, especially fresh produce. I also worry about traffic congestion on the I‑90 corridor, which makes my commute and the kids’ after‑school activities a nightmare. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like the only side that cares about public services, even if they’re sometimes too cautious. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem out of touch with the realities of a growing, diverse community like ours; their focus on tax cuts feels like it benefits people who already have a lot, not folks trying to make ends meet.
