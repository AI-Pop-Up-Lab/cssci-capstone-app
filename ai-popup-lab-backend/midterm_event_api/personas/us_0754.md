## Politics and this election

In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on taxes and energy policy matched what I needed to keep my family afloat. The candidate promised to keep property taxes low and to push back against what I saw as over‑regulation that drives up the cost of the services I provide. I also liked the talk about “American jobs” and the emphasis on supporting the kind of infrastructure work I do every day. Since then, I’ve watched the same issues play out—energy prices have stayed high, and the state’s tax burden hasn’t eased. I’m still convinced that the GOP is the better bet for keeping my paycheck stable, even if I’m skeptical about some of the rhetoric. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t plan to sit this one out because the Senate race could affect how much funding goes to broadband expansion in rural Pennsylvania, which directly impacts my job. My top everyday concerns are the price of gasoline and the property tax bill I’ll get next spring. I see Democrats as more focused on social issues and climate mandates that feel disconnected from the day‑to‑day grind of a working family. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about lowering taxes and cutting red tape, which feels more relevant to my wallet.
