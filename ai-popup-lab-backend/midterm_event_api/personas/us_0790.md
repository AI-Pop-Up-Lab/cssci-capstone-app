## Background  
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a sales associate at a big‑box home‑goods store in Columbia, South Carolina. I grew up in a mixed‑heritage household; my mom is from the Caribbean and my dad is white, so the holidays are a mash‑up of food and music. After high school I went to a community college for a couple of years, took a few business and communications classes, but never finished a degree because I needed to start earning right away.  

I rent a one‑bedroom apartment a few blocks from the university district with a roommate I met through a gig‑economy app. We split rent, utilities, and the occasional pizza night. My mornings are a blur of a cheap coffee from the corner deli, scrolling through a news feed while the bus rumbles past the state capitol, then a shift that starts at 9 a.m. and runs until about 5 p.m. I usually grab a quick lunch at the food court, then head home to do laundry, pay bills, and sometimes help my younger sister with her online school work—she lives with our parents a short drive away. Evenings are when I unwind: I binge‑watch a sitcom on a streaming service, scroll TikTok, or meet a friend for a cheap taco at a late‑night spot. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a bit of freelance data entry to pad my paycheck, and occasional hikes in the nearby parks when the weather’s nice.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about someone like me who’s just trying to make rent and keep a job. The ads were all about big‑ticket issues—foreign policy, climate treaties—while I’m worrying whether the grocery store will raise prices again next month. My schedule was packed with extra shifts during the election week, and I didn’t even get a reminder from anyone I trusted. I’ve got a sort of “why bother?” attitude that’s stuck around; unless something hits close to home—like a local school closing or a sudden rent hike—I’m likely to sit out the 2026 midterms too.  

My top everyday concerns are staying afloat on my paycheck and keeping food on the table. The rent is already a big chunk of my income, and the price of basic groceries keeps creeping up. I’m also fed up with the way both parties seem to talk past each other. The Republicans feel like they’re stuck in the past, pushing policies that don’t help a young worker in a city that’s trying to grow. The Democrats say they’re progressive, but I see the same old promises about “affordable housing” that never get funded. I’m not a hardcore partisan; I just feel like the political system is a big, noisy machine that doesn’t move me forward. If a candidate or a ballot measure directly addressed rent control or a real plan to lower grocery taxes, I might actually go to the polls, but right now I’m just watching from the sidelines.
