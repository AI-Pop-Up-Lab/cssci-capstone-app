## Background
I'm 65, and I live on a modest ranch just outside Riverton, Wyoming, in the big‑ticket WY‑1 district. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I took a job loading freight at the local grain elevator, then moved into ranch work full‑time. These days I’m a part‑time hand‑yman for a few neighbors—fixing fences, helping with winter snow removal, and doing the occasional odd job that pays cash. My wife passed away five years ago, so the house is just me and a big‑bone Labrador named Duke. My two kids live in Denver and Salt Lake City, so I get a call from them once a week, mostly about their jobs and the occasional family gathering they want me to attend.

My day starts before sunrise. I feed the cattle, check the water troughs, and haul hay for the winter pile. In the summer I’m out mending the irrigation lines, and in the winter I’m shoveling snow from the driveway and the barn doors. After lunch I might drive into town to pick up groceries, stop at the post office, or swing by the feed store. Evenings are quiet: I watch a couple of news clips on the TV, read the paper if I have one left, and then go to bed early because the next day starts the same way.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about folks like me who are just trying to keep a roof over our heads and a herd alive. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I was busy with a broken fence and a sick cow that needed fixing. I also figured my single vote wouldn't move the needle in a state that’s already solidly red, so I just stayed home. That feeling hasn't changed much. I’m sitting this one out in the 2026 midterms unless something blows up locally—maybe a big cut to water rights or a sudden hike in property taxes that hits my pocket directly.

My biggest worries right now are the rising price of diesel for the tractor and the cost of veterinary care for the herd. The next thing on my mind is the state’s property tax assessment; I’ve heard the county is looking at raising it, and that would bite hard. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats; they keep pushing climate regulations that feel like they ignore the real life of a rancher. The Republicans seem more in tune with my world, at least on the surface, but I’m still skeptical because they sometimes talk big and deliver little when it comes to the actual cash I need for equipment and land upkeep.
