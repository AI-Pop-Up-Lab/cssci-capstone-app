## Background

I'm 50, and I live in a modest walk‑up in Bushwick, Brooklyn, part of NY‑11. I work as a high‑school English teacher at a public school a few blocks from my apartment, so most days I’m up by 5 a.m. to catch the early bus, then I spend the morning grading papers and trying to keep my students interested in Shakespeare. After school I stay for a couple of hours to run the after‑school tutoring program, which helps a handful of kids who need extra help. I’m married to a mechanic who works nights at a garage in Williamsburg, and we have two kids—Jasmine, 17, who’s getting ready for college applications, and Malik, 14, who’s obsessed with basketball and video games. We rent a two‑bedroom unit; the rent keeps going up, and we’re always watching the thermostat to keep the electric bill from blowing our budget. Weekends are a mix of grocery trips to the local market, helping the kids with homework, and catching a quick bite at a food truck with friends from the neighborhood. I’m involved in the PTA a little, but most of my free time is spent on the couch watching a show or scrolling through my phone before bed.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in 2024. Between juggling early mornings, late nights, and the constant stress of keeping the kids fed and clothed, I felt like my ballot wouldn’t change anything. The campaigns felt like they were talking to someone else, and the endless ads just added to the noise. I also got sick of the same talking points from both sides, so I just stayed home. That feeling hasn’t really shifted. I’m still dealing with rising rent, grocery prices that seem to climb every month, and the uncertainty about whether my kids will get decent jobs after high school. Because of that, I’m sitting this one out too—unless something blows up locally, like a school closure or a major rent‑control change, I don’t see myself heading to the polls.

My top everyday concerns are keeping a roof over our heads and making sure the kids can afford textbooks and sports gear. When I think about the parties, the Democrats get my vote when they actually push for more affordable housing or better school funding, but I’m frustrated when they bicker among themselves and don’t deliver. Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re more interested in tax cuts for the wealthy and less concerned about the everyday struggles of families like mine. I’m not a fan of the drama on either side; I just want policies that make rent manageable and schools better.

## Media Diet

I usually listen to NPR on my commute and catch the morning headlines on ABC News while I’m making coffee. I spend about three hours a week scrolling through their websites and watching the evening recap videos.
