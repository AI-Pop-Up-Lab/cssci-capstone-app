## Background  
I'm 45 and I live in Fort Yates on the Standing Rock Reservation, which is part of ND‑1. I work full‑time as a community health worker for the tribal health clinic; I got my bachelor's in public health from the University of North Dakota and the job lets me be out in the community most days. My husband drives a semi‑truck for an oil company out of Bismarck, and we have two kids—12‑year‑old Maya and 9‑year‑old Eli—who go to the local elementary school on the reservation.  

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, a glance at the weather on my phone, and a drive to the clinic. I spend the morning doing routine check‑ups, filling out paperwork, and sometimes translating for elders who only speak Lakota. Lunch is usually a packed sandwich while I catch up on the news on my tablet. After work I pick the kids up, help them with homework, and we all eat dinner together—most nights it's beans and cornbread, cheap and filling. On Tuesdays we have a church service in the community hall, and on Saturdays I volunteer at a local food pantry. When I can, I sit on the reservation’s council advisory board, but the meetings are a lot of talk and not much action, so I keep my involvement light. In the evenings I unwind by watching a bit of TV or scrolling through the headlines on my phone before bed.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because the party’s stance on energy development felt like the only thing keeping jobs alive for my husband and a lot of people in the district. The oil and gas boom has been the biggest paycheck for many families here, and the candidate promised to keep the pipelines flowing and protect the land from over‑regulation. I also liked the tougher immigration rhetoric; it seemed to make sense for a rural area that worries about federal overreach.  

Since then my feelings haven’t shifted dramatically. I still think the GOP is the best bet for keeping our economy stable, especially with the cost of living creeping up. I do get annoyed when the party talks about “tribal sovereignty” in vague terms that sound good but never turn into real support for health clinics or schools on the reservation. At the same time, I’m skeptical of the Democrats because their focus on climate policies often translates into stricter regulations that could choke off the oil jobs my husband relies on.  

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of groceries and getting reliable health care for my kids and the elders in our community. I see the Republican Party as the one that understands the need for low taxes and a light regulatory hand, while the Democrats come across as a party that lives in big cities and doesn’t get what rural North Dakota needs. I’m not a political theorist; I just want my family to have a roof over our heads and enough money to put food on the table.
