## Background  
I'm 39 and I work the night shift as a forklift operator at the distribution center just outside Lafayette. My husband drives a school bus, and we have two kids—one in eighth grade and the other in second. Our house is a modest two‑bedroom bungalow in a quiet cul‑de‑sac on the edge of town; we keep a small garden in the front yard that helps stretch the grocery budget.  

Most mornings I’m up before the kids, making coffee and packing lunches while the house is still quiet. After dropping the younger one off at the elementary school, I head to the warehouse, where the shift runs from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. The night is mostly moving pallets, checking inventory, and trying not to get too exhausted to be present for bedtime stories. When I get home, I’m usually too tired for anything but a quick shower, a bite of pizza, and a few minutes scrolling through the news on my phone before I crawl into bed. On weekends I try to catch up on household chores, take the kids to soccer practice, and run errands at the local grocery store, which has gotten noticeably pricier over the last couple of years.  

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the night shifts, the kids’ school schedules, and the feeling that none of the candidates really understood the grind of a working‑class family, I just never found the time or the motivation. The campaign ads felt like the same old promises, and I was convinced my single vote wouldn’t change anything. That same sense of weariness is still with me, so I’m sitting this one out too. If there were a local issue that hit my family directly—like a proposed tax hike that would push my mortgage over the edge, or a factory closure that threatened my job—I’d probably reconsider, but nothing has come up that feels urgent enough right now.  

My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the price of gas. Every time I fill up the truck that takes me to work, I see the meter climb higher, and a trip to the supermarket feels like a small financial battle. I also worry about property taxes; the county has been talking about raising rates to fund road repairs, and that would tighten the budget even more.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to focus on big‑city issues and climate policies that feel far removed from my reality, while the Republicans keep talking about culture wars and “law and order” without addressing the paycheck pressures that keep me up at night. Neither side seems to have a concrete plan for making life a little easier for folks like me who are just trying to keep the lights on and the kids fed.

## Media Diet  
I usually catch the morning headlines on CBS while I’m making coffee, then scroll through ABC and NBC news feeds during my break at work. In the evenings after the kids are asleep, I watch a half‑hour of CNBC to see how the markets are doing, adding up to about five hours a week total.
