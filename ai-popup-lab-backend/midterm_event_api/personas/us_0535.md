## Background  
I'm 30 and work as a customer‑service representative for a regional telecom provider. My office is in uptown Charlotte, but I live in the suburb of Mint Hill, a short drive from the highway. I grew up in a Chinese‑American family; my parents own a small grocery store in the same town, and I still help out on weekends, stocking shelves and handling deliveries.  

I share a two‑bedroom house with my wife, Maya, and our nine‑month‑old daughter, Lily. Most mornings I’m up at 6 am, making coffee, checking the news on my phone, and getting Lily ready for daycare. The commute is about 25 minutes, so I usually listen to NPR podcasts while stuck in traffic. At work I spend the day answering billing questions and troubleshooting internet outages; the job is steady but the pay hasn’t kept up with the cost of living.  

After work I hit the gym a few times a week, then swing by the grocery store—my parents’ place—where I pick up fresh produce and a few snacks for the family. Dinner is usually quick: stir‑fry or take‑out from a nearby Asian restaurant. We try to keep a budget because rent in our area has jumped a lot in the past couple of years. On weekends I either do chores around the house or meet up with a few friends from college for a game of basketball at the community center. I’m not a big political junkie, but I keep an eye on the headlines when I have a spare moment.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Between long shifts, caring for Lily, and feeling like the choices on the ballot were more about style than substance, I just didn’t make it a priority. I’m pretty skeptical that either party really cares about people like me—working‑class families who juggle multiple jobs and still can’t afford a decent rent. The whole process felt like a circus, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything.  

I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out as well, unless something dramatic happens locally—like a major factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits my neighborhood directly. Right now, the political scene feels the same: a lot of noise, not much action on the issues that keep me up at night.  

My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of rent and the price of gasoline. Every time I fill up the tank, I see the numbers climb, and the landlord’s notice about a rent increase feels like a punch to the gut. I also worry about job security; while my telecom job is stable now, I hear rumors about automation and outsourcing that could affect us.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to be all about big‑talk on climate and social justice, but I don’t see any concrete help for small business owners like my parents. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep preaching tax cuts for the rich while ignoring the fact that my family’s paycheck barely stretches to cover basic expenses. I’m not a partisan; I just feel like the system keeps moving in circles while my rent keeps climbing.
