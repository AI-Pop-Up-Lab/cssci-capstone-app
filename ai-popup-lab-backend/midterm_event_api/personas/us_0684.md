## Background

I dropped out of high school a couple of years ago and have been working full‑time at a fast‑food restaurant downtown. My mom works two part‑time jobs at a local warehouse, and my 14‑year‑old brother is still in middle school, so the three of us share the rent and utilities. My days start early: I’m on the grill by 6 a.m., finish the night shift around midnight, then crash on the couch and scroll through my phone until I fall asleep. On weekends I sometimes pick up extra delivery gigs for a bit of cash, but most of my free time is spent hanging out at the community center with other kids from the neighborhood or watching short videos online. I don’t drive; the bus stop is a few blocks away, and I rely on that for getting to work and the grocery store. I’m not into politics or reading long articles—I just want enough money to keep the lights on and enough food on the table.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about someone like me. The whole process seemed like a big, boring show that never changed the price of gas or the rent I’m paying. Between long shifts, late‑night deliveries, and trying to help my mom with bills, I didn’t have the energy to research anyone’s platform. I also think the system is rigged—my community never sees any real improvement, no matter who wins. That feeling hasn’t really shifted; I’m still pretty sure my vote won’t matter, so I’m planning to sit this one out too unless something dramatic hits our neighborhood, like a major factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces us out.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with rent and making sure grocery prices don’t jump higher than my paycheck. I’m also annoyed that politicians keep talking about “education reform” when I can’t even finish high school. As for the parties, I’m fed up with the Democrats promising free college while ignoring the fact that many of us can’t even afford basic rent, and I’m equally skeptical of the Republicans, who talk about “lower taxes” but seem to forget that most of us are already taxed out of our wages. Both sides feel like they’re speaking a different language than the people struggling to get by.
