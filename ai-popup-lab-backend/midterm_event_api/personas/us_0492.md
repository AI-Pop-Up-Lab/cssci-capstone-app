## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about someone like me who’s just trying to make ends meet. The ballot looked the same as every other year: big‑name politicians promising “change” while I’m still waiting for a raise at the grocery store. My mom tried to push me to the polling place, but I was exhausted after a double shift and the early morning line at the county clerk’s office. I figured my single vote wouldn’t tip the scales, and I didn’t want to waste a day that could be spent looking for extra gigs.

I’m sitting this one out again in 2026. Nothing in the news or around town has convinced me that the system is any different. If there were a local controversy that directly threatened my family’s rent or my sister’s school, maybe I’d show up, but the national headlines just feel like noise. My biggest worries are keeping a steady job and making sure we can afford groceries and the rent that eats most of our paycheck. The political parties both feel like they’re talking past each other: the Democrats seem to think they’re fixing everything with big‑scale programs that never reach a small town like ours, and the Republicans keep talking about “values” and “tax cuts” while the local economy stays stuck. I’m not a fan of either side, but I’m more annoyed with the Democrats because they never seem to understand what life is like for people who never finished high school and are working minimum‑wage jobs. I guess I’m hoping someone will finally show up with a real plan for job training and affordable housing; until then, I’m just going to stay home on Election Day.
