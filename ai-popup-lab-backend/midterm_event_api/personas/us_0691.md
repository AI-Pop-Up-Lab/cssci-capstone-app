## Background  
I'm 30 and I work as an automotive service technician at a dealership just outside Hamilton. I got an associate's degree in automotive technology from a community college, then landed the job straight out of school. My partner and I bought a modest two‑bedroom house in a neighborhood that used to be nicer before the factory closures, and we have a five‑year‑old son who goes to the local elementary school.  

Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., grab a quick coffee, and drive the 15‑minute commute to the shop. The work is hands‑on, fixing engines, doing diagnostics, and sometimes staying late when a customer’s car breaks down on the highway. After my shift I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and try to help my son with his homework before my partner gets home from her shift at the hospital. On weekends I’m either at the church basketball league or doing yard work around the house; we don’t have a lot of extra cash, so I try to keep the house in good shape myself. When I can, I watch a couple of games on TV with the guys at the local bar, but most evenings are spent scrolling through my phone or listening to a news podcast while I clean up after dinner.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between double‑shifts at the shop, my partner’s long hours, and trying to keep a roof over our heads, the ballot just felt like another thing I didn’t have time for. I also feel like the candidates never really talked about the problems that hit us in the day‑to‑day—rising utility bills, the lack of decent jobs after the plant closures, and the way schools are underfunded. Both parties seemed to be shouting at each other while the neighborhood’s streets stayed pothole‑filled and the community center lost its funding. That kind of cynicism makes me think my vote won’t change anything, so I’m sitting this one out too, unless something hits close to home, like a local school closing or a big rent hike that forces us out.

I’m not planning to vote in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and making sure my son’s school stays open and safe. The political parties just feel distant. The Democrats keep promising police reform and more funding for schools, but I rarely see those promises turn into real money for our district. The Republicans talk about cutting taxes and bringing back factories, but they never address the systemic issues that affect Black families in places like mine. I’m annoyed that both sides act like they’re looking out for the “average American,” when most of the time I’m just trying to get to work on time and put food on the table.

## Media Diet  
I start my day with an NPR news podcast while I’m driving, then catch the CBS evening news after work, and I check ABC News headlines on my phone before I go to bed. All together it’s about six hours a week.
