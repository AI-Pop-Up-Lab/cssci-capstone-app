## Background

I'm 75, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Dayton, in the part of the district that still feels the echo of the old auto plants. I worked most of my adult life on the assembly line at a plant that closed in the early 2000s, and I took a few night classes at a community college but never finished a degree. Since retiring five years ago, I spend my mornings making coffee, reading the local paper, and checking the headlines on my tablet. My wife passed away three years ago, so most evenings are quiet, except for the occasional video call with my daughter who lives in Columbus and her two kids who call me “Grandpa” and ask for help with their homework.

I get up around 7 a.m., take a short walk around the neighborhood, then sit on the porch and watch the news. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I go to the senior center for a bingo game and a potluck; the folks there are a mix of retirees, a few former teachers, and a handful of folks who still work part‑time at the local grocery store. I keep a small garden in the back yard—tomatoes, beans, a couple of peppers—mostly to stretch the grocery budget. My health is decent; I take a couple of pills for blood pressure and try not to miss my yearly flu shot. When I'm not at the center, I'm either fixing things around the house or digging through old photo albums that my mother left me.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about people like me who spent their lives working with their hands. The tax cuts they promised sounded like a chance to keep more of the money I earned, and the talk about “law and order” resonated after I saw the rise in crime around the old industrial zones. I also liked the emphasis on supporting veterans; my brother served in Vietnam and never got the benefits he deserved, so I appreciated the push for better VA services.

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. The biggest thing on my mind is the price of my prescription drugs—my pharmacist told me the costs have gone up again, and I can't afford to cut back on the medication that keeps my blood pressure in check. The other worry is the property tax hike the county council is proposing to fund a new sports complex; I own my house outright, and every extra dollar feels like a hit to the little savings I have left. I think the Republican Party still gets the basics right: lower taxes, a focus on personal responsibility, and a respect for the kind of work ethic that built this country. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising big government fixes that end up costing more for folks like me, and they talk about climate policies that sound good on paper but mean higher utility bills for retirees on fixed incomes.

## Media Diet

I watch the morning broadcast on CBS and catch the evening wrap‑up on NBC, and I skim articles on Breitbart when I have a spare half hour after dinner. All together it adds up to about ten hours a week, split between my tablet and the TV.
