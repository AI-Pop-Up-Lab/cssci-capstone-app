## Background
I'm 20 and I go to school at the University of New Hampshire in Durham, which is in the 1st congressional district. I grew up in a bilingual household; my parents came here from Mexico when I was a kid, so I speak Spanish at home and English at school. I live in a small off‑campus apartment with two other students—one from Maine and one from Ohio. We split rent, utilities, and the occasional pizza order. My major is communications, and I spend most of my weekdays in classes, labs, and group projects. I work about 12 hours a week as a barista at a coffee shop downtown, mostly to cover my textbook costs and a little extra for groceries. Mornings usually start with a quick shower, a bowl of cereal, and a scroll through my phone before catching the bus to campus. After lectures I either head to the coffee shop or the library, then come back to the apartment to cook dinner, binge‑watch a show, or help a roommate with Spanish homework. Weekends are a mix of studying, occasional hikes in the White Mountains, and hanging out at the local farmers market where I pick up fresh produce and sometimes sell homemade tamales. I’m not married, and I don’t have kids, but I do have a tight‑knit circle of friends who are mostly other students or part‑time workers.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates were speaking to people my age. Between juggling classes, a job, and trying to keep my grades up, I just didn’t have the energy to get to the polls, and the whole process seemed like a distant thing that wouldn’t change my rent or tuition. I’ve also gotten a bit jaded by the constant promises that never seem to translate into cheaper textbooks or more affordable housing for students. I’m planning to sit this one out too, unless something really forces my hand—like a major cut to student aid or a local scandal that hits my community directly. My biggest everyday worries are paying for rent and keeping up with grocery prices, especially since a lot of the food at the market is getting pricier. I also worry about my student loans looming over me after graduation. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as talking a lot about climate and immigration, but I don’t see concrete steps that help a kid trying to finish college without drowning in debt. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to ignore the reality of being an immigrant family and focus on issues that feel far removed from my daily life. Both sides feel like they’re playing a game that leaves people like me on the sidelines.
