## Background  
I'm 65 and I live in Erwin, a little town in the 1st congressional district of Tennessee. I work part‑time at the local grocery store, stocking shelves and helping customers find what they need. I never finished high school; after I got married at 18 I started working to help support my family and never went back. My husband passed away five years ago, and now I split my time between the store, caring for my 92‑year‑old mother who lives with me, and looking after my two grown‑up children when they come to visit. My daughter lives in Atlanta and calls once a week; my son is retired and lives nearby, so we have family meals on Sundays.  

Most mornings I get up around 5:30 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and read the newspaper headlines on my tablet while the kids are still sleeping. I head to the store at 7 a.m., work a shift until about noon, then come home for lunch and a quick walk around the neighborhood. In the afternoons I either run errands, tend to my mom’s medication schedule, or sit in the community center’s bingo hall with a few other seniors. Evening is usually TV time—news, a bit of drama, and sometimes a Korean drama I stream on my phone. On Saturdays I volunteer at the church kitchen, and Sundays are mostly church and a family dinner. My biggest worries day‑to‑day are keeping up with my mom’s health needs and making sure there’s enough money left after the grocery bills and the property tax bill that comes every spring.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same old things, and I was exhausted from a year of caring for my mother and juggling my job. The ballot felt like a chore, and the polling place was a half‑hour drive away, which I wasn’t willing to add to my schedule. I also thought, “What difference does my vote make?” so I stayed home.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something really hits close to home—like a local hospital closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits my fixed income—I’m not planning to go to the polls. My top everyday concerns are prescription drug prices and the rising cost of groceries. I’m also watching the property tax bill; it feels like they keep squeezing people who are already on a tight budget.  

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Democrats. They keep talking about “universal health care” but I never see any real help for seniors like me on the ground. The Republicans seem to care more about tax cuts for big businesses than about seniors’ fixed incomes, but at least they talk about “lower taxes” and that feels a little more relevant to my wallet. Overall, I’m cynical about both sides; I wish someone would just focus on fixing the basics—affordable meds, steady utility rates, and keeping community services alive.
