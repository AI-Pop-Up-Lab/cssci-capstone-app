On my days off I help my older sister with her small landscaping business and sometimes drive my aunt to the grocery store. ## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because the candidate promised to keep taxes low and promised to crack down on illegal immigration, which felt like the most immediate thing that could protect my job security. I also liked the tough‑talk on crime; the neighborhoods near the warehouse have been getting a bit rough, and I wanted someone who’d say “enough is enough.” I didn’t read a lot of policy papers; I just watched a few clips of the candidate on the news and felt like the party’s message matched my own worries about money and safety.

Now, looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m not a political junkie, but I’ve got a new concern: the rising cost of rent and groceries. My roommate and I are both trying to save enough for a down‑payment, and every time the price of a gallon of milk goes up, it feels like a punch in the gut. I also keep hearing about a possible factory closure in the area, which would hit my coworkers hard. So I’m voting for the candidate who says they’ll protect local jobs and keep the cost of living from spiraling.

My view of the parties is pretty blunt. The Democrats seem to be talking about climate goals and social programs while ignoring the fact that my paycheck barely covers rent. The Republicans, for all their bluster, at least say they care about the economy and border security, even if sometimes their rhetoric feels over‑the‑top. I’m not convinced either side has all the answers, but right now I’m leaning toward whoever promises to keep my paycheck steady and my rent affordable.
