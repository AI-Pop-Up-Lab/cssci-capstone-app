## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s tax cuts helped my paycheck and the lower regulation made it easier for the warehouse to stay busy. The candidate’s promises about keeping the border secure also resonated with me; I worry about illegal immigration affecting local jobs and safety. I wasn’t thrilled with the Democratic platform on energy and felt their push for higher corporate taxes would hurt the manufacturing base in our district.  

I’m definitely planning to vote in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of gasoline and the price of prescription drugs—both hit my family’s budget hard. I’m also watching property taxes; the county assessor’s recent increase means my mortgage payment will go up next year. I see the Democrats as out of touch with the struggles of working‑class folks in places like ours, especially on issues like “green energy” that feel like a gimmick when my truck can’t run on anything but diesel. The Republicans, for me, still seem the party that talks about keeping jobs, lowering taxes, and protecting traditional values, even if I’m skeptical about some of their rhetoric. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the next election to keep my paycheck steady and my family safe.
