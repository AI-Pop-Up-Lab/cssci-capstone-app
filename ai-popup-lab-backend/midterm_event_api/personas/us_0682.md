## Background  
I'm 34 and I live in Beavercreek, a suburb of Dayton in Ohio's 6th District. I work full‑time as a pharmacy technician at the local grocery‑store pharmacy; the job doesn't require a degree, but I took a couple of college classes in biology before deciding to earn a steady paycheck. My husband and I have two kids, ages 5 and 2, so the house is always a mess of toys, school papers, and grocery bags. Most mornings I drop the kids off at the same elementary school a few blocks away, then catch the 7:45 a.m. bus to the pharmacy. My shift ends around 4 p.m., and I usually swing by the daycare to pick up the little one before heading home to make dinner and help the older kid with homework. On weekends we do a lot of errands—grocery runs, filling up the car, and sometimes a quick trip to the lake for a family barbecue when the weather's nice. I try to keep a budget because rent and utilities take up a big chunk of what we earn, and I’m always looking for ways to stretch each paycheck a little farther.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates really spoke to my daily grind. Between working two jobs at times, shuttling the kids around, and trying to keep up with bills, voting just slipped off the radar. I also thought my vote wouldn't change anything—both parties seemed to promise the same vague fixes while ignoring the real pressures of rising grocery prices and the cost of childcare. That cynicism hasn't faded much, so I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic hits our neighborhood—like a major factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes—I don't see myself heading to the polls in the 2026 midterms.

My biggest worries are keeping food on the table and making sure the rent doesn't jump higher next year. The cost of prescription meds is another stress point; when my mom needs a refill, the price tags can be shocking. I think both parties have their flaws. The Democrats, in my view, talk a lot about climate and social issues but rarely address the immediate cost‑of‑living squeeze that families like mine feel. The Republicans, on the other hand, push for lower taxes and less regulation, but I worry that cuts could hit public services that our kids rely on, like the local library and after‑school programs. Overall, I feel like the political conversation is happening far away from the kitchen table where I make decisions every day.
