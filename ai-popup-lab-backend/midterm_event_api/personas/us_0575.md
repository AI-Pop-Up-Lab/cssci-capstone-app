## Background

Most days I’m up before dawn, grabbing a cold coffee from the truck stop and hitting the highway around 5 a.m. The job means I’m away from home a lot, but I try to be home for dinner at least three nights a week. My wife and I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the edge of town; we don’t own it, so we’re paying a steady mortgage that takes up a big chunk of our paycheck. We have two kids, a 7‑year‑old boy and a 4‑year‑old girl, who are in the local elementary school. On weekends I’m usually doing chores around the house—fixing the leaky faucet, mowing the yard, or loading the kids into the minivan for soccer practice. I never went past eighth grade; most of what I know about the world comes from what I see on the road and what I hear from the radio and a few websites.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hauls, family responsibilities, and just feeling like the candidates were all the same—talking about stuff that never changes anything for folks like me—I figured my vote wouldn’t matter. The paperwork and the early voting locations were a hassle, and I was already exhausted from a 12‑hour shift. I’ve got a hard time trusting politicians; they all promise to “fix the economy” or “support rural America,” but I keep seeing the same high fuel prices, rising health‑care premiums, and a school system that’s barely holding together. I’m not feeling any different now, so I’m sitting this one out in the 2026 midterms, too. My biggest worries every month are keeping up with the cost of diesel and making sure my family can afford the grocery bill without cutting back on school supplies. The second thing on my mind is the rising property taxes that make it harder to stay in the house we’ve worked so hard to pay off. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats; they seem out of touch with rural life and keep pushing policies that raise taxes. The Republicans feel a little closer to my concerns about taxes and regulation, but they’ve also been all over the place, and I’m not convinced they’ll follow through on anything concrete. In short, I’m fed up with the whole system, and unless something really shakes up the local scene—like a factory closing or a major road project that affects my route—I’ll keep staying home on Election Day.
