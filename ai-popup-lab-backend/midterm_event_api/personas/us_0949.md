## Background  
I'm 40 and I work as a dental hygienist at a clinic in West Salem, just outside La Crosse in Wisconsin's 8th district. I grew up on the reservation near Madison, went to a community college for an associate degree in dental hygiene, and moved here after marrying my high school sweetheart. We have two kids—a seven‑year‑old boy and a five‑year‑old girl—who both go to the public elementary school down the road.  

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, a bowl of oatmeal, and getting the kids dressed and fed before I drive the three miles to the clinic. The mornings are busy; I clean and prep patients, take x‑rays, and do the usual hygiene work. My break is usually a sandwich at the staff kitchen while I scroll through a few headlines on my phone. After my shift ends at 3 p.m., I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from after‑school care, and we head home for dinner. Evenings are a mix of homework help, a bit of TV, and trying to squeeze in some chores before we all hit the bed. On weekends I help out at the tribal community center, run errands, and try to get the family out to the lake when the weather's good.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about keeping taxes low and protecting small‑business owners like the family‑run hardware store on my block. I also liked the emphasis on law and order; my brother works as a deputy sheriff, and I’ve seen how tougher sentencing can make a difference in our community. The Democratic platform seemed to focus on big‑city issues and policies that would raise my property tax bill, which I could barely afford with a single income and two kids.  

I'm voting this time around, too. The midterms feel like a chance to keep the balance that lets my kids stay in a decent school and my husband keep his job at the local manufacturing plant. My top everyday worries are the rising grocery prices that make a family of four stretch every dollar, and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on us each year. I’m also watching the debate over the new highway project that could bring more traffic but might also bring jobs.  

When I think about the parties, the Republicans seem to get the basics right for folks like me—lower taxes, support for the military, and a tougher stance on crime. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising big changes but never deliver on the stuff that matters day‑to‑day. I’m not a political scholar, but the way they talk on the news feels like they’re more interested in scoring points than solving the problems I see on my street.
