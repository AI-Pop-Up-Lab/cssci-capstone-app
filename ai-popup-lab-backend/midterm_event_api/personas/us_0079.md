## Background  
I'm 45 and I work as an HVAC technician for a regional service company that does most of its business in Pima County. My day starts around 5:30 a.m. when I grab a coffee and listen to NPR’s morning headlines while I drive my kids to the elementary school in Sahuarita. After dropping them off I head to the shop, where I spend most of the day fixing air‑conditioning units in homes and small businesses. The work is physically demanding, but the pay is steady enough to cover the mortgage on our modest two‑bedroom house and the car payments.  

My wife works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have two kids: a 13‑year‑old daughter who’s into soccer and a 9‑year‑old son who loves video games. Evenings are a blur of homework, dinner, and trying to keep the house from turning into a mess of laundry and tools. On weekends we usually take the family to the park or do some DIY projects around the house—often fixing a leaky faucet or installing a new light fixture. When I get a break, I like to watch a bit of sports on TV, especially the local baseball team, and I occasionally help my neighbors with small repairs. I’m not a big political junkie; most of my free time is spent just trying to keep the bills paid and the kids fed.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long shifts, two kids’ schedules, and a feeling that none of the candidates really understood what folks like me go through, the whole process just felt like a waste of time. The ballot looked the same as every other year: a bunch of names I didn’t recognize, a few promises about “building a better future” that never seemed to trickle down to the neighborhoods where we live. I tried to read a bit about the candidates on my phone, but by the time I got through the headlines, I was already late for the kids’ school bus. So I stayed home, told myself I’d keep an eye on local issues instead, and the next thing I knew it was Election Day.  

I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something blows up in our community—like a major layoff at the plant where a lot of my coworkers are employed, or a sudden hike in property taxes that would push us over the edge—I don’t see a reason to make the trip to the polls. My top everyday concerns are the cost of groceries and the rising rent for the few apartments we have left on the market. The price of fresh produce keeps creeping up, and every time the landlord mentions a rent increase, I have to think about cutting back on other things.  

When I think about the two big parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to talk a lot about immigration reform and climate policies, but I never see any concrete help for the kind of low‑income families that need a stable job and affordable housing. The Republicans keep pushing for tax cuts that sound good on paper, but they never address the fact that many of us are already working two jobs just to make ends meet. Both sides feel like they’re more interested in scoring points than actually solving the problems that hit my family day‑to‑day.  

So yeah, I’m not heading to the voting booth this November. If something dramatic happens that directly affects my kids’ school or my wife’s hours, I might reconsider, but right now I’m just trying to get through the month without another surprise bill.
