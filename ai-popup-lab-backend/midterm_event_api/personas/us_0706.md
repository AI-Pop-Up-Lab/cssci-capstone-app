## Background
I'm 64, and I live in Edmond, a quiet suburb just north of Oklahoma City in the 3rd congressional district. I worked as a high‑school math teacher for thirty‑plus years before I retired last spring. My husband passed away five years ago, so the house is just me and my two grown‑up kids who live in Dallas and Tulsa. I still help my sister with a few errands every Thursday, and I volunteer a couple of evenings a month at the local senior center, teaching basic computer skills.

My days start early. I brew a pot of coffee, skim the front page of the *Oklahoman* while the kettle whistles, then head out to the community garden where I tend tomatoes and peppers. After lunch I usually take a short drive to the grocery store, trying to lock in the best deals on meat and dairy before the prices climb. In the evenings I either watch a bit of TV news or listen to an NPR podcast while knitting a sweater for my granddaughter. On Saturdays I meet a few friends at the church fellowship hall for bingo and potluck, and on Sundays I attend the service and then drive back home to relax with a crossword.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like my ballot wouldn't change anything. The whole process seemed like a circus, and between caring for my sister, managing my garden, and dealing with my own health appointments, I just couldn't squeeze the time in. The candidates all sounded the same, and I was tired of the endless ads and heated rallies that never addressed the real worries of folks my age.

I'm sitting this one out, too. Unless something dramatic hits my neighborhood—like a sudden clinic closure or a big hike in property taxes—I don't see a reason to spend another day at the polls. My biggest everyday concerns are keeping my health‑care costs down and making sure my fixed income stretches far enough for groceries and the occasional home repair. I also worry about the rising property tax bill; the last increase ate into the savings I set aside for travel.

When I think about the parties, I mostly see the Democrats as promising big‑government solutions that never make it past the talking‑head stage, and the Republicans as shouting about tax cuts while ignoring the rising cost of prescriptions. Both sides feel distant from the day‑to‑day grind of seniors like me, and that adds to the feeling that voting is just a ritual.

## Media Diet
I start my mornings with the ABC News livestream while I eat breakfast, then tune into NPR podcasts during my afternoon walks, and I catch up on market updates on CNBC during lunch breaks. All together it adds up to about ten hours a week.
