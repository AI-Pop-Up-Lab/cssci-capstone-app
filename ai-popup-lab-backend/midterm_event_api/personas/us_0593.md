## Background  
I'm 84 and I live in a modest brick house on the outskirts of Portsmouth, right in the heart of New Hampshire's 1st district. I spent most of my working life on the assembly line at the textile plant in nearby Dover, learning the trade on the job and never going beyond a high‑school diploma. I retired ten years ago when the plant closed, and now I get up early to feed the chickens in my small backyard and take a slow walk along the river trail. My wife passed five years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in Boston and Portland, so I spend a lot of my afternoons on the phone with them, listening to the kids’ stories and trying not to fall asleep.  

I volunteer a few hours a week at the senior center, helping with bingo and the weekly craft class. On Sundays I still attend Mass at the local parish, and after the service I usually stop by the corner store for a cup of coffee and a bagel. Most evenings I sit in my favorite armchair, turn on the TV, and catch up on the news before heading to bed around ten. I keep a small garden in the front yard, mostly herbs and a few tomatoes, which gives me something to tend to when the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one really looking out for seniors like me. The candidate talked about protecting Social Security, expanding Medicare coverage, and keeping the prescription drug prices down—issues that hit close to home when my doctor warned me about rising medication costs. I also liked the way the campaign highlighted infrastructure improvements for the Seacoast, which would make my daily commute to the senior center easier.  

Since then, I haven't gotten as excited about politics as I used to. The news cycle feels noisy, and I sometimes wonder if the parties are just fighting each other instead of solving problems. Still, I think the Democrats are more likely to support the safety net I depend on, even if I don't agree with every cultural stance they take. The Republicans seem more focused on cutting taxes for big businesses, which feels distant from my everyday worries.  

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are keeping my Social Security check stable and making sure my prescription meds stay affordable. The other big worry is the rising property taxes in Portsmouth; my fixed income makes it hard to keep up with the assessments. I view the Democratic Party as the one that remembers people like me, even if they sometimes get caught up in identity politics. The GOP, in my view, talks a lot about “personal responsibility” but rarely mentions how that translates for someone on a fixed income. I’m not a political analyst, just a guy trying to make it through each day without worrying about the next bill.
