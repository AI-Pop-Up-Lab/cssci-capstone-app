## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Missoula. I work as a regional sales rep for a company that sells irrigation equipment to farms across the Flathead Valley. I took a few classes at the local community college but never finished a degree, so on paper I have “some college.” My wife, who retired from the county library a few years ago, and I have two adult children—one lives in Seattle, the other is back home helping me with the garden. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the diner down the street, read the headlines on my phone while the truck idles, then head out to the highway. The job keeps me on the road a lot, but I try to be home for dinner at least four nights a week. On weekends I’m either fixing up the old barn on our property or taking the kids’ grandparents out to the local farmers market. I’ve been a Montana resident my whole life, grew up in a small town near Great Falls, and have always felt a strong tie to the land and the community.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about the rising cost of my wife’s prescription meds and the fact that the proposed cuts to the Rural Health Clinic program would have hit our area hard. I also liked the candidate’s stance on protecting the River Trail and keeping the forests from aggressive logging. I didn’t feel the Republican alternative was offering anything that would help my family or the farms I work with.  

I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the price of gasoline—everything from the truck’s fuel to the cost of shipping the equipment I sell—and the property tax hikes the county is proposing to fund a new sports complex. I’m also keeping an eye on the Senate race because I think the balance of power will affect how much federal money gets funneled into western Montana for water projects.  

When it comes to the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones more willing to keep the social safety net alive and to push for sensible environmental policies that matter to my work. The Republicans, in my view, keep talking about “cutting red tape” but that often means rolling back protections that keep my community safe from floods and wildfires. I’m not a fan of the constant partisan bickering; I just want policies that let me keep my truck running, my family healthy, and the land we rely on productive.
