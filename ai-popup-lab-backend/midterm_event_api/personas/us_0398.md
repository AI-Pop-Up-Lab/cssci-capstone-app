## Background

I grew up in Baltimore, went to college at the University of Maryland, and moved back here after graduation to be close to my parents. My wife and I have two kids—one in high school and the other in middle school—so most evenings are a scramble of homework, dinner, and trying to keep the house from looking like a construction site. I usually start my day at 6 a.m., grab a coffee, and hop on a Zoom call before the kids are up. After work I drive the kids to soccer practice or the library, then we all eat together. On weekends I do a bit of gardening, mow the lawn, and help my sister with her grocery runs. My parents still live in a senior community nearby, so I swing by their place at least once a week to bring food and check in. I’m a member of the local Buddhist temple, but I also attend the community’s Fourth of July fireworks because it’s a good excuse to see friends.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s tax proposals would help my family keep more of our paycheck, and I was frustrated with what I saw as endless regulation that made my job harder. The candidate’s promise to protect small‑business tech contracts and push back on what I consider overreach in education policy also resonated with me. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration handles the federal budget and the new infrastructure bills—some of the spending feels wasteful, but a few broadband projects are useful for my line of work. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’m not a fan of sitting out just because the system feels messy. My biggest worries right now are the rising mortgage payments and the cost of gasoline for the family’s weekend trips. I also watch the property‑tax assessments that keep creeping up on our home. I think the Republican Party is the only one that talks about cutting taxes and limiting government size, even if their messaging can be a little over‑the‑top. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free stuff but end up raising taxes and adding layers of bureaucracy that hurt small businesses like the one I contract with. I’m not thrilled with either side, but I’m leaning toward the GOP for now.
