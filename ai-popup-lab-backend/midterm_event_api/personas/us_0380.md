## Background
I'm 20, and I live in a small apartment above a laundromat in Salem, right in the middle of Massachusetts’ 1st congressional district. I’m in my second year at a community college, taking a mix of general education classes and a couple of intro courses in environmental studies. To pay the rent and the ever‑rising tuition, I work evenings at a coffee shop downtown and pick up occasional shifts as a stocker at the local grocery store. My parents still own a house in a suburb a bit farther north, and I visit them on Sundays for a family dinner that usually ends with a heated debate about politics.

My day usually starts at 7 am with a quick shower, a bowl of cereal, and scrolling through the NPR morning brief while I sip a black coffee. Classes run from 9 am to 2 pm, with a break in between for a quick bite at the campus food court. After my last class I head to the coffee shop for a three‑hour shift, then either head home to study or swing by the grocery store for a late‑night stock. Weekends are a mix of homework, catching a game with friends at the local bar, and trying to find a decent deal on a used bike. I don’t drive; I rely on the commuter rail and a bike for most trips. My roommate, also a student, keeps the place messy, but we get along because we both need the rent split.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really talking about student debt relief and climate action—issues that hit me hard as a college student trying to figure out how to pay back loans while watching the sea level rise on the news. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding community college funding, which would make my own education cheaper. At the time I was pretty optimistic, thinking maybe a few more progressive policies could actually change things for people like me.

Since then, I’ve gotten a bit jaded. The promises about tuition freezes haven’t materialized, and the cost of rent in Salem keeps creeping up. Still, I think the Democrats are the better bet for my generation—at least they’re pushing for more affordable housing and a stronger safety net. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem stuck in a past that doesn’t care about climate or student debt; their focus on tax cuts for the wealthy feels like a slap in the face when I’m trying to stretch a paycheck.

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and getting some real relief on my student loans. I’m also watching the local job market; a lot of my friends are stuck in part‑time gigs that don’t pay enough to cover basic expenses. I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to care about these issues, while the GOP feels like they’re more interested in cultural wars than in fixing the everyday problems that affect me and my peers.
