## Background
I'm 80, and I live on a small farm just outside Williston in the 1st District. I never went to college; after high school I learned how to fix tractors and run a wheat operation from my dad. I retired about ten years ago, but I still tend a garden, keep a few chickens, and help my neighbor with his combine when the harvest rolls around. My wife passed a few years back, so it's mostly me and my two grown‑up children who visit on the holidays. Most mornings I sit on the porch with a cup of black coffee, watch the sunrise over the fields, and flip through the local paper before heading out to the feed store or the senior center. Afternoons are usually spent doing a bit of maintenance on the barn, checking the propane tanks, or watching a baseball game on TV. Evenings are my time for the church service on Wednesdays and a quick call to my daughter in Fargo. I drive a pickup I’ve had since the '80s, and I still keep a watchful eye on the price of diesel and the cost of feed, because those numbers dictate how comfortable my budget feels.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party understood what folks like me need: lower taxes, fewer regulations on farming, and a strong stance on border security that keeps the market stable. I was also tired of the constant talk about climate policies that seemed to ignore the realities of planting and harvesting in the Badlands. The candidate promised to keep the farm bill steady, and that mattered more than any glossy campaign ad. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s trade talks have gone, especially with Canada and Mexico, because a bad deal hurts my crop prices. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t see a reason to sit this one out unless the ballot looks like a repeat of the same old promises. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that my broadband still drops out when I try to video‑chat with my granddaughter. I also worry about property taxes creeping up as the county tries to fund new road projects. As for the parties, I think the Republicans still have the best shot at keeping government from getting in the way of a farmer’s life, while the Democrats seem more interested in urban issues that don’t translate to the plains. I’m not thrilled with any politician, but I’m leaning toward the side that promises to keep my taxes low and my market open.
