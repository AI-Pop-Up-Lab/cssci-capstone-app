## Background
I'm 65, and I live in Socorro, a small town about an hour east of Albuquerque in NM‑3. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I spent a few years working odd jobs on construction sites and loading docks. When I was 30 I got a steady gig as a maintenance worker for the city water treatment plant, and I've been doing that ever since. The work is mostly physical—checking pumps, fixing valves, cleaning filters—but the schedule is regular enough that I can be home for dinner most evenings.

My wife passed away five years ago, so I'm living alone now. My two kids moved to Phoenix and Denver when they were in their twenties; I talk to them on the phone once a week, mostly about health and what they're doing for work. On weekends I like to drive out to the nearby Bosque del Apache to watch the birds, or sit on my porch with a cold beer and the radio. I get my groceries at the local market, and I try to stretch my Social Security checks and the modest pension from the city job to cover rent, utilities, and a little extra for meds. I don't have a car anymore; I rely on the town shuttle for the occasional trip to the grocery store or the clinic.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates really cared about folks like me. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I was too busy fixing a busted pump at the plant when the voting day rolled around. The mail‑in ballot I got was confusing, and I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything. After that, I just shrugged it off and kept my focus on getting through each month without missing a rent payment.

I'm sitting this one out too. The midterms feel like the same old song—big promises, loud ads, and then nothing that helps with my rising water bill or the cost of my prescription meds. Unless something blows up locally—like a major water outage or a scandal that directly hits my community—I don't see a reason to step into the booth.

My biggest worries right now are the utility bills and my health care costs. Every time the water rates go up, I have to cut back on other things, and the co‑pay on my meds is a constant pinch. I also worry about property taxes; the county keeps trying to raise them to fund projects that don't seem to benefit the small towns.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as a bunch of city folks who talk about climate change and student debt, which feels far removed from my daily grind. The Republicans get my attention with talk about lower taxes and less regulation, but when I look at the state legislature, they still let the water rates climb and don't push hard enough for senior benefits. So I'm stuck feeling like both sides are more interested in scoring points than solving the problems that keep me up at night.
