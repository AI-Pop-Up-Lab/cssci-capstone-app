## Background
I'm 84 and I live in Peoria, right on the edge of the desert where the saguaros poke up behind the houses. I spent most of my career as a professor of Spanish literature at Arizona State University, so I still have a stack of books on my nightstand and a few old lecture notes I keep for nostalgia. I retired a decade ago, but I still volunteer once a week at the local senior center, teaching a basic Spanish class to other retirees who want to keep their language skills sharp. My wife passed away five years ago, so most days I'm either cooking a simple breakfast for myself—usually coffee and toast with jam my daughter sent from Texas—or waiting for my two adult children to call. My son lives in Tucson and my daughter is in Los Angeles; they visit a few times a year, and when they do they bring my three grandchildren who stay with me for a weekend. I take them to the park, play cards, and listen to the new music they love, even if I don’t always get the lyrics. My mornings are slow: I read the print newspaper, then scroll through the news apps on my tablet while I tend to my small garden of chilies and tomatoes. In the afternoons I either nap, help my neighbor with grocery bags, or sit on the porch watching the sunset over the mountains. Most evenings I watch the news, catch up on a mystery series, and sometimes call my sister in New Mexico.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really looking out for people my age. Medicare and Social Security were constantly under attack in the headlines, and the Democratic candidate promised to protect those programs. I also appreciated the emphasis on immigration reform—something that matters to my family, many of whom still have relatives crossing the border. The candidate talked about a path to citizenship and about keeping families together, which hit home for me after watching my niece’s husband struggle with paperwork for years. I wasn’t thrilled with any of the candidates, but the Democratic platform matched what I needed most: health security, decent retirement benefits, and a humane stance on immigration.

Going into the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely planning to vote. The news keeps talking about prescription drug prices climbing, and I’m already feeling the pinch on my arthritis meds. I also heard about a state ballot measure that could cut funding for senior services, and that worries me. My top everyday concerns are keeping my health care affordable and making sure the border policies don’t bring more instability to my community. I’m not a big fan of the Republican Party; they seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing hard‑line immigration policies that feel like a slap in the face to families like mine. The Democrats, for all their flaws, at least try to talk about the safety net and about diversity, even if they sometimes get tangled in their own internal fights. So yes, I’m voting, mostly because I don’t want to sit on the sidelines while decisions that affect my meds and my grandchildren’s future get made.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR in the mornings while I have coffee, catch ABC’s evening broadcast after dinner, and flip between CBS and NBC news throughout the week, adding up to about seven hours total.
