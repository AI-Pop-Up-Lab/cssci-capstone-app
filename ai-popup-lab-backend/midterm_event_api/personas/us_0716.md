## Background

I live in Muskogee, a small city on the Arkansas River, in a modest two‑bedroom house I bought with my wife a couple of years ago. We have a three‑year‑old son who keeps us on our toes, and my parents, both retired teachers, still own a place a short drive away that we visit on weekends. My day usually starts at 6 am with a quick coffee while scrolling through the headlines on my phone, then I drop the kid off at the local preschool and head to the office. I spend most of the day in front of a dual‑monitor setup, writing code and troubleshooting bugs. After work I either pick up my son from soccer practice or go to the gym, then we eat dinner together and I help with homework before we all crash around 10 pm. On Saturdays I do a bit of yard work and run errands at the grocery store; Sundays are mostly family time, sometimes a barbecue with my parents or a drive to the nearby state park.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Republican ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was better for the energy sector that keeps a lot of jobs in our district, and because I was worried about higher taxes and regulation that could hurt small business owners like the ones my dad knows. The campaign ads that talked about protecting the oil pipeline and keeping the cost of living down resonated with me, and I trusted the incumbent’s promise to keep federal spending in check. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have played out. I’m still leaning Republican, but I’m a bit annoyed that some of the promises about lowering gas prices haven’t materialized and that the national conversation has gotten so polarized. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms – I’m voting – because I think my vote still matters in a swing district, especially for the Senate seat that could decide the balance of power. My biggest everyday worries are the rising price of gasoline and the cost of health insurance for my family; the other thing that keeps me up at night is whether the local school district will have enough funding to keep class sizes small. I view the Democrats as too focused on big‑government solutions that ignore the realities of folks who work in trades and manufacturing, while I see the Republicans as more in tune with my economic concerns, even if I don’t agree with everything they say on social issues.
