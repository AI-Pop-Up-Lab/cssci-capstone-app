## Background  
I'm 65 and I live in Pittsfield, right in the heart of Massachusetts' 1st district. I spent most of my working life as a medical‑records clerk at the local hospital; I retired three years ago after the hospital downsized. My husband passed away five years back, and my two grown kids live in Boston and Springfield, so I mostly hear from them on the phone or through FaceTime. I have a little garden behind the house where I grow tomatoes and beans, which keeps me busy on Saturdays. Most weekdays I head to the senior center around 9 a.m. for a low‑impact exercise class, then swing by the grocery store for a few items—milk, bread, the occasional treat for my grandson when he visits in the summer. In the evenings I usually watch a bit of TV, read the paper, or listen to NPR while I knit. I drive a modest sedan to the pharmacy and the post office, and I try to walk the few blocks to the library whenever the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security, Medicare, and keeping prescription drug prices from climbing out of reach. I also liked the promise of more funding for community health centers, which mattered to me as a former hospital employee. Since then, I've kept an eye on how those promises are playing out; the rollout of the new prescription‑price‑cap rule has been slow, and I've heard talk about cuts to senior services in the state budget. That makes me a bit uneasy, but I still think the Democrats are the better bet for protecting the safety net I rely on.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I put a reminder on my phone for Election Day. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the property tax hike the town council is proposing to fund a new road project. I also worry about the lack of decent jobs for younger folks in the Berkshires—lots of people I know are moving out because they can't find work. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more attentive to seniors and health care, while the Republicans keep talking about tax cuts that mostly help the wealthy and push for deregulation that feels risky for small businesses like the ones in my town.

## Media Diet  
I usually watch the morning broadcast on NBC News while I have coffee, then tune into NPR during my drive to the senior center. In the evenings I catch up with ABC News and sometimes scroll through CBS News articles, adding up to about six hours a week.
