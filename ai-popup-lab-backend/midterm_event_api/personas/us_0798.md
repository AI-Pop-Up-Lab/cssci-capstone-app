## Background
I'm 20, and I live in a small rental house on the edge of Florence, South Carolina, in the 6th district. My parents own a farm a few miles out, but I moved out after high school to be closer to the community college where I'm taking a couple of associate‑degree classes in business. I work evenings at a fast‑food restaurant, flipping burgers and taking drive‑through orders, and on weekends I pick up shifts at a local warehouse when they need extra hands. My day usually starts with a quick breakfast of cereal while scrolling through my phone, then I head to campus for a morning class that ends around noon. After that I usually hit the gym for an hour, then go to my job until about 10 p.m. I get home, make a cheap dinner—often ramen or frozen pizza—watch a short video on YouTube, and try to squeeze in some gaming before I finally crash. I have a younger sister who still lives at home, and we keep in touch mostly through texts; she’s finishing high school and plans to go to a trade school. Money is tight, so most of my paycheck goes to rent, utilities, and a small stash for school fees.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about a kid trying to finish community college and keep a job. Between classes, work, and trying to pay my bills, I didn't have the energy to get to a polling place, and the whole campaign messaging just sounded like the same old promises. I also think a lot of the political ads are just noise; they never address the real stuff that matters to me, like tuition hikes or the rising cost of gas. Because of that, I’m probably going to sit this one out again in 2026 unless something blows up locally—maybe a big factory closing or a sudden change to student loan policies that hits me directly. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure I can afford enough gas to get to work and school. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as talking about big ideas but never delivering anything that helps a working‑class kid in a small town. The Republicans, to me, seem more about tax cuts for the wealthy and cultural battles that don’t change my paycheck. Both feel out of touch, but I’m a little more annoyed by the Democrats because their policies feel like they would raise my taxes or cut funding for community colleges. Still, I don’t feel strongly enough to get involved; I just keep watching what happens and hope the right thing works out on its own.
