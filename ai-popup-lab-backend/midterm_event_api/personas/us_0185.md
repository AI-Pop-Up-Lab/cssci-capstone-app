## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s tax policies and deregulation stance were the only things keeping my household’s budget from spiraling. My husband’s business thrives when there’s less red tape, and I appreciate the emphasis on school choice—our kids are in a charter school that’s been a better fit than the neighborhood public option. I also worry about the national debt and think the GOP’s focus on fiscal responsibility aligns more with my own concerns about rising property taxes and the cost of living.  

Since then, I’ve watched the Democrats push for higher corporate taxes and more regulation on tech, which I see as a threat to my job security and to small businesses like my husband’s. I’m not a political scholar; I just hear what’s on the news and what my friends at work say. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—I’ll be at the polls on the first Tuesday in November. My top everyday worries are the ever‑increasing grocery prices and the looming property‑tax reassessment that could force us to move again. As for the parties, I think the Republicans are the only ones who actually care about keeping taxes low and giving families the freedom to choose schools, while the Democrats seem more interested in identity politics and expanding government programs that feel out of touch with ordinary folks like me.
