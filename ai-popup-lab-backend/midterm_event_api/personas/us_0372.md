## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the north side of Worcester, not far from the Worcester Polytechnic campus. I work full‑time as an accountant for a regional insurance company; I've been crunching numbers there for the past 22 years, after getting my bachelor's in finance from a state university. My day starts around 6 a.m. with a quick coffee and the morning news on my phone, then a commuter train ride into the downtown office. After work I usually swing by the local gym for a short weight session, then head home to make dinner—often something simple like baked salmon and veggies, because I’m trying to watch my cholesterol. My wife, who retired from the school district a few years ago, spends most of her time knitting and volunteering at the community center. We have two adult children: a daughter who lives in Boston and a son who’s back in the South caring for his own family. My mother, who’s in her late 80s, still lives nearby and I help out with grocery runs and doctor appointments when I can. Weekends are a mix of church on Sunday, a bit of yard work, and catching up with friends at the local bar that has a jukebox and cheap wings.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because health care and Social Security felt like the most immediate issues for me and my mom. The candidate talked about protecting Medicare and expanding prescription‑drug coverage, and that resonated. I also liked the emphasis on investing in public schools and job training, which seemed to matter for my kids’ futures. Since then I’ve stayed pretty plugged into what’s happening—though I’m not a political junkie, I keep an eye on the big headlines and what they mean for my pocket. The midterms are coming up, and I’m definitely going to the polls again; I figure every vote counts, especially when the balance of power in Congress can swing how much funding reaches the community health centers that my mom relies on.

My top everyday concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax bill on our apartment building. Rent has been steady, but the city keeps proposing new fees for things like sidewalk repairs, and that adds up. I also worry about the job market for younger folks—there’s a lot of tech hype, but not enough decent wages for people without a college degree in our district. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least try to protect the social safety net, even if they sometimes get bogged down in infighting. The Republicans, on the other hand, come across as more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that keep people like my mom safe; that feels like a slap in the face to someone who’s spent a lifetime working a steady job and just wants to retire without a huge medical bill.
