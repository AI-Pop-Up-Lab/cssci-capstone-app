## Background
I'm 40, and I live in a modest rental house on the edge of Newark, just a short drive from the I‑95. I work full‑time as a customer‑service supervisor at a regional utility company; the job mostly involves answering calls, handling billing disputes, and training a handful of new hires. My husband is a journeyman electrician, and we have two kids—12‑year‑old Maya and 9‑year‑old Lucas—who go to the public middle and elementary schools a few miles away. Most mornings I’m up by 6 a.m., making coffee, packing lunches, and shuffling the kids onto the school bus. After a quick commute I’m at the call center by 8 a.m., where I spend the day on a headset, trying to keep a calm voice while people yell about power outages or high bills. When I clock out at 5 p.m., I pick up the kids, swing by the grocery store, and try to squeeze in a short walk with my husband before we all collapse on the couch. Weekends are a mix of soccer practice, a bit of yard work, and catching up on the laundry. Money is tight enough that we keep a careful eye on the utility bills, and we’re always looking for a cheaper place to live if something opens up.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates really cared about people like me who are just trying to pay the electric bill and keep the kids fed. Between the long hours at work and the kids’ schedules, I never found the time to get to a polling place, and the whole process just seemed like another hassle I could skip. I’m not a big fan of the political theater; it feels like the parties are more interested in scoring points than fixing the potholes on our streets or keeping gas prices from blowing up. That feeling hasn’t changed much, so I’m sitting this one out too—unless something huge happens locally, like a major plant closure or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits us directly. My day‑to‑day worries are simple: rent is creeping up and groceries are getting more expensive every month. I’m also worried about the reliability of the power grid, especially after a few blackouts last winter. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are all talk about climate and social programs, but they forget the working‑class families who can’t afford higher taxes. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing cultural battles that don’t affect my kids’ school. Both sides feel distant, and I’m not convinced either will change that vibe anytime soon.
