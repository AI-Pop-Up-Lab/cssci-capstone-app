## Background  
I'm 30 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in Loveland, right on the edge of the old downtown district. I work full‑time as an HVAC technician for a regional service company; the job mostly has me driving around the Front Range, fixing furnaces in the winter and air‑conditioners in the summer. After I got my associate’s degree in mechanical systems from a community college in Fort Collins, I landed the apprenticeship that turned into a steady gig. My paycheck is decent but barely covers the rent, utilities, and the student loans I still have from the program.  

I share the place with a roommate, a fellow tradesperson who does carpentry. We split the rent, take turns cooking, and usually meet up for a quick beer after our shifts. Most evenings I’m either scrolling through my phone or watching a game on the TV—college football is the only sport I follow consistently. On weekends I try to catch up on grocery shopping at the local co‑op, swing by the gym for a couple of weight‑lifting sessions, and sometimes help my mom with a few errands; she lives a couple of hours away in Pueblo and relies on me for rides to her doctor appointments. My daily routine is pretty set: wake up around 6 am, grab a coffee, drive to the job site, work until about 4 pm, then head back, eat, and either relax or do some side‑hustle work fixing small appliances for neighbors.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing just felt like a circus—candidates yelling at each other, ads promising the moon, and a sense that none of them really cared about folks like me who are trying to make ends meet. I work long hours, and the polling place was a half‑hour drive away; by the time I got there, the line was already moving slower than traffic on I‑25 during rush hour. I figured my single vote wouldn’t tip the scales, so I stayed home.  

I’m sitting this one out again in 2026. Unless there’s a local issue that hits me directly—like a proposal that would raise property taxes on my apartment building or a sudden change to how HVAC contractors are licensed—I don’t see a reason to make the trip. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and the price of groceries not spiraling out of control. The cost of a decent meal for two has been creeping up, and when rent hikes come, it feels like I’m constantly playing catch‑up.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats talk a lot about social justice, but I feel like they forget about the day‑to‑day struggles of Black working‑class folks in places like Loveland. The Republicans seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing cultural issues that don’t affect my paycheck. Both sides act like they’re the only game in town, and I’m left watching from the sidelines, hoping someone will finally address the real, gritty problems that keep me up at night.

## Media Diet  
I usually catch up on NPR news in the mornings while I’m driving to work, and I skim CNBC headlines during my lunch break. It adds up to about two hours a week total.
