## Background
I'm 50, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Murfreesboro, right in the 7th district. I work full‑time as an administrative assistant at a regional health clinic, handling patient paperwork and scheduling. My husband works night shifts at the local warehouse, so most evenings I’m the one cooking dinner and helping our two kids—16‑year‑old Maya and 13‑year‑old Jamal—with homework. We get the kids to school by 7:30 am, I’m at the clinic by 8, and I’m usually done around 5 pm. After work I pick up Maya from soccer practice, grab groceries at the big box store, and try to squeeze in a quick call with my sister in Memphis. Sundays are the only day we’re all together; we go to church, then sit on the porch and watch the same old sitcoms while the kids argue over the remote. Money is tight, so I keep an eye on the utility bills, and I’m always looking for coupons or a better deal on the internet.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like the choices were the same old story—candidates promising change but never delivering anything that mattered to me or my family. Between long work hours, caring for the kids, and trying to stretch every paycheck, casting a ballot felt like a waste of time. I also get sick of the endless ads that talk about “unity” while my neighborhood still deals with underfunded schools and rising rent. That cynicism hasn’t gone away, so I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out too, unless something dramatic hits our community—like a hospital closure or a major scandal that forces me to pay attention.

My biggest everyday worries are the grocery bills that keep climbing and the property taxes that make it harder to keep the house in good shape. I’m also worried about the cost of health care; a single emergency visit can wipe out a month’s wages. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats talk a lot about racial justice but I don’t see that turning into real jobs or better schools for us. The Republicans keep pushing tax cuts for the wealthy and say they’ll “protect” us, but I see my street still dealing with potholes and a lack of public transport. It feels like they’re both playing a game that leaves ordinary folks like me stuck in the middle.
