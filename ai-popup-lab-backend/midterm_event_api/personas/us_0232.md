## Background
I'm 50, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Cedar Rapids, right in the 2nd congressional district. I work full‑time as a forklift operator at the regional distribution center just off I‑80. The job is steady, the pay is enough to keep the lights on, but it’s physically demanding—lifting pallets all day, listening to the hum of the conveyor belts, and watching the clock for my 30‑minute lunch break. My wife works part‑time at a nearby grocery store, and we have two kids: a 17‑year‑old daughter who just started community college and a 14‑year‑old son who spends most of his free time on his phone or playing basketball at the local park.

Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., pour a cup of coffee, and check the weather on my old smartphone before heading out. After my shift ends around 4 p.m., I swing by the kids’ school to pick them up, then we all eat dinner together—usually something quick like spaghetti or a frozen pizza. Evenings are a mix of helping the kids with homework, watching a game on the TV, or scrolling through the news sites on my laptop while the kids are in their rooms. On weekends I do a bit of yard work, help my parents with a few errands, and meet up with a couple of buddies at the local bar for a couple of beers and some talk about the farm market, the upcoming county fair, or whatever’s going on in town.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on taxes and regulation matched what I needed as a working‑class family. The candidate promised to keep the federal government from meddling too much in small‑business operations, and that sounded like a win for my job at the warehouse and for the local farms that my parents still own a few acres of. I also appreciated the tough talk on immigration and crime—issues I hear about on the news and from folks at the bar who say they’re worried about safety in the community.

Since then, I’ve stayed pretty much the same. The economy feels a little tighter; gasoline is still pricey, and the grocery bills keep creeping up. I’m not glued to politics, but when I hear about a new tax proposal that could affect my paycheck, I pay attention. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the cost of living—especially rent for my daughter’s dorm and the price of groceries—as well as ensuring there are decent jobs in the area that don’t require a college degree. I think the Democrats keep talking about “big ideas” but they don’t understand how hard it is to make ends meet when you’re working a manual job. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least seem to focus on keeping taxes low and not over‑regulating the kind of work I do. I’m not a political scholar, I just want a government that lets me keep my job and afford a decent life for my family.
