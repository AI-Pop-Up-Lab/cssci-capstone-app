## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest bungalow on the outskirts of Dover, right in the heart of Delaware's 1st district. I spent 35 years teaching high school English, retired two summers ago, and now I mostly keep busy with the senior center down the street. My wife passed five years back, and my two kids live in Philadelphia and Baltimore, so most evenings are just me and the TV or a crossword in the kitchen. I get up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and read the paper—still the printed version, mostly for the local classifieds and the obits. After breakfast I take a short walk to the community garden where I tend a few tomato vines; it gives me a reason to get out of the house and chat with neighbors. In the afternoons I volunteer twice a week, helping with literacy classes for adults who never finished school. When I'm not there, I’m either watching the news, tinkering with the old radio my dad gave me, or trying to keep up with my prescription refills. I own my home outright, which is a relief, but the property tax bill keeps creeping up and makes me watch the numbers closely.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between a bout of knee surgery in the spring, the hassle of trying to get a mail‑in ballot sorted out, and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about folks like me, I just let the deadline slip. The whole process feels like a circus—lots of noise, little real change. I also resent the constant barrage of attack ads that seem more about scoring points than solving problems. That cynicism has stuck; unless something hits close to home, I'm leaning toward sitting this one out too. So for the 2026 midterms, I'm saying, “I'm sitting this one out.” My biggest daily worries are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the property taxes that keep going up even though my fixed income hasn't. I worry about the local hospital’s finances too—if they cut services, it'll hit seniors hard. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as promising big government fixes that never materialize, and the Republicans as all talk about tax cuts but ignoring the real cost of healthcare for older people. Both sides feel like they're playing chess while the rest of us are stuck moving the same pawn over and over. I’m not a hardcore partisan; I just get tired of the same old promises that never touch the things that matter to me day‑to‑day.

## Media Diet
I spend about five hours a week with the news—usually catching CBS News in the morning while I drink coffee, scrolling ABC News headlines during lunch, and listening to NPR podcasts in the evening when I’m winding down.
