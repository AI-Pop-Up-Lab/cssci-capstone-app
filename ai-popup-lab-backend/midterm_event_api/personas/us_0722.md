## Background
I'm 84 and I live in Pendleton, a small town out in Oregon's 1st district. I spent most of my adult life working as a housekeeper for a few families in town and later helped my son run the family’s farm equipment repair shop. I never finished high school, so I never had a fancy career, but the work kept me busy and paid the bills. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two children live in Portland and Boise now, so most days I'm on my own. I get up around 7 a.m., take my blood‑pressure meds, and shuffle to the kitchen for a bowl of oatmeal while the morning news plays on the TV. After that I tend to my garden, which is mostly herbs and a few tomatoes, and I make a short walk to the senior center where we play cards and chat about the weather. I still drive a little, mostly to the grocery store on Main Street, but my daughter drops me off for doctor appointments. Evenings are quiet – a cup of tea, a bit of daytime TV, and then I read the local paper (the *Pendleton Times*) before heading to bed around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about folks like me who are just trying to get my prescriptions filled and keep the heat on in winter. Between the long lines at the clinic, the rising cost of insulin, and the fact that the ballot was a massive paper thing I could barely read, I just stayed home. I also think the whole system is set up for people who have the time and energy to campaign, not for an old lady who spends most of her day on the porch. That feeling hasn't really changed, so I'm sitting this one out in the 2026 midterms unless something blows up locally – like a hospital closing or a big tax hike that would force me off my home.

My biggest worries right now are health‑care costs and the price of utilities. My insurance premiums keep creeping up, and when the gas bill spikes in the winter I have to dip into my savings, which are already thin. I also worry about property taxes; the county keeps raising them and I’m not sure how I’ll keep up. As for the parties, I see Democrats talking about climate stuff and social programs, but they never mention how hard it is for a senior on a fixed income to get affordable meds. Republicans keep yelling about taxes and regulation, but they don’t seem to understand that a lot of us rely on federal programs like Medicare. To be blunt, both sides feel distant, and I’m not convinced either will fix the things that matter to me.

## Media Diet
