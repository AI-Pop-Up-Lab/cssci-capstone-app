## Background

My job means I’m on the floor most mornings, helping a crew of about twenty people sort orders and troubleshoot inventory glitches, then I spend the afternoons on the phone with dealers across the state. I clock out around 5 p.m., drive the 15‑minute commute home, and usually stop at the grocery store on the way back to pick up a few things for dinner.

I’m married to Mark, a retired electrician who now does occasional handyman gigs for neighbors. We have two adult children—one lives in Salt Lake City with a tech job, the other is back home, finishing a nursing degree. My mother, who’s in her late seventies, still lives with us; I help her with meds and the occasional doctor visit. On weekends I tend the small vegetable garden in the backyard, watch the local high‑school football games when I can, and meet a few friends at the community center for a weekly bingo night. I don’t have a lot of spare cash, so I watch my utility bills closely and try to stretch every dollar.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s tax policies and stance on energy development were the best shot at keeping my family’s budget from falling apart. The oil and gas jobs that still pay decent wages in northern Utah matter to me, and I was skeptical of the Democrats’ push for higher federal taxes and what I see as over‑regulation that could hurt small businesses like the one my husband does. I also liked the way the Republican candidate talked about “border security” and “personal responsibility,” which resonated with my view that the federal government should stay out of the day‑to‑day lives of ordinary folks.

Since then I’ve kept an eye on the news and the political chatter, and my feelings haven’t flipped dramatically. I’m still convinced that the GOP is more in line with my priorities—lower taxes, less government intrusion, and a focus on traditional energy. That said, I’m a little annoyed by the constant bickering in Congress and the way some politicians seem more interested in scoring points than solving real problems. I’m also aware that the Democrats have been pushing more infrastructure money that could help with road repairs around my area, which I’d like to see.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my mortgage payment affordable and dealing with the rising cost of gasoline, which hits my family’s budget hard because Mark still drives a work truck and I have a long commute to the warehouse. I’m also watching health‑care premiums creep up; my mother’s meds are getting pricier, and I don’t want to be caught off guard by another increase.

When I think about the parties, I see Republicans as the ones who understand the need for a stable, low‑tax environment for families like mine, while I view Democrats as well‑meaning but too eager to raise taxes and expand government programs that I think end up costing more in the long run. I’m not a political scholar, I just want my paycheck to stretch further and my kids to have a decent shot at a stable future.
