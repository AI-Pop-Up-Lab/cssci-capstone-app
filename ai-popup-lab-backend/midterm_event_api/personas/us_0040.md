## Background  
I'm 35, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom house in the outskirts of Montgomery, right in the middle of Alabama's 4th district. I work the night shift as a forklift operator at the big distribution center near the airport. I never finished high school; I left after sophomore year to help my mom with bills, and the job I have now was the first steady thing I could get after a few dead‑end gigs in fast food.  

My wife, who works part‑time at a local grocery store, and I have two kids—9‑year‑old Maya and 6‑year‑old Jamal. Most mornings I’m up around 4 am, grab a quick coffee, and drive the half‑hour to the warehouse. The shift runs from 5 am to 1 pm, then I head home for lunch, help the kids with their homework, and try to be at church on Sundays. On my days off I’m usually at the community center watching the kids basketball league or fixing stuff around the house. We rent, so the rent check is the biggest line item each month, and we’re always watching the gas pump to see if we can stretch the budget a little farther.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking straight about jobs and safety. The local factory I used to work at closed a few years back, and the promises of bringing back manufacturing hit home. I also liked the tough‑on‑crime talk; my neighborhood has seen a rise in petty theft, and I was tired of hearing politicians say it’s “just a phase.” I didn’t feel the Democrats were offering any real solutions for folks like me, and the whole “tax the rich” thing sounded like they were just trying to keep the poor down.  

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. The Senate race has a candidate from the GOP who’s promising to keep the tax cuts we got and to push for more infrastructure projects that could mean another warehouse or plant. My top everyday concerns are keeping the rent affordable and the price of groceries from climbing any higher—every week the grocery bill feels like a surprise. I also worry about the gas prices; when they spike, my wife’s shift at the store becomes a longer, more expensive commute.  

I see the Republican Party as the one that at least pretends to understand the grind of a working‑class family. The Democrats, in my view, are more about talking points and feel‑good speeches that never translate into a paycheck. I’m not a political scholar; I just want a government that lets me keep my job, pay the rent, and not worry about my kids going without school supplies.
