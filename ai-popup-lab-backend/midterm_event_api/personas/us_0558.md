## Background
I'm 65 and I've been working as a maintenance mechanic at the local grain elevator outside Minot for the past thirty‑odd years. I never finished high school; I left after ninth grade to help my family on the ranch when my dad got sick. My wife, Maria, runs the small grocery store we own downtown, and we have two grown‑up kids who live in Fargo and one grandchild who visits in the summer. Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, checking the diesel tanks and making sure the conveyor belts are greased. After my shift ends around 3 p.m., I swing by the store to pick up a few groceries, then we both head home for dinner. Evenings are usually spent watching the news on the TV in the living room while we eat, and on weekends I like to take the truck out to the nearby lake for a few hours of fishing. I’m not into gadgets; my phone is mostly for calls and the occasional text from my kids.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like none of the candidates really cared about folks like me. The whole process just seemed like a circus, and between long hours at the elevator and the store, I never found the time to get to a polling place. I also thought my vote wouldn’t change anything—both parties keep talking about big promises, but I keep seeing the same high fuel prices and health‑care bills that bite into my paycheck. That feeling of cynicism hasn’t really faded, so I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms unless something major hits my community, like a factory closing or a real push to help small‑business owners with taxes.

My biggest worries these days are the rising cost of gasoline and the price of prescription meds. When the price at the pump goes up, it eats into the budget for everything else, and I’ve got to decide whether to skip a doctor’s visit because the co‑pay is too high. I also keep an eye on property taxes; the county keeps nudging them up and it feels unfair when my retirement savings aren’t growing fast enough.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats. They talk a lot about climate policies and social programs, but I don’t see any real help for rural North Dakota or for people who never got a college degree. The Republicans used to feel more like my side because they talk about lower taxes and protecting the oil and gas industry, but lately they’re all about culture wars and I feel like they’re ignoring the everyday struggles of older workers. So, I’m basically annoyed with both, but I’m a little more inclined to listen to the GOP when they actually talk about keeping gas cheap and taxes low.
