## Background  
I'm 45 and work as an HVAC technician for a regional service company that sends me out to homes and small businesses across the 4th District. I live in a modest rental house on the outskirts of Clarksville, close enough to the Fort Campbell base that a lot of my neighbors are military families. My wife runs a part‑time daycare out of her apartment, and we have two kids – a 12‑year‑old daughter who just started middle school and a 9‑year‑old son who is obsessed with video games.  

Most mornings I get up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and check the news on my phone while the kids are still asleep. I head to the garage at 6:30 a.m., grab my toolbox, and drive to my first job by 7:15. The work is pretty physical – pulling old units, installing new compressors, troubleshooting thermostats – and the pay is steady but never feels like a lot when you factor in rent, car payments, and the kids’ school fees. I usually finish by 3 p.m., swing by the grocery store for a quick run, and pick the kids up from school. Evenings are a mix of helping with homework, watching a bit of TV, and trying to squeeze in a workout at the local gym before we all head to bed around 10 p.m. On weekends I do odd‑hour calls, help my wife with the daycare paperwork, and try to catch a baseball game at the minor‑league park when I can.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because the campaign talked a lot about expanding broadband in rural areas, something that would help my kids with schoolwork and my wife’s daycare stay connected. I also liked the promise of more funding for community health clinics – the nearest one is a half‑hour drive away, and we’ve had to travel for basic check‑ups. At the time I felt like the Democrats were the only side actually listening to the everyday concerns of folks like me, even if I didn’t agree with everything they said.  

Since then, I’ve been watching the state legislature’s budget battles and the headlines about rising property taxes. The Democrats in Washington still push for big‑ticket items like climate‑related infrastructure, which sounds good but feels distant from my day‑to‑day bills. The Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes, but the cuts never seem to reach renters or middle‑class families; they mostly benefit the wealthy or big corporations. I’m not a political scholar, but I’ve gotten tired of the same old promises that never change my paycheck.  

I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that my landlord just raised the rent again – both things that feel out of my control. I also worry about the local hospital’s ability to stay open; if it closes, the nearest emergency room is over an hour away. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more willing to talk about social services, but I’m skeptical about how much they’ll actually fund. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep yelling about “law and order” and “tax cuts” while ignoring the fact that my kids can’t afford the same extracurriculars they had when they were younger. Neither side feels like they’ve got my full confidence, but I’m leaning toward the Democrats again because they at least acknowledge the problems I care about.
