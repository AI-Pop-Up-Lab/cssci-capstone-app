## Background
I'm 84, and I live in the small town of Stanton, right in the heart of Michigan's 10th district. I spent most of my life working on the assembly line at the nearby auto plant; I never finished high school, but the job taught me how to read a wrench and keep a steady pace. The plant closed when I was in my early seventies, and I’ve been on a modest pension ever since. My wife passed away five years ago, and my two adult children live in Grand Rapids and Detroit, so most days I’m on my own. I get up around 7 a.m., make a cup of coffee, and flip on the local radio for the news before heading out to the senior center. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I attend a bingo night, and on Sundays I go to mass at the nearby church. I drive my old Chevy to the grocery store a few times a week, pick up a bag of oranges and a loaf of bread, and try to keep up with my blood‑pressure meds. In the evenings I sit on the front porch, watch a bit of TV, and sometimes call my daughter to hear about her grandkids.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Medicare and Social Security, which are the two things that keep me afloat now that I’m on a fixed income. The Republican platform seemed all about cutting taxes for corporations and cutting government programs, and that didn’t sit right with a retiree who depends on those programs. I also liked the way the Democrat talked about immigration reform; even though I’m Hispanic, I’ve seen families in my community struggle, and I wanted policies that would give them a chance.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening in Washington, but I mostly care about how things affect my day‑to‑day life. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t have the energy to sit this one out, and I think my voice still matters for the issues that matter to me. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and my property tax bill, which keeps creeping up even though my pension hasn’t grown. I also worry about the local health clinic staying open; if it closes, I’ll have to drive farther for my regular check‑ups.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like the ones who remember seniors and working‑class folks. They push for expanding Medicare and trying to keep drug prices down. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to be more interested in deregulating big business and cutting taxes for folks who don’t need the money. I’m not a political scholar; I just know which side seems to have my back when the rent‑check or the pharmacy bill comes due.
