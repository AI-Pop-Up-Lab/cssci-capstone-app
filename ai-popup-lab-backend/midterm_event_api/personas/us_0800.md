## Background  
I'm 40, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Myrtle Beach, just a few minutes from the highway that brings the tourists in every summer. I work the night shift as a forklift operator at the local distribution center that ships furniture and appliances up the coast. The job's physically demanding, but it pays the bills and lets me see my kids before school. I'm married, and my wife works part‑time at a grocery store, so we juggle two jobs between us. We have two kids—an 11‑year‑old daughter who plays soccer on the community field and a 7‑year‑old son who loves video games and is always begging for the newest console.  

My days start early: I get up around 5 a.m., make coffee, and catch the 5:30 am news feed on my phone while scrolling through the headlines. After dropping the kids off at school, I head to the warehouse and work until about 2 a.m., then drive home for a quick shower and a bite before heading back out. Weekends are a mix of family time—barbecues with neighbors, a quick trip to the beach when the weather's nice, and the occasional church service on Sunday morning. I keep a small garden in the backyard, mostly tomatoes and peppers, which helps stretch the grocery budget a bit.  

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate. I was motivated mainly by concerns over health care access for my wife’s chronic condition and the push to expand Medicaid in South Carolina. The Democratic platform also talked about investing in infrastructure, which I hoped would bring more stable jobs to the area and reduce the seasonal ups and downs of tourism work. I didn’t feel the Republican candidate addressed those issues in a way that mattered to my family, and I was also uneasy about the rhetoric around voting rights that seemed to threaten my community’s ability to vote.  

Going into the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely planning to vote again. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the uncertainty around the local school funding—my daughter’s school is dealing with budget cuts that could affect after‑school programs. I also keep an eye on the property tax hikes that have been discussed at the county level; any increase would squeeze our already tight budget. I’m not a political scholar, but I see the Democratic Party as the one more likely to protect Medicaid and push for more community health resources, while I think the Republicans are more focused on cutting taxes for businesses, which sometimes feels like they forget about working families like mine. That said, I’m skeptical of both sides when promises sound too good to be true, and I’m quick to tune out the political ads when they feel like empty noise.
