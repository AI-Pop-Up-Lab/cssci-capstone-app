## Background
I'm 30, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom condo in the Kakaʻako district of Honolulu, right near the waterfront. I work as a research analyst for the University of Hawaiʻi’s Marine Science Institute, where I spend most days crunching data on coral health and writing grant reports. My job usually starts at 8 a.m.; I grab a coffee from the corner shop, hop on the bus, and spend the commute scrolling through the morning headlines on my phone. After work I often head to the gym or take a quick surf session at Ala Moana Beach before cooking dinner—usually something simple like poke bowls or stir‑fried veggies with rice. 

I live with my partner, Kai, who’s a graphic designer, and we’ve been together for three years. We don’t have kids yet, but my parents visit from Maui a few times a year, and we take them out for a luau or a hike up Diamond Head. On weekends I volunteer a few hours at a local after‑school program that teaches elementary kids about marine conservation. My rent is a big chunk of my paycheck, so I keep a close eye on the market and try to save for a down‑payment on a house someday.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I turned out to vote for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one taking climate change seriously, which matters a lot living on the islands. I also liked the emphasis on protecting public lands and funding for public schools—issues that affect my work and my kids’ future. I was pretty pumped to see a candidate who promised to keep tourism sustainable, since that’s the backbone of our economy but also a strain on our ecosystems.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. The biggest things on my mind are the soaring cost of housing and groceries; everything seems to get more expensive each year, and my paycheck doesn’t stretch as far as it used to. I’m also worried about how rising sea levels might affect my neighborhood and the research sites I work at. I’m leaning toward the Democrat who’s pushing for stronger rent control measures and more funding for renewable energy projects, but I’m not thrilled with the whole political circus. The Republicans, in my view, still seem to downplay climate risks and push for policies that would make it harder for low‑income families to afford rent. I’m not a fan of the partisan drama, but I’ll keep voting as long as the candidates address the day‑to‑day stuff that hits my wallet and my work.
