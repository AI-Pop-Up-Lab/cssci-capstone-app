## Background
I'm 55 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Wheeling, right in the middle of the 1st district. I work full‑time as a cashier at the local grocery store; the job's mostly standing behind a register, bagging groceries, and sometimes stocking the freezer aisle when a shift runs short. I didn't finish high school, so I never went to college, but I learned a lot on the job and from the younger coworkers who sometimes help me with the new point‑of‑sale system.

I'm married to a retired coal miner who now does odd jobs around the house. We have two grown‑up kids; one lives in Charleston and the other is in a nearby town, both working in construction. My parents live in a small town two counties over, and I drive up to see them every few weeks, bringing groceries and helping with the yard. Most mornings I wake up at 5:30 am, make coffee, and catch a quick news clip on my phone before heading to work. After my shift ends around 7 pm, I usually stop by the community center for a bingo night or a church potluck, then come home, make dinner, and watch a bit of TV before bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about my health insurance and the rising cost of my prescription meds. The candidate promised to protect Medicare and to keep the local clinic open, which mattered a lot to me and my husband, who still has some lingering health issues from his mining days. I also liked that the Democrat talked about job training programs for people like my kids who want to stay in the area instead of moving out.

Since then, I've kept an eye on what the parties are doing, but I still feel like the system is mostly the same. The Republicans keep pushing for lower taxes, which sounds good, but they also want to cut funding for the community health center that my husband relies on. That makes me uneasy. The Democrats, on the other hand, keep talking about expanding health coverage, but sometimes I hear them ignore the struggles of coal‑dependent towns, which is where a lot of my neighbors live.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the price of groceries—especially fresh produce that I need for my family's health—and the fact that my property tax bill went up last year, making it harder to keep the house in good shape. I think the Democrats are more likely to address the health and cost‑of‑living issues that affect me daily, even if they don't always get the rural economy right. The Republicans seem more interested in cutting regulations and taxes, which could help some small businesses, but I'm skeptical about how that would affect the health services I depend on.
