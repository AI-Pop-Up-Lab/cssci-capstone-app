## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Fairbanks. I spent most of my adult life teaching physics at the University of Alaska Fairbanks, and after retiring I still keep a stack of textbooks and a battered old laptop for the occasional online lecture or research hobby. My wife passed away five years ago, and my two children live in Seattle and Denver, so most weeks I’m on my own, though we talk on video calls every Sunday. My days start early with a strong cup of coffee, the morning paper, and a quick walk with my old Labrador, Milo, around the neighborhood. I volunteer twice a week at the local senior center, helping run a computer class for other retirees. Grocery shopping is a weekly chore—prices have been creeping up, especially for fresh produce, so I try to stretch my budget by buying frozen veggies and hunting for sales. In the evenings I usually watch the news, read a bit on my tablet, and sometimes work on a hobby project building a small solar charger for my garden lights. I keep a modest pension, a small investment portfolio, and a bit of social security, which covers my basic expenses.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more likely to protect funding for the university and research grants that mattered to my field, and because their stance on expanding broadband in rural Alaska hit close to home. I also appreciated the focus on health‑care subsidies that helped keep my prescription costs manageable. Over the past two years I’ve watched the political conversation get louder and more polarized, and while I still lean Democratic, I’m not a fan of the endless shouting. I think the Republicans in Congress have been too quick to roll back environmental regulations that are crucial for Alaska’s fragile ecosystems, and their tax proposals often seem aimed at big oil companies without considering the impact on small communities like mine. That said, I’m skeptical of any party that promises sweeping changes without a clear plan, and I get annoyed when politicians ignore the practicalities of life in a remote state.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve already filled out my ballot in advance. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of heating fuel as winter approaches, and the lack of reliable high‑speed internet that makes it hard to stay connected with my kids and keep up with medical appointments online. I think the Democrats are generally more attentive to these issues, but I wish they’d be more realistic about budget constraints. The Republicans, in my view, keep talking about “energy independence” while ignoring the real cost of keeping homes warm in the far north.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR and the major network sites most evenings after dinner, and I also skim ABC, CBS, and NBC news feeds in the mornings while I’m having coffee. It adds up to about nine hours a week, split between a half‑hour morning scroll and a couple of longer sessions after work.
