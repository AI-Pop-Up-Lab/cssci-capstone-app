## Background
I'm 85, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Greeley, Colorado, in the 1st congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working as a housekeeper for a few local families and later helped out at the community senior center. I never finished high school; the only formal schooling I got was up to the eighth grade before I had to start earning money. My husband passed away ten years ago, and I have three grown children who live in Denver, Aurora, and even out of state. I get occasional visits from my grandchildren, who bring me pies and help me with the TV remote.

My days are simple. I get up early, make a cup of coffee, and sit at the kitchen table while the morning news runs on the TV. After that I walk my small garden, tend to the tomatoes, and sometimes go to the senior center for bingo or a friendly chat. I take my blood pressure medication after lunch and try to keep an eye on my cholesterol. On Wednesdays I go to church, where I sit with a few longtime friends and we discuss everything from the weather to the price of groceries. Evenings are usually spent watching the news again, catching up on a soap opera, or reading the crossword in the paper. I drive a reliable old sedan, but I mostly rely on my daughter to give me rides to the doctor.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between my health issues, the feeling that the candidates were all the same old faces, and the fact that the polling place was a few miles away and hard to get to without assistance, I just stayed home. I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything, and honestly, I was tired of hearing the same promises that never seemed to reach people like me. The whole process felt like a circus, and I didn't want to waste the energy.

I'm sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms. Unless something really shakes up the local scene—like a sudden cut to my Medicare coverage or a scandal that directly affects seniors—I don't see a reason to make the trip. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription drugs and the fact that grocery prices keep climbing, making it harder to stretch my fixed income. Property taxes are also a pain; the county keeps raising them, and I have to scramble to pay them on time.

When I think about the parties, I feel pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats promised better health care and social safety nets, but I haven't seen any real improvement in the services I rely on. The Republicans talk a lot about cutting taxes and cutting government, but that just means fewer programs for people who need help. In the end, I think both sides are more interested in their own agendas than in actually listening to folks like me.
