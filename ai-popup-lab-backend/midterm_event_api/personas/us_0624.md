## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Santa Fe, just a short drive from the Plaza where I grew up. I spent 35 years teaching elementary school math, but I never finished a bachelor's degree because I needed to work full‑time to support my family. I retired five years ago, and now most of my days revolve around a routine that feels comfortable. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and sit on the porch watching the sunrise while the radio plays classic country tunes. After that I tend to my garden—tomatoes, chilies, and a few rosemary bushes—because fresh veggies keep the grocery bill down.

My husband passed away three years back, so I spend a lot of time with my daughter, who lives in Albuquerque and visits us on weekends. We usually go to the local farmers market or take a short drive to the art galleries downtown. In the afternoons I volunteer at the public library, shelving books and helping the occasional teenager find a good novel. Once a week I attend a small Bible study group at the church across the street; the fellowship there is one of my main social outlets. I still drive my old sedan to the grocery store, the pharmacy, and the community health clinic, where I’m on a senior discount card. The cost of heating in winter and water bills in summer are the biggest line items in my budget.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about my health coverage and the rising cost of my prescription meds. After the pandemic, I saw how fragile the safety net can be, and the Democratic platform promised to protect Medicare and push for more affordable drugs. I also liked the emphasis on protecting public lands, which matters a lot to me here in New Mexico where the desert and mountains are part of everyday life.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are being acted on. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for seniors like me, even though the party sometimes seems divided and slow to deliver. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about cutting taxes and deregulating energy, but that feels like it would hurt the environment I love and make it harder for people on fixed incomes to get help.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the price of my blood pressure medication and the property tax hikes that the county council is proposing to fund a new road project. I also worry about water restrictions as the Rio Grande runs low; any policy that ignores that will hurt my garden and the whole community. I view the Democratic Party as the one that at least tries to address these issues, even if they stumble sometimes. The Republican Party seems more focused on business interests and less on the everyday struggles of older folks, which makes me uneasy.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR while I garden in the mornings and catch up on ABC News and Newsweek articles in the evenings, totaling about five hours a week.
