## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Hamilton Township, Mercer County, right inside the 2nd congressional district. I work as an HVAC technician for a regional service company; I picked up the trade after getting an associate degree at a community college and have been on the road for about thirty years. My wife and I own a modest two‑bedroom house that we bought when the kids were still in high school. Our two adult daughters live out of state now, and we help take care of my mother, who has arthritis and needs a little help getting around. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30, eat a quick coffee and toast, then drive to the shop where I spend eight hours fixing furnaces and air conditioners for homes and small businesses. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the local VFW hall to catch a game on the TV. Weekends are a mix of yard work, fixing stuff around the house, and visiting my mom’s church service on Sundays. I’m not a big traveler; most of my free time is spent in the backyard with a grill or watching the news on my phone while I wait for the kids to finish their shifts at the restaurant where they work.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on taxes and energy made more sense for someone in my line of work. The idea of cutting corporate taxes and rolling back regulations seemed like it would keep my company busy and help keep my paycheck steady. I also liked the talk about securing the border, even though I don’t have any direct connection to immigration issues; it just felt like a safe bet for “law and order.” Since then I’ve watched the headlines and the rhetoric got louder, but I still think the GOP is the better choice for keeping costs down on things like fuel and property taxes. I’m definitely going to the polls again for the 2026 midterms; I don’t have the time or desire to sit this one out, especially with the upcoming state budget debates that could raise my property tax bill again. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of gas for my truck and the ever‑increasing property taxes that make it harder to stay in the house we’ve paid off. I also keep an eye on health‑care premiums because I’m getting older and any jump in costs would hit my family hard. As for the parties, I see Democrats as the ones pushing more taxes and expanding government programs that feel out of touch with working‑class folks. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about letting people keep more of what they earn and less government interference in my daily work. I get annoyed when the media spins everything into a culture war, but I still think the GOP is the side that looks out for my pocketbook.
