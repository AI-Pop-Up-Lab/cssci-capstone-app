## Background  
I'm 25 and I live in a small apartment on the north side of Muskegon, right near the lakefront factories. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I picked up a job stacking pallets at the local distribution center. The shift starts at 5 a.m., so I’m usually up before sunrise, grabbing a coffee from the corner donut shop and scrolling on my phone while I wait for the truck. After the 10‑hour shift I come home, take a quick shower, and either cook something cheap—usually ramen or frozen pizza—or order takeout if I have a little extra cash. I share the place with a roommate who works as a bartender; we split rent and utilities, which is about $800 a month total. On weekends I sometimes help my mom with yard work in the suburbs, or I meet up with a few friends at the local bar for a couple of drinks. I don’t have kids, and I’m not in a serious relationship; most of my free time is spent watching short videos on my phone or playing a little bit of Fortnite when I can find the bandwidth.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about people like me who are just trying to make rent and keep a steady job. The campaign ads were all the same—big promises and empty talk—so I just stayed home and watched the news later, if I bothered. I’m not really into the whole political drama; it feels like a game for people with too much time and too much money. I’m planning to sit this one out too, unless something huge happens locally—like a big plant closing or a sudden spike in utility bills—that forces me to pay attention. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rent going up and the price of groceries, especially fresh produce. I also worry about the local job market; if the warehouse cuts shifts, I’m out of work fast. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to want higher taxes and more regulation, which feels like a burden when I’m already stretched thin. The Republicans talk a lot about “law and order” and “cutting taxes,” but they also push policies that make it harder for workers like me to get decent health coverage or a living wage. Neither side feels like they’re listening to the folks who actually have to put food on the table.
