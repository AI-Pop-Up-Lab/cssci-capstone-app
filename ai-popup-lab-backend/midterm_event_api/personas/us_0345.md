## Background
I'm 55 and I live in the small town of Bogalusa, right in the heart of Louisiana's 5th congressional district. I work full‑time as a dental hygienist at the local clinic, a job I got after completing a two‑year program at a community college in Baton Rouge. My husband, who used to work in the lumber yard before he retired two years ago, helps out with the garden and keeps the house warm in the winter. We have two adult children—one lives in Houston with a family, the other is back home taking night classes to finish a degree. Most days I get up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and head to the clinic for a 7 a.m. shift. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids’ laundry, and then we all sit down for dinner around 6 p.m. On weekends I like to fish at the nearby lake, go to the local church service on Sunday, and sometimes help out with the community clean‑up day. Money is tight enough that we keep a careful eye on the utility bills and try to stretch the grocery budget, but we get by.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on lower taxes and less regulation would help small‑business owners like the dental clinic where I work. I also liked the talk about protecting “traditional values,” which resonated with what I hear at church. Since then, I’ve been watching the news and hearing a lot of complaints about the national debt and the way the Democrats keep pushing for higher taxes on the middle class. That makes me skeptical of any big‑government solutions. I’m not really into the political theater; I just want the government to stay out of my paycheck and let me keep what I earn.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are the rising cost of gasoline and the fact that the local hospital is talking about cutting some services, which would affect a lot of folks in our town. I also worry about property taxes going up because the county keeps buying up land for new development. When I think about the parties, the Republicans seem more in tune with my desire for lower taxes and less interference, even if I don’t agree with everything they say. The Democrats feel like they’re always promising more programs that will end up being paid for by higher taxes, and that just doesn’t sit right with me. I’m not a political analyst, I just vote for the side that promises to keep my bills down and let me keep working without the government looking over my shoulder.
