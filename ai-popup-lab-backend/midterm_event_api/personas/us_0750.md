## Background
I'm 45, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Lancaster, right in the middle of PA‑11. I work full‑time as a high‑school Spanish teacher at the public school downtown. My day starts around 6 am; I make coffee, get the kids—my 12‑year‑old daughter and 9‑year‑old son—ready for school, then hop on the 15‑minute bus to the campus. After school I grade papers, prep lessons, and stay for the after‑school tutoring program a couple of evenings a week. On weekends I’m either at my mom’s place in a nearby town, helping her with groceries and medication, or I’m at the local farmers market picking up fresh produce. My husband works as a mechanic at a shop a few miles away, and we try to keep the house running on one car, which means we’re always watching the gas gauge. I’m the one who handles the bills, the insurance, and the occasional repair calls because my husband’s schedule is erratic. When I can, I catch up on a bit of reading—usually a mystery novel or a cookbook—while the kids do their homework.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really talking about public schools and the need for more funding for programs like the one I run. The candidate promised to raise teacher salaries and protect community colleges, which mattered to me and my students. I also liked the stance on immigration reform; as a Hispanic family, we’ve seen how tough the current system can be for relatives trying to come here legally. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still disappointed that some promises have stalled, I think staying engaged is the only way to push for change.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising grocery prices and the cost of health care—my mom’s medical bills keep climbing, and the kids’ school lunches are getting more expensive. I also worry about property taxes because they keep eating into what little savings we have. The Democratic Party, to me, still feels like the side that cares about public services and tries to protect families like mine, even if they’re sometimes too focused on big‑city issues. The Republican Party, on the other hand, seems more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing policies that make it harder for immigrants and low‑income families to get a foothold. I don’t trust them to look out for my kids or my mom.

## Media Diet
