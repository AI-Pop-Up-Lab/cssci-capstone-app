## Background

I'm 65 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Bangor, right in the middle of Maine's 2nd congressional district. My wife Rosa and I have been married for 38 years; she retired from the local library a few years back, and I spend most of my days doing part‑time maintenance work at the community college where I once took some night classes but never finished a degree. I grew up in a Spanish‑speaking household in Boston, moved here in my thirties when I got a job on a lobster boat, and never really left. Our two kids are both out of state—one in Texas working as a software tester, the other in Ohio teaching high school Spanish—so most evenings are just the two of us watching the news or tinkering around the house.

My day starts early, around 6 a.m., with a strong cup of coffee and a quick scan of the headlines on my tablet while I feed the chickens in the backyard. After that I head to the college, where I’m on call for any plumbing or HVAC fixes that come up. The job isn’t demanding, but it keeps me moving and lets me stay in touch with a younger crowd. When I’m not at work, I’m often at the senior center down the road, playing cards or helping out with the Spanish conversation group I started a few years ago. On weekends I like to take Rosa to the farmer’s market, pick up fresh berries, and sometimes we’ll drive up to Acadia for a short hike. Money is tight enough that we watch our budget closely, but we’re not strangers to the occasional repair bill or the rising cost of prescription meds.

## Politics and this election

I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really paying attention to health‑care costs and the need for better broadband in rural areas. My wife had a rough time last year getting her prescription refilled, and the promises about expanding telehealth felt like something that could actually help us. I also liked the candidate’s stance on protecting the Atlantic fisheries, which is still a big part of the local economy even though I’m not on a boat anymore.

Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are more likely to push for the kind of social safety net we need as we get older, but I’m also annoyed when they get tangled up in culture wars that feel far removed from my day‑to‑day concerns. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about “big government” and cutting taxes, which sounds good until you think about how that would affect funding for the senior center programs I rely on. I also don’t trust the rhetoric about loosening gun restrictions; here in Maine, hunting is part of life, and I’m not comfortable with policies that could make it harder for responsible hunters to get licenses.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top worries right now are the price of groceries—everything from fresh fish to the bread at the market is climbing—and the cost of health‑care, especially the premiums for my Medicare Part D plan. I also worry about property taxes; the town has been looking at raising rates to fund road repairs, and that would bite right into our fixed income. My view of the parties is pretty straightforward: Democrats are at least trying to address the things that matter to seniors, even if they’re not perfect, while Republicans seem more interested in fighting over issues that don’t affect my daily life.

## Media Diet

I listen to NPR’s morning news feed while I’m making coffee, then catch the evening updates on ABC News after dinner. I also skim a couple of articles on CBS News during my lunch break at the college, which adds up to about six hours a week.
