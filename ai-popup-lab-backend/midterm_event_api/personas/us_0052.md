## Background
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a warehouse associate at a distribution center just outside Little Rock. I grew up in a small town near Conway, and after a couple of semesters at a community college I decided to quit and start earning money to help my parents with the mortgage. I still live in a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who’s in a similar stage—both of us trying to save enough for a down payment. My days start early; I’m usually up by 5:30 am, grab a coffee from the corner Denny’s, and catch the 6:15 am bus to the warehouse. The shift runs until about 3 pm, then I head home, do a quick grocery run at the local Walmart, and spend the evening either watching YouTube, scrolling through my phone, or helping my roommate with his job applications. On weekends I either work a second shift or help out on my dad’s farm a few hours, hauling feed and fixing fences. I’m not married, no kids, and I try to keep a low profile—nothing fancy, just the usual routine of a young guy trying to get by.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing feels like a circus, and between long shifts, trying to make rent, and the constant stream of ads promising change that never shows up, I just didn’t see the point. I figured my single vote wouldn’t move the needle, and the candidates all sounded the same—either too extreme or too vague. The polls and the news kept shouting about “the battle for the soul of the nation,” but I’m more worried about whether my paycheck will cover my utilities next month. I’ve been pretty cynical about the system for a while now, so I’m leaning toward sitting this 2026 midterm out as well, unless something really hits home—like a major factory closing in the district or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces me out of my apartment.

My top everyday concerns are paying rent and keeping grocery prices from climbing any higher. The warehouse pay is decent but not enough to cushion a sudden increase in living costs, and I see the price tags at the store creeping up every week. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are stuck in Washington talking about climate goals and social programs that feel distant from a guy who’s just trying to get home after a 10‑hour shift. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep promising tax cuts and “pro‑business” policies, but I’m not convinced they’ll actually help someone on the lower end of the wage scale. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the day‑to‑day problems that people like me face. So far, I’m not motivated enough to go to the polls unless a candidate shows up at the warehouse and actually talks about wages, health benefits, or affordable housing.
