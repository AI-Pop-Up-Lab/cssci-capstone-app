## Background  
I'm 80, and I live in Watertown, NY, right in the heart of the 18th Congressional District. I spent 35 years teaching history at the local high school before I finally retired last spring. My wife passed away five years ago, so the house is just me and a few pictures of family on the walls. My son lives in Syracuse and visits a couple of times a year, and my daughter is out in Florida with her own kids. I help out at the senior center twice a week, mostly serving coffee and shuffling cards, which keeps me out of the house and gives me a reason to get dressed.

My days are pretty routine. I get up early, make a strong cup of coffee, and flip through the printed New York Post while the kettle whistles. After breakfast I tend to the garden out back—tomatoes, beans, a few perennials—that's where I spend a good chunk of the morning. Around noon I take a short walk to the post office, pick up my mail, and sometimes stop at the grocery store for a few essentials. Evenings are for TV; I usually have the news on while I eat dinner, then I settle into a chair with a crossword puzzle before heading to bed. I drive a reliable old sedan, and I’m still comfortable with a little bit of DIY around the house, even if my knees protest.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for retirees like me. I was tired of hearing about ever‑higher taxes on my property and the push for more government‑run health programs that seemed to ignore the fact that most of us already have Medicare and just want the premiums to stay low. The candidate’s stance on border security also resonated; I’ve seen the impact of illegal dumping on the lake, and I wanted stricter enforcement. I wasn’t thrilled with the whole campaign circus, but the Republican ticket felt like the lesser of two evils for my wallet and my sense of personal responsibility.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. I’ve been watching the local Senate race and the House race, and I think my vote can still tip the scale on issues that affect my community, like funding for the senior center and the state’s property‑tax cap. My top everyday concerns are keeping my property tax bill from blowing up and making sure my prescription drug costs don’t climb any higher. I also worry about gas prices because I still drive to the grocery store and the senior center, and any spike hits my fixed income hard.

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward. The Democrats seem more interested in expanding programs that end up costing us more in the long run, and they keep talking about big‑government solutions that feel detached from the real world of small‑town New York. The Republicans, for the most part, talk about cutting waste and protecting our pockets, which I appreciate, even if I sometimes think they could be a bit more compassionate about folks who truly need help. I’m not a political theorist; I just want the next four years to be less about tax hikes and more about keeping the basics affordable.
