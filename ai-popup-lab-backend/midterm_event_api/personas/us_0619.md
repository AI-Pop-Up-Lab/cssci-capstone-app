## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Mount Laurel, a quiet suburb in New Jersey's 6th district. I work as a forklift supervisor at a large warehouse near the highway; the job's physical but the shift schedule is predictable, so I can be home for dinner most nights. My wife and I have been married 22 years, and we have two kids – a 19‑year‑old daughter who's at a community college out of state and a 15‑year‑old son who rides the bus to high school. Most mornings I drop the son off, grab a coffee from the corner deli, and head to the loading dock. After my shift I usually swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for the family, and try to help my son with his math homework. Weekends are a mix of yard work, catching a baseball game on TV, and occasional trips to my parents' house in Edison. I don't have a car beyond the work truck and our family sedan, so I rely on the highway for commuting and the occasional ride‑share when I need to run errands farther away. I grew up in a modest immigrant household; my parents worked long hours in a textile factory, and they stressed the importance of hard work and staying out of trouble.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate. I wasn't thrilled about any of the options, but the Republican platform felt like a step backward on immigration and health care, and the Democrat promised to protect the community health clinic that my wife uses for her asthma. I also liked the promise of more funding for public schools, which mattered for my son's education. I didn't feel like I was voting for a perfect vision, just the lesser of two evils, and I hoped the administration would keep the pressure on Congress to pass some modest infrastructure upgrades that would improve the roads near my warehouse.

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns are the rising property taxes on my home and the steady climb in gasoline prices that make my commute more expensive. I also keep an eye on health‑care costs because my wife's medication bills keep creeping up. I think the Democratic Party is generally more in tune with working‑class families like mine, especially on health and education, but sometimes I get frustrated with the endless talking points and lack of concrete action. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for big corporations and pushing stricter immigration rules that feel disconnected from my everyday reality. I don't trust them to look out for people who are just trying to get by.
