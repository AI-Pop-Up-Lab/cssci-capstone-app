## Background  
I'm 60, and I work as a dental hygienist at a busy practice in Henderson, Nevada, right in the heart of the 3rd congressional district. My husband retired from the construction business a few years ago, so I'm the one who still drives to work most mornings, dropping off the kids’ college books at the university in Las Vegas before my shift starts at 8 a.m. After work I usually stop by the grocery store on Boulder Highway, where I chat with the same cashiers about the price of avocados and the latest telenovela on TV.  

We have two adult children—one lives in Phoenix with a family, the other is back home caring for my aging mother, who needs help with her medication. On Sundays we attend mass at a local Spanish‑speaking parish, and after that I volunteer a few hours a month at the community center, helping with English‑as‑a‑second‑language classes. My days are a mix of patient charts, traffic on I‑15, and trying to keep the house cool when the desert heat spikes. I’m also part of a neighborhood watch group that meets once a month to discuss safety and pothole repairs.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to health‑care access, which matters a lot in my line of work, and because I appreciated the stance on immigration reform that would make life easier for my extended family back in Mexico. I also liked the emphasis on expanding community health clinics, something I see a real shortage of in our area.  

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My biggest worries are the rising rent in Henderson—my landlord just raised the lease again—and the cost of prescription drugs, which are getting harder to afford on my husband’s fixed pension. I’m also watching what the Republican candidates are saying about border security; I feel they often ignore the contributions of immigrant families like mine and push policies that could hurt my brother who runs a small landscaping business that employs several other Hispanic workers. The Democrats, in my view, are still the better bet for protecting social services, even if they sometimes stumble on economic messaging. I’m not thrilled with either side, but I’m more inclined to stick with the party that at least tries to address the issues that hit my family directly.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m getting ready for work, catch the ABC News evening wrap‑up on my tablet after dinner, and skim the CBS News headlines during my lunch break, adding up to about five hours a week.
