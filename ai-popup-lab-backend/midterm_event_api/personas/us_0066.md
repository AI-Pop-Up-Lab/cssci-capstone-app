## Background
I'm 55 and I live in the foothills of Prescott, Arizona, in a modest ranch‑style house that I helped buy when my kids were still in high school. I work as a regional manager for a construction firm that builds commercial projects across the state. My job means I’m on the road a lot, checking sites, meeting with subcontractors, and making sure the crews stay on schedule. I have a master’s degree in civil engineering, which I earned after I’d already been working for a few years; the extra credential helped me move up into management.

My husband, who retired from the Navy a decade ago, spends most of his time tinkering in the garage and helping out at the local veterans’ center. We have two adult children—one lives in Phoenix, the other in Tucson—so the house is quieter than it used to be, but we still get regular visits, especially around holidays. My typical day starts around 5:30 am with a quick coffee while scrolling through the news on my phone. I’m at the office by 7 am, lunch is usually a sandwich at the job site, and I’m home by 6 pm. Evenings are for cooking dinner, a little TV, and sometimes a church gathering on Wednesdays. On weekends I either tend to the garden, do some DIY projects around the house, or run errands at the grocery store and the local pharmacy.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s focus on lower taxes and fewer regulations was the best way to keep my business profitable and the local economy growing. The border security promises also mattered to me; living near the northern part of the state, I’ve seen a lot of traffic that crosses state lines, and I’m worried about illegal activity that can hurt small towns like ours. I wasn’t thrilled with the Democrats’ push for higher corporate taxes and what I see as a tendency to over‑regulate the construction industry, which would make it harder to get jobs done on time and on budget.

Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the national conversation has shifted. Inflation has been a pain, especially when it squeezes the cost of building materials and the price of groceries at the same time. I’ve also heard a lot of talk about the federal debt, and that worries me because I’m saving for a comfortable retirement. The Republican candidates this year keep promising to keep taxes low and to protect the border, which aligns with what I care about. The Democratic side, in my view, keeps talking about big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with the everyday struggles of people who run small businesses or work blue‑collar jobs.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax increase that the county has proposed to fund new schools. I also keep an eye on immigration policy because I think it directly affects public safety and the local labor market. As for the parties, I think the GOP generally understands the need for a leaner government and respects personal responsibility, while the Democrats often seem more interested in expanding government programs and raising taxes, which I see as a threat to my business and my family’s financial security.
