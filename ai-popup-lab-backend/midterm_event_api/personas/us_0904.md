## Background  
I'm 50 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the north side of Burlington, right near the waterfront trail. I work as a dental hygienist at a downtown practice; the job's steady hours and decent benefits are why I stuck with the associate‑degree program at the community college after high school. My partner, a freelance graphic designer, works from home most days, and we have a teenage daughter who just started her senior year at the local high school.  

My mornings start at 6:30 a.m. with a quick espresso from the corner coffee shop, then a 15‑minute bike ride to the clinic. I usually grab a bagel and skim the headlines on my phone while waiting for the elevator. The workday is pretty routine—cleaning, patient education, and a lot of insurance paperwork. After my shift ends at 3 p.m., I head back home, pick up my daughter from school, and we either cook dinner together or order a pizza on nights when I'm too wiped out.  

Evenings are low‑key: we might watch a Netflix series, do a puzzle, or I’ll catch up on the news while my partner sketches. On weekends I like to hike the Green Mountains, visit the farmer’s market, and volunteer a few hours at the local food pantry. I don’t own a car; the bike and occasional rideshare get me where I need to go. My rent is $1,250 a month, and with heating bills creeping up each winter, budgeting feels like a full‑time job.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was finally taking climate change seriously and protecting health‑care access—two issues that hit close to home as a health‑care worker and a mother of a teen. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding broadband in rural parts of Vermont, which would help my partner’s freelance business.  

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for protecting our environment and keeping health‑care affordable, even if I’m frustrated by the occasional infighting and slow progress. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for big corporations than helping families like mine. Their rhetoric on “energy independence” feels like a code for rolling back the clean‑energy standards we need to keep our winters bearable.  

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the steep property taxes that make it hard to plan for the future. I’m also watching the local race for state senate because the winner will influence how much funding our schools and the community health center get. I’m not a political theorist, but I’m pretty sure the party that backs renewable projects and protects Medicaid will be the one I support.

## Media Diet  
I usually listen to NPR’s morning news while I bike to work, then catch the ABC evening update after dinner. On the weekends I skim a few articles on Newsweek to see what’s happening nationally.
