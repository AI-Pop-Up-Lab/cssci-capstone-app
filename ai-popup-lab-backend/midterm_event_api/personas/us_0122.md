## Background  
I'm 19 and I work full‑time as a cashier at a grocery store in the Fair Haven section of New Haven. I live in a tiny two‑bedroom walk‑up with a roommate I met at the job; we split rent and utilities, and we each keep a small kitchen for quick meals. My parents still live in Hartford, so I drive down on Sundays to help my mom with grocery shopping and to catch up on family news. Most weekdays I start my shift at 7 a.m., finish around 3 p.m., then head straight to the community college campus for a couple of English‑as‑a‑Second‑Language classes. I’m trying to get an associate’s degree, but the cost and the need to keep working make it a slow grind. After class I usually stop by the local library to use the internet, then come home, make a cheap dinner—often ramen or a frozen pizza—and scroll through my phone before bed. On weekends I either work the evening shift at the store or hang out at the park with friends, playing pickup basketball or just sitting on the bench and listening to music. I don’t own a car; the bus route is decent enough to get me to work, school, and the occasional trip to my parents’ house.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and that my vote wouldn’t change anything. Between juggling a full‑time job, night classes, and trying to keep my rent from going up, there was no time or energy left to fill out a ballot, look up the candidates, or even figure out which polling place was open. The ads were all the same—talking about “change” or “law and order” without touching on the real stuff that matters to me, like affordable housing or the price of groceries. I also got the sense that both parties were just different flavors of the same old promises, and that didn’t motivate me to show up.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic hits my neighborhood—like a big rent hike or a local school closure—I don’t see a reason to make the trip to the polls. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and dealing with the rising cost of food; every week I’m watching my budget get tighter and the price of a pound of chicken go up a couple of dollars. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats because they keep talking about climate policy and student debt relief while the city council keeps raising property taxes that hit renters like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to ignore the fact that a lot of families like mine are immigrants or kids of immigrants, and they push for stricter immigration rules that feel hostile. Overall, I’m fed up with the political chatter and just want policies that keep my rent low and my grocery bill manageable.
