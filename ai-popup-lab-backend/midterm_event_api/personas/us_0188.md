## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was more honest about keeping taxes low and protecting jobs like the ones I have at the warehouse. I’m tired of hearing about big‑government solutions that never seem to reach people like me, and the GOP’s message about “law and order” and “personal responsibility” resonated when I was trying to make ends meet. The Democratic ads kept talking about climate policies and student loan forgiveness, which felt far away from my daily grind. I also liked the candidate’s tough stance on illegal immigration; even though my family came here from abroad, I think the borders need to be tighter so people like me don’t get stuck in a system that seems to favor newcomers over long‑time residents.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing. If the next Congress can pass something that helps keep wages from stagnating or at least stops the price hikes at the store, I’ll be paying attention. I see the Republican Party as the one that actually talks about job security and lower taxes, even if they sometimes sound harsh. The Democrats feel like they’re more interested in identity politics and big‑government programs that don’t trickle down to people working minimum‑wage jobs. I’m not a political scholar, I just want a government that lets me keep my paycheck and not worry about my next rent check.
