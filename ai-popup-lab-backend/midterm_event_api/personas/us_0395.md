## Background  
I'm 84 and I live in a little house on the edge of Upper Marlboro, right in the middle of Maryland’s 5th district. I spent most of my working life as a carpenter for a regional contractor, and after I retired I picked up a few evening classes at the community college but never finished a degree. My wife passed away five years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids who live in Baltimore and the D.C. suburbs. They call me on Sundays and bring over a casserole now and then, but most days I’m on my own.  

My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee on the porch while I read the paper—mostly the local edition, but I skim the national sections for headlines. After that I take a short walk around the neighborhood, sometimes stopping to chat with the neighbor who runs the hardware store. I still do a little handyman work for folks on the block, fixing leaky faucets or hanging shelves. In the afternoons I watch a bit of TV, usually the news, and then I head to the senior center where I help with a woodworking class. Evenings are spent watching a game on the TV or listening to the radio before I turn in around ten. I keep an eye on my Medicare statements and try not to let the heating bill get too high in the winter.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for retirees like me. The talk about cutting taxes on Social Security and protecting the border resonated with my belief that the government should stay out of my wallet as much as possible. I also liked the emphasis on “law and order,” which made sense to someone who’s seen a lot of change in the neighborhoods I grew up in.  

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. The biggest thing on my mind is keeping my Medicare benefits intact and making sure the prescription drug prices don’t keep climbing. I’m also worried about the property tax hikes that the county council has been talking about; my fixed pension can’t stretch much farther. I’m not excited about politics, but I feel like if I stay home the other side will just push through whatever they want.  

My view of the parties is pretty blunt. The Democrats seem to think every problem can be solved by more government programs, and that usually means higher taxes for people like me. I get the occasional good idea from them—like expanding broadband in rural areas—but most of the time it feels like they’re trying to control everything. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk a lot about cutting taxes and supporting the military, which I like, but they also spend a lot of time bickering on the news and seem to forget about everyday folks. Still, I think they’re the better bet for keeping my retirement income safe.

## Media Diet  
I split my six hours of news watching between the morning and evening. I usually catch a quick roundup on NBC News before work, then later in the day I scroll through articles on CNBC and skim the headlines on Breitbart to see what’s happening in the country.
