## Background  
I'm 50, and I live in Ada, a small town just north of Oklahoma City that falls in the 3rd congressional district. I work as a customer‑service supervisor for the regional electric cooperative; most days I start at 7 a.m., grab a coffee, and drive the 20‑minute stretch to the office where I field calls about outages and billing questions. I have some college credits from a community college where I studied business, but I never finished a degree because I needed to start working to help support my family.

I'm married to a mechanic who runs a shop out of our garage, and we have two kids—17‑year‑old Maya, who just finished her senior year and is prepping for college applications, and 14‑year‑old Ethan, who spends most of his free time on his phone playing video games. My mother lives with us; she’s in her late 70s and needs help with meds and groceries, so we all chip in. My day usually ends with a quick dinner, a load of laundry, and then a few minutes scrolling through news sites before I fall asleep around 11 p.m. On weekends I try to get the kids to the park or help Maya with SAT prep, but often we end up watching a movie together or doing chores around the house.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole process was a circus—candidates yelling at each other, ads that all sounded the same, and then the same old promises that never seem to change anything in my life. Between long shifts at work, caring for my mom, and trying to keep Maya on track for college, I just couldn't fit a trip to the polls into my schedule. I also get the sense that my vote doesn't matter; the district leans heavily Republican, and the Democratic side never seems to address the practical stuff I deal with day to day, like rising utility rates or the cost of my mom's prescriptions.

I'm sitting this one out again in 2026, unless something really forces me to—like a major outage that leaves the whole town in the dark for weeks, or a sudden change to health‑care coverage that would hit my family hard. My top everyday concerns are the electric bill, which keeps creeping up even though I work for the co‑op, and the cost of health care for my mother and the kids. I think both parties are missing the mark: the GOP talks about lower taxes but seems to ignore the fact that lower taxes on the wealthy don't translate to lower bills for folks like me, while the Democrats keep pushing big‑government solutions that feel distant from our rural‑suburban reality. I’m pretty much an independent who just wants the next election to actually improve the things I deal with daily.
