## Background  
I'm 79, and I live in the little borough of Bellefonte, just a short drive from the campus in State College. I spent most of my working life as an HVAC technician after getting an associate’s degree at the local community college, and I retired ten years ago when I qualified for full Social Security benefits. My wife of 55 years still keeps the house tidy, and our two grown‑up kids live in Pittsburgh and Philadelphia, so we get occasional visits from the grandkids in the summer.  

My days are pretty routine now. I get up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and read the printed newspaper while the dog, a rescued mutt named Buster, waits for his morning walk. After a short walk around the neighborhood, I tend to the vegetable garden behind the house—tomatoes, beans, a few herbs. Around noon I usually have a sandwich and turn on the TV for the local news, then I spend the afternoon either fixing things around the house, chatting with my wife on the porch, or watching a game of the Phillies on the big screen. In the evenings I like to listen to an NPR podcast while Buster naps, and before bed I often flip through a crossword puzzle.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt that the party cared more about Medicare and prescription drug prices, which matter a lot when you’re on a fixed income. I also liked the candidate’s promise to protect Social Security from any cuts, something that keeps my mind at ease when I hear talk about entitlement reform. The campaign rallies in State College gave me a chance to see a few folks from my community, and that personal connection nudged me over the line.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the property tax hikes the county has been proposing to fund new road projects. I also keep an eye on whether the next round of infrastructure spending will include anything that helps seniors like me stay mobile and safe. I think the Democrats generally get the senior issues right, even if they sometimes get tangled up in culture wars I don’t follow. The Republicans, in my view, talk a lot about cutting taxes but that mostly helps the wealthy; I’m not convinced they’ll protect Medicare or Social Security the way I need them to.
