## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on taxes and small‑business support resonated with my own financial worries. My job isn’t exactly high‑pay yet, and the idea of lower corporate taxes and fewer regulations seemed like it could eventually translate into better wages or at least a healthier economy for people like me. I was also fed up with the constant partisan fights I saw on the news; the Democrats, in my view, were pushing for more government hand‑outs and higher taxes that would hit middle‑class earners hard. I didn’t feel a strong personal connection to the Democratic candidates, so I went with the GOP ballot.

Going into the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely planning to vote again. My biggest everyday concerns are still rent and grocery prices—both have crept up since last year, and my student loan payments are still a heavy weight. I also keep an eye on local job growth because the tech scene in the suburbs can be volatile. I’m not a party‑hardcore; I think the Republicans are generally better for business and give me hope that taxes won’t keep rising, but I’m annoyed when some of their rhetoric turns overly combative or ignores social issues that matter to my friends. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more focused on identity politics and big‑government solutions that feel disconnected from my day‑to‑day financial reality. Overall, I’m leaning toward the GOP candidates who promise pragmatic economic policies, even if I’m not thrilled with every single thing they say.
