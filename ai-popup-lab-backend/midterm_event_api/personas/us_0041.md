## Background
I'm 75, and I live in a modest ranch-style house on the outskirts of Rogers, Arkansas, right in the middle of the 1st congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working as a bank teller and later as a clerical assistant for the county clerk's office. I never went beyond the two‑year community college program I took after high school, but those years gave me enough skills to handle the paperwork and the occasional computer work. I retired about eight years ago, and since then my days are a mix of routine and small projects.

My husband passed away three years back, so it's just me now, but I have two grown children who live in Little Rock and Dallas. They call most weekends, and my granddaughter visits once a month and stays for a few days. I keep busy with my garden—tomatoes, beans, and a little patch of herbs—because fresh veggies are cheaper than the grocery store these days. I also volunteer twice a week at the senior center down the road, helping with bingo and reading to folks who need a little company. Church on Sunday mornings is still a big part of my week; I sit in the pews of the local Baptist congregation, sing hymns, and stay after service for coffee and gossip.

Mornings start early for me. I brew a cup of coffee, flip on the TV, and catch the local news before the national segments come on. After that I tend to the garden, walk the short trail behind my house, and then spend a few hours on the computer checking my email and the news sites I trust. Evenings are for a quick dinner, a bit of TV, and sometimes a phone call with my daughter. I keep a small notebook of bills and appointments; staying organized is the only way I can keep my head above water on a fixed income.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about keeping taxes low and protecting the values I grew up with. The candidate promised to keep Medicare and Social Security intact, and that was enough for me. I also liked the emphasis on a strong military and the idea that the government shouldn't be telling me how to run my farm or my garden. The Democrats seemed to want more regulations and higher taxes, and that just didn't sit right with me after working my whole life to save a little.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes the county has been talking about. The pharmacy down the road raised prices on my blood pressure meds again this month, and I have to stretch my budget tighter than ever. At the same time, the county assessor sent out notices that my property tax could go up another few hundred dollars next year, which feels like a slap in the face after I've already paid for the house for decades.

When I think about the parties, I see Republicans as the ones who understand ordinary folks like me. They talk about keeping the cost of living down and protecting the right to own guns, which matters when you have to hunt for food in the winter. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more interested in pushing big ideas that never touch the real problems on Main Street. I don't trust their promises about free college or big government programs; they just sound like more taxes and more bureaucracy. So far, I'm leaning heavily toward the GOP, and unless something dramatic happens—like a scandal that hits my own community—I plan to keep supporting them.
