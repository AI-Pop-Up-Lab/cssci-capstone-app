## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a small house on the edge of the Warm Springs Reservation, just outside the town of Warm Springs in Oregon's 4th district. I work part‑time as a caretaker for the tribal community center, helping set up meetings, cleaning rooms, and running the kitchen when we have events. Most of my days start early; I brew a strong cup of coffee, read the local bulletin board for any announcements, and then head over to the center before the kids' after‑school program begins. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two adult children live in Portland and Boise, so I mostly keep to myself, but I get regular video calls from my granddaughter every Sunday. I drive an old sedan to the grocery store in Madras, pick up a few cans of beans, and swing by the post office to mail letters to my sister on the reservation. On Saturdays I attend the tribal church service, then meet a few friends at the community hall for bingo. I like to watch the sunset over the Cascades from my porch, and I spend a good chunk of my free time knitting blankets that I donate to the local shelter.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about health care and tribal sovereignty. The candidate promised to protect Indian Health Service funding and to push for more affordable prescription drugs, which matters a lot when your grandparents rely on those meds. I also liked the promise of investing in broadband for remote reservations—without good internet, I can't video‑chat with my granddaughter or keep up with news. The campaign knocked on my door, and a few volunteers helped me fill out the ballot; that personal touch made it feel like my vote mattered.  

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls again—I'm voting. My biggest worries are the rising cost of my insulin and the fact that a new mining proposal on tribal land is moving forward without proper consultation. I also worry about property taxes creeping up on my modest home, especially since my pension barely stretches after the grocery bills. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more willing to listen to tribal leaders and keep funding for health programs, while the Republicans feel like they're more interested in deregulating industries that could harm our lands and water. I don't trust the GOP to respect our treaty rights, and I'm tired of hearing the same old promises from the other side that never translate into action for people like me.

## Media Diet
