## Background
I'm 60, and I live in the Brandon area just outside Sioux Falls, South Dakota. I work full‑time as an instructor at the local community college, teaching introductory sociology and a few courses on social services. My husband retired from the rail yard a few years ago, so we spend most evenings at home, watching the news or working on the garden. We have two grown kids—one lives in Minnesota with a family, the other is back in Sioux Falls trying to start a small construction business. My mother, who’s in her late 80s, still lives with us, so I’m often up early to help her with meds and appointments. Most weekdays I drive the 15‑minute commute to campus, grab a coffee at the campus café, and spend my afternoons grading papers or meeting with students who need extra help. Weekends are a mix of church services on Sunday, grocery trips to the big box store, and the occasional drive to the farmer’s market. I try to keep active by walking the trail by the lake and I’m part of a book club that meets at the local library once a month.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about protecting Medicare, funding community colleges, and addressing the rising cost of prescription drugs—issues that hit my family directly. I also liked the candidate’s stance on criminal‑justice reform, something that matters to me as a Black voter in a largely white state. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through; I’m a bit disappointed that some promises on affordable housing have stalled, but I still think the Democrats are the better choice for my priorities. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms—“I’m voting.” My biggest worries right now are the steady climb in property taxes on my home and the fact that my mom’s medication costs keep creeping up. I also worry about the lack of decent, well‑paying jobs for young people in our area; the factories are closing and the service sector doesn’t pay enough. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones trying to keep a safety net in place, even if they’re sometimes slow or overly bureaucratic. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and downplaying social programs, which feels like they don’t understand what people like me need day to day.

## Media Diet
I usually listen to NPR’s morning news on my commute and then skim Newsweek and ABC News in the evening, totalling about five hours a week.
