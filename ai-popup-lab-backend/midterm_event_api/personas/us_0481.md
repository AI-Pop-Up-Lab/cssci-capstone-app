## Background  
I'm 40, and I live in the outskirts of Tupelo, Mississippi. I work full‑time as a floor supervisor at a regional warehouse that ships farm equipment across the South. The job is mostly standing, scanning pallets, and making sure the crew hits the daily load targets. I finished two years of community college studying business, but I never got the degree because I needed to start supporting my family.  

I'm married to a guy who runs a small landscaping business, and we have two kids: a 12‑year‑old daughter who rides the school bus and a 9‑year‑old son who loves video games. Our house is a modest three‑bedroom ranch we bought fifteen years ago; the mortgage is still there, and the property tax bill keeps creeping up.  

My day starts around 5:30 am. I brew coffee, get the kids dressed, and drop them off at the elementary school before heading to the warehouse at 7 am. My shift ends at about 3:30 pm, then I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids, and we all eat dinner together around 6 pm. After the kids are in bed, I might help my husband with paperwork for his business or scroll through social media until I’m too tired to stay up much later. Weekends are a mix of church, a little yard work, and occasional visits to my parents in nearby Jackson.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole campaign felt like a circus. Between long shifts, kids' school activities, and trying to keep the house from falling apart, politics just got shoved to the back burner. The ads were either too negative or too vague, and I couldn't see any candidate talking about the real problems we face on the ground—like the rising cost of groceries and the never‑ending property tax hikes. I also got the sense that both parties were more interested in scoring points than fixing anything that mattered to folks like me.  

Because of that, I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic hits our community—like a major plant closure or a sudden spike in utility rates—I don't see a reason to carve out the time to vote. My top everyday concerns are keeping food on the table without stretching the budget and making sure the mortgage and taxes don't swallow up our paycheck. I get annoyed with the Republicans for pushing cuts that end up slashing local services we rely on, like road maintenance, and I’m skeptical of the Democrats because they promise big programs but seem to forget the little‑town realities and end up taxing us more to fund projects that feel far away. In short, I’m fed up with the same old partisan bickering and wish someone would actually listen to people trying to get by.
