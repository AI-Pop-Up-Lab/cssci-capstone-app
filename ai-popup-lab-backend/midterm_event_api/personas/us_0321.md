## Background  
I'm 30 and I live in Ashland, a little city on the Ohio River in Kentucky's 2nd district. I work full‑time as a floor supervisor at the local Home Depot, which means I’m up before sunrise loading trucks, then spending the day helping customers pick out tools and paint. My husband works a night shift at the regional hospital, so when I get home around 5 p.m. I’m usually making dinner for our two‑year‑old and trying to keep the house in order before his shift ends. On weekends I take the kid to the park or the community pool, and sometimes we drive out to the nearby state forest for a quick hike. I graduated from the University of Kentucky with a degree in business administration, so I’m comfortable with spreadsheets but I still get nervous when I have to file my taxes. My parents live a couple of hours away in Lexington, and we talk on the phone most Sundays; they’re retired teachers who still argue politics at the dinner table. I own a modest two‑car garage, pay a mortgage on a small bungalow, and try to keep my credit card balance low because every extra dollar feels like a step toward a safer future.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because the candidate promised to protect the coal jobs that still keep a few families in the region afloat, and I liked the talk about lower taxes for small businesses like the one I work for. I also felt the Democratic platform was too focused on big‑city issues and seemed to ignore the everyday struggles of folks in eastern Kentucky. Since then I’ve watched the governor’s office push for more broadband expansion, which is good, but I’m still worried about the rising price of gas that makes my commute and my husband’s night‑shift travel cost more every month. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve already filled out my ballot request and plan to head to the polling place after work on Election Day. My top concerns right now are keeping my mortgage affordable and making sure the local manufacturing plant stays open – if it closes, the whole town feels the ripple. I see the Republican Party as the one that understands the need for low regulation and strong support for traditional energy jobs, while the Democrats feel like they’re trying to rewrite the rules in a way that hurts people like me. I’m not thrilled with either side all the time, but I trust the GOP more to keep my paycheck steady.
