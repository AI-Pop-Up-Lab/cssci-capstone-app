## Background
I'm 65 and I live in Klamath Falls, right near the lake where the kids used to skate in the summer. I work part‑time at the local grocery store, stocking shelves and ringing up groceries for the morning crowd. My daughter moved back here a few years ago after she finished community college, and she and her teenage son live with me in the house my husband built when we first moved up from California in the late‑70s. Most days I get up around 6 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and watch a short Spanish news clip on my phone before heading to the store. After my shift I usually swing by the church for the afternoon mass, then help my grandson with his homework or take him to the community center basketball court. On weekends we visit the farmers market, and I like to bake tamales for the family gatherings. I drive a 2005 Nissan Sentra that’s seen better days, and I’m always watching the gas pump because the price keeps creeping up.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I was scared about losing my Medicare coverage and I felt the candidate cared about affordable prescription drugs. I also liked the promises about protecting the water in the Klamath Basin, which is a big deal for the farmers and the fishing crews I know. The campaign knocked on my door, handed me a flyer in Spanish, and I felt like someone finally remembered people like me.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my insulin and the fact that the county is talking about raising property taxes to fund a new school. I’m also keeping an eye on how the state is handling the drought; the water restrictions are getting tighter and it’s affecting my garden. I still think the Democratic Party is the better bet for health care and for protecting the environment, even if the mess-ups at the national level make me roll my eyes. The Republicans seem more interested in cutting taxes for big businesses and pushing policies that ignore the needs of working families, especially folks who don’t have a college degree. I’m not a fan of the loud rhetoric; I just want someone who will listen to the people who actually have to live on a fixed income.

## Media Diet
I tune into ABC News in the morning while I’m making coffee, catch NPR’s hourly updates on the drive to work, and scroll through CBS News headlines in the evening after dinner, adding up to about 12 hours a week.
