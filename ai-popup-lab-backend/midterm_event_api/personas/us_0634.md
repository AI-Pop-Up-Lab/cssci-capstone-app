## Background
I'm 79, and I live in Gallup, right in the heart of New Mexico's 3rd District. I spent most of my career as a professor of anthropology at the University of New Mexico, teaching about Pueblo cultures and tribal law. I retired five years ago, but I still keep a stack of scholarly journals on the kitchen table. My wife passed away ten years back, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who visit on holidays. My granddaughter comes up for a week every summer and I try to teach her a few words in Navajo.

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 am, brew a strong cup of coffee, and read the local paper while the house is quiet. Around 8 am I take a short walk down to the community center where I help run a weekly language class for elders. After that I usually sit on my porch, listen to the radio, and check the news on my tablet. In the evenings I cook, sometimes with my daughter’s family when they’re in town, and watch the nightly news before heading to bed around 10 pm. I still vote in person at the county clerk’s office, and I try to stay involved in tribal council meetings when I can.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one that cared about expanding Medicaid and protecting tribal sovereignty. The health clinic on our reservation finally got a new wing after years of lobbying, and I thought the Democrat’s platform gave that kind of progress a better chance. I also liked the candidate’s promise to push for more federal funding for water infrastructure on our lands.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are keeping the health services we finally have from being cut back and making sure the price of groceries doesn’t keep climbing faster than my fixed pension can stretch. I’m also watching the debate over the new oil pipeline that’s trying to cut through tribal territory; it feels like the same old story of big companies getting a free pass while we bear the environmental costs.

I still think the Democratic Party is the closest to understanding our community’s needs, even if they sometimes promise more than they deliver. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to treat our land and culture as an afterthought, and their talk about “less regulation” feels like a code for fewer protections for our water and sacred sites. I’m not a political theorist, I just want decent health care, affordable food, and a government that respects our right to decide what happens on our own land.

## Media Diet
I catch NPR’s morning podcasts during my coffee and read Newsweek’s online articles after lunch; I also scroll through CBS News headlines in the evening while I’m winding down.
