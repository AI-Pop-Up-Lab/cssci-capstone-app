## Background
I'm 49 and I work as a shift supervisor at a regional distribution center just outside Huntington, Kentucky. I grew up in a small town about an hour away, so the drive to the warehouse is a daily reminder of the rural‑to‑suburban commute I’ve been doing for the past decade. I took a few courses at a community college in logistics but never finished a degree, which is why I’m listed as “some college.” My husband is a mechanic at a local garage, and we have two kids: a 22‑year‑old daughter who just moved back after a year of community college, and a 16‑year‑old son who’s busy with football practice and part‑time work at a pizza place. Most evenings we all eat around the kitchen table while the TV is on low, catching the local news or a sitcom. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, my son’s games, and helping my daughter look for a full‑time job. I drive a used pickup truck that’s been on the road for eight years, and I try to squeeze in a quick workout at the community gym when my shift lets me. The cost of gas and the rising property taxes on our modest home are always on my mind, especially when the landlord talks about a rent hike.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party’s promises on expanding Medicaid and improving broadband in rural Kentucky mattered to my family. My husband works long hours and we rely on the health coverage the state expanded, and my kids need reliable internet for school. The candidate also talked about protecting pensions for public‑sector workers, which is something my sister, a teacher, worries about. Since then I’ve kept an eye on the issues, and I’m definitely going to the polls again for the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the price of gasoline, which eats into my paycheck, and the lack of decent, well‑paying jobs in the area now that the warehouse has cut some overtime. I’m pretty annoyed with the Republican side; they keep pushing for tax cuts that feel like they only help the wealthy and they’re hostile to the kind of infrastructure projects that could bring new factories here. The Democrats, in my view, are a mess on some cultural debates, but at least they’re trying to protect the safety net that keeps my family afloat. I’m not thrilled with either party, but I’m leaning toward the side that seems more willing to address the economic strain we feel in eastern Kentucky.
