## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a modest bungalow in the Socastee area of Myrtle Beach, South Carolina, inside the 6th congressional district. My parents came here from Taiwan in the 1960s and ran a small grocery store that closed when I was a teen. I dropped out of school after the tenth grade to help out, but I did finish high school at the local public school. For most of my adult life I worked on the assembly line at a textile mill in nearby Conway, then moved to a warehouse job after the mill shut down. I retired about ten years ago when the warehouse closed, and now I get a modest Social Security check plus a small pension from the union.

I'm a widower; my wife passed away five years ago. We have two grown children—one lives in Charlotte, the other in Atlanta—and a handful of grandchildren who visit every summer. My days are simple: I wake up early, brew a strong cup of coffee, and flip through the local paper (the *Post and Courier*) while the TV is on low. I take a short walk around the neighborhood, stop by the senior center to play cards with a few friends, and then spend the afternoon either fixing things around the house or watching the news. Evenings are for a bit of TV, a light dinner, and sometimes a call with my daughter.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. At my age, I feel the whole process is a circus—campaign ads on TV, endless rallies, and the same promises that never seem to reach people like me. The ballot felt like a choice between two sides that both ignore seniors' real worries: rising prescription drug costs, the lack of affordable home repairs, and the fact that my fixed income is squeezed by property tax hikes. I also had a bout of pneumonia that winter, and the recovery left me too drained to bother with the whole voting thing. The feeling was that whatever I did, the outcome wouldn't change anything for me, so I stayed home.

I'm sitting this one out, too. Unless something dramatic happens—like a local scandal that threatens the senior center I volunteer at, or a candidate who actually promises to freeze property taxes for seniors—I don't see a reason to go to the polls. My top everyday concerns are the cost of my heart medication and the rising property taxes on my home. I think the Democrats are all about big government programs that never get delivered to small towns, and the Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes but forget that seniors already live on tight budgets. Both parties seem more interested in scoring points than solving the problems that keep me up at night.

## Media Diet
I get about eleven hours of news a week, mostly split between morning and evening. I watch the CBS evening news, catch ABC’s morning updates, and tune into NBC at night, plus I listen to NPR podcasts while I’m in the garden.
