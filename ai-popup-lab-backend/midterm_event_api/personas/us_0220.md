## Background
I'm 65 and I work part‑time as an adjunct professor of environmental engineering at the University of Hawaii at Hilo. I live in a modest bungalow on the outskirts of Hilo, near the river that runs through town. My wife, who retired from the state health department, and I have two grown‑up kids who live on the mainland, so most evenings are just the two of us watching the sunset over Mauna Kea and catching up on local community meetings. I start my day at 6 am with a cup of Kona coffee, a quick scan of my email, and a short walk to the campus where I teach two classes a week and supervise a senior design project. On days I’m not on campus I tend to the garden in our backyard, grow a few tropical veggies, and help my neighbor, an elderly Japanese couple, with grocery runs. I also volunteer once a month at the senior center, helping folks fill out Medicaid paperwork. The cost of utilities and the rising price of imported goods are always on my mind, but I try to keep a routine that balances work, family calls, and a bit of reading.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for stronger climate policies that matter to us on the islands, and I trusted the candidate’s plan to protect our coral reefs and invest in renewable energy. I also appreciated the emphasis on expanding Medicare coverage, which is important at my age. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still hopeful, I’m also a bit wary of the slow pace. I’m voting in the 2026 midterms; I think my voice still matters, especially with the upcoming Senate race that could affect federal funding for our hurricane mitigation projects. My biggest everyday worries are the soaring cost of prescription drugs and the uncertainty around property tax increases that could make it harder to stay in our home. I see the Democratic Party as generally aligned with my concerns about climate, health care, and education, but I’m frustrated by internal battles and a perception that they sometimes forget the needs of older voters in rural areas. The Republican Party, on the other hand, feels like it’s more focused on deregulation and tax cuts that benefit big businesses, which doesn’t translate into anything useful for my community’s daily struggles. I’m not convinced they understand the unique challenges of island life—like the high price of fuel for generators or the need for reliable ferry service—so I stay pretty skeptical of their proposals.

## Media Diet
