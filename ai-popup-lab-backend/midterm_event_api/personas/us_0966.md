## Background  
I’m 84 and I live in Beckley, right in the heart of the 2nd district. I spent most of my adult life working as a clerk at the local county clerk’s office; I never finished a degree, but I took a handful of night classes at the community college and learned enough to keep my paperwork straight. I’m widowed now, and my two grown‑up children live in Charleston and Pittsburgh, so I mostly hear from them on the phone or through video calls on Sundays.  

My day starts early with a cup of black coffee and the morning paper, which I still read even though I mostly get the news from the radio and a few TV clips. After breakfast I tend to my garden – tomatoes, beans, a few herbs – and I’m on my porch most afternoons, watching the traffic go by on Route 19 and listening to the local station. I go to the Methodist church every Wednesday for the mid‑week service and attend the Sunday worship, where I catch up with old friends. In the evenings I sit in the living room, flip on the TV, and catch the nightly news or a documentary. I drive a modest sedan to the grocery store once a week, and I’m on a fixed income from Social Security and a small pension, so I watch my spending carefully.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Medicare, prescription‑drug costs, and protecting Social Security – the three things that keep me afloat at my age. The Republican message seemed focused on cutting taxes for businesses and pushing cultural issues that didn’t affect my day‑to‑day life. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in broadband for rural areas; I need reliable internet to keep in touch with my kids.  

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are keeping my health‑care coverage affordable and making sure the pharmacy benefit manager reforms actually lower the price of the meds I need. The other big worry is property taxes – my house is getting older and the tax bill keeps creeping up, which squeezes my limited budget. I see the Democratic Party as the one more likely to protect seniors and push for decent health‑care policies, even if they sometimes argue too loudly about climate stuff that feels far removed from my reality. The Republicans, in my view, are more interested in cutting programs that help people on fixed incomes and spend a lot of time bickering over cultural battles I’d rather stay out of.  

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m having coffee, and I catch the evening broadcasts on CBS and ABC after dinner; that’s roughly six hours a week total.
