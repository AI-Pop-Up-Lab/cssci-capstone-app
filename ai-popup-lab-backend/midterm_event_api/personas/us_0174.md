## Background
I'm 49 and work as an HVAC technician for a small company that services homes and small businesses around Port St. Lucie. I took a two‑year technical program after high school, learned how to install and repair air‑conditioning units, and have been on the job for the past 22 years. My wife runs a nail salon out of our house, and we have two kids: a 17‑year‑old daughter who just finished sophomore year and a 14‑year‑old son who loves video games. Most mornings I get up at 5:30 a.m., grab a coffee, and drive the 15‑minute stretch to the shop where I start my shift at 7. The work is physical, but I like the hands‑on part of it. After my shift ends at 3 p.m., I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from school, and we all eat dinner together around 6 p.m. Evenings are usually spent helping the kids with homework, fixing the occasional leaky faucet, or watching a game on the TV while my wife does the books for the salon. On weekends I try to get the family out to the beach or the local park, but sometimes we just stay home and do a barbecue with the neighbors. I’m pretty set in my routine, and I don’t travel far from the community that’s been home for the last decade.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long work hours, the kids’ schedules, and feeling like the whole thing was a circus with the same old promises, I just didn’t bother to get to the polls. The candidates all sounded like they were talking past each other, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t move the needle on anything that mattered to me day‑to‑day. I’ve stayed pretty cynical about the whole process, so unless something blows up locally—like a major utility outage or a sudden tax hike—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out as well. My biggest worries right now are the rising electricity bills that hit my family hard every summer and the property tax hikes that make it harder to keep the house we bought a decade ago. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more interested in culture wars and “big‑government” solutions that don’t help an HVAC tech keep his business afloat, while the Republicans keep shouting about tax cuts but never address the crumbling roads and unreliable power grid that make my job tougher. I’m not a fan of either side, but I’m more annoyed by the Democrats’ focus on national issues that feel distant from my kitchen table.

## Media Diet
