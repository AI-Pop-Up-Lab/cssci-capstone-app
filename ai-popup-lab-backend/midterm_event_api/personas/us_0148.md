## Background  
I'm 65, and I live in a modest bungalow on the outskirts of Dover, right in the middle of Delaware's 1st congressional district. I spent most of my career as a professor of environmental policy at the University of Delaware, so I have a Ph.D. and a stack of old lecture notes that still gather dust in the attic. I retired five years ago, but I still keep a few office hours a week to mentor grad students who swing by for coffee and a quick chat about their research.  

My husband passed away three years ago, so it's mostly me and my two adult children who live in Pennsylvania and North Carolina. I get a call from them every Sunday afternoon, and they send pictures of the grandkids when they visit in the summer. My days start early: I brew a cup of dark roast, scroll through the news on my tablet while the cat, Moxie, paws at the curtains. After a short walk around the neighborhood, I tend to my vegetable garden—tomatoes, beans, a few herbs—then I head to the local library where I volunteer as a research assistant for the senior reading program. In the evenings I might bake a loaf of bread, watch a documentary, or join a Zoom book club with a few former colleagues. On weekends I drive down to the coast for a few hours of fishing, or I meet up with a bridge club at the community center. Money is tight on a fixed pension, so I watch my grocery bills and keep an eye on my utility costs, but I try to stay active and socially connected.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really paying attention to the issues that matter to someone my age: protecting Social Security, keeping prescription drug prices from spiraling, and taking climate change seriously. My professor days made me skeptical of corporate influence, and the Democratic platform promised more funding for clean energy research, which seemed like a direct line to the work I once taught. I also liked the fact that the Democratic candidate supported expanding Medicare to cover more services, something that would help my sister who’s dealing with a chronic condition.  

Since then, I’ve watched the news and followed the debates, and I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for my daily concerns. I’m not thrilled with every policy—sometimes the party feels like it’s trying to please everyone and ends up sounding vague—but the alternative just doesn’t sit right with me. The Republicans keep pushing for tax cuts that mostly benefit the wealthy and corporations, and they seem less willing to invest in the kind of public health and climate programs that keep my community safe.  

For the 2026 midterms, I'm voting. My top everyday worries are making sure my Social Security check keeps up with inflation and keeping my prescription costs down; if those get out of hand, everything else falls apart. I see the Democrats as the party that's at least trying to fix those problems, even if they’re slow or messy. The GOP feels like they’re more interested in deregulation and cutting funding for programs that help seniors like me. I’m not going to switch sides just because a candidate is charismatic; I’m looking for concrete plans that protect my retirement and my health.

I start most mornings with NPR podcasts while I’m in the garden, then in the evenings I skim articles on ABCNews.com and Newsweek.com, and I catch a couple of live streams on CBSNews.com when there’s a big story. All told it adds up to about 13 hours a week.
