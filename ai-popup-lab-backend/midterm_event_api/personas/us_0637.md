## Background
I'm 55, and I live in Farmington, right on the edge of town where the highway meets the desert. I work full‑time as a forklift operator at the local warehouse that ships building supplies to the surrounding ranches. My shift starts at 6 a.m.; I grab a quick coffee from the stand next to the truck lot, hop on the belt, and spend the next eight hours moving pallets, counting inventory, and dealing with the occasional broken fork. After my 2 p.m. break I head home, where my wife—who retired from the school district a few years back—keeps the house running. We have two adult children; the oldest lives a few counties away and visits on holidays, the younger moved out to Albuquerque for a tech job. Most evenings I’m on the porch watching the sky turn pink, fixing the old grill, or scrolling through my phone while the kids’ soccer games play on the TV. Weekends are for the farmer’s market, a quick trip to the nearby national forest for a hike, and helping my neighbor fix his fence. I own a modest ranch‑style house, pay a mortgage, and try to keep the garden alive even when the water restrictions bite.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt the whole thing was a circus—campaign ads everywhere, the same old promises, and none of them seemed to touch the real grind of getting to work on time or keeping the utility bill down. My schedule was packed with overtime and a few sick days for my wife, and I just didn’t see the point of squeezing into a polling place. I also think a lot of the candidates talk past folks like me; they’re either too focused on big‑city issues or on national rhetoric that never reaches Farmington. So I sat it out, and unless something dramatic hits my community—like a major factory closing or a water‑shortage emergency—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out too. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the way the water restrictions keep tightening; every month I’m watching my budget shrink. I’m also uneasy about the property tax hikes the county council floated last year. As for the parties, I see Democrats as out of touch with rural New Mexico, always talking about climate policies that sound good on paper but ignore the cost to ranchers. Republicans, on the other hand, promise lower taxes and less regulation, but lately I feel they’re veering into extreme rhetoric that makes me uneasy about the tone of politics overall. I’m not a fan of either side, but I’m a little more tolerant of the GOP because at least they seem to understand the need for cheaper energy and fewer red‑tape hurdles for small businesses.
