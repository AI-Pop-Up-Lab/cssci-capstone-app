## Background
I'm 40 and live in a modest house on the outskirts of Burlington, the biggest town in VT‑1. I work full‑time as a high school math teacher, which means my mornings start early with lesson planning and grading before the kids arrive. My spouse runs a small bakery downtown, and we have a ten‑year‑old daughter who rides the bus to a public elementary school a few miles away. Most weekdays I drop her off, head to school, and after classes I stay for a faculty meeting or help a few students with after‑school tutoring. Evenings are a mix of grading, cooking dinner, and catching up on a Netflix series when we can. On weekends we shop at the farmer’s market, tend to a tiny vegetable plot in the backyard, and sometimes volunteer at the local community center, teaching basic computer skills to seniors. The cost of living has crept up, so we keep a close eye on the budget, especially when the grocery store raises prices on produce we try to grow ourselves.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more likely to protect public education funding and push for climate policies that matter to us in Vermont. I was also motivated by the candidate’s stance on affordable healthcare, which hits home with my husband’s bakery not offering a robust insurance plan. Since then I’ve stayed pretty engaged; I’ve attended a town hall on broadband expansion and signed a petition to keep the local school budget from being slashed. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—I’m not planning to sit this one out. My biggest worries right now are the rising property taxes that make it harder to stay in our home, and the continuing shortage of qualified teachers, which is making my job tougher and my students’ learning experience uneven. I also keep an eye on the cost of prescription drugs because my mother lives nearby and needs regular medication. When it comes to parties, I see the Democrats as the more attentive to the issues that affect my family and community, even if they sometimes feel too centrist on climate urgency. The Republicans, in my view, seem more focused on tax cuts for big corporations and less on funding for schools or rural infrastructure, which feels out of touch with the reality of living in a small New England state.

## Media Diet
I usually listen to NPR in the mornings while making coffee, and I skim headlines on CBS News and ABC News during my lunch break. In the evenings I read a few articles on Newsweek to get deeper analysis, adding up to about five hours a week total.
