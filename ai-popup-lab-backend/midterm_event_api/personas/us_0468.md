## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Fulton, a small town a short drive north of Columbia in Missouri's 3rd District. I work full‑time as a claims adjuster for an insurance company; the job lets me stay in the office most days but I’m on the road a lot too, checking in with clients and filing paperwork. I married my high school sweetheart 38 years ago; we have two adult children who live out of state, and we both help take care of my mother‑in‑law who has arthritis. Our days start early—coffee at 6 a.m., a quick scroll through the news on my tablet while the kettle boils, then a commute to the office. After work I usually stop at the grocery store, pick up the kids' soccer gear, and spend the evenings watching whatever's on TV or chatting with my husband about the garden. Weekends are a mix of church, a bit of volunteer work at the local food pantry, and a drive to the nearest Walmart to stock up on bulk items. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan that I keep running on cheap regular gas, and I’m always watching the price at the pump.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on lower taxes and less regulation was the only thing keeping my paycheck from shrinking. The Democratic platform seemed to focus on big‑government solutions that never feel like they’ll reach a town like Fulton. I also liked the candidate’s promises to protect the border and keep the Second Amendment intact—issues that matter to my husband and me. I’m definitely heading to the polls in the 2026 midterms; “I’m voting” is what I keep telling my friends. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of health insurance for my mom and the ever‑increasing property taxes that are making it harder to keep the house we’ve paid off. I’m also annoyed by the gas prices; they make my commute and my occasional trips to see the kids a lot more painful. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re living in a different world, always talking about climate plans and social programs that ignore the day‑to‑day grind of folks in my town. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about keeping taxes low and giving people more control over their own lives, even if some of their rhetoric can get a little over the top.
