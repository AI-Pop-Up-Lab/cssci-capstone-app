## Background
I'm 30 and work as a dental hygienist at a downtown clinic in Winter Haven. I grew up in a nearby suburb, went to a community college for an associate's in dental assisting, and stayed in the area because my mom needed help with her house after a flood a few years back. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with my partner, who works as a delivery driver, and we have a nine‑month‑old daughter who sleeps most of the day while we get ready for work. My mornings start around 5:30 am; I make a quick coffee, check the kids’ diaper bag, and drive the 15‑minute commute while listening to a local Spanish‑language radio station. The clinic opens at 8 am, and my shift runs until 2 pm, with a short lunch break where I usually run to the grocery store to pick up staples. After work I pick up my daughter, take her to the park, and then head home to cook dinner—mostly rice, beans, and whatever the grocery flyer has on sale. Evenings are a mix of helping my partner with paperwork, scrolling through social media, and trying to squeeze in a Spanish‑language TV show before we all crash. Weekends are mostly chores, visits to my mom’s house, and the occasional family barbecue with neighbors from the church.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between long shifts, caring for a baby, and feeling like none of the candidates really understood the day‑to‑day grind of a working‑class family, I just let the day pass. The whole process feels like a circus—campaign ads that talk about big ideas while my rent keeps creeping up, and the same old promises that never translate into anything for us. I also worry that my limited English makes it harder to navigate the ballot, so I just stay home. I'm sitting this one out too; unless something dramatic hits the local community—like a school closure or a major utility outage that directly affects us—I don't see a reason to waste the time.

My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent and the price of groceries. Every paycheck feels tighter, and when the gas price jumps, my partner’s deliveries get cut down, which means less money for the kids. I think both parties are out of touch: the Democrats talk a lot about immigration and social programs, but the funding never reaches the neighborhoods we live in. The Republicans keep yelling about taxes and “big government,” yet they seem to forget the people who are already struggling to pay their bills. It feels like a game where the rules are written by folks who never had to work two jobs to make ends meet.
