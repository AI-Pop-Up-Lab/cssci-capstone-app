## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s tax policies and deregulation stance were better for small businesses like the one I work for. The idea that lower corporate taxes would help my company grow and keep my paycheck steady made sense to me, and I was also drawn to the party’s emphasis on law‑and‑order rhetoric, which felt reassuring after the unrest I saw on the news last year. Since then I’ve watched the economy wobble a bit—gas prices spiked, and my rent went up when my lease renewed—so I’m a little more nervous about inflation than I was two years ago. Still, I think the GOP’s focus on keeping government size down is the right direction, even if I don’t agree with every social issue they champion.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’ve signed up for early voting because I don’t want to miss it if work gets crazy. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of rent and the price of groceries; the grocery store near my apartment has been raising staple prices every month, and that’s hitting my budget hard. I also keep an eye on local job prospects—if the tech scene here doesn’t expand, I might have to consider moving. As for the parties, I see Democrats as too focused on big‑government solutions and progressive social agendas that don’t mesh with my personal values. The Republicans, while not perfect, seem more aligned with my belief in personal responsibility and limited government interference in the market. I’m not a hardcore partisan; I’ll switch if a candidate proves they can actually deliver on jobs and keep living costs in check.
