## Background  
I'm 65 and I live in the town of Dover, right in the heart of Delaware's 1st congressional district. I work part‑time at the local grocery store, stocking shelves and helping customers during the morning shift. I retired from a 30‑year run as a school bus driver when I turned 60, but the extra cash from the grocery job helps cover my mortgage on a modest two‑bedroom house I bought after my kids moved out. My daughter lives in Wilmington and visits a few times a year, and my grandson, who’s in college, calls me every Sunday to talk about his classes. Most days I wake up early, make a cup of coffee, and take a short walk around the neighborhood before my shift starts. After work I usually stop by the community center for the senior bingo night or a knitting circle, and on weekends I tend to my small garden of tomatoes and herbs. I drive a used sedan that’s been reliable for years, and I try to keep up with the local church’s outreach programs when I can find the time.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to issues that affect me directly—like Social Security, Medicare, and affordable prescription drugs. The campaign ads I saw on the news highlighted the need to protect seniors from cuts, and that resonated after I saw a neighbor’s prescription costs skyrocket last year. I also appreciated the candidate’s promise to invest in broadband for rural parts of the state, which would make it easier for me to video‑chat with my grandson and keep up with online banking.

Since then, I’ve been watching how the new administration handles the economy and health care. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better choice for protecting the safety net, but I’m also a bit skeptical about some of the rhetoric about “getting everything fixed in a single election.” I’ve seen a few promises fall short, and that makes me keep an eye on the details rather than just the party label. For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls—my Medicare coverage and the property tax rate on my home are the two things I worry about most. If the Republican candidate pushes for cuts to senior benefits or tries to raise property taxes to fund other projects, I’ll be voting to stop that. I’m not a big fan of the GOP’s stance on climate change either; the rising heat makes it harder to garden, and I’m not convinced they’re taking it seriously enough. Overall, I view the Democrats as the party that at least pretends to care about ordinary folks like me, even if they sometimes miss the mark.
