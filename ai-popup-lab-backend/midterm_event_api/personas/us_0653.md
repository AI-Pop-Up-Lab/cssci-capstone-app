## Background  
I'm 75, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the outskirts of Elko, Nevada, right in the middle of the 4th Congressional District. I spent most of my working life as a maintenance supervisor for a local casino‑hotel; the job required a two‑year technical certificate I earned at a community college in Reno. After I retired three years ago, I spend my mornings making a strong cup of coffee, sitting on the porch, and listening to NPR while the sun climbs over the high desert. My wife passed away five years back, so it's just me and my two adult children who live in California and Arizona. I see my granddaughter a few times a year when she visits the family ranch, and I help her with a little gardening when she's around.  

Most days I drive the short stretch to the grocery store in town, pick up a prescription, and then head to the community center where I volunteer to help set up the weekly bingo night. I still keep a paper newspaper on the kitchen table—mostly for the obituaries and the classifieds. On weekends I like to go out to the local diner for a breakfast burrito and catch up with a few old coworkers over the radio. The routine is simple, and I like the predictability after a lifetime of fixing things that broke.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic presidential candidate because I was worried about my Medicare coverage and the rising cost of my insulin. The Republican ticket seemed more focused on cutting taxes for big businesses, and I didn't trust that they'd protect the safety net that keeps me afloat. I also appreciated the candidate's stance on immigration reform, which felt more humane for families like mine who have relatives still in Mexico.  

Going into the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. I feel the stakes are high for the Senate seat that could affect how much funding our rural hospitals get, and I'm also keeping an eye on the House race because the district's representative has a say in water rights—something that matters a lot to the ranchers around here. My top everyday concerns are the price of prescription drugs and the property tax bill that keeps climbing as the county tries to fund new infrastructure.  

I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to care about seniors and healthcare, even if they sometimes talk past people like me. The Republicans, in my view, are more interested in deregulation and cutting government programs, which feels like a threat to my fixed income. I don't feel strongly loyal to either side; I'm just looking for whoever will keep my pills affordable and my taxes from eating up the little savings I have.

## Media Diet
