## Background
I'm 19 and I live in Gallup, right in the middle of NM‑3. My mom works part‑time at the local health clinic and my little brother is still in middle school, so the three of us share a small two‑bedroom house. I graduated from high school two years ago and haven't gone to college; instead I pick up shifts at the grocery store down the road, stocking shelves and checking out customers. Most weekdays I start work around 2 p.m., finish around 10 p.m., and then I’m home late, scrolling on my phone or watching a bit of TV before I crash. On weekends I sometimes do extra hours at the store or help my mom with errands—grocery runs, picking up prescriptions, that sort of thing. I drive an old sedan my dad left me, and I’m saving up for a bike so I can get around town without relying on the bus. In my free time I hang out with a couple of friends from school, play video games, and watch a lot of YouTube clips about music and sports. I don’t really have a lot of spare cash, so I’m careful about what I spend, especially on food and clothes.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything—none of the candidates seemed to care about the problems we face in Gallup, like the rising cost of rent and the shortage of decent jobs for people my age. Between long shifts at the store and taking care of my brother, I just never got around to filling out a ballot, and the whole process felt like a hassle I couldn’t fit into my schedule. I also get a lot of the same old talk from the news and from folks around me: “the system is broken,” “they’re all the same.” That kind of cynicism has stuck with me, so I’m leaning toward sitting this one out too, unless something really dramatic happens locally that forces me to pay attention.

I’m definitely not planning to vote in the 2026 midterms; I’m “sitting this one out.” My biggest everyday worries are paying the rent on time and keeping up with grocery prices, which keep creeping up even though my paycheck stays the same. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re always promising more programs but never delivering anything that reaches my neighborhood, and the Republicans seem to ignore the fact that many of us are struggling to make ends meet. Both sides feel distant, and I’m not sure either is listening to people like me.
