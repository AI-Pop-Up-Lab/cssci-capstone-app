## Background
I'm 55 and I work as a civil engineer for a consulting firm that does a lot of road and bridge projects around north‑central Georgia. I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the outskirts of Gainesville, near the foothills of the Blue Ridge. My wife and I have been married for 30 years; our two kids are both in their late twenties—one lives in Atlanta with a tech job, the other is back home helping my wife with a small home‑based bakery. Most mornings I grab a coffee, read the headlines on my phone while the kids are still sleeping, then drive about 30 minutes to the office where I spend the day in meetings, reviewing plans, and occasionally heading out to a site to check on construction crews. After work I usually hit the gym for an hour or take the dog for a walk around the neighborhood. Weekends are a mix of church service on Sunday, a bit of hunting or fishing with friends, and family meals that stretch into the evening. I keep a fairly tight budget because property taxes have crept up, but I’m comfortable enough to take a few short trips to the coast each summer.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the party’s stance on lower taxes and a strong national defense felt like the safest bet for someone with a steady paycheck and a mortgage. I also liked the talk about cutting red tape for infrastructure projects—something that directly mattered to my day‑to‑day work. Since then I’ve gotten a bit more skeptical of the hype around “big government” solutions, especially after seeing the price of gasoline spike and my grocery bill climb higher than I’d like. I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I don’t have the energy to sit this one out again, and there’s a Senate race that could affect funding for the road projects I’m involved with. My top concerns right now are the rising property taxes that are squeezing my household budget and the cost of prescription drugs, which my wife needs for a chronic condition. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the folks who understand that people need to keep more of what they earn, while the Democrats seem more interested in expanding programs that end up being funded by higher taxes. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the government to stay out of my paycheck as much as possible and keep the roads in decent shape.
