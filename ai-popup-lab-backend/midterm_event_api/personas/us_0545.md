## Background
I’m 70 and I work part‑time as a dental hygienist at a clinic in Bismarck. I grew up on a farm near Minot, went to a community college in Grand Forks for an associate’s in dental assisting, and moved to the capital after I got married. My husband passed away five years ago, so it’s just me now, but I see my daughter’s kids every other weekend and help out with bedtime stories. I own a modest house in a quiet neighborhood on the east side of town; the yard needs a lot of work, but I like having space to grow a few tomatoes in the summer. My day usually starts with a strong cup of coffee and the morning news on the TV while I read the paper. I drive the short 15‑minute commute to the clinic, spend the morning cleaning and assisting the dentist, and then head home for lunch. In the afternoons I’m often at the local senior center, playing bingo or knitting, and I volunteer a few hours a week at my church’s food pantry. Evenings are for TV, a bit of internet browsing, and catching up with my sister on the phone.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I’ve always trusted the party that talks about low taxes, protecting the right to own a gun, and keeping the government out of my personal life. The candidate promised to keep Medicaid funding steady, which matters to me because I’m on Medicare and need reliable coverage for my arthritis meds. I also liked the emphasis on “traditional values” – it felt like the party understood what people like me grew up with. Since then, I’ve watched the new Congress and I still think the Republicans are the better choice for keeping costs down and preserving the freedoms I value. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out, especially with the upcoming debate over prescription drug price caps and the state’s property tax hike. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of my insulin and the fact that my property tax bill is climbing every year. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising big‑government solutions that never seem to work and they’re out of touch with folks who live in the plains. The Republicans may not be perfect, but they at least talk about the issues that hit my wallet and my way of life.
