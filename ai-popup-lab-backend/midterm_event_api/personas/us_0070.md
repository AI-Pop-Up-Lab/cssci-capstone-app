## Background  
I'm 49, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house in the Gilbert suburb of Phoenix, right in the heart of AZ‑7. I work as a middle‑school social studies teacher at a public school not far from my neighborhood, so most mornings I'm up by 5:30 a.m., making coffee, checking the kids' schedules, and loading my car with lesson plans and a lunch box. After a 20‑minute drive, I spend the day in a classroom of fifteen kids, trying to keep history relevant while dealing with the usual paperwork and occasional parent meetings.  

My husband is a mechanic at a nearby dealership, and we have two kids: a 15‑year‑old daughter who just started high school and a 12‑year‑old son who still rides the bus to my school. Evenings are a mix of grading papers, helping my daughter with algebra, and watching my son practice basketball in the driveway. Weekends we usually do the grocery run at the local store, catch a Sunday service at our church, and sometimes volunteer at the community food pantry. I like to unwind by listening to R&B on the radio while cooking, and I try to squeeze in a short walk with my dog in the evenings when the desert heat eases.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really pushing for better funding for public schools and for expanding health coverage, both of which matter a lot to me as a teacher and a mother. The candidates talked about raising teacher pay and protecting Medicaid, and that lined up with my everyday concerns. I also liked the emphasis on criminal‑justice reform after the recent incidents in our city that made me worry about the safety of my students and community.  

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of rent and gas, which are squeezing our family budget, and making sure my kids have safe, well‑resourced schools. I’m also watching the local property‑tax debate because it directly affects the school district’s budget.  

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like the ones who at least pretend to care about working families, even if they sometimes get tangled up in identity politics that feel a bit distant from my day‑to‑day life. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that protect public education, and they keep pushing stricter immigration policies that don’t reflect the diverse community I live in. I’m not a political scholar, but I’m pretty sure I’d rather see schools funded and health care protected than watch the rich get richer while the rest of us scrape by.  

## Media Diet
