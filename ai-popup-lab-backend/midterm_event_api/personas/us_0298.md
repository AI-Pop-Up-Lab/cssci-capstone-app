## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate promised to keep taxes low and protect the kind of jobs that keep folks like me on the plant floor. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the government to stay out of my paycheck and not tell me what to do with my truck. The Democratic side seemed more interested in big‑city issues and “green” policies that felt like they’d hurt the manufacturing base. Since then, I’ve watched the cost of groceries climb and the gas prices stay high, which makes me nervous about putting more money into the family budget. I’m planning to vote again in the 2026 midterms because I think my voice still matters, especially if the local congressional race brings up trade deals that could affect the plant. My top everyday worries are keeping the kids fed without breaking the bank and making sure my wife’s part‑time hours stay steady. I see the Republican Party as the one that cares about working‑class folks in places like ours, while the Democrats feel disconnected from the real struggles of people who don’t have a college degree. I’m not thrilled with any politician, but I lean toward the side that talks about “getting the country back on track” rather than “changing the system” in ways that seem abstract.
