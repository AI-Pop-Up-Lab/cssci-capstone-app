## Background
I'm 74, and I live in a modest house on a quiet street in Fresno, right in the heart of California's 29th district. I grew up in a farming family, went to community college for a couple of years but never finished a degree, and spent most of my adult life working as an administrative assistant at the local high school. I retired five years ago, but I still swing by the school once a week to help with the after‑school program for kids who need extra tutoring. My husband passed away three years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in the Bay Area. They visit when they can, and I get a lot of video calls from my three grandchildren who are in high school and college. My days are simple: I get up early, make a pot of tea, and take a short walk around the neighborhood before the heat gets too strong. Then I do my grocery run, catch up on a bit of TV news, and spend a couple of hours at the community center where I knit and play cards with other seniors. I try to keep the house tidy, tend to my small balcony garden of herbs, and make sure I have enough medicine for my arthritis and blood pressure.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate talked about protecting Medicare and making sure prescription drugs stayed affordable – something that matters a lot when you’re on a fixed income. I also liked the emphasis on immigration reform; my parents came here from Taiwan, and it feels good to see policies that recognize families like ours. The campaign felt like it was listening to everyday folks, and I felt my vote could actually keep the safety net we rely on.

I'm definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that keep climbing every year. It’s getting harder to stretch my pension and Social Security checks. I also worry about the local job market – a lot of the younger people I see at the community center are stuck in low‑wage gigs, and there aren’t many good opportunities for them to stay in the area. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are generally on the right track for seniors and immigrants, but sometimes they get tangled up in internal fights that slow things down. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to care more about cutting taxes for big corporations and less about the little guy trying to pay for his meds. I get fed up with the constant bickering and wish both sides would focus more on practical solutions instead of scoring political points.
