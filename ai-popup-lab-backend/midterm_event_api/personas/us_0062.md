## Background
I'm 40 and work as the night shift supervisor at a large grocery store in Peoria, right in the heart of AZ‑5. My parents moved here from Taiwan when I was a kid, so I grew up speaking a mix of English and Mandarin at home, but I never went to college; I got my high school diploma and jumped straight into retail. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment with my husband, who does construction work, and our 8‑year‑old son, Malik. Most mornings I’m up around 5 a.m. to make a quick coffee, get Malik ready for school, and drop him off at the elementary across the street before heading to the store for the early shift. After work I usually grab dinner from the food court, help Malik with his math homework, and then we watch a sitcom before I’m asleep by 10 p.m. On weekends we do grocery runs at the big warehouse club, try to keep the house clean, and sometimes visit my mom’s sister who lives nearby. I drive a used sedan that’s seen better days, and I’m always looking for ways to stretch the paycheck to cover rent, utilities, and the occasional car repair.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything, and the whole campaign felt like a circus—ads everywhere, politicians shouting at each other, and none of them talking about the real struggles of people like me who are just trying to keep a roof over our heads and put food on the table. Between the night shifts, Malik’s school schedule, and my husband’s odd hours, there was never a quiet moment to even read a ballot guide. I also get a lot of “your vote doesn’t matter” vibes from friends who say the system is rigged, so I just stayed home. I’m sitting this one out too, unless something huge and personal—like a big price hike on utilities or a local school closure—makes me feel like I have to act. My biggest everyday worries are the rent going up and grocery prices climbing faster than my paycheck. I’m also anxious about health insurance costs for Malik as he gets older. When I think about the parties, the Democrats sound like they’re promising a lot of social programs but I’m not sure they’ll actually fund them, and the Republicans keep talking about “lower taxes” while I see property taxes creeping up in my neighborhood. Both sides feel distant, and I’m mostly annoyed that they keep arguing over issues that don’t touch my daily grind.
