## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on a quiet street in New Britain, right in the heart of Connecticut's 2nd district. I spent most of my working life as a junior high school secretary after taking a few classes at the local community college—never finished a degree, but it gave me enough confidence to handle the paperwork and the occasional budget spreadsheet. I retired about ten years ago, and since then my days revolve around a routine that keeps me busy enough not to feel useless.

Every morning I brew a pot of coffee, sit at the kitchen table, and read the paper while the radio hums NPR news briefs. After breakfast I tend to my garden—tomatoes, a few herbs, and a small patch of roses that my daughter helped plant when she moved back for a short stay a few summers ago. I walk the block to the nearby grocery store, usually around 10 a.m., where I chat with the clerk about the price of milk and the latest town council meeting. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I go to the local senior center for a bingo game and a low‑impact exercise class; the social aspect is as important as the activity itself.

My husband passed away five years back, and my two children live in different states now—one in Massachusetts, the other in Florida. They call weekly, and sometimes they visit for a weekend, but most of my time is spent with my neighbor, Mrs. Alvarez, who helps me with the occasional heavy lifting and we share a love for classic TV reruns. Sundays are reserved for church services at the small Methodist congregation down the road, followed by a potluck lunch with a handful of longtime friends. Evenings are quiet: I watch a news program or a drama on TV, then read a few chapters of a mystery novel before bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the candidate was the only one who talked straight about keeping taxes low enough for retirees like me and protecting Social Security from any cuts. I also liked the emphasis on law and order, especially after hearing about a string of burglaries in our neighborhood last summer. I wasn't thrilled by any of the platforms, but the Democratic side seemed to want to raise taxes and push for big changes that felt risky at my age. I trusted the promise of stability more than the talk of sweeping reforms.

Now, looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. I think my vote still matters, especially for the Senate race where the balance could tilt one way or the other. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hike the town approved last year to fund a new school wing. Both of those hit my budget hard, and I keep an eye on any proposals that might ease those pressures.

My view of the parties is pretty blunt. The Republicans, in my experience, know the importance of a steady paycheck and a predictable tax bill, but sometimes they get caught up in culture wars that feel irrelevant to my day‑to‑day life. The Democrats, on the other hand, keep talking about climate change and social justice, which are noble ideas, but they also seem to want to raise taxes to fund everything, and that scares me. I wish they would focus more on fixing the healthcare system and keeping property taxes reasonable for seniors.
