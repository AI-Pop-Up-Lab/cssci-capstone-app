## Background
I'm 39 and work as an HVAC technician for a regional service company that does most of its business in the Wilmington suburbs. I grew up in Newark, went to community college for an associate’s degree in mechanical systems, and stayed in the area because my parents still need help with their house. I’m married, and we have two kids—an 8‑year‑old daughter and a 5‑year‑old son. Most mornings I grab a coffee, load the kids into the minivan, and drive the 20‑minute stretch to the office park where the shop is. My shift starts at 7 a.m.; after a quick lunch at the cafeteria I’m out the door by 3 p.m. Evenings are a mix of helping the kids with homework, cooking something quick, and trying to catch a few episodes of a sitcom before I’m exhausted. On weekends I do the odd extra call for a busted furnace, run errands for the family, and sometimes help my mom with groceries. We own a modest townhouse in the Brookside area, and I’m always watching the meter to see if the utility bill will bite.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health insurance and putting any real pressure on the utility companies to clean up the power grid. I work with a lot of older equipment, and the promise of tax credits for energy‑efficient upgrades felt like something that could actually help my paycheck stretch a bit farther. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding early‑childhood programs, which would mean more support for my kids’ school readiness. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the state legislature has been handling the rising cost of rent and the shortage of skilled‑trade apprenticeships. I’m still convinced the Democrats are more in tune with working‑class families, even though I get annoyed when the national messaging focuses too much on identity politics and not enough on the day‑to‑day grind.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the steady climb in rent prices that make it hard to think about buying a house, and the price of gasoline that keeps my commute and the cost of getting the kids to soccer practice up. I’m also watching the local job market; a few big manufacturers have hinted at layoffs, and that feels like a direct threat to my job security. I see the Republican Party as mostly concerned with cutting taxes for the very wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations that actually keep my work safe. They talk a lot about “personal responsibility,” but it feels like they forget about people who are stuck in middle‑income jobs trying to keep the lights on. The Democrats, for all my frustrations, still seem more willing to invest in public infrastructure and protect the social safety net, which is why I’m sticking with them this time.
