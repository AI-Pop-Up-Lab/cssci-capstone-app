## Background
I'm 25 and work the night shift loading trucks at the distribution center just outside Morgantown. I dropped out after two years of community college when I needed the paycheck to help my mom cover the mortgage on our two‑bedroom house. I still live with her and my younger sister, who’s in her senior year of high school. Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, grabbing a coffee from the corner deli, then heading to the warehouse where I stay until about 2 a.m. After my shift I come home, try to squeeze in a quick workout at the gym, and wind down with video games or scrolling TikTok before crashing. On my days off I’ll drive out to the mountains for a hike, or meet up with a couple of friends at the local bar to watch the football game. I don’t own a car, so I rely on the bus to get to work and the occasional ride‑share when I need to run errands farther away. My budget is tight; most of my paycheck goes to rent, utilities, and putting a little away for my sister’s college fund.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything and I was exhausted from juggling work, family responsibilities, and trying to make ends meet. The whole campaign seemed like a circus, and the candidates kept talking about issues that felt far removed from my daily grind—foreign policy, climate accords, that sort of thing. I also missed the polling day; my shift ran late and I couldn’t get a ride to a polling place. That experience left me pretty cynical, and unless something really hits close to home—like a factory closing in the area or a sudden spike in gas prices—I’m planning to sit this one out too. I’m not filing any absentee ballot; I’m just not going to the polls.

My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent and making sure the price of gasoline doesn’t keep climbing. The other thing that keeps me up at night is whether the warehouse will stay open if the company decides to relocate. When I think about the two big parties, the Democrats feel like they’re pushing policies that ignore the struggles of folks in places like mine—high taxes, too much regulation. The Republicans talk a lot about “bringing back jobs,” but most of the promises feel like slogans; I haven’t seen any concrete investment in our region. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than solving the real problems that affect my family’s paycheck.

## Media Diet
