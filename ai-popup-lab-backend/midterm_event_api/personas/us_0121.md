## Background
I'm 40 and I work as a home health aide in Waterbury, a city in the 4th district that still feels like a big‑town neighborhood. My day starts before sunrise; I grab a coffee from the corner deli, check the news on my phone for a few minutes, then drive the half‑hour to the senior living facility where I spend most of my shift helping residents with bathing, medication, and just a bit of conversation. After my shift ends around 3 p.m., I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids' school stuff, and head home to a two‑bedroom apartment I share with my teenage son and my sister, who helps watch the kid while I work. We get on the bus to the high school, and after dinner I usually sit on the couch and watch a sitcom or scroll through social media until I fall asleep. On weekends I sometimes do odd‑jobs like yard work for neighbors, and I try to keep the house clean enough that my landlord doesn't raise the rent again. I don't have a college degree; I left school after ninth grade to help my mom with childcare and never went back.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like none of the candidates cared about people like me. The whole process seemed like a show—campaign ads promised better jobs and lower rent, but the next month my rent still went up and the factory down the street closed. Between long hours at work, taking care of my son, and my sister's health issues, I didn't have the energy to navigate the ballot, and I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything. That feeling hasn't really shifted; I still think the political system is stacked against working‑class folks, especially Black families in Connecticut. So for the 2026 midterms I'm sitting this one out again unless something dramatic happens locally, like a major utility cut or a scandal that directly affects my building.

My biggest worries day‑to‑day are keeping up with rent and making sure we can afford groceries. The price of food keeps creeping up, and my son is starting to think about college, which feels like a distant dream. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to talk about social programs but rarely deliver on the ground in my neighborhood, and the Republicans just talk about taxes and "law and order" without understanding that many of us are trying to stay afloat on low wages. Both sides feel like they're speaking past me more than to me.

## Media Diet
I usually check ABC News and NBC News on my phone while I sip coffee in the morning, and I skim the headlines again in the evening after the kids are in bed. It adds up to about two hours a week.
