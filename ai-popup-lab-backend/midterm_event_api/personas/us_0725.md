## Background  
I'm 20 and I work part‑time as a barista at a coffee shop downtown Bend. I’m also taking a couple of community‑college classes in graphic design, but I’m not sure if I’ll finish the degree. My mom lives in Portland and visits once a month; my dad’s retired and stays in Seattle, so I’m on my own most weeks. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who’s studying nursing; we split rent, utilities, and the occasional grocery run. My days start early— I’m up by 6 a.m., grab a quick breakfast of toast and coffee, then bike the three miles to the shop. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to noon, so I have the afternoons free for class, homework, or the occasional gig helping a local nonprofit with flyer design. Evenings are usually spent scrolling TikTok, watching a show on Netflix, or meeting friends at a nearby park if the weather’s nice. I try to keep a budget because rent in Bend has been creeping up, and I’m saving for a car, but money is always tight.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between juggling school, work, and a rent‑that‑keeps‑going‑up, voting just felt like another thing I didn’t have time for, and honestly I was pretty fed up with the choices. The presidential race seemed like a replay of old arguments, and I didn’t think any of the candidates cared about the things that mattered to me—affordable housing, a livable wage for service workers, and climate‑related jobs in a state that’s still fighting wildfires. I also felt like my vote wouldn’t make a dent in a system that seemed stacked against folks like me. So I stayed home, and I’m leaning the same way for the 2026 midterms. Unless there’s a local issue that hits close to home—like a proposed rent‑control measure that finally gets on the ballot—I’m planning to sit this one out.

My biggest everyday worries are rent and grocery prices. The rent increase last year forced me to cut back on streaming subscriptions, and the price of fresh produce keeps climbing, making it harder to eat healthy on a student budget. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats for promising change but not delivering on housing assistance, and the Republicans feel like they’re talking about “law and order” while ignoring the climate crisis that directly impacts my community. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than solving the problems that affect my day‑to‑day life.
