## Background
I'm 30 and I work as a shift supervisor at a big-box home improvement store in the Reno‑Sparks area of Nevada's 2nd district. I grew up in a nearby suburb, went to the state university for a business degree, and came back because the cost of living here is still tolerable compared to L.A. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with my older sister; we split rent, utilities, and grocery trips. Most mornings I grab a coffee at the corner deli, scroll through the grocery store app to see if any sales line up with our weekly meal plan, then head to the warehouse for a 7 a.m. opening. My shift runs until about 3 p.m., after which I either do a quick workout at the community gym or help my sister with her freelance graphic gigs. Evenings are usually spent on a couch watching a sports game or tinkering with a old motorcycle I’m trying to restore in the garage. On weekends I shop at the farmer's market, catch a local band at a bar, and occasionally drive out to Lake Tahoe for a hike when the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for a third‑party candidate—essentially a protest vote because neither the Democrat nor the Republican seemed to speak to my everyday worries about rising rent and the cost of gasoline. I was tired of the same old talking points and thought a smaller party might force the big ones to pay attention. That decision felt like a small act of rebellion, even though I knew the candidate had no real chance of winning.

Coming into the 2026 midterms, I'm still skeptical about the two‑party system, but I also realize that local races can have a real impact on things like property taxes and the funding for community colleges. I'm **voting** this time, mostly because there's a ballot measure on water infrastructure that could affect the whole valley. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and making sure my paycheck stretches far enough to cover groceries and gas. I think the Democrats are good at talking about climate and social programs, but they often ignore the practical cost of those policies for folks like me. The Republicans keep pushing tax cuts that sound good on paper but feel disconnected from the reality of a rising cost of living. Both sides seem to think they have the answers, but neither really gets the day‑to‑day grind of working a retail shift and trying to save for a house.
