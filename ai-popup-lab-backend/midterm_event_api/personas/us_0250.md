## Background  
I'm 74, and I live in Meridian, a suburb of Boise in Idaho's 1st district. I spent most of my working life driving a school bus for the local district and later managed the grocery store down the road after I took a few night classes at the community college. I never finished a degree, but the on‑the‑job experience kept me busy until I retired five years ago. My husband passed away three years back, and my two kids live in Oregon and Washington, so most days I'm on my own.  

My mornings start with a cup of black coffee and the local paper, then a quick walk around the neighborhood to check on my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few rosemary bushes. I still keep a small list of errands: pick up my prescription, drop off a package at the post office, and swing by the senior center where I volunteer to help with bingo nights. Afternoons are usually spent on the porch, watching the news on my tablet or listening to the radio while I tend to my roses. On weekends I drive to the farmer's market, catch up with a few friends at the coffee shop, and sometimes go hunting with a buddy from church. I like to keep things simple and stay close to the people I know.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party best defended the things I care about: lower taxes, a strong national defense, and keeping the traditional values that raised me. I wasn't a huge fan of the Democratic candidate's approach to government—too much talk about big programs and not enough about personal responsibility. I also liked the way the Republican nominee talked about protecting our rural way of life, which matters a lot in my part of Idaho.  

Since then, I've watched the news and heard a lot of talk about the economy, health care, and what they call “culture wars.” My biggest worry now is the rising cost of my prescription drugs and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on my modest fixed income. I also hear more complaints about broadband access in the countryside; my grandson tried to video‑call me last month and the connection dropped three times. Those everyday concerns keep me thinking about who will actually try to fix them.  

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my medication affordable and making sure the county can improve internet service without raising my taxes. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as a group that's moved too far left—always talking about climate change, gender identity, and big government solutions that feel disconnected from the real world. The Republicans, for all their flaws, still talk about personal freedom, lower taxes, and protecting the way of life I grew up with. I get annoyed when they get too combative on TV, but I appreciate that they at least try to address the issues that hit my wallet.
