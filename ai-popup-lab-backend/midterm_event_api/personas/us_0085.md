## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest bungalow in Santa Maria, right in the middle of California's 45th district. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I worked a series of low‑wage jobs—first as a dishwasher, then as a housekeeper for a local hotel, and most recently as a part‑time caregiver for an elderly couple down the street. I get up around 6 a.m. to make a quick tea, turn on the morning news, and then head out to the grocery store before the crowds build up. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in the Bay Area, so I mostly keep to myself, but I do help my youngest grandson with his math homework on Saturdays when he visits. I belong to the local senior center where we play bingo, do a bit of exercise, and occasionally attend a church service on Sundays. My days are pretty routine: a morning walk, a few hours of work, a quick lunch at the diner on Main, and then evenings spent watching the news or chatting with my neighbor about the price of gas.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate. I did that mainly because I was worried about my Medicare coverage and the rising cost of prescription drugs—things that hit someone my age hard. I also liked the candidate's promises to protect DACA recipients; a lot of my extended family came here that way, and I felt the party was more compassionate about immigration. Since then, I haven't been glued to every political drama, but I still keep an eye on the issues that affect my pocketbook. The midterms feel like another chance to push back against the cuts to senior services that some Republican proposals hinted at, so I'm definitely going to the polls again this November. I'm voting.

My top everyday concerns are the price of my prescription meds and the property tax bill I get each spring. I also worry about grocery prices; the last few months have been a squeeze. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones more likely to protect Social Security and health programs, even if their messaging sometimes feels like a lot of talk and not enough action. Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations that affect my community's air quality. I get annoyed when they talk about "border security" without acknowledging the families I know who came here legally. Overall, I feel the parties are both stuck in their own bubbles, but I lean toward the side that at least pretends to care about seniors.
