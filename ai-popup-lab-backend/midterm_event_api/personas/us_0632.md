## Background
I'm 49 and I live in a modest house on the east side of Santa Fe, NM, not far from the Plaza where the street musicians play on weekends. I work full‑time as a high school social‑works teacher at the local public school; the job lets me stay close to the kids in the community and deal with the kinds of problems I see every day—housing instability, food insecurity, and the occasional run‑in with the law. I earned a bachelor's degree in sociology from the University of New Mexico, so I’m used to reading research reports, but I’m not a scholar; I just try to make sense of the stats when they show up in staff meetings.

I’m married to a nurse who works the night shift at the regional hospital, so most evenings we’re either cooking together or catching up on the kids’ homework before they go to bed. Our oldest, Maya, is 22 and just started grad school in Denver; the youngest, Jamal, is 17 and still in high school, playing basketball on a community league that meets after school on the gym floor. On Sundays we attend a Black‑American Baptist congregation downtown; the pastor’s sermons often touch on social justice, which keeps my mind buzzing about how policies affect people like us.

My daily routine is pretty set: I leave home around 6:30 am, drive the 15‑minute commute to school, spend the day in classrooms and the counselor’s office, then head home around 4 pm. After dinner (usually tacos or a quick stir‑fry), I either grade papers or watch a news segment while the kids finish their chores. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs—big ticket items like meat and produce have gotten pricey—family visits, and occasional trips to the farmer’s market. I keep a small savings account, but I’m always watching the next utility bill.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable healthcare and better funding for public schools—two things that matter to my family every day. The Republican platform seemed more focused on cutting taxes for big corporations and tightening immigration rules, which felt like a step backward for a community with a lot of immigrant families and a high need for social services. I also liked the Democratic stance on criminal justice reform; my work with at‑risk youth makes me see how the current system can trap kids in a cycle I try to break.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration is handling the budget for education. The state legislature has been debating cuts to school funding, and I’m nervous that my students could lose resources. The rising cost of groceries and the recent hike in property taxes on my home have also been on my mind a lot. Still, I think the Democrats are more likely to push for relief measures, even if the rollout is sometimes messy.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the ever‑rising grocery prices and the property tax bill that just came in—both are squeezing our household budget. I also worry about health insurance premiums, especially with my wife working night shifts and the kids growing up. As far as the parties go, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least try to address these issues, even if they don’t always get it right. The Republicans, to me, seem more interested in cutting programs that help people like my students and my family, and they often talk about “law and order” in a way that feels like it ignores the root causes of the problems we face. I’m not a fanatic; I just feel the left side is more in tune with my day‑to‑day reality.
