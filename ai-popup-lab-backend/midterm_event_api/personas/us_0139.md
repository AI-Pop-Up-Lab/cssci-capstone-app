## Background
I'm 30, and I live in the Vernon‑East Hartford area of Connecticut's 2nd district. I grew up on a reservation in western Connecticut, but after high school I moved to the suburbs to be closer to work. I didn't go to college; instead I took a job straight out of school loading trucks at a regional distribution center. After a few years I was promoted to shift supervisor, so now I’m responsible for a crew of about a dozen people, making sure pallets get out on time and the safety paperwork stays straight. My days start early—by 5 a.m. I’m already at the warehouse, grabbing a coffee from the corner deli and checking the schedule on my phone. After a ten‑hour shift I usually head home, swing by the grocery store, and try to get a quick dinner in before the kids (my 4‑year‑old and my partner’s 7‑year‑old) need to be put to bed. On weekends I help my mom with yard work and sometimes volunteer at the local tribal cultural center, teaching kids about our language and traditions. I drive a used pickup, keep a modest budget, and try to stretch every paycheck because rent in the area has been creeping up.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus that didn't care about people like me. The candidates on the ballot were all career politicians from the big parties, and none of them ever talked about the real issues that hit my family—rising rent, the cost of groceries, and the fact that my kids' school is always looking for extra funding. I was also working two extra shifts that month to cover a sudden car repair, so even if I wanted to cast a ballot I didn't have the time or the energy to navigate the voting process. It left me with a bitter taste, and I figured sitting out was the only honest thing to do.

Since then, I've kept an eye on local news and heard a lot of talk about the midterms, but nothing has changed my view enough to make me head to the polls. My top everyday worries are still keeping a roof over our heads and making sure we can afford the food on the table. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to promise a lot of social programs but never deliver on the ground level—especially for low‑income, minority families. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk about cutting taxes and "personal responsibility," but their rhetoric feels like it ignores the reality of folks who are already stretched thin. So, I'm sitting this one out too. If a candidate from either side actually showed up at the community center, talked about affordable housing, and listened to our concerns, maybe I'd reconsider, but for now I’m not planning to vote.
