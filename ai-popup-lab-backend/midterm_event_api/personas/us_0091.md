## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on taxes and regulation hit closer to home than the Democrats’. The tax cuts for small businesses helped my company keep more cash for research, and I was skeptical of the Democrats’ push for higher corporate taxes and stricter data‑privacy rules that I thought would slow innovation. I also liked the Republican emphasis on tougher immigration enforcement, which felt more in line with what I heard from friends in the tech community about workforce stability. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the new administration’s policies have affected the economy. Inflation has eased a bit, but gas prices are still high for my daily commute, and the housing market in Santa Barbara keeps getting pricier, making it harder for younger families to buy.  

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are property taxes—my condo assessment went up again this year—and the rising cost of groceries, which is eating into my retirement savings. I’m also watching the local job market; the tech sector here is shrinking a bit, and I worry about layoffs. I see the Republican Party as the one more likely to keep taxes low and give businesses the flexibility they need to grow, while I view the Democrats as favoring big‑government solutions that end up costing more in the long run. I’m not a political scholar, just someone trying to keep the bills manageable and the family comfortable.
