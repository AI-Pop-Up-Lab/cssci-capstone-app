## Background
I'm 20 and I live in a rented two‑bedroom apartment in Burlington, just a few blocks from the waterfront. My mom and dad are members of the Abenaki community up on the reservation near St. Johnsbury, so I visit them on holidays and help out with the seasonal harvest. I work part‑time at the local grocery store, stocking shelves and helping customers on the afternoon shift, then I head to the community college downtown for a couple of introductory courses in digital media. My roommate is a fellow student, and we split the rent and utilities, which leaves a lot of wiggle room for pizza nights and streaming marathons. Most mornings I grab a coffee at a corner café, scroll through the news on my phone while waiting for my shift, and try to squeeze in a quick study session before work. Evenings are usually a mix of homework, video games, and the occasional hike up the Green Mountains when the weather's nice. I don't have a car; I bike or use the public bus to get around, which keeps me pretty active but also means I'm always watching the bus schedule.

## Politics and this election
I turned out for the 2024 presidential election and voted for the Democratic candidate. I was motivated mainly by the climate platform and the promise of expanding health coverage—issues that hit close to home because my grandparents on the reservation have been dealing with water quality problems for years, and my aunt struggles with medical bills. I also liked the idea of more federal support for tribal sovereignty, which the Democrats emphasized more than the Republicans did. Since then, I've stayed interested in politics, but my involvement is pretty low-key; I mostly talk about it with friends at the college cafeteria or when I'm on a shift and a coworker brings up a news story.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. My top everyday concerns are the rising rent in Burlington and the price of groceries, which have been climbing faster than my paycheck. I'm also watching the state’s push to expand renewable energy projects, because I want good jobs that don't ruin the land my family depends on. I think the Democratic Party is still the better choice for me—at least they seem to care about climate and health, even if they sometimes get bogged down in internal fights. The Republicans feel like they’re more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and less on the issues that affect ordinary folks like me or my community on the reservation. I’m not a big fan of the partisan bickering, but when I have to pick, I lean Democratic.
