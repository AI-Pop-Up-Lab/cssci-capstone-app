## Background  
I'm 55 and I live in Norfolk, a small town on the edge of the Platte River in Nebraska's 2nd district. After high school I went to community college for a couple of years, took a few business and health‑administration classes, and then started working at the regional medical center as a medical records clerk. I still go in three mornings a week, mostly pulling charts and entering data for the hospital's new electronic system. The pay isn't great, but the benefits are solid, and I like the steady routine.  

My husband works part‑time at a feed store, and our two kids have both moved out—one is a teacher in Omaha and the other is a mechanic in Lincoln. We still have my mother living with us; she's in her late seventies and needs help with meds and grocery trips. Most weekends I take her to the senior center, drop the kids' groceries at the local co‑op, and then head to my church's food pantry volunteer shift. Mornings start early with a cup of black coffee, a quick scan of the local paper, and a 20‑minute drive to the hospital. After work I swing by the grocery store for a few hours, then come home, cook dinner, and watch a bit of the news before bed.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I was worried about my mother's health care costs and the rising price of prescription drugs. The incumbent senator's promise to protect Medicare and expand telehealth felt like the only thing that could keep my mom from having to choose between medicine and rent. I also liked the Democratic platform on public education; my daughter’s school needed more funding for tech upgrades, and the party’s stance on that seemed more realistic.  

Since then I've been keeping an eye on how the administration has handled the farm droughts and the ongoing teacher shortage. I still think the Democrats are the better bet for protecting seniors and keeping schools afloat, even if I sometimes feel they’re a bit out of touch with the realities of a small‑town farmer like my husband. The Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes, but I worry that the cuts will end up hitting local services that we rely on, like road maintenance and the community health clinic.  

I'm definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are my mother's health‑care costs and the rising property taxes that keep making it harder to afford our house. I also keep a close eye on the local farm policy because a bad harvest can ripple through the whole town. I view the Democratic Party as the one more likely to protect the social safety net, even if they need to do a better job of listening to folks in places like Norfolk. The Republican Party feels more focused on big‑business tax cuts, which I’m skeptical about when most of us are just trying to keep the lights on.
