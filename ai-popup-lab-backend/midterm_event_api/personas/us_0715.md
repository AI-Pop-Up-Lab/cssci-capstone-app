## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Woodward, Oklahoma, right in the middle of the 1st congressional district. I spent most of my working life at the local grain elevator, loading and unloading sacks of wheat and corn. I never went to college, so after I retired at 62 I picked up a few handyman gigs—fixing fences, painting porches—for a little extra cash. My wife passed away five years ago, and I have two grown kids who live in Tulsa and Oklahoma City. We still get a big Sunday dinner when they come back home for the holidays, and I try to stay in touch by phone most evenings.

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., make a cup of coffee, and listen to the local radio while reading the paper. After a light breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood, sometimes stopping to chat with Mrs. Jenkins next door about the garden club. Around 9 a.m. I head to the community center where I volunteer once a week helping with the senior computer class. In the afternoons I either run errands—grocery store, pharmacy, the post office—or sit on the front porch watching the kids play ball on the street. Evenings are for dinner, a bit of television, and then I usually read a mystery novel before bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security and Medicare, which are the two things keeping me afloat now that I'm on a fixed income. The Republican ads kept talking about tax cuts for the rich and cutting government programs, and that just didn’t sit right with me after a lifetime of hard work and watching my kids struggle to pay for college. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in rural broadband, something we desperately need out here for telehealth and staying connected with family.

Since then I've kept an eye on how the policies are playing out. The Medicare Part D premiums went up a bit, which hurts, but I still think the Democrats are more likely to protect the benefits I rely on. I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit out when the next round of budget decisions could affect my prescription costs and the funding for the senior center where I volunteer. My top everyday concerns are keeping my health insurance affordable and making sure the local hospital stays open—if they shut down, the nearest one is over an hour away. The other big worry is the rising price of groceries; everything from meat to fresh produce seems to cost more each year.

When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least talk about the safety net, even if they’re not perfect. The Republicans, in my view, are mostly focused on cutting taxes for big businesses and don’t seem to understand how those cuts end up hurting folks like me who depend on steady, government‑funded services. I get annoyed when the talk is all about “trickle‑down” economics and not about who’s actually paying the bills at the checkout line.
