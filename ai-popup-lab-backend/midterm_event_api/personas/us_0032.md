## Background
I'm 55 and I work as a senior research analyst for a biotech firm that partners with the University of Alabama in Huntsville. I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the outskirts of Huntsville, near the Redstone Arsenal. My wife, who retired from the local school district after 30 years, spends her days gardening and volunteering at the community center. We have two adult children: a daughter who’s finishing her master’s in environmental policy out of state, and a son who’s back home working as an electrician. Most mornings I grab a coffee, read the headlines on my phone while the kettle boils, and then drive the 15‑minute commute to the lab where I spend my day crunching data and writing grant proposals. Evenings are usually a quick dinner with my wife, a video call with my daughter, and then I unwind by watching a documentary or catching up on a podcast while the kids’ dog snores on the couch. On weekends I like to hike the nearby Monte Sano trails, help my wife with the garden, and occasionally meet friends for a barbecue at the local community park.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on science funding and healthcare was the only thing that matched my professional concerns and personal worries about my parents’ medical bills. I was also motivated by the candidate’s promises to invest in renewable energy projects that could give my firm more research contracts. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are being acted on. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are more likely to protect the kind of research money my job depends on, but I’m also annoyed by the constant partisan bickering that seems to stall any real progress. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out again, especially with the upcoming state budget debates that could cut funding for the university labs. My biggest everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs for my aging parents and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on our mortgage. I view the Republican Party as increasingly focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and less attentive to the needs of middle‑class families like mine, while the Democrats, despite their flaws, at least talk about expanding healthcare and protecting research grants. That said, I’m skeptical of any candidate who talks a big game without delivering, and I’m watching the local races closely because a few state legislators could swing the budget one way or the other.
