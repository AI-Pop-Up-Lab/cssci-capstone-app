## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a small house on the outskirts of Coventry, right in the middle of Rhode Island's 2nd district. I spent most of my adult life working as a nursing aide at the local assisted‑living facility; I retired about ten years ago when I hit the mandatory retirement age. My husband passed away three years back, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids who visit on weekends, plus a handful of grandkids that come by for holidays and summer barbecues. 

My days are a simple routine. I get up around 6:30 a.m., brew a cup of coffee, and read the newspaper—usually the print version of the *Providence Journal*—while the kettle whistles. After breakfast I tend to my garden; I have a few raised beds where I grow tomatoes, beans, and herbs, which helps keep the grocery bill down. Around noon I head to the senior center for a bingo game or a knitting circle, depending on the day. In the late afternoon I might take a walk along the trail near the reservoir, or sit on my porch and watch the kids playing on the street. Evenings are for TV, a bit of phone time with my daughter, and sometimes a prayer meeting at the community church. I keep a modest budget, mostly watching my Social Security checks and my modest pension from the nursing home job.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Medicare and Social Security, which are the lifelines for people my age. The candidate promised to protect the prescription drug discount program and to push for more funding for home‑care services, which directly affect my daily life. I also liked the emphasis on climate resilience for coastal towns in Rhode Island—those rising tides are a real worry for my grandchildren’s future.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. My top concerns are keeping my prescription drug costs low and making sure the property tax rates in my town don’t keep climbing, because that would force a lot of seniors like me out of our homes. I also worry about the availability of reliable public transportation for people who can’t drive anymore.

When I think about the two parties, the Democrats feel like the ones who remember us older folks in their policy talks. They talk about protecting Medicare, funding senior services, and even talk about climate impacts on our shoreline. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem mostly focused on cutting taxes for businesses and wealthy folks, which I’m not sure helps anyone who’s living on a fixed income. I don’t say the GOP is all bad—I’ve known a few good Republican neighbors who are friendly and help out at the community garden—but overall I feel the party’s priorities don’t line up with my everyday worries. That said, I’m not a political scholar; I just go with what feels like it’ll keep my health care and my home secure.
