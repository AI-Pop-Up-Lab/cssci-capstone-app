## Background  
I'm 65, and I live in a modest duplex on the outskirts of Portsmouth, New Hampshire, right in the 1st Congressional District. I work part‑time as an administrative assistant for the community college down the road; the job is mostly filing, answering phones, and helping faculty with scheduling. I never finished my associate's degree in business because I needed to start working to support my family, but I still take a few night classes now and then just to keep my mind sharp.  

My husband passed away three years ago, so I'm a widow. My two grown children live in Boston and Portland, but they visit for holidays and help out when I need a hand around the house. My mother, who is 88, lives with us because she can’t manage the stairs in her old apartment. I’m the one who makes sure she takes her meds, cooks her meals, and gets her to the senior center twice a week.  

A typical day starts with a cup of coffee on the porch while I listen to NPR’s “Morning Edition.” I walk the dog, a rescued mutt named “Mochi,” around the neighborhood before heading to the college around 8 a.m. My shift ends at 2 p.m., leaving me time to run errands—grocery store, pharmacy, the post office—before I come back home to prepare dinner. In the evenings I often watch the local PBS station for a news recap, then switch to ABC’s national broadcast for a quick look at the headlines. On weekends I volunteer at the church’s food pantry and sometimes help out at the town’s senior center, where I lead a knitting circle.  I like to catch up on market news on CNBC when I’m lounging on the couch, especially if there’s talk about retirement savings or prescription‑drug pricing.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attuned to the health‑care issues that affect me and my mom. The candidate’s promise to protect Medicare and push for lower prescription‑drug costs hit close to home; I’ve been watching the price of insulin climb every year, and it’s a real worry. I also liked the emphasis on expanding broadband in rural parts of New Hampshire, which would make it easier for my kids to stay connected and for senior services to run smoothly.  

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what the new administration is doing, but I’m still not convinced they’ll get everything done. The Republicans in the House keep talking about cutting taxes, but most of my tax money goes toward property taxes and school funding, and I don’t see how lower federal taxes would help my situation. I’m also skeptical about their stance on climate regulation; the flooding we had last winter reminded me how fragile our coastal town can be.  

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are prescription‑drug prices and property taxes. The first one hits my wallet every month, and the second one keeps creeping up on my mortgage payment. I think the Democrats are generally more supportive of policies that could lower drug costs, while the Republicans seem more interested in cutting government programs that I rely on, like Medicaid expansions. I don’t feel completely comfortable with either side, but I’m leaning toward the party that promises to protect seniors and keep health‑care affordable.

## Media Diet  
I listen to NPR in the mornings, catch ABC’s evening news after dinner, and scroll through CNBC when I have a spare half hour on the weekend, adding up to about eight hours a week.
