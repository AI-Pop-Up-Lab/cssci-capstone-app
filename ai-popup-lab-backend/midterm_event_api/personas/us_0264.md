## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Loves Park, a suburb of Rockford in Illinois’ 15th district. I work full‑time as a shift supervisor at a regional warehouse that ships auto parts across the Midwest. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., then I pick up my two kids from school—Mia, 17, who’s gearing up for senior year, and Ethan, 20, who’s in community college. My husband, Dave, does odd‑job work for a local contractor, so between his gigs and my overtime we scrape by on a modest household income. Most evenings we eat dinner together at home, usually something quick I can throw together after a long day—often spaghetti or a frozen pizza, unless we’re lucky enough to get fresh produce from the farmers market on Saturdays. On Sundays we attend a small Native American cultural group that meets at the community center, and we try to keep up with family obligations like my mother’s yearly health check‑up in Chicago. I drive a ten‑year‑old pickup that I bought used; it’s reliable enough for the commute but I’m always watching the gas gauge. My free time is mostly spent watching TV with the kids, scrolling my phone, or doing a bit of gardening when the weather’s nice.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on the economy and border security was the only thing keeping my family afloat. The last few years have been rough—rising property taxes in the district, grocery prices that seem to climb every month, and the cost of keeping the truck running. The Republican candidate promised to protect small‑business owners and push back against what I see as excessive regulations that make it harder for warehouses like mine to stay competitive. I also appreciated the tough talk on immigration; I hear stories from coworkers about job competition and it feels like the Democrats aren’t addressing that at all.

Since then, I’ve been watching how the party’s policies play out in the state legislature. Some of the tax cuts helped my paycheck a bit, but I’m still worried about the upcoming budget talks and whether they’ll raise school taxes again. I’m not a political junkie, but I do feel that the Democrats keep talking about climate goals and social programs while ignoring the day‑to‑day grind of folks like me. That’s why I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out unless something crazy happens that forces me to stay home.

My top everyday concerns right now are the cost of gasoline and the rising property tax bill on my home. Every time I fill up, I feel the pinch, and the tax bill that comes every spring cuts into what we can put toward college tuition for Ethan. As for the parties, I see Republicans as the ones trying to keep taxes low and the economy moving, even if they’re not perfect. The Democrats, in my view, are more focused on big‑picture ideas that don’t translate into anything I can feel in my wallet. I’m not a fan of the political theater, but I’m leaning toward voting for the GOP again because they seem to care more about my immediate financial worries.
