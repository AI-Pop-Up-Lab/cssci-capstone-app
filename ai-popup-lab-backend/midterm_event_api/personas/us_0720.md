## Background
I'm 45 and I live in a small town called Shawnee, right in the middle of Oklahoma's 3rd district. I work as an HVAC technician for a regional service company; the trade school I went to gave me the certifications I need, and I spend most weekdays on the road fixing furnaces and air conditioners for homes and small businesses. My wife works part‑time at the local community college cafeteria, and we have two kids—one in middle school and the other in high school. Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, making coffee and checking the weather so I know which jobs will need heating or cooling work. After a quick breakfast I load the truck, grab my tools, and head out. My lunch break is usually a sandwich in the driver’s seat while I listen to a mix of country radio and the occasional news clip on my phone. Evenings are family time: we eat dinner together, help the kids with homework, and sometimes watch a basketball game or a movie on the couch. On weekends I try to keep the house in order, mow the lawn, and run errands like picking up groceries and refilling the gas tank. When I have a spare hour, I’ll go fishing at the nearby lake or help out at the tribal community center, where I volunteer for cultural events.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like my ballot didn’t matter and the whole process just added more stress to an already packed schedule. The ads were all the same—big promises that never seemed to touch my life, and the candidates kept talking about issues that felt far away from my day‑to‑day concerns. I’ve got rent and a mortgage to keep up with, rising grocery prices, and the cost of fuel for the truck that’s my livelihood. Those worries make a campaign that focuses on abstract policy feel like background noise. I also get frustrated with the constant bickering on the news; it seems like both parties are more interested in scoring points than solving real problems for folks like me. Because of that, I’m leaning toward sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something hits close to home—like a major layoff at the company I work for or a serious threat to the tribal lands we share. My top everyday concerns are keeping the family’s budget balanced and making sure the gas prices don’t keep climbing. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats; their tax proposals feel like they’d hit the middle class hard. The Republicans get my attention when they talk about energy jobs and lower regulations, but even they sometimes ignore the reality of small‑town life. Bottom line: I’m not going to the polls this November; I’m sitting this one out.
