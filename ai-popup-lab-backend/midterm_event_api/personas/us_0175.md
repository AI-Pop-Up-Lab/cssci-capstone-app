## Background
I'm 30, and I work the night shift loading trucks at the big distribution center out near the I‑95 corridor in Jacksonville. I didn't finish high school; I dropped out after sophomore year to help my mom pay the bills when my dad left. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment in the Arlington neighborhood with my mom and my 19‑year‑old sister, who just started community college. My mom works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we split the rent and utilities. Most mornings I wake up around 5 am, grab a coffee from the corner stall, and catch the bus to the warehouse. The job is hard, the heat in the summer can be brutal, and the pay barely covers the rent, but it’s steady. After my shift ends around 2 pm, I swing by the fast‑food place for a cheap lunch, then head home to do laundry and help my sister with her homework when she has it. In the evenings I usually watch some YouTube clips or play a bit of basketball at the community court with a few friends. Sundays are the only day we all get together; we go to church, then Mom cooks a big lunch and we sit on the porch catching up. I don't have a lot of spare cash, so I try to stretch every dollar, especially when gas prices spike.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates really talked about the things that matter to folks like me—rent going up, grocery prices, and getting a decent job after high school. Between long shifts and caring for my family, I couldn't fit a polling place into my schedule, and I didn't trust that my vote would change anything. The campaign ads were all about big‑ticket issues that felt far away from my daily grind, so I just stayed home. I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something blows up locally—like a big layoff at the warehouse or a sudden rent hike that forces us out—I don't see a reason to drag myself to the polls. My biggest worries right now are keeping the apartment and making sure we can afford food without having to skip meals. I also worry about the heat waves getting worse and whether the city will do anything about cooling centers. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats promised more help for low‑income families, but I haven't seen any real change in my neighborhood, and sometimes their talk feels like empty slogans. The Republicans keep yelling about law and order and cutting taxes, but that usually ends up benefiting people who already have money, while we keep getting hit with higher utility bills. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than actually fixing the problems that keep me up at night.
