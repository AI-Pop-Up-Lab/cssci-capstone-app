## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on the edge of West Palm Beach, right in the 16th district. I grew up in a farming family down in the Everglades, went to college in Miami and earned a bachelor's in business administration. I spent most of my working life running a small auto‑repair shop that I sold when I hit sixty‑five. My wife passed five years ago, so now it's just me, my daughter who lives in Orlando, and the occasional visit from my two grandkids in the summer. 

My days are simple. I get up around 7 am, make a strong Cuban coffee, and read the paper while the radio plays classic boleros. After that I tend to my garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few orange trees—that's where I spend a good chunk of the morning. Around noon I head to the local senior center for bingo or a game of checkers, and I try to catch the weekly Mass at the nearby church. In the evenings I sit on the porch, watch the news on my old TV, and sometimes call my daughter to check in. I drive a well‑kept sedan to the grocery store once a week and keep an eye on my blood pressure meds.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping taxes low, protecting the border, and preserving the kind of community I grew up in. I’ve always trusted candidates who talk about personal responsibility and who say they’ll keep government from getting too big. The Democratic side, in my view, pushes for higher taxes and policies that feel like they’re written for big cities, not for folks like me who just want a quiet life and affordable healthcare.

I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising property taxes on my home and the cost of prescription drugs I need for my heart condition. The other thing that keeps me up is the uncertainty about Medicare changes that keep getting tossed around. I still think the Republican Party is the better bet for keeping my taxes down and for giving seniors like me a voice, while I see the Democrats as more focused on big‑government programs that don’t translate to the everyday needs of older, working‑class people.

## Media Diet
I usually watch the morning segments on CBS News and then flip to Breitbart or the New York Post in the evening while I’m on the porch. It adds up to about eight hours a week, split between a quick glance in the morning and a longer sit‑down after dinner.
