## Background  
I’m 84 years old and I live in a modest bungalow in the West Tampa neighborhood of the 16th congressional district. I grew up in a farm family in the Everglades, earned a bachelor’s degree in business administration back in the 1960s, and spent most of my working life running a small auto‑parts shop that I eventually sold when I was 68. My wife passed away three years ago, and I now split time between my two adult daughters—one in Orlando and the other in Miami—and the occasional weekend visit from my three grandkids.  

My days are simple. I get up around 6 a.m., make a strong Cuban coffee, and read the newspaper while the radio plays classic boleros. After a short walk around the block with my old Labrador, I sit at the kitchen table and scroll through a few news sites on my tablet. I still like to tinker with the car in the garage, but most of my time now is spent at the local senior center, playing cards, and attending the weekly mass at St. Catherine’s. I keep a modest budget; my Social Security check and a small pension cover my rent, utilities, and a little extra for groceries and the occasional treat for the kids when they’re in town.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one standing up for the things that matter to me: keeping taxes low, protecting the border, and respecting traditional values. I remember the campaign ads about “protecting American jobs” and “strong families,” and those messages resonated with someone who has spent a lifetime working hard and watching the country change.  

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m not interested in sitting this one out; I want to make sure the folks in Washington keep their promises about Medicare and Social Security. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that my property taxes have crept up even though my income hasn’t. I’m also uneasy about the talk of expanding Medicaid—I'm already on a fixed income and don’t want any surprise cuts to the benefits I rely on.  

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re always trying to overhaul the system in ways that could jeopardize the stability I’ve built. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about preserving the things that have worked for generations. I’m not a fan of the political theater; I just want policies that keep my retirement secure and my community safe.

## Media Diet

I talked it over with my neighbor at the senior center, and after hearing a few complaints about safety, I decided I was against it, even though I hadn’t read the official plan. That’s how I usually decide—by gut feeling and the conversations that happen around me.
