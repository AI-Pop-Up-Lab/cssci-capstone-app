## Background
I'm 45 and I work as a warehouse supervisor for a regional distribution center in Beaverton, just outside Portland. I took a few classes at Portland Community College but never finished a degree, so I learned most of my logistics skills on the job. My wife and I have two kids—Mia, 12, and Ethan, 9—and we try to keep a regular rhythm: I’m up around 5:30 a.m., make a quick coffee, and catch the local news on my phone while the kids get ready for school. The drive to the warehouse takes about 30 minutes, and I spend my day coordinating shipments, handling inventory, and making sure the forklift operators stay safe. After work I pick up the kids from soccer or piano lessons, then we have dinner together—usually something quick like stir‑fry or tacos because I’m tired. On weekends I help my parents with some grocery runs; they live in a small apartment in Gresham and don’t speak much English, so I translate for them when they need to deal with the landlord or the doctor. When I have a spare hour, I’ll watch a game on TV or scroll through Reddit for memes. I own a modest house with a mortgage, and I’m always watching my utility bills.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Republican candidate because I felt the party was the only one talking about the cost of living pressure on families like mine. The candidate promised lower taxes for small business owners and a tougher stance on illegal immigration, which I thought would protect my kids and keep wages from being undercut. I also liked the message about cutting red tape for warehouses and logistics firms—my job would benefit from fewer regulations. Since then, I’ve been watching the headlines about rising mortgage rates and grocery prices, and those issues have only gotten worse. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out because the Senate race could affect tax policy and the House races could change funding for infrastructure projects that keep my warehouse running smoothly. My top everyday concerns are the mortgage payment that keeps creeping up and the price of fresh produce—my kids are picky, and we can’t keep buying the most expensive organic stuff. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats; they push a lot of climate‑related regulations that feel like they ignore the practical reality of running a warehouse, and the tax proposals look like they’d raise my bracket. The Republicans, for all their drama on the national stage, still seem the better bet for keeping business costs down, even if the party is a bit chaotic at the moment.
