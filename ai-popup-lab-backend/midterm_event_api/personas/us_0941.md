## Background
I'm 55 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Altoona, just outside Eau Claire. I grew up in a Taiwanese immigrant family, so the kitchen always smells like soy sauce and garlic. After getting an associate’s degree in dental hygiene, I’ve worked most of my adult life at the local community health clinic, cleaning teeth and teaching patients how to take care of their mouths. The job is steady, the pay is enough to keep the mortgage and the kids’ college tuition covered, but it never feels like a career ladder—just a reliable routine.

My husband, a retired factory line supervisor, spends his days tinkering in the garage and watching sports on the old TV. Our two kids have moved out; the older one is a software developer in Madison and the younger is finishing a nursing program in Milwaukee. I still cook most of our meals, but we all chip in with groceries and the occasional take‑out. My mornings start with a quick walk around the neighborhood, a cup of black coffee, and scrolling through the news on my phone before I head to the clinic at 8 a.m. After work, I usually pick up the kids’ laundry, tend to the garden, and unwind with a bit of K‑drama on Netflix. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, church potlucks, and helping my husband with his woodworking projects.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because health care was front‑and‑center for me. My mother’s arthritis meds got pricier, and the candidate’s promise to protect Medicare and expand prescription‑drug coverage felt like something that directly helped my family. I also liked the emphasis on funding for community health centers—places like the one I work at. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still skeptical about a lot of the rhetoric, the concrete steps on Medicaid expansion in Wisconsin convinced me that the Democrats are at least trying.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hike that’s been gnawing at my budget. I’m also tired of hearing about “tax cuts for the rich” when my own tax bill keeps creeping up. The Republican Party feels like it’s more interested in cutting social programs than fixing the gaps that people like my patients rely on. I don’t think any party is perfect, but the Democrats seem more willing to address the health‑care and education issues that affect my daily life, whereas the GOP often talks about “smaller government” in a way that looks like they want to shrink the safety nets I depend on.
