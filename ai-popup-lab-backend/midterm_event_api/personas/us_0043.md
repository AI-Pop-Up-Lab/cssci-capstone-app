## Background
I'm 30, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in the North Little Rock area of AR‑3. I work full‑time as a customer‑service supervisor at a regional warehouse that ships parts for the automotive industry. The job pays enough to cover rent and utilities, but I still keep a tight budget because the market there has been squeezing wages. My mornings start at 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee from the corner diner, then I hop on the 6:15 bus to the distribution center. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., with a half‑hour lunch break that I usually spend scrolling through my phone or catching up on a podcast.

After work I either run errands—like picking up groceries at the discount store down the road—or meet up with my roommate, a fellow college‑dropout who does freelance graphic design. We split the rent and take turns cooking; most nights it’s something simple like tacos or spaghetti. On weekends I like to hike the local trails or visit my parents in Hot Springs, which is a two‑hour drive away. I’m not married, don’t have kids, and I’m not in a serious relationship right now; I spend my free time watching a mix of reality TV and true‑crime documentaries. My parents are originally from Mexico and the Philippines, so I grew up bilingual in English and Spanish, though I don’t use Spanish much outside the family.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my ballot wouldn’t change anything, and the whole process just seemed like a waste of time. Between juggling a demanding job, the high cost of living, and trying to keep my apartment in good shape, I couldn’t fit voting into my schedule. The candidates all sounded the same to me—big promises that never touch everyday folks like me. I also get annoyed by the constant negative ads and the feeling that politicians only care about donors and lobbyists. Because of that, I’m planning to sit this one out as well in the 2026 midterms, unless something huge hits my community—like a major factory closing or a scandal that hits my neighborhood directly.

My top everyday concerns are keeping up with rent and dealing with rising grocery prices. Every week I have to stretch my paycheck a little further, and when food prices jump, I feel the pinch immediately. The political parties feel like two sides of the same coin to me. The Democrats talk about “change” but seem out of touch with the cost‑of‑living struggles in Arkansas, while the Republicans keep pushing tax cuts that mostly help the wealthy and big corporations. I’m not a fan of either side; they both seem more interested in scoring points than solving real problems for people like me.
