## Background
I'm 35 and I live in the west side of Tucson, right in the part of AZ‑6 that borders the Santa Cruz River. I work full‑time as a cashier at a big‑box grocery store, which is about a fifteen‑minute drive from my apartment. I’m a single mom to two kids—Jaden, 8, and Maya, 5—so most of my day revolves around getting them ready for school, dropping them off at the neighborhood elementary, and then heading to my shift. My mornings start at 5 am; I brew a cheap coffee, check the news on my phone while the kids eat cereal, then hop in the car. After work I pick the kids up, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick grocery run before cooking dinner. We rent a two‑bedroom place; the rent went up last year, and we’re always watching the utility bill. On weekends I try to catch a late‑night basketball game at the community center or help Maya with a school project. I don’t have a college degree, but I’ve taken a few online courses in basic computer skills to keep up at work. When I can, I sit on the porch with a cheap paperback and listen to the street kids playing.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about things that matter to me—affordable healthcare, keeping the grocery store’s wages decent, and protecting funding for my kids’ schools. I was also fed up with the constant talk about border walls; I see people crossing the river every day, and the rhetoric felt like it ignored the real lives here. The campaign rallies in Tucson were loud, but I went to a few neighborhood meetings and heard a local organizer talk about expanding Medicaid, which hit home when my cousin needed a prescription and the costs were sky‑high. That made me turn out to the polls, even though I was exhausted after a double shift.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming, I’m definitely voting. The ballot has a Senate race and a House race, plus some state propositions about property taxes and water rights—both of which bite my wallet. My top everyday worries are rent staying affordable and grocery prices climbing faster than my paycheck. I’m still leaning Democratic because I think they’re more likely to push for rent control measures and keep SNAP benefits strong. I’m annoyed with the Republicans; they keep talking about “border security” and “family values” while ignoring the fact that my kids’ school can’t afford new textbooks. I don’t trust them to understand the daily grind of a single mother working two jobs. Still, I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I’ll vote for a Republican if they actually propose a real plan to lower utility costs, but so far none have spoken my language.
