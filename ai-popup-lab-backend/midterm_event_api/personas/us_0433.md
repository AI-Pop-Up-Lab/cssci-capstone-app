## Background

I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Saginaw, right near the old downtown factory that still hums with the sound of metal stamping. I work as a maintenance supervisor at the automotive parts plant down the road; I've been there for almost twenty years, climbing from a line mechanic after I took a couple of years of community college in engineering but never finished a degree. My wife, Carol, retired from the local hospital a few years back, and we have two adult kids—one lives in Detroit with a tech job, the other moved up to Canada to teach. My mother, who’s in her late 80s, still lives with us because the assisted living options are too pricey and she doesn’t like leaving the neighborhood. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30, grab a cup of coffee, and read the headlines on my phone while the kettle boils. I head to the plant around 7, spend the day making sure the conveyor belts don’t break down, checking safety logs, and dealing with a crew that’s a mix of retirees and younger apprentices. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and try to catch a game on TV before I’m in bed by 10. On weekends I do a bit of yard work, help my mother with her meds, and sometimes meet up with old high‑school buddies at the local bar for a couple of beers and a round of pool.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for the kind of jobs that keep our plant running. The candidate promised to keep regulations from choking off manufacturing, and that meant a lot to me when I see the plant’s managers worrying about new environmental rules that could cost us layoffs. I also liked the talk about cutting property taxes; my wife and I have been watching the county assessors’ office hike our bill every year, and that’s money we can’t spare when we’re already paying for my mom’s care. Since then I’ve watched the same party’s mess with the debt ceiling and the constant bickering in Washington, and I’m still convinced they’re the better of the two, even if they don’t always get the details right. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—there’s a Senate race that could affect the auto industry’s tax breaks, and I’m not going to let anyone else decide that for me. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rising gasoline prices that make my commute and my truck runs cost more, and the fact that health‑care premiums keep creeping up even though I’m not sick. The Democrats, in my view, love to talk about climate change and social programs, but when it comes to actual factories and small‑town taxes they’re either out of touch or just looking for votes. The Republicans mess up sometimes, but at least they’re talking about keeping the jobs that feed families like mine.
