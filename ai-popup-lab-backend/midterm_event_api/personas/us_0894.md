## Background
I'm 25 and I work the night shift loading trucks at a regional distribution center just outside Charlottesville. I grew up in a small town in the county and never went to college; the high school diploma got me the job I have now. I still live in my parents' house because rent in the area has gone up and I can’t afford a place of my own on a warehouse salary. My dad runs a farm a few miles away, so I drive out there on weekends to help with the harvest and to pick up fresh eggs for the family. My mom does the cooking and keeps the house running while I’m at work. I’m single, but I have a roommate who’s in a similar situation – we split the utility bills and take turns doing the grocery runs. My day usually starts with a quick coffee from the gas station, then a half‑hour drive to the warehouse. The night shift runs from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., and after I get home I’m exhausted, so I spend most of my free time scrolling on my phone, watching a bit of TV, or hanging out with friends at the local bar on Friday evenings. I’m not into any clubs or organized activities; I just try to keep a steady paycheck and stay out of trouble.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two jobs, taking care of my dad’s farm chores, and dealing with the constant hassle of trying to keep the lights on at home, I just never got around to it. The whole process feels like a performance for people who already have a seat at the table, and I don’t see a lot of difference between the candidates. I also get a lot of negative ads that feel like they’re screaming at me from the TV, which just makes me tune them out. Because of that, I’m planning to sit this one out too. I might show up if something huge hits the community – like a factory closing or a major road project that threatens my family’s farm – but otherwise I’m not motivated to cast a ballot.

My biggest everyday worries are the price of gas and the stability of my job. When the price of diesel spikes, my commute to the warehouse gets pricey, and if the warehouse cuts hours, I’m looking at a real financial crunch. The other thing that keeps me up is the cost of groceries; even basic staples feel like they’re getting more expensive every month. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats. They seem to push policies that ignore rural concerns and make taxes higher, which hurts people like my dad. The Republicans aren’t much better in my view; they talk a lot about “freedom” and “small government,” but many of them are pushing for policies that would raise my insurance costs or threaten the kind of farming my family does. So I’m stuck feeling like both sides are looking out for themselves more than for folks like me.
