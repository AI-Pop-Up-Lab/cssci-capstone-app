## Background

I'm 79, and I live in a modest townhouse in Arlington, just a few blocks from the Metro. I spent most of my career as a professor of African American history at a state university, so I’m used to lecturing, grading papers, and attending endless faculty meetings. I retired five years ago, but I still keep a few books on my nightstand and I volunteer twice a week at the local senior center, helping run a computer class for other retirees. My wife passed away three years back, and I have two grown children—one lives in Richmond and the other in Washington, D.C.—who visit around holidays. My adult grandson stays with me on weekends and helps me with the garden; we grow tomatoes and peppers in the small yard. My days usually start with a cup of black coffee and the morning paper, then a walk around the neighborhood with my old Labrador. Afternoons are for checking my email, reading a bit of news, and sometimes catching a game on TV. Evenings are quiet: a simple dinner, a bit of TV, and then I’m usually in bed by ten.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s focus on lower taxes and a strong national defense aligned with my own concerns. After decades watching budget cuts hit public universities, I appreciated the talk about fiscal responsibility, even if I’m not sure it always works out. I also liked the candidate’s tough stance on crime; living in a city that’s gotten a little less safe over the years, I wanted someone who promised to back the police. I wasn’t thrilled with the Democratic platform either—too much emphasis on big‑government programs that seem to ignore the realities of older folks trying to stretch a fixed income.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that keep biting into my pension checks. I also keep an eye on the local school board because my grandchildren still visit sometimes, and I want decent schools for them. As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats; they talk a lot about equity but often push policies that raise my taxes and make the government seem even bigger. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting waste and keeping the military strong. I don’t expect miracles, but I feel a Republican candidate is more likely to listen to an old‑timer like me who just wants his money to go farther.

## Media Diet

I tune into ABC News and CNBC in the mornings while I have coffee, and I catch NPR podcasts in the evenings after dinner. All together it’s about 13 hours a week.
