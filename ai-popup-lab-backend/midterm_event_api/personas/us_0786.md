## Background
I'm 20 and I live in Inman, a little town on the edge of the Upstate. My mom works two jobs at a local diner and a cleaning service, and my 14‑year‑old brother is still in middle school, so the house is always busy. After I graduated from high school last year I got a job as a cashier at the grocery store down the road. My shift is usually 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., then I swing by the fast‑food place for a second shift on weekends. I don't have a college plan right now; I’m saving up for a truck so I can help my mom with the family’s small landscaping side hustle. Most evenings I’m either scrolling on my phone, watching a few YouTube videos, or helping my brother with his homework. On Sundays my family goes to the local Catholic church, and after that we might grill something in the backyard if the weather’s nice. I drive a beat‑up sedan my dad gave me, and I take the bus to work when the car is in the shop.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party cared more about keeping prices down and protecting our community from the illegal‑immigration headlines that keep popping up on the news. My mom says she worries about crime and the cost of gasoline, and the Republican candidate talked about cutting taxes for small businesses like the diner where she works. I wasn’t thrilled about any of the choices, but the Democratic side seemed to want higher taxes and more regulation, which felt like a threat to the little bit of stability we have.

I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. I’m not a huge political junkie, but the idea of losing the few things we’ve managed to keep—steady hours at the store, a decent wage, low property taxes—keeps me from sitting this one out. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of groceries and the fact that my mom’s hours keep getting cut when the store is short‑staffed. I also worry about the local highway repairs; it’s a pain when the road is closed and it adds extra miles to my commute.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats. They keep talking about “climate justice” and “social programs” but I don’t see how that helps my mom pay the rent or my brother afford a new backpack. The Republicans, for all their talk about “family values,” sometimes sound like they’re ignoring the fact that many in my community are Hispanic and need a little more respect. Still, I feel they’re the better bet for keeping taxes low and letting small businesses stay open.
