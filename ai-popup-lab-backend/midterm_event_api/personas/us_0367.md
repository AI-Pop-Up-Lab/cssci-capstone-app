## Background
I'm 74 and I work part‑time as a dental hygienist at a community health clinic in Brockton, Massachusetts. I got my associate degree in dental hygiene from a local community college right after high school, then spent a few years raising my kids before returning to the field. My husband, a retired machinist, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on a quiet street not far from the downtown bus line. Every morning I take a short walk to the corner store for a coffee and a bagel, then head to the clinic where I clean and polish teeth for about twenty patients. After work I swing by the local church for the senior fellowship meeting on Wednesdays, and on Sundays I help my daughter with groceries for her family in nearby Plymouth. My granddaughter visits me on Saturdays; we bake empanadas together and I watch the news while she does her homework. The rent is low because we own the place, but property taxes keep creeping up, so I keep an eye on the local assessor's office. Most of my day is routine, but I like to stop by the community garden on the corner when the weather’s nice.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping my prescription meds affordable and protecting Social Security. The campaign ads kept talking about lowering drug prices, which hit home after my doctor raised my insulin cost last year. I also liked the promise of more funding for community health clinics like mine. Since then, I’ve kept a low profile but still watch the news; I’ve heard a lot of talk about the Republican push to cut Medicare, which worries me. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms – I can’t afford to sit this one out. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that make it harder to stay in my home. When I think about the parties, Democrats seem more attuned to seniors and working families, while Republicans feel like they’re talking about tax cuts for the rich and cutting programs I rely on. I’m not a political scholar, but I’ve learned that the party that looks out for the little things – like my pharmacy bill and my tax bill – is the one I’ll keep supporting.

## Media Diet
