## Background
I'm 20 and I live in Spartanburg, South Carolina, right near the edge of the downtown district where the older brick buildings meet the newer apartments. I never finished high school; I left after sophomore year to help my mom cover the rent after my dad stopped working. I work part‑time at the local grocery store, stocking shelves and ringing up customers on the evening shift, usually from 4 p.m. to midnight. On weekends I pick up extra hours in the fast‑food kitchen down the road, because the paycheck from the store barely keeps up with the rent on my one‑bedroom place. I share the apartment with my mom, who works as a home health aide, and my 15‑year‑old brother, who is still in school. We get up early, grab a cheap coffee from the corner shop, and head out before the sun is up. After my shift I often have to do dishes and laundry before I can even think about homework—although I’m not in school, I try to keep up with a GED class online when I can find a quiet hour. My free time is mostly scrolling TikTok on my phone, chatting with friends from the neighborhood, and trying to stretch the grocery budget enough for a weekend trip to the lake with my brother.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing felt like a circus and I was just too busy trying to make ends meet. The ballot looked like a list of promises that never touch people who are juggling two jobs just to keep a roof over their heads. I also felt like my voice wouldn’t count—my district leans red, but the candidates both seemed to talk about big‑picture stuff while ignoring the fact that my mom can’t afford the rising cost of prescription meds and I can’t even afford a decent pair of shoes. I keep telling myself that if I don’t have the time or the energy to vote, I’m better off staying home and not getting my hopes raised and then let down.

For the 2026 midterms I’m sitting this one out, too. Unless something blows up locally—like a sudden closure of the grocery store or a policy that directly hits my family’s paycheck—I don’t see a reason to waste another Saturday at the polls. My biggest everyday worries are paying rent on time and keeping grocery prices low enough that I don’t have to skip meals. I also worry about the local job market; the store’s hours get cut whenever there’s a downturn, and there’s never any real training or promotion path for someone like me. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about immigration reform and student debt relief, but none of that helps my mom or me right now. The Republicans keep yelling about tax cuts and “hard work,” but they never seem to care about low‑wage workers who can’t afford a decent rent. I guess I’m just fed up with the same old promises that never translate into anything tangible for folks on the ground.
