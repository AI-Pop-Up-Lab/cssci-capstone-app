## Background

I never finished high school; after a few years working at the local grocery store I got married and raised three kids. My husband passed away ten years ago, so now it's mostly me, a couple of grown‑up children who live in Atlanta and Charlotte, and my great‑grandson who visits on Sundays. I spend my mornings making coffee, checking the mail, and tending to the garden where I grow tomatoes, beans, and a few herbs. I still drive my old sedan to the church three times a week; the pastor knows me by name and I sit in the same pew every Sunday. After church I usually stop by the diner for a biscuit and a cup of tea before heading home to knit or watch a bit of TV. I keep a small budget, paying my property taxes and the electric bill, and I rely on Social Security and a modest pension from the grocery store years. On Tuesdays I go to the senior center for bingo, and on Saturdays I help a neighbor with yard work in exchange for a few fresh eggs. My days are simple, and I like the routine because it keeps me busy and out of the house.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party best understood my concerns about taxes, the right to own a gun, and keeping the federal government from meddling in my life. I liked the candidate’s promise to protect Social Security from cuts and to keep the cost of prescription drugs down, even if I wasn’t sure how realistic those promises were. The Democratic side seemed too focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that felt far removed from my farm‑yard reality. I also trusted the local Republican organizer who knocked on my door and talked about preserving traditional values; that personal touch mattered more than any TV ad.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m not one to sit this one out; I think my vote still matters, especially with the Senate race shaping up and the House seat being contested by a newcomer I don’t trust. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription medication and the property tax hike the county proposed last month. If those bills keep climbing, I’ll be forced to cut back on food or heat. I’m also nervous about the shortage of home‑care aides; my sister lives far away and I can’t afford a full‑time caregiver. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who at least talk about protecting seniors and keeping taxes low, even if they sometimes argue loudly on TV. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free college and big government programs that will end up raising my taxes or forcing me to rely on programs I don’t trust. I’m not a political scholar, just a grandmother who wants her meds, her garden, and her peace of mind.
