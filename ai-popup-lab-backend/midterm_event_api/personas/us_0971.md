## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in Wheeling, right on the Ohio River. I work as a maintenance supervisor at the local steel plant; the job mostly needs the welding and equipment‑troubleshooting skills I picked up at community college. My shift starts at 6 a.m., so I’m usually at the plant by seven, doing rounds, ordering parts, and training a few younger guys who are just starting out. I’m married to a retired school secretary, and we have two adult children—one lives in Charleston and the other is in Ohio, both with families of their own. My mother, who’s 84, still lives with us because she can’t manage the nursing home out here, so a lot of my evenings are spent helping her with meds and making sure she’s safe. On Saturdays I head to the nearby church for the potluck lunch; on Sundays I sit in the pews for the 10 a.m. service and then drive back home to work on my garden. I drive a beat‑up pickup that’s seen better days, and when I have a spare hour I watch the local high school football game on TV or try to catch up on the news before bed.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between a grueling work schedule, caring for my mom, and feeling like none of the candidates really understood the problems of a small‑town steel worker, I just stayed home. The whole thing felt like a show where the same talkers keep promising better jobs and cheaper health care, but the plant still cuts shifts when the market dips. I’ve gotten used to thinking that my single vote won’t move the needle, especially when every campaign ad seems to target big‑city voters, not folks like me who are trying to keep a roof over our heads and a steady paycheck. I’m sitting this one out too, unless something blows up locally—like a plant closure or a sudden hike in property taxes—that forces me to show up at the polls. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription meds and whether the plant will stay open long enough for my kids’ kids to have a job here. I’m also watching the utility bills; the winter heating bill keeps creeping up and my wife says we can’t keep splurging on fixes for the old furnace. As for the parties, I see Democrats as all talk about “justice” but they forget the day‑to‑day grind of a worker on the shop floor, and I see Republicans as pushing social issues that feel out of touch with the reality of a Black family trying to make ends meet in West Virginia. Neither side feels like they’re really on my side, so I’m pretty disillusioned.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR’s morning podcasts while I’m driving to the plant, and I catch the evening briefs on ABC News and CBS News after dinner. That adds up to about seven hours a week between the three outlets.
