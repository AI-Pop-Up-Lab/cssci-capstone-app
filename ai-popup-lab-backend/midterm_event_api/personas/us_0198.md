## Background

I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Albany, Georgia, right in the 8th congressional district. I never finished high school; I left after eighth grade to help my parents on a small farm nearby. I spent most of my working life picking peaches, hauling crates, and later doing odd jobs as a handyman for a few local contractors. I retired a few years ago when the farm was sold, but I still take on a couple of repair gigs to keep the lights on and to stay busy.

I'm widowed; my wife passed away five years ago after a long battle with diabetes. We raised three kids together, and now all three live in different states—one in Atlanta, another in Texas, and the youngest back in Florida with her own family. I get phone calls most Sundays and occasional video chats, but the house feels pretty quiet during the week. My day usually starts with a cup of strong coffee, a quick scan of the news on my tablet while the rooster outside crows, and then a walk around the neighborhood to check on the garden and the mail. I spend a few hours in the afternoon fixing a leaky faucet, painting a fence, or helping a neighbor with yard work. In the evenings I watch a bit of TV, read the local paper, and try to keep up with my grandchildren’s school updates through group chats. I get on a bus to the grocery store once a week; the prices keep creeping up, so I’m always looking for sales and coupons.

## Politics and this election

I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate promised to protect Social Security and Medicare, which are my lifelines now that I'm on a fixed income. The Republican opponent kept talking about cutting taxes for the wealthy and slashing government programs, and that just didn’t sit right with me after a lifetime of hard labor. I also liked the Democratic stance on expanding community health clinics—my wife relied on one a lot, and I still remember how grateful we were when they helped her get the medication she needed.

This time around, I'm definitely going to the polls. I'm voting. My biggest worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that property taxes keep climbing even though my pension doesn’t stretch as far as it used to. I also keep an eye on immigration policies because a lot of my extended family still lives in Mexico, and any harsh crackdown makes visits and remittances harder. I tend to see the Democratic Party as the one that looks after older folks like me, keeps the safety net intact, and is at least trying to address climate issues that affect our farms. The Republicans, in my view, are more interested in deregulating businesses and tightening immigration rules, which feels like they’re ignoring the daily grind of people who don’t have a college degree or a big bank account. I’m not a political scholar; I just know which side seems to care more about the things that matter to me.

## Media Diet
