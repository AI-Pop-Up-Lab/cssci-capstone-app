## Background
I'm 20 and I live in a small apartment above a bike shop in Burlington, right in the heart of VT‑1. I'm juggling the last two years of an associate’s program in environmental studies at a community college while working evenings at a local grocery store. My parents moved here from Taiwan when I was a kid, so I grew up speaking a mix of English and Mandarin at home, and the whole family still helps out with the family‑run take‑out place a few towns over on weekends. Most days I’m up before 8 a.m., sprinting to my shift, then hitting the campus library for a couple of hours of classes or labs. After work I usually meet up with a couple of friends at the coffee shop on Church Street, where we talk about music, climate protests, and the latest TikTok trends. On Sundays I help my mom prep meals for the family restaurant and try to squeeze in a hike up the Green Mountains when the weather’s nice. My rent is modest but rising, and I’m constantly budgeting between tuition, groceries, and the occasional concert ticket.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Green Party candidate instead of either major party. I was fed up with the usual back‑and‑forth between the Democrats and Republicans and felt the Green platform actually addressed the climate and social justice issues that matter to me. I also liked that the candidate was a young activist from Burlington, someone I could see at local rallies. The campaign felt grassroots, and I thought my vote could help push the bigger parties to take climate policy more seriously.

Since then, I’ve stayed pretty skeptical of the two‑party system. I still think the major parties are more about fundraising than real change, but I’m also aware that third‑party candidates rarely get traction beyond the ballot. For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls; I’ve already signed up for early voting because I don’t want to miss the chance to support a progressive Democrat who’s promising a concrete climate bill, even if I’m not thrilled about the whole thing. My top everyday worries are paying rent and keeping my tuition from ballooning out of reach. I’m also annoyed by how quickly the news jumps from one scandal to the next, making it hard to keep track of what actually matters. I see the Democrats as the lesser of two evils when it comes to social programs, but I’m frustrated that they still compromise on big environmental legislation. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re trying to roll back everything I care about—climate action, student debt relief, and even basic civil liberties. I don’t trust them to listen to someone my age, and I think their rhetoric is more about culture wars than solving real problems.
