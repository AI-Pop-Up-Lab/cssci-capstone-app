## Background

I'm 20 and I live in Santa Maria, right in the middle of California's 25th district. I grew up in a two‑bedroom apartment with my mom, who works double shifts at a nearby warehouse, and my 16‑year‑old brother, who just started high school. After I got my high school diploma I didn't go straight to college; I needed to help with rent, so I took a job as a cashier at a grocery store downtown. My shifts are usually split between the morning rush and the evening close‑out, so my days start early, with a quick coffee from the corner shop before I hop on the bus to the store. When I'm not working I’m either scrolling on my phone, helping my brother with his homework, or catching up on a few episodes of a streaming show before I crash. I drive a beat‑up sedan that my mom bought used; the car is more of a necessity than a hobby. On weekends I sometimes work extra hours at a local farmers market stall, selling fresh produce, because the extra cash helps us keep up with the rent that keeps creeping up. I’m not in school right now, but I’m thinking about getting a GED and maybe some community‑college classes if I can save enough.

## Politics and this election

I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my voice wouldn't matter; the whole process seemed like a show put on for the media. Between juggling two jobs, my mom's unpredictable schedule, and trying to keep the lights on, voting just didn’t make the cut. I also get sick of hearing the same talking points from both parties and never seeing any real change in my neighborhood—rent keeps rising, and the grocery store where I work still has only a handful of fresh items. That feeling of cynicism kept me from even bothering to register until after the election, and even then I haven't taken the step to get a ballot.

I'm sitting this 2026 midterm out, too. Unless something huge hits our community—like a major rent‑control proposal that actually passes or a scandal that forces a local official out—I don't see a reason to make the trip to the polling place. My top everyday concerns are paying the rent on time and keeping grocery prices from shooting up even more. I’m also worried about my brother’s future; the cost of college feels like a distant dream.

When I think about the parties, I’m more annoyed with the Republicans. Their talk about “law and order” feels like a code for cracking down on people like my mom who work low‑wage jobs. The Democrats, at least on the surface, seem to care about social programs, but I feel they’re too busy with internal fights and don’t push hard enough for affordable housing or better wages. I’m not a political analyst, I just get frustrated when I see the same promises repeated and never delivered.

## Media Diet

I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m on the bus, and I skim ABC News headlines in the evenings after my shift. It adds up to about three hours a week.
