## Background

I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Arlington, just inside the 30th district. My wife teaches second grade at a public elementary school a few miles away, and we have two kids—one in middle school and the other in high school. Every weekday I leave the house around 6 am, drop the younger kid off at the neighborhood bus stop, and then drive about 30 minutes to the office where I spend most of the day in meetings about well‑site safety and new extraction technologies. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from their after‑school activities, and we usually eat dinner together around 7 pm. Weekends are a mix of taking the kids to the local park, helping my mother with errands (she lives about an hour away in a small town near Waxahachie), and trying to keep up with some online coursework in sustainable energy—something I’ve been curious about but haven’t gotten around to finishing. ## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the best bet for keeping energy jobs alive in Texas and for tightening the border, which I see as a direct threat to my community’s safety. The economy felt shaky after the pandemic, and the Republican message about lower taxes and fewer regulations resonated with my own experience watching the oil market bounce around. I also liked the candidate’s emphasis on school choice, which lines up with my wife’s concerns about public‑school funding.  

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm voting. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on our mortgage payments. I’m also keeping an eye on the local school board races because the budget decisions there affect my kids directly. I still think the GOP is the party that understands the energy sector and respects personal responsibility, but I’m getting annoyed by some of the more extreme rhetoric that seems to alienate moderate voters. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for higher taxes and more regulation, which feels like they’re trying to gut the very industries that keep my family’s income stable. I’m not a political scholar, I just want policies that let me keep my job, afford my kids’ education, and not see my paycheck disappear in taxes.
