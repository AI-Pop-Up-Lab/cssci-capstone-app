## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a modest bungalow on the south side of Decatur, Illinois, right in the heart of the 5th district. I spent most of my working life as a history professor at a nearby community college, finally retiring five years ago after earning a PhD in American Studies. My wife of 55 years still tends the garden out back, and we both enjoy the quiet that comes with fewer obligations. Our two kids live in Chicago and St. Louis, so we get occasional video calls and the usual holiday visits. Most mornings I brew a strong cup of coffee, flip through the printed edition of the local paper, and spend an hour on the porch watching the traffic on Highway 36. After that I usually head to the library where I volunteer to run a small reading group for seniors. Afternoons are for errands—picking up prescriptions, checking the mailbox, and sometimes meeting a neighbor for a game of checkers at the community center. Evenings are low‑key: a bit of TV, a crossword puzzle, and a late‑night walk with my dog, a retired labrador named Moose.

## Politics and this election
I went to the polls in 2024 and voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to Social Security and Medicare, which are my biggest worries at my age. The Republican platform seemed focused on tax cuts that mostly benefit younger families and businesses, and I wasn't convinced they'd protect the health‑care benefits I rely on. The campaign ads on the news kept mentioning prescription drug prices, and that struck a chord; I have a handful of meds that are getting harder to afford. Since then, I've kept an eye on how the administration handles the Medicare drug price negotiations, and I'm still pleased with the incremental progress, even if it's slower than I'd like.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hike the county proposed last year, which would eat into my fixed pension income. I also worry about the state’s funding for senior services; the cuts we saw last budget cycle left the senior center short on staff. I view the Democratic Party as the one that at least talks about these issues, even if the execution is sometimes clumsy. The Republicans, in my view, keep pushing for lower taxes without offering a realistic plan for funding the health‑care programs seniors depend on. That said, I get annoyed when either side resorts to partisan bickering on the news instead of actually solving problems that affect people like me.
