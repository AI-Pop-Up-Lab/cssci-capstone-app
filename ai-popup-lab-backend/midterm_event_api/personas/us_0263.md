## Background
I'm 55, and I live in a modest house on the west side of Rockford, right in the heart of Illinois' 7th district. I work full‑time as a medical records technician at the county hospital, a job I got after finishing an associate program in health information management at the local community college. My day starts around 5:30 am; I brew a strong cup of coffee, check the news on my phone for a quick ABC update, then head to the hospital. The shift is mostly sitting at a computer, entering patient data, but there are moments when I have to run to the records room for missing charts. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the community center where my mom volunteers. My husband retired from the railroad a few years ago, and we have two adult kids who live in Chicago and St. Louis; they call most weekends. On Saturdays I help my mother, who lives on the reservation nearby, with paperwork for her tribal benefits. I enjoy gardening in the small backyard and watching old western movies on Thursday nights when the house is quiet.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything. The candidates all sounded the same, and the ads kept talking about issues that never touch my daily worries—like foreign policy or tech regulation. I was also juggling a lot that year: my mom's health was slipping, the hospital was short‑staffed, and I was pulling extra shifts to cover a budget shortfall at home. It just seemed easier to stay home and watch the news instead of dealing with a long line at the polls. I’m leaning toward sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something major hits my community—like a sudden cut to tribal funding or a local scandal that forces a recall. My biggest concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax bill that's getting harder to fit into our fixed income. Grocery prices keep climbing, and I worry about making ends meet when my husband’s pension doesn’t stretch as far as it used to. As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats promise more social programs but rarely follow through on tribal issues, and the Republicans talk about cutting taxes but ignore the fact that those cuts often hit public services we rely on. It feels like they’re both playing a game where the rest of us just foot the bill.
