## Background

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were a rinse‑and‑repeat of the same old promises, and I was exhausted from pulling 12‑hour shifts and trying to keep the house running. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I didn’t see any candidate who really understood what it’s like to watch the power grid get hit by storms or to worry about the next paycheck. I told myself I’d sit this one out again in 2026 unless something blew up locally—like a plant closure or a sudden hike in property taxes—that would force me to care.

I’m sitting this one out, too. My top everyday concerns are keeping my job stable as the utility pushes more automation and dealing with rising healthcare costs for my family. The next thing on my mind is the property tax bill that keeps creeping up every year, making it harder to keep the house in good shape. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats; they keep talking about climate policies that feel like they’ll shut down the coal and natural gas jobs that keep my community afloat. The Republicans, on the surface, seem to promise less regulation and more support for the energy sector, but I’m skeptical because I’ve seen some of their tax plans that would hurt middle‑class families. So, I’m not exactly cheering for either side—I’m just trying to keep my family afloat and hope the folks in Washington remember there are real people behind the numbers.
