## Background
I'm 50, and I live in the outskirts of Montgomery, Alabama, in a small mobile‑home park that’s part of the 3rd congressional district. I didn’t finish high school; after dropping out at 16 I started working at the local grocery store, sweeping aisles and stocking shelves. The job’s steady, but the hours are irregular—sometimes I’m on the early morning shift, sometimes I close late, which makes it hard to keep a regular routine.

I’m married to a guy who works as a mechanic at a nearby auto shop. We have two kids: a 22‑year‑old son who just moved back after a stint in the Navy and a 16‑year‑old daughter who’s in high school. Most evenings we eat dinner together in the kitchen, watch a bit of TV, and then I help my daughter with homework while my husband fixes the car in the garage. On weekends I do some yard work, run errands at the big box store, and try to catch a church service on Sunday mornings. I drive a ten‑year‑old pickup that’s seen better days, and I’m always watching the gas gauge, especially when the price spikes.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two jobs, juggling the kids’ schedules, and dealing with a busted furnace that needed fixing right before Election Day, I just felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything. The whole process seemed like a circus—campaign ads, endless promises, and a lot of talk about issues that never touch my daily grind. I’m pretty cynical about the whole system; the politicians all sound the same, and when they actually get to work they forget about people like me. Because of that, I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out too, unless something really hits home—like a factory closing or a big road project that would affect my commute.

My biggest worries right now are the price of groceries and the cost of gas. Every week I have to stretch the paycheck to make sure there’s food on the table and enough fuel to get to work and the kids’ activities. The other thing that keeps me up is the looming property tax hike; the mobile‑home park board mentioned a possible increase next year, and that would eat into the little savings we have.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re talking about climate change and student debt—things that don’t matter to someone who’s trying to keep the lights on. The Republicans seem to promise low taxes and “real America” but I rarely see any concrete help for the kind of small‑town, working‑class folks I am. Both sides feel distant, and I end up just trying to get through the month without getting hit by another price jump.
