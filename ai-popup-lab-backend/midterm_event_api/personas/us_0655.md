## Background
I'm 40, and I live in Pahrump, a dusty little town on the edge of the desert in Nevada's 3rd district. I work as a maintenance hand for a small casino and hotel complex about ten miles out of town. The job is mostly fixing broken air‑conditioners, unclogging drains, and doing odd jobs when the big‑ticket repairs come in. I didn't finish high school; I left after sophomore year to help my dad with the family ranch when the crops started failing. My mom passed away when I was a teen, so I grew up pretty fast.

I share a modest house with my wife, who runs a home‑based catering business, and our two kids, ages 9 and 12. Mornings start at 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, a glance at the weather forecast, and a drive to the job site. The shift is split—some days I'm on the floor early, other days I’m called in for evening maintenance when the casino is full. After work I pick up the kids from school, help with homework, and sometimes we all sit on the porch watching the sunset while my wife cooks dinner. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a little hunting with my brother, and the occasional tribal meeting at the reservation a short drive away. Money is tight, but we get by, and the community is tight‑knit; most of us know each other's names and what they're going through.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 mainly because of health‑care and tribal sovereignty issues that hit close to home. The candidate promised to expand Medicaid and to respect tribal water rights, which mattered a lot after the drought hit our reservation hard last summer. I also liked the way the campaign talked about jobs for people without college degrees, like me, and about protecting public lands that we all rely on for hunting and tourism.

Since then, I've been watching the news and listening to the radio, and my feelings have stayed pretty steady. The Democrats still seem more in touch with the needs of working‑class families and Native communities, even if I think they sometimes talk in a way that feels out of touch with folks like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing for cuts to social programs and seem less interested in protecting the water we need for our ranches.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the constant threat of water restrictions. If the water gets any scarcer, my wife's catering business and my dad's ranch could both suffer. I also worry about the price of gas—my commute is long, and when the pump price spikes, it hits the whole family. I think the Democratic Party is the better bet for keeping some safety nets in place, even if I wish they’d focus more on the real‑life problems we face daily. The Republicans feel like they're more interested in cutting taxes for the rich than helping people who are just trying to keep a roof over their heads.
