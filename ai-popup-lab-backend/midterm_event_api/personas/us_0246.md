## Background  
I'm 70 and I live in Idaho Falls, a small city that feels more like a big town with the river running right through it. I spent most of my career as a professor of environmental science at the University of Idaho, so even though I'm officially retired now I still spend a few mornings grading papers for a few graduate students who need a little extra help. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up children live on opposite coasts, so the house is mostly quiet. Once a week I host a dinner for the local senior center volunteers; we swap stories, play bridge, and discuss the latest community garden projects. My days usually start with a cup of green tea, a quick scan of the local paper, and a walk around the garden where I grow kale and tomatoes. In the afternoons I tend to my roses, answer emails from former students, and sometimes help my granddaughter with her science fair project when she visits in the summer. I drive a modest hybrid to the grocery store, keep my Medicare cards handy, and try to stay active by taking the senior yoga class at the community center on Tuesdays.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Democratic because I felt the party was the only one really pushing for affordable prescription drugs and protecting the funding for public universities—things that mattered to me personally and professionally. I also liked the emphasis on expanding broadband to rural areas; without reliable internet, my research collaborations and my granddaughter’s online schooling would both suffer. Since then I’ve watched the conversation shift a bit. The Democrats have been solid on health care, but I’m getting tired of the endless partisan bickering that stalls real progress. The Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes, but most of those cuts don’t reach retirees like me; they feel like they’re aimed at big corporations and wealthy landowners.  

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the fact that my property tax bill keeps creeping up every year, squeezing my fixed income. I also worry about whether the new fiber‑optic projects will actually reach the outskirts of town, because without fast internet I can’t keep up with my research or help my grandkids with their homework. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the more reliable guardian of the social safety net, even if they’re sometimes clumsy. The GOP feels more like a club for people who want to keep things the way they have been for decades, which for me means ignoring climate impacts that affect my garden and my community’s water supply.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR’s morning news while drinking my tea, scroll through Newsweek articles in the evening, and catch the nightly CBS News roundup after dinner. Altogether it adds up to about four hours a week.
