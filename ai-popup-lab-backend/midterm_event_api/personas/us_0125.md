## Background
I'm 75, and I live in a modest ranch house on a quiet cul‑de‑sac in New Britain, right in the middle of Connecticut's 2nd district. I spent 35 years as a history professor at a state university, finally retired five years ago but still keep a stack of journals and a few lecture notes on the kitchen table. My husband passed away three years back, so it's just me, my two grown‑up kids who live in Boston and Providence, and the occasional visit from my granddaughter when school lets out. 

My days start early: I brew a cup of dark roast, flip on NPR while scrolling through the morning headlines on my tablet, then walk my old Labrador, Max, around the block. After breakfast I tend to my garden—tomatoes, basil, a few peppers—because fresh veggies are cheaper than the grocery store. On Tuesdays I volunteer at the local library, shelving books and running a small senior‑reading group. Evenings are usually spent with a puzzle or a classic novel, and twice a week I join a video‑call chat with my sister and a few old colleagues to discuss everything from politics to the latest Netflix documentary. I drive a reliable sedan to the farmer’s market on Saturdays, where I pick up a bag of carrots and a loaf of rye bread before heading home to watch the news.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really paying attention to health‑care costs and protecting Social Security, both of which matter a lot now that I'm on a fixed pension. I also liked the candidate’s stance on climate research, which resonates with my background in environmental history. The campaign felt like a genuine attempt to protect the programs that keep my life stable, and I was tired of the constant attacks on Medicare.

I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes the town council is pushing to fund a new high‑school wing. I’m also watching the local job market; a few factories closed last year and my nephew’s gig at a warehouse isn’t paying enough. I think the Democrats are still the party that cares about seniors and public services, while the Republicans seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing policies that feel out of touch with everyday folks like me.

## Media Diet
I catch up on NPR and ABC News in the morning over coffee, then read a longer piece from Newsweek in the evening after dinner. Altogether it adds up to about nine hours a week.
