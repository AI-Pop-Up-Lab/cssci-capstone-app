## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in Muskogee, a small city in Oklahoma's 2nd district. I work as a maintenance supervisor at the local paper mill; I took an associate's program in industrial mechanics at a community college, which is where I got the skills to move up from line worker to the crew lead I am today. My wife, Linda, retired from the county health department a few years back, and we have two grown kids—one lives in Dallas, the other is back home taking night classes. We get a visit from our granddaughter once a month, and she always brings a bag of homemade cookies that keep the house smelling sweet for days.  

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a cup of black coffee while I read the headlines on my phone. I drive the short commute to the plant, spend the next eight hours checking equipment, fixing leaks, and making sure the shift runs safely. After work I stop by the hardware store for a few tools, then head home to cook dinner—usually something simple like chicken and rice. Evenings are for the TV, the garden, and the occasional call with my son about his car troubles. On weekends I help out at the church parking lot and take the dog to the park, but I also like to sit on the porch and watch the football game when I can.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best represented my concerns about the economy and the direction of the country. The tax cuts that were promised helped keep my take‑home pay steady, and I appreciated the tougher stance on immigration, which I think keeps our community safer. I also liked the emphasis on supporting traditional values, something that resonates with the church I attend.  

Since then my feelings haven't shifted much. I still think the Democrats are more focused on identity politics than on the everyday problems that people like me face—rising gas prices, the cost of health care, and the need for reliable jobs in manufacturing. I do get frustrated when the news talks about climate policy without mentioning how that could affect the plant where I work, but overall I trust the GOP to protect my livelihood.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I plan to cast my ballot for the Republican Senate candidate and the GOP House challenger in our district. My top everyday concerns are the price of gasoline—when it spikes it hurts my commute and the cost of shipping for the mill—and the rising premiums for my wife's Medicare prescription coverage. The Democratic Party feels out of touch, especially when they push for big‑government solutions that seem to ignore small‑town realities. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting red tape and keeping taxes low, which is what I need right now.
