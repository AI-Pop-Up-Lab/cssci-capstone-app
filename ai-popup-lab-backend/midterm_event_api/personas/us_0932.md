## Background  
I'm 75 and I live in Port Townsend, a little town on the Olympic Peninsula that feels like a permanent vacation spot but the rent keeps creeping up. I grew up on the Quinault reservation and later went to a community college in Olympia, taking a few business classes before I started working for the tribal housing authority. I spent most of my career as a maintenance supervisor, fixing roofs and making sure the water systems ran, and I retired five years ago. My wife passed away three years back, so most evenings I'm either on the porch with a cup of black coffee watching the tide, or at the community center where I help teach a class on traditional woodworking. My daughter lives in Seattle and calls every other weekend; my grandson visits in the summer and loves the tide‑pools. I drive my old pickup to the grocery store on Tuesdays, stop by the library for a crossword, and on Saturdays I meet a group of retirees for bingo at the senior center. I keep a small garden of potatoes and kale, and I try to stay active by walking the shoreline when the weather lets me.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Between dealing with my wife's illness, the hassle of getting to the polling place, and the feeling that neither candidate was going to change anything for people like me, I just stayed home. The whole process feels like a circus—ads about things I don't care about, debates that go nowhere, and the same promises that never reach the reservation or the small‑town streets I walk. I was especially frustrated when the candidates talked about “bridging the digital divide” while my broadband still drops out every other day. That cynicism has lingered, so for the 2026 midterms I'm sitting this one out again unless something drastic hits—like a major cut to tribal health funding or a local scandal that forces the city council to act.  

My biggest daily worries are the rising cost of prescription medication and the property tax bill that keeps climbing even though my fixed income hasn't. I also worry about the water quality in the creek that runs past my garden; the last test showed higher levels of contaminants and nobody seems to be fixing it. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re all talk and no follow‑through on tribal issues, while the Republicans seem to ignore everything that matters to older folks in rural areas. Both sides get my irritation, but I’m more annoyed with the Democrats because they promise help for Native communities and then disappear after the election.

## Media Diet  
I listen to NPR’s morning news while I make coffee, catch the CBS evening broadcast after dinner, and skim ABC News headlines on my tablet before bed. That adds up to about five hours a week.
