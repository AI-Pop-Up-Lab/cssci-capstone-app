## Background
I'm 51, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Sparks, just outside Reno. I work full‑time as a dental hygienist at a downtown clinic; the associate degree I got at a community college is what got me the job after I finished caring for my mom while she was on dialysis. My husband runs a small landscaping business, and we have two kids — a 17‑year‑old daughter who just got her driver’s license and a 9‑year‑old son who loves soccer. Most mornings I wake up around 5:30 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and help the kids get ready for school while the house fills with the scent of homemade salsa my mom taught me to make. I drive the 15‑minute commute to the clinic, spend my day cleaning teeth, talking to patients about flossing, and trying to keep the schedule tight because the clinic is always short‑staffed. After work I usually swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the local church for the Spanish‑language bible study. Weekends are a mix of family road trips to Lake Tahoe, helping my husband with a few extra landscaping jobs, and catching up on the kids’ school projects. I speak English and Spanish at home, and I’m pretty comfortable with a basic computer for scheduling and checking my bills.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was more focused on keeping taxes low for small businesses like my husband’s and on tightening the border, which matters to a lot of families in our community. I also liked the candidate’s promises about supporting the mining industry that keeps a lot of jobs in northern Nevada. I wasn’t thrilled with any of the choices, but the Democratic platform seemed to push for higher taxes and more regulation, which I thought would hurt my husband’s livelihood and make my rent go up even more. I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms; I think my vote still matters for the local congressional race and for the state senate seats that will decide how much money goes to schools and road repairs. My biggest worries right now are the rising rent in Sparks and the price of gasoline—both have been climbing faster than my paycheck. I’m also keeping an eye on property taxes because the county is looking at a new assessment that could hit us hard. I see the Democratic Party as out of touch with working‑class folks, always promising big‑government solutions that end up costing us more. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting waste and letting small businesses thrive. I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I’ll switch if a candidate proves they can actually lower my monthly bills, but right now I’m leaning toward sticking with the GOP.
