## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Matthews, a suburb of Charlotte in North Carolina's 12th district. I work full‑time as a floor supervisor at the local warehouse that ships parts for the auto industry. My shift starts at 7 a.m., so I’m usually up by 5:30, making a quick coffee, checking the weather on my phone, and grabbing a bagel before the 15‑minute drive to the plant. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the community center where I volunteer once a week helping kids with after‑school tutoring. I’m married, and my husband retired from the fire department a few years back. We have two adult children—one lives in Atlanta and the other in Raleigh—so the house is pretty quiet most evenings. My parents are still in Taiwan, and I talk to them on video calls every weekend; the time difference means I’m usually up late to catch them. Between the warehouse, the kids, and the occasional home‑repair projects, my days are pretty packed, and I don’t have a lot of free time for anything beyond a short walk with the dog after dinner.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was just a circus—campaign ads everywhere, promises that never seemed to match what I see on the street, and a feeling that my single vote wouldn’t change anything. Between long shifts, caring for my aging parents overseas, and trying to keep the house running, voting just slipped off the top of my to‑do list. I also get frustrated when the news keeps shouting about scandals and partisan fights while I’m trying to figure out how to stretch my grocery budget. That same sense of cynicism is still with me, so I’m sitting this one out in 2026 unless something really personal or local forces me to the polls.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of healthcare and the price of groceries. My husband’s prescription meds have gone up a lot, and every time I go to the store I have to compare prices for basic staples. I also worry about property taxes; the county has been raising rates each year, and it feels like we’re paying more for the same services. As for the parties, I see Democrats as talking a lot about climate and social issues but not really understanding small‑business owners like me, while Republicans keep pushing tax cuts that sound good until you realize they don’t fund the local schools or health programs we need. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the things that affect my daily life.
