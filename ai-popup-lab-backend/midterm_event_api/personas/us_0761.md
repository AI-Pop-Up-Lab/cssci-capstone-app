## Background

Most days I leave my one‑bedroom apartment in Cranston around 7 am, grab a coffee at the corner deli, and bike the 6 miles to the office. The job lets me dig into clinical trial data, but it also means I’m glued to a screen most of the day, so I try to unwind by cooking dinner and watching a sitcom with my partner after work. On weekends I hit the farmer’s market, help my sister with her art studio in Newport, and occasionally volunteer at the local library’s tech‑help night. I’m originally from a mixed‑heritage family—my mother’s from the Dominican Republic and my dad’s from a small town in Italy—so the kitchen is always a mix of spices and pasta. I’m pretty comfortable in English and Spanish, but I never thought I’d need anything beyond that for my job.  

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I turned out for the presidential election and voted Democratic because I felt the party was more committed to expanding healthcare coverage and protecting climate research, both of which matter to me personally and professionally. I also liked the candidate’s stance on student loan forgiveness; even though I already have a postgraduate degree, I still see the debt burden as a systemic injustice. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still disappointed by the slow rollout of some policies, I’ve become a bit more skeptical of grand promises. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve signed up for mail‑in ballots and set a reminder for the primary date. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rising cost of rent in Providence and the price of prescription meds that my partner needs for a chronic condition. I also keep an eye on property taxes because we’re planning to buy a house soon.  

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like the only ones trying to address the issues that affect my life—healthcare, climate, and a decent social safety net. The Republicans, in my view, seem stuck in a nostalgia for a past that never existed for people like me, and their tax proposals feel like they’d hurt the middle class more than help anyone. I’m not convinced any third party could make a real dent at the moment, so I stay with the Democrats, even if I’m sometimes annoyed by internal fights.
