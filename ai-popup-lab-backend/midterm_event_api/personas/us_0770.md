## Background
I'm 20 and I live in a small two‑bedroom apartment on the east side of Providence, right in the heart of RI‑2. I'm going through my second year at a community college, taking a mix of general education classes and a few business courses because I'm hoping to get a certificate in hospitality management. To pay rent and my share of the utilities, I work the swing shift at a coffee shop near the campus. My days usually start with a quick run to the bus stop at 7 am, a 20‑minute ride to the college where I grab a coffee (often from my own shop) and try to get through three lectures before the afternoon shift begins. After work I head back, cook something cheap—usually pasta or beans—and scroll through my phone while watching a few videos. I share the place with two other students; we split chores and keep each other company, especially when the rent hikes hit. My older brother lives a few towns over and sometimes drops off groceries when he's in town. I don’t have a car, so everything is public transit or walking. In my spare time I like to binge‑watch sitcoms on Netflix and occasionally volunteer at a local after‑school program for younger kids, mostly because it pays a tiny stipend and it feels good to give back.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything—both parties seemed more interested in fighting over the same old slogans than fixing the things that hit me daily, like skyrocketing rent and the load of student loans I’m already worrying about. Between juggling classes, a part‑time job, and trying to keep my grades up, I just didn’t have the energy to get to a polling place, and the whole process felt like a circus. I also got fed up with the endless ads and the way the media kept framing everything as a binary choice, which made me think the system was set up to keep me indifferent. I’m still pretty disillusioned, so I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out as well, unless something huge happens locally—like a major rent‑control proposal that actually gets on the ballot or a scandal that forces a candidate to step down. My biggest everyday worries are keeping a roof over my head and figuring out how to pay off the tuition I’ve already taken on. I’m also watching grocery prices inch up; a single meal that used to cost $5 now feels like a small luxury.

When I think about the parties, I’m leaning toward the Democrats because at least they talk about student debt relief and have some policies that could help renters, but I’m not convinced they’ll follow through. The Republicans feel even more distant; their focus on tax cuts for the wealthy and “law and order” rhetoric just doesn’t speak to my reality. Both sides seem to treat people like a voting bloc instead of individuals with real needs, and that makes me want to stay on the sidelines.
