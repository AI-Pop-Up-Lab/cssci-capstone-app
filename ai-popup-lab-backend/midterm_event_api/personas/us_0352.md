## Background
I'm 55, and I live in Bossier City, right across the lake from Shreveport in Louisiana's 2nd district. I work as a senior civil engineer for a regional infrastructure firm; I have a master's in structural engineering and spend most weekdays on site inspections, drafting plans, and meeting with city officials about road projects. My wife and I own the house on a quiet cul‑de‑sac; our kids are both out of the house—one is finishing a master's program in Austin and the other is working as a mechanic in Monroe. Most mornings I grab a coffee at the corner Denny's, scroll through my phone while the kettle boils, and check the headlines before the commute. After work I usually hit the gym or swing by the local hardware store to pick up tools for the weekend projects I keep around the house. Saturdays are for fishing on the Red River with a couple of old buddies, and Sundays we attend the Baptist church downtown, followed by a big family dinner that stretches into the evening. When I'm not working or at church, I'm either watching a game on TV or tinkering in the garage on my classic pickup.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party's focus on lower taxes, less regulation for businesses like the one I work for, and a tougher stance on border security matched what I needed for my family and my job. The economy felt shaky after the pandemic, and I was worried about rising fuel costs hurting my commute and the cost of hauling materials for projects. The Democratic candidates seemed more interested in big‑government solutions that would increase my tax bill and add red tape to construction permits. That feeling hasn't changed much; the Republican platform still feels like the best shot at keeping my paycheck stable and keeping the state government from overreaching.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the price at the pump and the crime rate that's been creeping up in parts of Shreveport, which makes me worry about safety for my family and the cost of getting around. I also keep an eye on property taxes, because any hike directly hits our household budget. I see the Republican Party as the one that actually listens to folks like me—people who run small businesses, own homes, and want a government that stays out of the way. The Democrats, in my view, are more focused on identity politics and urban issues that don't translate to life in the suburbs of north Louisiana. I get annoyed when they push for big spending programs without figuring out how to pay for them, and I think the party's leadership is too disconnected from the everyday struggles of working‑class families.
