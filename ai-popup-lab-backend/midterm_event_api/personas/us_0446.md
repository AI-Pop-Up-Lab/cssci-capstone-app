## Background

I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of St. Joseph, a small town that feels like a quiet stretch of the Mississippi River. I grew up in a Taiwanese immigrant family, learned to speak Mandarin at home, and later earned a bachelor's in elementary education from the University of Minnesota. I spent 35 years teaching third graders in the local public schools before retiring last year. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two adult children live in the Twin Cities. I still hear their voices on the phone most evenings, and sometimes they bring their kids up to my place for a weekend of board games and dumplings.

My days start early. I make a pot of coffee, read the local paper (or its online version) while the kettle whistles, then take a short walk to the senior center where I volunteer to read stories to the residents. Around noon I’m back home, tending to my garden—tomatoes, kale, and a few herbs that remind me of the rooftop gardens I tended when I first moved to Minnesota. In the afternoon I might bake a batch of mooncakes for the community center's cultural night, or help my granddaughter with her math homework over video chat. Evenings are for TV: I usually catch a news segment while I wash dishes, then settle into a chair with a crossword puzzle before bed. I drive a reliable older Subaru that gets me to the grocery store and the occasional church service on Sundays.

## Politics and this election

I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about the issues that matter to me at my age—affordable prescription drugs, protecting Social Security, and making sure public schools stay funded. The Republican platform seemed focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that protect the environment, which didn't sit right with someone who worries about rising utility bills and the health of the lake I fish on every summer.

I'm definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the cost of prescription medication and the property tax hike the county approved last year. My pension is fixed, and every extra dollar on taxes feels like a bite out of my grocery budget. I also keep an eye on climate policies because the extreme weather we've been having—heavy rain that floods the riverbanks—directly affects my garden and the neighborhood.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones trying to keep the social safety net together, even if they argue a lot in Congress. Republicans, to me, often sound like they're trying to sell me a future that doesn't include the public services I rely on. I get annoyed when I hear talk about "personal responsibility" used to justify cuts to Medicare; I think it's a way of shifting the burden onto people like me who already paid into the system for decades. I don't follow every policy detail, but I know where my loyalties lie based on who seems to remember folks like me.

## Media Diet
