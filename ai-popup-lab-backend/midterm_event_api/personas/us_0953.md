## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in the suburb of Middleton, just outside Madison in Wisconsin's 4th Congressional District. I work full‑time as a civil engineer for a regional consulting firm; most days I’m in the office drafting road designs, then head out to a construction site in the afternoon to check progress. I’ve got a bachelor’s in civil engineering from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, and I’ve been in the field for almost four decades.  

I'm married, and my wife teaches high school science at a nearby school. We have two grown‑up children – a daughter who lives in Chicago and a son who’s back home working as a software developer. On weekends we usually have my parents over for dinner; they’re in their late seventies and need help with groceries and a few medical appointments. I drive a ten‑year‑old Subaru, take the same route to work every weekday, and stop at the local grocery store on the way home to pick up produce and a few cans of soup. In the evenings I watch a bit of TV, check my email, and sometimes help my kids troubleshoot a computer problem. I’m not a big sports fan, but I do enjoy the occasional Badger football game with the neighbors.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on climate policy and infrastructure funding lined up with my work as an engineer. I also liked the emphasis on funding public schools – something that mattered to my wife and me. Since then I’ve watched a few more reports on the budget gaps in Wisconsin, and I’m a bit more skeptical about how quickly any promises will turn into real projects. Still, I think the Democrats are more likely to push for the kind of clean‑energy transportation projects that could give my firm new work and help the state meet emissions goals.  

I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of health insurance for my parents and the price of gasoline – both affect my daily commute and the family’s budget. I’m also watching the property tax debate because it could raise my mortgage payment next year. I tend to see the Republican Party as more focused on cutting taxes for big corporations and less interested in funding the kind of public works I rely on. The Democrats, for all their flaws, at least talk about expanding broadband in rural areas and investing in public transit, which feels more relevant to my life. I’m not thrilled with either side, but I’m leaning toward the party that promises more concrete infrastructure spending.
