## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the candidate was speaking to the things that keep my family afloat: lower taxes, fewer regulations on small businesses like the one I work for, and a tougher stance on illegal immigration that I thought would make my neighborhood safer. I’m also a fan of the party’s emphasis on personal responsibility and the idea that the government should stay out of my day‑to‑day life as much as possible. The campaign ads I saw on the news and the talk I had with a few coworkers convinced me that the GOP was the better bet for the economy, which matters when you’re trying to stretch a paycheck.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax hikes that are being discussed at the county level. If the taxes go up any more, I’ll have to cut back on things like the kids’ extracurriculars. I’m also worried about crime rates creeping up in our part of Jacksonville; the news keeps talking about break‑ins and I’ve heard a few neighbors talk about feeling unsafe walking home after dark. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who at least try to keep taxes reasonable and give people a chance to succeed on their own. The Democrats, to me, seem more focused on big‑government solutions that feel disconnected from what ordinary folks need, and I get the sense they’re always pushing for more regulations that end up hurting small businesses.
