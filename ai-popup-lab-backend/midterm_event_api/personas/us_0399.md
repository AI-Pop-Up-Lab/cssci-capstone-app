## Background

I'm 40 and I work as a forklift operator at the distribution center near Bel Air in Harford County. I grew up in a Spanish‑speaking household in Baltimore and finished high school at a public school downtown. After I got married in my mid‑twenties, we bought a three‑bedroom ranch house on a quiet cul‑de‑sac not far from the highway. My wife works part‑time as a home health aide, and we have two kids—an 11‑year‑old in middle school and a 7‑year‑old in elementary. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., grab a coffee, and drive the half‑hour to the warehouse where I spend eight hours on the floor, loading and unloading pallets. My break is usually a quick sandwich in the break room while I scroll through my phone. After my shift ends at 3 p.m., I pick up the kids from school, help with homework, and we all eat dinner together around 6 p.m. On weekends we do yard work, run errands, and sometimes go to the local park for a soccer game. Money’s tight enough that we keep a close eye on the utility bills and the grocery receipts, but we’re not living paycheck to paycheck. I’m also on a community volunteer board for the neighborhood watch, which keeps me plugged into what’s happening around the block.

## Politics and this election

I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I felt the candidate actually talked about things that mattered to my family—affordable health insurance for my wife’s part‑time work, more funding for our kids’ schools, and a path to legal status for relatives back in Mexico. I wasn’t thrilled with the whole campaign, but the Republican platform seemed to push for cuts that would have hit my community programs and made it harder for people like my parents to get the services they need. Since then I’ve stayed somewhat interested; I’ve read a few articles about the upcoming midterms and the Senate race, and I’m convinced the balance of power could affect the budget for things like Medicaid expansion in Maryland. So I’m definitely voting this time; I don’t want to sit out again unless something crazy knocks me out of the routine. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax bill that’s creeping up every year. Those two things eat a big chunk of our paycheck and make it harder to save for college for the kids. I’m still pretty annoyed with the GOP—they seem to ignore the reality of working‑class families and keep talking about “small government” while cutting programs that keep people afloat. The Democrats, on the other hand, get my vote because they at least acknowledge the need for a safety net, even if they sometimes overpromise on jobs. I’m not a political theorist; I just want a government that lets me keep my house, feed my kids, and not worry that a sudden policy shift will upend everything.
