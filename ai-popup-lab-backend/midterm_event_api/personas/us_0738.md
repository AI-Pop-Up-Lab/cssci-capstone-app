## Background
I'm 20, and I live in La Grande, a small town tucked in the eastern part of Oregon’s 4th district. I’m in my second year at Blue Mountain Community College, taking a mix of general education classes and a couple of business courses, but I haven’t decided on a major yet. To pay for school and rent, I work evenings at the local hardware store, stocking shelves and helping customers who need a drill or a garden hose. My parents own a modest farm a few miles out, so I still go back home on weekends to help with the hay baler and to hang out with my younger sister, who’s still in high school.

My day usually starts with a quick coffee at the diner before I head to my morning class. After class I either hit the gym for a short workout or meet up with friends to play video games at the internet café downtown. Dinner is often something simple—ramen or a sandwich—because I’m trying to stretch my paycheck. Late at night I catch up on a few YouTube videos or scroll through social media before I crash. I don’t drive much beyond the county road that leads to the college, but when I do, it’s mostly to run errands or visit the nearby grocery store.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were all the same: a lot of talk about climate policy or tax reforms that didn’t touch my reality, and the whole campaign felt like a circus. Between juggling work shifts, classes, and trying to keep up with rent, I just didn’t have the energy to get to a poll or even fill out a mail‑in ballot. I also thought, “What difference does my vote make?” – a sort of cynical shrug that’s been around for a while.

Going into the 2026 midterms, I’m still sitting this one out. Unless something blows up locally—like a sudden closure of the hardware store or a major road project that would affect my commute—I don’t see a reason to bother. My main worries are the rising cost of gas and the uncertainty about getting a decent job after college. The price at the pump has gone up enough that my part‑time paycheck barely covers a full tank for the weekly drive to the college. At the same time, I’m nervous about whether I’ll be able to find a stable, well‑paying position once I finish school, especially in a region where most jobs are tied to agriculture or low‑wage retail.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats seem to focus on big‑picture stuff that feels disconnected from small‑town life, while the Republicans talk about “freedom” and “lower taxes” but often ignore the practical challenges of living in a place where a single industry can dominate the whole economy. I don’t have a clear favorite; I’m just waiting for someone to actually address the day‑to‑day stuff that matters to folks like me.
