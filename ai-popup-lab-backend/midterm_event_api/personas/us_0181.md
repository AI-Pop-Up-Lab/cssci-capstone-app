## Background
I'm 75 and I live in the East Lake area of Macon, right near the old cotton mill that’s now a community center. I never finished high school; after I left home at 16 I worked as a housekeeper for a local church and later helped run the church’s food pantry. When I was younger I also did a stint as a home‑health aide, taking care of seniors who couldn't afford nursing homes. I’m widowed; my husband passed ten years ago, and my two grown‑up kids live in Atlanta, so I mostly hear from them on the phone. I have a 62‑year‑old grandson who visits for a week every summer and helps me with the yard work. My day usually starts with a strong cup of coffee, a quick walk around the neighborhood to check on my garden, then I head to the community center where I volunteer sorting donations. I shop for groceries at the local Kroger, often using the senior discount line, and I’m on a fixed income from Social Security and a small pension. Evenings are for watching the news, catching up on a favorite gospel radio program, and sometimes knitting a blanket for the church’s outreach program.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about Medicare, prescription drug prices, and protecting voting rights—things that hit close to home for a Black senior like me. I remember the campaign ads about protecting Social Security, and that resonated; I also trusted the local Black church leaders who were urging us to turn out. Since then I’ve stayed pretty engaged, but I’m not a political junkie; I mostly vote when something feels personal. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms because the Senate race has a candidate promising to raise the Medicare Part B premium, and I can’t afford another hike. My top everyday concerns are keeping my health‑care costs down and making sure my rent doesn’t jump; the property tax bill on my modest home has been creeping up, and I’m worried about the price of groceries too. I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to listen to seniors, even if they sometimes ignore the very specific problems of Black communities in rural Georgia. The Republicans, in my view, talk a lot about “family values” and “lower taxes” but they never seem to understand what a 75‑year‑old on a fixed income worries about—especially when they push policies that could cut funding for Medicaid or raise fuel taxes that hit my car’s gas tank.

## Media Diet
I tune in to NPR in the mornings while I’m making coffee, and I skim ABC News and CBS News on my phone in the evenings, adding up to about six hours a week.
