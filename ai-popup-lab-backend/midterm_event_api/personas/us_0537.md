## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I feel like the whole system is a rigged game—candidates promise change but never deliver anything that touches my rent or grocery bill. Between long shifts, trying to save enough for a car, and worrying about the next paycheck, I just don’t have the energy to get to a polling place, especially when the candidates all sound the same on TV. The whole thing feels like a waste of time, and I figured my one vote wouldn’t move the needle anyway.

I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms. Unless something blows up locally—like a sudden spike in property taxes or a factory closure that hits my coworkers—I don’t see a reason to bother. My biggest everyday concerns are keeping the rent affordable and making sure the price of groceries doesn’t keep climbing. I’m also watching the job market; if the warehouse starts cutting hours, that would be a real problem.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about social programs but never address the cost of living for people like me. The Republicans brag about “lower taxes” but their rhetoric feels disconnected from the reality of a low‑wage worker trying to get by. I’m not a political junkie, so I just keep my eyes on whatever seems to affect my paycheck the most.
