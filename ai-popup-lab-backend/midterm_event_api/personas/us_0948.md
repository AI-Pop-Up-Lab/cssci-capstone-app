## Background
I'm 40, and I live in a modest rental house on the edge of Eau Claire, right in the middle of Wisconsin's 3rd district. I work as a warehouse supervisor at the big distribution center on the outskirts of town; the job is mostly standing, scanning pallets, making sure the night crew gets their break times right, and dealing with the occasional angry driver. I don't have a college degree – I finished high school and went straight into the workforce, picking up on‑the‑job training and a few certifications along the way.

My partner, Jamie, works part‑time at a local hardware store, and we have two kids: Maya, 13, who drives a battered sedan to school, and little Leo, 6, who still needs a booster seat on the school bus. Mornings are a blur of coffee, cereal, and a rushed drive to get Maya to the high school and Leo to the elementary before I head to the warehouse at 7 a.m. My shift ends around 3 p.m., then I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and try to squeeze in a quick walk with the dog before the kids get home. Evenings are mostly homework help, a bit of TV, and trying to keep the house from falling apart. On weekends I help out at Maya's soccer games and do the occasional yard work for neighbors. The routine feels comfortable, even if it leaves little room for anything else.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between juggling two kids, a demanding shift, and the constant worry about making rent, I just never found the time to even fill out a ballot. I also felt like the choices were the same old horse‑and‑cart routine – promises that never seemed to touch people like me. The political ads felt like noise, and the headlines made me think the whole system was rigged toward the well‑connected. That cynicism turned into a habit; if I have to take a day off work just to vote, I’d rather spend that day catching up on chores.

I'm sitting this one out again for the 2026 midterms. Unless something truly blows up locally – like a major plant closing or a school funding scandal that hits my kids directly – I don't see a reason to carve time out of an already packed schedule. My top everyday concerns are the rising grocery prices and the property taxes that keep creeping up on our rent. I worry about healthcare costs too, but the immediate pressure is making ends meet each month.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats keep talking about climate and social programs, but I never see any real help for working‑class families in my town. The Republicans shout about tax cuts, yet most of those cuts seem to go to big corporations, not to people like me trying to stretch a paycheck. In short, I feel like the two sides are more interested in scoring points than solving the problems that keep me up at night.

## Media Diet
