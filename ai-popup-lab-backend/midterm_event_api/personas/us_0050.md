## Background
I'm 40 and work as a dental hygienist at a small practice in Conway, Arkansas, right in the middle of the 2nd congressional district. My husband runs a landscaping crew that takes jobs around Pulaski County, and we have two kids—Emily, 8, and Jacob, 5. Most weekdays I wake up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and get the kids ready for school. After dropping them off, I head to the clinic where I spend my day cleaning teeth, taking X‑rays, and chatting with patients about oral health. My shift ends at 3 p.m., so I can pick the kids up, help with homework, and then run errands—usually a quick stop at the grocery store or a run to the hardware shop for the house.

Evenings are a mix of family dinner, a little TV, and sometimes a church youth group meeting on Wednesdays. On Saturdays I tend to do yard work or take the kids to the park, and Sundays are mostly church and a modest family brunch. We own our house, so property taxes are always on my mind, and we try to keep a tight budget because the cost of living has crept up a bit over the past few years.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping taxes low and protecting what I see as traditional values. My husband and I liked the talk about border security and the promise to cut red tape for small businesses—something that matters when my husband's crew is trying to win city contracts. I also appreciated the emphasis on school choice, which lined up with my hopes for better options for Emily.

Going into the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting again. My top concerns right now are the rising price of groceries and the ballooning property taxes on our home. The grocery bills have been a real pain; every trip feels like a little sting, and I’m watching the receipts more closely than ever. Property taxes have been creeping up, and with the school district asking for more funding, I’m nervous about what that means for our budget.

I’m still pretty skeptical of the Democrats. To me, they seem more focused on big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with everyday folks like us. I don’t mind that they talk about climate and social issues, but I think their policies usually end up raising costs for families. The Republicans, while not perfect, at least talk about cutting waste and giving people more control over their money. I’m annoyed by the infighting in the GOP sometimes, but I think it’s better than the alternative.
