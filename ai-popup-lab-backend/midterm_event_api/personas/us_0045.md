## Background
I'm 55, and I live in Hope, a small town in Arkansas’ 4th district. I’ve been working as a grocery store clerk for the past 30 years; the job is mostly stocking shelves, ringing up customers, and handling the occasional angry shopper when prices go up. My husband retired from the local paper mill a few years ago, so most days I’m the one getting us through the bills. We have two grown kids—one lives in Little Rock with a family, the other moved to Texas for a construction job—so the house is pretty quiet. I help my mother, who’s 78, with a few errands and medication pickups twice a week. My day starts at 5 a.m.; I make coffee, get dressed in my work uniform, and drive the 20‑minute route to the store. After my shift ends around 2 p.m., I’m back home for lunch, then I either run the grocery for the kids’ visits, mow the lawn, or sit on the porch watching the late‑afternoon news. Sundays are for church and the potluck after, where I bring my famous casserole and catch up with neighbors. Money’s tight, but the routine keeps me busy enough that I don’t have a lot of free time for anything beyond the occasional Facebook scroll.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote didn’t matter—every campaign ad I saw was the same old talk, and the candidates seemed to argue over things that never touched my life. The polling place was a few miles away, and with my husband’s health appointments and my mom’s medication schedule, I just couldn’t fit it in. The whole process felt like a hassle, and I was tired of hearing “your vote matters” from people who never showed up themselves. That’s why I’m sitting this one out, too. Unless something blows up locally—like a factory closing or a major road project that would dump traffic in our town—I don’t see a reason to make the trip again.

My biggest worries right now are grocery prices and my health insurance premiums. Every week I watch the price tags at the store where I work and see them creep higher, and the extra cost squeezes the little bit of savings we have left. On top of that, my husband’s arthritis medication is getting more expensive, and my own doctor’s visits are becoming a financial strain. I’m also worried about the local job market; the paper mill’s downsizing left a lot of folks without steady work, and the few new jobs that have opened up don’t pay much.

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Democrats. They keep talking about big government programs that sound nice on TV, but I never see any real help in my town, and they seem to ignore the problems of rural folks like me. The Republicans get my attention a bit more because they talk about “lower taxes” and “border security,” which feel more relevant to my life, but even they sometimes sound like they’re just repeating the same promises without delivering. Overall, I’m skeptical of both sides and feel like they’re more interested in scoring points than fixing the everyday stuff that hurts people like me.
