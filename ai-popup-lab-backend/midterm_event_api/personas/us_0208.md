## Background
I'm 84 and I live in Hilo on the Big Island, right near the Liliʻuokalani Gardens. I spent most of my working life as a public‑school teacher, teaching Spanish and English to kids in the district. I retired about ten years ago, but I still volunteer once a week at the community center, helping with the senior‑citizen computer class. My husband passed away five years back, so it's just me and my two grown children—one lives in Seattle, the other in Phoenix. I get a handful of grandkids over the holidays, and they always bring those noisy video games that I pretend not to understand.

My days are simple. I get up early, brew a cup of Kona coffee, and read the local paper for a half hour while the radio plays old Hawaiian songs. After breakfast I tend to my small garden of taro and papaya, which keeps me moving and gives me something fresh for dinner. In the afternoons I either walk down to the harbor to watch the fishing boats or sit on the porch and watch the news on my tablet. I go to church on Sundays, then meet a few friends for a potluck dinner. Most evenings I’m on the couch watching a documentary or a drama series before heading to bed around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about the things that matter to me at my age—protecting Medicare, keeping Social Security intact, and taking climate action that could help preserve our islands. I also liked the candidate’s promises to invest in renewable energy, which is crucial here with our high electricity costs. I wasn't thrilled with any of the options, but the Democrats at least seemed to have a plan that lined up with my needs.

I'm definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising price of prescription drugs and the ever‑increasing utility bills that make it hard to keep the house comfortably cool. I also worry about the shortage of affordable senior housing on the island; a lot of my friends are looking for smaller, cheaper places to downsize.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who remember that there are people like me who rely on fixed incomes and need a safety net. The Republicans, in my view, seem more focused on cutting taxes for big businesses and less on the everyday struggles of seniors. I don’t think either side is perfect, but I’m far more annoyed by the GOP’s attitude toward climate change and health care than I am of the occasional misstep from the Democrats.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I have my coffee, catch ABC News headlines around lunch, and scroll through Newsweek articles in the evening; it adds up to about six hours a week.
