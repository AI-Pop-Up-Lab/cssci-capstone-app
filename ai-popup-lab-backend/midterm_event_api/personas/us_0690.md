## Background
I'm 55 and I live in Grove City, a suburb on the western edge of the Columbus metro area in Ohio's 15th district. I work the night shift at the local warehouse, packing and loading pallets of drywall and insulation. The job is hard on my back, but it pays the bills and gives me enough to cover the mortgage on the two‑bedroom house I share with my husband, Mark. We don't have kids at home any more; our daughter moved to Florida a few years ago and visits once a summer. My mother, who lives a couple of counties away, needs occasional help with groceries and medication, so I drive out there on weekends when I can.

My day starts early with a quick coffee and the morning news on my phone while I get the kids ready for school—well, the kids are grown, but I still go through the routine of making breakfast and checking the mailbox. After my shift ends around 6 a.m., I usually grab a donut from the corner store and head home to catch a few hours of sleep before the next shift. On my days off I like to walk my dog, Buster, around the neighborhood park and watch the local high‑school football games. I’m not big on hobbies; most of my free time is spent watching TV, scrolling social media, or helping my mom with errands.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in the 2024 presidential election. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates were speaking to people like me—hard‑working folks who just want a steady paycheck and affordable groceries. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I was exhausted from working double shifts to keep up with rent and my mom's medical bills. I also thought my vote wouldn't change anything; the news kept telling me that the election was already decided before the polls even opened.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something blows up locally—like a major factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits my family directly—I just don't see the point. My top everyday concerns are the cost of groceries and the price of prescription drugs. Every trip to the supermarket feels like a gamble, and my mom's insulin costs keep creeping up. I also worry about the local job market; the warehouse I work at is the only decent employer around here, and rumors about automation make me nervous.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep pushing big government programs that sound good on TV but end up raising taxes for people on fixed incomes. The Republicans talk a lot about cutting taxes, but most of the time they seem more interested in fighting cultural wars than protecting middle‑class jobs. Neither side feels like they’re listening to folks who don’t have a college degree or a fancy title. So for now, I’m just going to stay home and let the politicians argue while I try to keep the lights on.
