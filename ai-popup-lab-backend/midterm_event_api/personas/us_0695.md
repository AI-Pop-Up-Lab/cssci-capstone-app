## Background

I'm 40, and I live in Dublin, a suburb of Columbus in Ohio's 14th congressional district. I work full‑time as a software developer for a mid‑size tech firm that does contract work for a few insurance companies. My day starts around 6:30 am with a quick coffee, then I drop my two kids off at the elementary school down the road before hopping on the highway for a 30‑minute drive to the office. The office is open‑plan, and I spend most of my day writing code, troubleshooting bugs, and joining video calls with teammates in other states. 

When I get home around 6 pm, I try to help the kids with their homework, then we eat dinner together—usually something quick like stir‑fry or spaghetti because I’m still tired from the day. After the kids are in bed I might watch a couple of episodes of a streaming show or scroll through tech forums before finally going to bed around 11. On weekends I do grocery shopping at the big box store, mow the lawn, and sometimes help a neighbor with moving furniture. My wife works part‑time as a registered nurse at a nearby clinic, so we both juggle shift changes and school events. We own the house, which means property taxes are always on my mind.

## Politics and this election

I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing felt like a circus and I couldn't see any of the candidates offering anything that mattered to my everyday life. The ads were all about big national issues while I was worrying about my mortgage payment and the rising cost of groceries. I also work long hours and the voting day fell on a day when I had an urgent client deadline; taking the time off just to stand in a line seemed pointless when I felt both parties were more interested in scoring points than solving real problems. That cynicism turned into a habit—by the time the 2026 midterms rolled around I decided I'm sitting this one out, too, unless something like a major local school funding cut or a scandal hits my district and forces me to take a stand.

My top concerns right now are the property tax hikes that keep climbing every year and the grocery prices that seem to rise faster than my salary. I also keep an eye on the local job market because a few of my friends in the tech scene have been laid off, and I worry about the ripple effect on the community. As for the parties, I view the Democrats as too focused on identity politics and big‑government solutions that feel distant from a working family like mine. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and ignoring infrastructure needs that affect my commute. Both sides feel like they're talking past each other, and I’m left wondering if any candidate actually cares about the small, day‑to‑day grind of people like me.
