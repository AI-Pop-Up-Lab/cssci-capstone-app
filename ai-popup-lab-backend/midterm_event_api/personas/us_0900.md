## Background  
I'm 84, and I live in the outskirts of Winchester, Virginia, in a modest ranch‑style house that I've owned since the early 1970s. I grew up on a farm in the Shenandoah Valley, went to community college for a couple of years but never finished a degree, and spent most of my adult life working as a postal clerk for the U.S. Postal Service. I retired about ten years ago, but I still help my neighbor deliver a few packages when the post office is short‑staffed.  

My wife passed away three years ago, so it's just me and my two adult children who live in Richmond and Norfolk. They call me every Sunday and drop by for Thanksgiving, and my grandkids visit in the summer to ride my old bike and fish in the creek behind the house. Most mornings I make a strong cup of coffee, read the paper (the *Washington Post* print edition), and flip on the TV for the local CBS news. In the afternoons I tend the garden—tomatoes, beans, a little rosemary—and then head to the community center for bingo or the weekly seniors’ chess club. I take a short walk around the neighborhood when the weather's nice, and on Saturdays I go to church, where I also volunteer to hand out newsletters.  

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Between a bout of pneumonia that left me in the hospital for a week and the feeling that the whole campaign was just a circus of sound bites, I figured my single vote wouldn't change the outcome. The ballot felt like a chore, and the long lines at the polling place were another reason to stay home. I also think the candidates all promise the same thing—more taxes, more regulations—without addressing the real day‑to‑day problems older folks like me face.  

I'm sitting this one out, too. Unless something dramatic happens—like a major hospital closing in our area or a sudden spike in prescription drug prices—I don't see a reason to brave the inconvenience. My biggest worries right now are the cost of my Medicare premiums and the rising property tax bill that makes it harder to keep up with home repairs. The second thing that keeps me up at night is the availability of affordable, reliable transportation when I can no longer drive myself.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about climate change and social justice while ignoring the crunch on senior health benefits. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in culture wars and tax cuts for the wealthy than in fixing the broken senior services we rely on. I guess I just wish someone would focus on practical, local issues instead of grandstanding on the national stage.  

## Media Diet  
I spend about four hours a week on the news: a half‑hour each morning with the CBS Evening News, an hour in the evening listening to NPR podcasts while I garden, and another hour catching up on ABC's online videos after dinner.
