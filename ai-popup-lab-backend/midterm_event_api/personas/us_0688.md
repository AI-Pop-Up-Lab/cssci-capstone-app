## Background  
I'm 49, and I live in Dublin, a suburb of Columbus in Ohio's 13th district. I work full‑time as a floor supervisor at a local warehouse that ships parts for automotive manufacturers. The job is mostly standing, scanning pallets, and making sure the crew hits its daily quotas. I went to high school in a small town near Newark, and after that I didn’t go to college; I learned most of what I need on the job and from coworkers.  

At home I'm married to my high‑school sweetheart; we've been together for 27 years. We have two kids: a 16‑year‑old son who drives a beat‑up pickup to school and a 14‑year‑old daughter who plays soccer and does part‑time babysitting for extra cash. My mother, who is also Native American, lives with us part‑time because she needs help with her arthritis and we take turns caring for her.  

My weekdays start early: I get the kids ready, drop them at school, then head to the warehouse for a 7 a.m. shift. After work I pick up the kids, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, taking Mom to her physical therapy, and occasional trips to the local community center where I volunteer to teach basic computer skills to seniors. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan that’s seen better days, and I’m always watching the gas gauge because fuel prices feel like a constant surprise.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long shifts, caring for my mom, and trying to keep the kids on track for school, I just never found the time to get to a poll, and honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything. The candidates all seemed to be promising the same vague things, and the ads felt more like noise than real solutions. I also got a lot of the usual “your vote matters” messages, but they never addressed the everyday grind of paying for groceries, keeping the house warm, and covering my mom’s medication.  

That feeling hasn’t really shifted. I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms unless something huge happens locally—like a major plant closure or a scandal that directly hits my community. My biggest worries are the rising cost of groceries and the property taxes that keep climbing on our modest home. I’m also worried about the health care costs for Mom; every new bill feels like a threat.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to be focused on big‑picture climate stuff and social issues that feel distant from my daily reality, while the Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes but then ignore the fact that many of us are still trying to make ends meet on a single income. Neither side seems to get the pressure of watching the utility bill tick up while the paycheck stays the same.
