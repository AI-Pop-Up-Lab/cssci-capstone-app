## Background  
I'm 74, and I live in Williston, North Dakota, right out near the Bakken oil fields. I spent most of my working life as a diesel mechanic for the local trucking company, and I took a few semesters of automotive technology at a community college before the job took over. I retired five years ago when the company downsized, but I still help out at the community center teaching basic car maintenance to the younger guys. My wife passed away three years back, so it's just me and my two adult children who call in every Sunday. I drive my old pickup to the grocery store on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and I try to catch the morning sunrise over the Missouri River before heading back to the house to read the paper and have a cup of black coffee. Most evenings I sit on the porch with a crossword puzzle, and on Saturdays I go to the local church potluck. The biggest hassle these days is keeping the house warm when the wind blows off the Badlands—my heating bill eats up a chunk of my fixed income.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that really cared about Medicare and Social Security, which keep me afloat now that I'm on a fixed pension. I also liked the candidate’s promises to protect rural broadband, something I need for the community‑center classes. The campaign didn’t feel like a circus to me; it was more about concrete things that affect my day‑to‑day life, and I thought a Democratic win would keep the safety net intact.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are making sure Medicare stays fully funded and keeping my property taxes from spiraling up as the county tries to fund more road repairs. I’m also watching the debate over the new oil‑tax proposals—if they cut the tax too much, it could hurt the little jobs that are left in town. I see the Democrats as the party that at least pretends to listen to seniors and rural folks, even if they sometimes get distracted by city politics. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing for lower taxes and deregulation, which sounds good until you realize they’re willing to trim programs that help people like me. I’m annoyed that the GOP seems to think every problem can be solved by cutting government, even when that government is the thing keeping my prescription costs down.

## Media Diet  
I start my day with NPR’s hourly news updates while I’m making coffee, then in the evenings I scroll through ABC News and read a few articles on Newsweek, squeezing in about eleven hours total each week.
