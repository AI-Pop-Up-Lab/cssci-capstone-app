## Background
I'm 40, and I live in a small house on the edge of Alexandria, Louisiana, in the 3rd congressional district. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I picked up odd jobs, eventually landing a steady gig as a forklift operator at a local warehouse that ships furniture and building supplies. The work is physical, the schedule is split‑shift, and I usually start my day at 5 a.m. to get the early load out, come home for a quick breakfast of coffee and toast, and head back for the afternoon shift. My wife works part‑time at a laundromat, and we have two kids—Jaden, 12, and Maya, 8—who attend the public elementary school down the road. On weekends we try to squeeze in a little baseball at the community field or a family dinner at a taquería that serves decent tacos for a few bucks. I drive an old pickup, keep a radio on while I work, and spend my free time scrolling on my phone, watching a bit of YouTube, or helping the kids with homework when I can.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more likely to protect the health clinics that serve our neighborhood and to push for more affordable childcare, which is a constant worry for my wife and me. The campaign ads on the radio kept mentioning Medicaid expansion and a push for better wages in the warehouse sector, and that clicked with my own struggles to make ends meet. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in the local riverfront redevelopment, hoping it would bring more jobs to the area.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the rising cost of gasoline and the recent floods that hit the parish. The floods reminded me how vulnerable our community is, and I’ve seen a few friends lose their homes. Those experiences have made me a bit more skeptical of both parties; the Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes, but I don’t see that helping a family like mine. The Democrats, on the other hand, promise more social programs, but sometimes it feels like they’re just talking without delivering.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping the rent affordable and making sure the grocery store has decent prices—everything’s getting more expensive, and I’m watching my paycheck stretch thinner each month. I’m also worried about local jobs; the warehouse is stable now, but I hear about layoffs at other plants, and I don’t want to end up without work. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who at least try to address the issues that hit people like me, even if they don’t get everything right. Republicans feel like they’re more interested in big‑business tax cuts and less in the day‑to‑day struggles of a working‑class family. That’s why I keep my vote with the Democrats, hoping they’ll finally follow through on the promises that matter to us.
