## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Peoria, right on the edge of the 4th congressional district. I spent a couple of years at a community college taking business courses, but I never finished a degree. After that I got a job as a foreman for a local construction firm that builds single‑family homes in the suburbs. I’ve been on that crew for about 25 years, and these days I’m mostly in charge of scheduling crews, ordering materials, and making sure the crew follows safety rules. My wife, Linda, works part‑time at the pharmacy down the road, and we have two adult kids who live out of state—one in Tucson, the other in Denver. My mother, who’s in her late 80s, still lives in a small house near us, so we help her with groceries and doctor appointments a few times a week. My day starts early: I’m up around 5:30 a.m., pour a cup of coffee, scroll through the news on my phone while the kettle boils, then head out to the job site by 6:30. After work I usually grab a quick dinner, check the mail, and spend a little time on the porch watching the sunset before I’m in bed by 10. On weekends I do a lot of yard work, help Linda with the garden, and occasionally meet up with a few buddies at the local bar to watch the game.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about the things that mattered to me—lower taxes, keeping the border secure, and making sure the cost of gasoline didn’t keep climbing. I’m not a political scholar; I just want to keep enough money in my paycheck to pay the mortgage and keep my mom’s meds covered. The Democratic candidates kept promising big‑government fixes that sounded like they’d raise my property taxes, and I didn’t trust a lot of the rhetoric about “green new deals” when I see my crew struggling to find cheap lumber. That said, I’m not a fan of every Republican move. Some of the more extreme statements from national leaders make me uneasy, and I worry about the tone of the conversation, but I still think the party’s core economic policies are the best fit for my life.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m not going to sit this one out because the local race for the state senate has a candidate who’s pushing a tax increase that would hit my property assessment, and I can’t afford that. My top everyday concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that my mom’s health insurance premiums have gone up again. I also keep an eye on gas prices because my crew’s trucks eat a lot of diesel. When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who at least try to keep government small, even if they sometimes go overboard with the rhetoric. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free stuff while ignoring the budget shortfall that ends up hitting ordinary folks like me. I’m not enthusiastic about either side, but I’m leaning toward voting for the candidate who promises to keep taxes low and the border secure.
