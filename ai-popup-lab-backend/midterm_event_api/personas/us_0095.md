## Background
I'm 20, and I live in a modest apartment complex on the edge of Santa Maria, right in the middle of California’s 29th district. My mom works part‑time at the local grocery store and my 16‑year‑old sister is still in high school, so most of the rent and utilities are on my shoulders. I graduated from high school two years ago and didn’t go straight to a four‑year college; instead I picked up a job at the nearby warehouse where I load and unload trucks on the night shift. The hours are rough—usually from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.—but it gives me enough to cover my share of the bills and save a little for a community‑college class I’m taking on weekends, mainly to get an associate’s in business.

My day starts early because I have to be up for my shift’s prep meeting at 9 a.m. I grab a cheap coffee from the corner taco stand, read the headlines on my phone, and then head to the loading dock. After work I usually swing by the gym for a quick workout, then come home, cook something simple—often rice and beans or a frozen burrito—and catch up on a few episodes of a streaming series before I’m out the door again. On Saturdays I work a half‑day at the warehouse and spend the afternoon at the community college, where I’m taking an intro to accounting class. Sundays are the only day I get to relax a bit, hang out with my friends at the local park, and help my mom with chores. I’m not a big fan of big‑city life, but I like the mix of cheap rent, a decent job, and being close to my family.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was more in touch with my community’s concerns—immigration reform, a path to citizenship for families like mine, and stronger environmental protections for the coast that we rely on for tourism jobs. I also liked the fact that the Democratic candidate talked about tuition relief, which mattered to me as a community‑college student. I wasn’t thrilled with any candidate, but the Republican platform seemed to ignore the struggles of working‑class families and pushed policies that would raise my cost of living, like higher taxes on gasoline and less funding for public schools.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. I’m still worried about rent hikes—my landlord just mentioned a possible increase next year—and grocery prices keep climbing, especially fresh produce. The job market feels shaky, too; the warehouse has been cutting overtime lately, and I’m not sure how long the demand will stay high. Those two issues—housing and food costs—are what keep me up at night.

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward. The Democrats, for all their promises, still move too slowly on things like affordable housing, and sometimes they get caught up in national drama that doesn’t affect us here. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to care more about tax cuts for the wealthy and less about the everyday grind of people working multiple jobs. I’m not a hardcore partisan; I just want policies that keep my rent affordable and my grocery bill from blowing up. If a candidate can actually push for rent control at the city level or protect the minimum wage, I’ll consider them, regardless of party.
