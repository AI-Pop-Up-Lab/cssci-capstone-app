## Background
I'm 45 and I work as a community health aide for the Fort Berthold Reservation. I grew up on the reservation, went to a regional college in Bismarck for a couple of years, but never finished a degree because I needed to start supporting my family. My husband is a truck driver, and we have two kids—one in middle school and a teenager getting ready for college. We own a modest house just outside Parshall, and most weekends I'm either helping my mom with groceries or taking the kids to the tribal community center for after‑school programs.

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, then I drive an hour to the health office in Mandan where I fill out paperwork, coordinate transportation for elders to medical appointments, and run health‑education workshops. After my shift ends at 3 p.m., I usually pick up the kids from school, help with homework, and sometimes we all head to the local grocery store where prices have been creeping up. Evenings are a mix of cooking, watching a bit of TV, and scrolling through the news on my phone before I’m dead tired by 10 p.m. I don’t have a lot of spare cash, so when we can, we go camping on the reservation’s lake—it's the only thing that feels like a break.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates really cared about what’s happening on the reservation or about the rising cost of diesel for my husband’s rig. The whole process seemed like a circus, and I was swamped with work and kids. I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything, so I stayed home. That feeling hasn’t really shifted; I’m still skeptical about the whole system. I’m sitting this one out too, unless something dramatic hits our community—like a major clinic closing or a new policy that directly threatens our water supply.

My biggest worries right now are the grocery bills and the price of fuel. When diesel spikes, it hurts my husband’s income and makes it harder to get supplies to the remote parts of the reservation. The other big thing is the lack of stable jobs for our youth; my teenage son is already talking about leaving the reservation for work. I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least talks about tribal sovereignty and health care, but I feel their promises are vague and never fully funded. The Republicans, on the other hand, push for less regulation, which sometimes translates to fewer resources for our community. Overall, I’m annoyed with both sides for taking my concerns for granted.
