## Background
I'm 30 and work as a diesel mechanic at a maintenance shop that services the oil rigs and the local fleet of snowplows in Fairfair (the Fairbanks area of AK‑1). I got my associate degree in automotive technology from a community college in Anchorage, then moved up north for the higher pay and the chance to be out in the wilderness. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house with my partner, Maya, and our six‑month‑old daughter, Lian. We split the chores: I take the early shift, usually starting at 5 a.m., because the shop needs someone on before the sun comes up to get the rigs ready for the day. After a 10‑hour shift I come home, help Maya with the baby, and we eat whatever's on the stove—mostly frozen pizza or whatever we can grab at the local grocery. On weekends I like to go dog‑sledding with a friend or hunt for moose; it's the only way I feel the season slows down. My paycheck goes mostly to the mortgage, the heating bill (which spikes in March), and the occasional flight home to see my parents in Seattle. I watch the news on my phone while waiting for the furnace to heat up, and I try to keep up with the local community board at the library when I can.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about the rising cost of prescription meds and the fact that the incumbent seemed indifferent to the flood of health‑care clinics we need up here. The campaign also talked about protecting the boreal forest and supporting renewable energy projects that could give us jobs without destroying the landscape I love. I still think the Democrats are the only ones who get the health‑care safety net right, even if they sometimes sound like they're trying to turn Alaska into a mainland state.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the price of gasoline and the looming property tax hike the borough council is debating. If the tax goes up, we might have to cut back on heating, which is a nightmare in a place that can freeze to -40 °F. I also keep an eye on the debate over the new mining lease on the north slope; if that goes through, it could bring jobs but also wreck the salmon runs. I view the Democratic Party as more compassionate about health and the environment, but I feel they don't always grasp how remote life works out here. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk a lot about resource development and lower taxes, which sounds good until you realize they often ignore the long‑term damage to the land and the fact that we already pay a lot in federal taxes for things we can't control.

## Media Diet
I start my day with NPR's morning news while I’m getting the shop warmed up, then scroll through ABC News and Newsweek articles in the evening after the kids are down. I clock about six hours a week across those sites.
