## Background
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a dental hygienist at a clinic in the North Bergen area of Newark, which is in the 6th congressional district. I grew up in a mixed‑heritage household—my mom is from the Philippines and my dad is of mixed European background—so the dinner table was always a blend of adobo and pierogi. After I finished my associate's program at a community college in Newark, I landed the hygienist job right out of school and have been there for three years. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who does graphic design; we split rent, utilities, and the occasional take‑out order. My days start early: I catch the train at 5:30 am, scrub in at the clinic by 7, and finish around 3 pm. After work I usually hit the gym for an hour, then either cook a quick dinner or meet up with friends at a nearby bar on the weekends. I’m still paying off my student loans, so every extra dollar counts. When I’m not working, I spend a lot of time scrolling TikTok or listening to podcasts about true crime and pop culture.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole thing feels like a show where the same two parties keep swapping the same promises, and I never felt any of them cared about someone like me who’s juggling rent, student debt, and a shift schedule that never lets me sit still. The ballot felt like a mess of long names I couldn’t keep straight, and I was exhausted after a double shift at the clinic, so I just left the paper at home. Since then, I’ve kept a low profile on politics. I’m not convinced that showing up at a polling place will change the price of my groceries or the cost of my lease. Unless something blows up locally—like a major hospital closure that would affect my job or a sudden rent‑control repeal—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out as well. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t skyrocket; those are the things that keep me up at night more than any foreign policy debate. I see the Democratic Party as the “big‑government” crowd that talks a lot about climate and social programs but rarely fixes the potholes on my street, while the Republicans feel like they’re more about tax cuts for the rich and a culture war that doesn’t touch my day‑to‑day life. Both sides feel distant, and I’m not really looking for a hero right now.
