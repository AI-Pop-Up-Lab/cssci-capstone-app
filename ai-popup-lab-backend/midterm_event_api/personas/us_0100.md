## Background
I'm 55 and I live in the outskirts of Fresno, right near the foothills of the Sierra Nevada. I work full‑time as a maintenance supervisor for a community college; the job mostly involves fixing HVAC systems, keeping the labs running, and training a handful of junior techs. I got my associate degree in mechanical engineering technology at a local community college, which is why I ended up in this trade instead of a four‑year university track.

I rent a modest two‑bedroom house that I share with my wife, who runs a small home‑based bakery out of our kitchen. We have two adult kids—one lives in Los Angeles and the other is still in college in Sacramento—so the house is usually quiet except for the occasional delivery truck or the neighbor's dog barking. My mornings start around 5:30 am; I brew a strong cup of coffee, check the weather, and skim the news while the furnace hums. My workday runs from 8 am to 4:30 pm, with a short lunch break where I sometimes catch up on a podcast or a quick text with my sister in Oregon. After the shift I head home, help my wife prep for the next day’s orders, and usually unwind by watching a game or scrolling through the news on my tablet before bed around 11.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democrat because I felt the party was more attentive to the rising cost of living that’s been squeezing my family’s budget. Gas prices were up, the rent we pay has barely kept pace with wages, and the candidate’s platform on expanding community college funding resonated with my own work in education maintenance. I also liked the stance on renewable energy incentives, which could mean cheaper electricity for the campus I maintain.

Since then, I’ve gotten a bit more skeptical about the whole system. The promises about affordable housing haven’t materialized in my area, and the local Democratic office seems more focused on national talking points than on fixing the potholes on our county roads. Still, I think the Republican side is even less likely to address the practical issues that matter to a maintenance supervisor—like funding for technical training programs or protecting the middle class from sudden tax hikes. So, for the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting; I’m going to the polling place on Election Day, even if I’m only casting a ballot for the Senate and House races.

My top everyday concerns right now are the grocery bills that keep creeping up and the reliability of the water supply for our neighborhood, which has been spotty after a recent drought. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who at least talk about social safety nets, even if they’re slow to act, while I view Republicans as more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and less on the day‑to‑day struggles of people like me.
