## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Fairbanks, a few blocks from the river that cuts through town. I work as an administrative assistant at the regional health clinic, typing up patient records, scheduling appointments, and fielding calls from people who need help navigating their insurance. I finished a couple of years of community college before I started working full‑time, so I never got a degree but the job keeps me busy and pays the bills.

I'm married to a retired truck driver who now spends most of his time fixing up our old house. We have two grown kids—one in Seattle, the other in Anchorage—who call once a week. My mother, who moved here from the Lower 48 after retiring, lives with us and needs help with her medication and mobility. Most mornings I wake up at 5:30, brew a strong cup of coffee, and catch the NPR morning podcast while I drive the 15‑minute commute to the clinic. After my shift ends at 3 p.m., I usually swing by the local diner for a quick lunch, then head home to do laundry, help my mom with dinner, and maybe run to the grocery store before it gets dark. Winter evenings are short, so I spend a lot of time inside, scrolling through the news on my tablet or watching a rerun of a sitcom before I go to bed around 10.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same old things, and I didn't see any of them really caring about what it's like to live in a place where the cost of heating can eat up half a paycheck. Between long clinic hours, taking care of my mom, and the endless paperwork, voting just felt like another thing I couldn't fit into the day. I also get a lot of political ads that sound like they're made for people in the lower 48, not for folks who hunt, fish, and worry about the price of a gallon of diesel.

I'm sitting this one out, too. Unless something really blows up locally—like a big cut to the heating assistance program or a highway closure that messes up our supply routes—I'm not going to make the trip to the polls. My biggest worries right now are keeping the house warm this winter and making sure groceries stay affordable. The price of fresh salmon and the cost of a decent winter jacket are what keep me up at night more than any national debate.

When I think about the parties, I feel like both of them are missing the mark for Alaskans like me. The Democrats talk a lot about climate change but don't seem to understand how that translates to stricter regulations on the very fisheries that support our community. The Republicans keep pushing oil and gas development, but that often means more traffic on the roads I have to drive on and higher fuel prices for the trucks that bring supplies in. Both sides seem to forget that we have a lot of Black residents here who are trying to build a stable life in a place that can feel isolated. I get annoyed when politicians use big‑city rhetoric that doesn't match the reality of a town where the nearest major airport is a three‑hour drive away.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR's morning news while I'm driving, then in the evenings I scroll through CBS News and ABC News articles on my tablet, adding up to about four hours a week.
