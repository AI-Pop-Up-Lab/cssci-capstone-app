## Background
I'm 30 and work as an HVAC technician for a small family‑run business in Meridian, just outside Boise. After getting my associate’s degree in mechanical systems at a community college, I landed an apprenticeship and never went back to school. My day starts early; I’m usually at the job site by 6 a.m., crawling into cramped crawl spaces to fix furnaces or install new air‑conditioners. The work is physically demanding, but I like the hands‑on part and the steady pay—enough to cover my rent on a modest two‑bedroom apartment a few blocks from the office.

I live with my older sister, who works part‑time at a local grocery store, and our roommate, a college student who’s studying computer science. We split the rent and utilities, and on weekends we take turns cooking, usually something quick like tacos or a pot of chili. I’m not big on hobbies; when I have free time I watch a few YouTube videos about new HVAC tech, play basketball at the community center, or scroll through social media while waiting for a break. I go to church once a month, mostly to see family, and I try to keep in touch with my parents back in Seattle through weekly FaceTime calls.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on taxes and small‑business regulation was the only thing that could keep my paycheck from shrinking. The Democratic platform seemed to focus on big‑ticket items that didn’t touch my day‑to‑day life, and the messaging from the GOP felt more in line with my own concerns about rising gas prices and the cost of living. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on the local economy; the HVAC market is still steady, but I’m worried about a possible recession hitting construction projects.

I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rent increase that’s been announced for next year and the price of gasoline, which eats into my budget when I have to drive to jobs outside the city. I also worry about the shortage of skilled labor—if the training pipelines dry up, my whole career could get shaky.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty sure the Republicans are the ones pushing for lower taxes and less red tape for tradespeople like me. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about climate goals and social programs that sound nice but end up costing more for people on the ground. I don’t trust the media to give me a balanced picture; I prefer sources that cut the fluff and stick to the numbers.
