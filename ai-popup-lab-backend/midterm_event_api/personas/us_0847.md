## Background
I'm 45 and I live in Plano, a suburban neighborhood not far from the Dallas‑Fort Worth corridor. I work full‑time as a high school math teacher at a public school a few miles north of town, which means my days start early—usually a 20‑minute drive to the campus, a quick coffee from the school cafeteria, then three periods of algebra and geometry before lunch. After school I stay for a faculty meeting, then head home to pick up my two kids, ages 9 and 12, from after‑school programs. My husband works in IT for a regional firm, so we both juggle shift changes to make sure someone is always home for dinner. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a soccer game for my son, and helping my daughter with her science fair project. My parents, who immigrated from Taiwan, live in Houston; we see them a few times a year, mostly for big holidays. I don't have a car beyond the family sedan, so I rely on the highway and occasional rideshare for longer trips. In my spare time I like to knit scarves and binge‑watch Korean dramas when the kids are asleep.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between long school days, endless grading, and the feeling that neither candidate was addressing the real issues that affect my family—like the rising cost of property taxes and the grocery bills that keep creeping up—I just felt it was a waste of time. The campaign ads were all the same, promising change but never delivering anything concrete for a suburban teacher juggling a household. I also get annoyed by how both parties seem to focus on national drama while ignoring the potholes on our local roads or the underfunded after‑school programs. So I stayed home, watched the news later, and told myself I could pay attention next election if something actually mattered to me.

I'm sitting this one out, too. Unless there’s a sudden scandal that hits my school district or a local ballot measure that threatens my kids' education, I don’t see a reason to go to the polls again. My biggest everyday worries are the property tax hikes that keep making our mortgage feel heavier and the grocery prices that make a family of four stretch each paycheck. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as a bit too caught up in identity politics and not enough on practical school funding, while Republicans feel like they’re pushing a hard‑line immigration stance that doesn’t reflect the diversity of our community. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than solving the problems that hit my kitchen table.
