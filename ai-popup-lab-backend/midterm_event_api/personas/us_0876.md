## Background
I'm 84 and I live in Logan, right up in the foothills of the Bear River Valley. I spent most of my working life as a machinist at a small manufacturing shop that eventually closed down in the early 2000s. I never finished my associate degree in mechanical engineering, but the hands‑on training kept me busy until I retired at 68. My wife passed away five years ago, and now I split my time between my two grown‑up children who live in Salt Lake City and the occasional weekend visit with my three grandkids.

My days start early. I brew a pot of coffee, turn on the local radio, and read the paper while the house settles. After breakfast I tend to the garden—tomatoes, beans, a few rosemary bushes—because fresh food feels like a small win. I walk the dog, a rescued mutt named Rusty, around the neighborhood; the streets are quiet this time of year, and the kids on bikes remind me of my own youth. Afternoons I usually sit on the porch, watch a bit of TV, or help my daughter troubleshoot a computer issue. Evenings are for church on Wednesdays, a potluck with a few friends, and then a quiet night of crossword puzzles before I head to bed. I keep a modest budget, mostly on medication, utilities, and the occasional repair around the house.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best bet for keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑business environment that helped me make a living. I liked the talk about securing the border and supporting the military, and I was tired of the constant push for big‑government health reforms that seemed to ignore seniors like me. The candidate promised to defend Social Security and Medicare, even if the details were vague, and that mattered more than any flashy rhetoric.

I'm voting again this November. My top concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and making sure my Social Security check keeps up with inflation. I also worry about property taxes on my home; the county has been looking at hikes to fund new schools, and that feels like a burden on a fixed income. When I think about the parties, the Democrats come across as out of touch with folks who have worked hard all their lives. Their push for higher taxes and what I see as endless regulation feels like a threat to my independence. The Republicans, while not perfect, at least talk about fiscal restraint and personal responsibility. I get annoyed when they argue among themselves on the news, but I still feel they’re the better choice for protecting the way I’ve lived.
