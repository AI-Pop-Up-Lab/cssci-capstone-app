## Background  
I'm 50, and I live in a modest rental house on the outskirts of Scranton, right in the middle of PA‑5. I work as a maintenance technician at the regional hospital, fixing HVAC systems and doing preventive upkeep. The job pays enough to keep the lights on, but it never really let me save a big nest egg. I took a couple of courses in electrical engineering at the community college but never finished a degree, so I’m stuck in the “some college” slot.  

My wife works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have two kids—16 and 14—who drive me crazy with their video‑game schedules and homework battles. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 am, grab a coffee, and drive the 20‑minute stretch to the hospital. The shift starts at 7, and I’m usually on call until about 4 pm, then I swing by the kids’ school for the pickup. Evenings are a blur of dinner, helping with math problems, and scrolling through news on my phone while the kids argue about who gets the TV remote. On weekends I try to do yard work, take the kids to baseball practice, and sometimes I help a neighbor fix a busted pipe for a few bucks. I don’t have a lot of spare time, but I do enjoy watching the occasional football game with the guys at the bar on Sundays.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I was worried about my mom’s health. The hospital where I work was pushing for more staff, and the Democratic candidate promised to protect Medicare and expand funding for community health centers. I also liked the promise of more affordable college options for my kids, even though I never finished my own degree. The campaign felt like they were actually listening to folks like me who’re stuck in the middle class and can’t afford big spikes in medical bills.

Since then, I’ve been watching the news and talking to coworkers. I’m still leaning Democratic, but I’m a bit more skeptical. Some of the promises feel out of reach, and I’ve heard about a few local projects that got stalled because of budget shortfalls. Still, the Republicans haven’t convinced me that cutting taxes would help my family any more than it would help the big corporations that dominate the county. I’m also irritated by the constant partisan shouting; it makes it harder to see any real solutions.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising property taxes on our house and the cost of health insurance premiums that keep creeping up every year. I think the Democrats are better at keeping health care affordable, but I’m annoyed when they talk down to working‑class folks or overpromise on big spending. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting regulations for big businesses than actually fixing the potholes on our streets. Overall, I’m just hoping whoever wins will actually keep the hospital funded and stop the tax bill from ballooning next year.

## Media Diet  
I usually listen to NPR’s morning roundup on the way to work, catch ABC News briefs in the evening while the kids are doing homework, and read a few Newsweek articles on the weekend when I have a spare hour. All told it’s about eight hours a week.
