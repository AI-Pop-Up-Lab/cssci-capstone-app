## Background
I'm 65 and I live in Hammond, right on the edge of the industrial strip that borders Chicago. I grew up in a bilingual household; my parents came from Mexico and worked in the steel mills. After high school I earned an associate’s degree in health sciences and spent most of my adult life as a dietary aide at the local nursing home. I’m retired now, but I still pick up a few shifts when the schedule is short because the pension isn’t enough to cover the rising cost of my medication and the property taxes on the modest house I own with my late husband’s name still on the deed.

My daughter lives in Indianapolis with her family, so I get occasional visits from my two grandkids in the summer, but most weeks I’m on my own. My days start early: I brew a strong cup of coffee, flip on the morning news, and then head to the grocery store before the crowds. I walk my dog, Beto, around the neighborhood park, and I’m a regular at the local Catholic church where I volunteer in the food pantry. Evenings are usually spent watching a sitcom or catching up on a telenovela on the Spanish channel, and I try to get to bed by ten so I’m not too tired for the next day’s chores.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on immigration and law‑and‑order resonated with my own concerns about safety in my community and the impact of illegal immigration on jobs that my neighbors and I rely on. I also appreciated the candidate’s promises to keep taxes low for retirees like me, and the talk about protecting Social Security from any cuts. I wasn’t thrilled about every single policy, but the alternative just felt like more government meddling that never seemed to help people on the ground.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top worries right now are the soaring cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hike the county board is pushing this year. I’m also watching gas prices because I still drive to the nursing home a few times a week. The Democratic Party, from my perspective, talks a lot about climate goals and social programs, but I rarely see that turning into concrete help for seniors in my town. The Republicans, even if they’re not perfect, at least keep the conversation focused on keeping taxes down and protecting the borders, which feels more relevant to my daily life.
