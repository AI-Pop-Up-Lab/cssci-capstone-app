## Background  
I'm 45, and I work full‑time as a dental hygienist at a clinic in Loveland, right in the middle of Colorado's 8th district. My husband runs a landscaping business out of our house, and we have two kids—one is a junior in high school and the other just started sophomore year. Most mornings I get up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and drop the younger one off at the bus stop before heading to the clinic for the 7 a.m. shift. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from practice, and we all eat dinner together around 6 p.m. On weekends I either help my mom with errands—she lives a couple of hours away in the mountains—or I’m at the kids' soccer games. When I can, I like to hike the nearby foothills, but lately the cost of gas and the price of a decent pair of hiking boots have been a pain. I’m a citizen of the United States, and I’ve lived in Colorado since I was a kid, moving around the state a few times before we finally settled here about ten years ago.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I turned out to vote for the Democratic candidate. I was motivated mainly by concerns over my family’s health insurance and the rising cost of college tuition for my older child. The Democratic platform’s promises about expanding coverage and putting more money into public schools felt like the safer bet for us. I also liked the emphasis on clean‑energy investments because I see the smoke from the nearby oil fields every day and worry about the air my kids breathe.  

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. My top everyday worries are the mortgage payment creeping up as property taxes rise, and the price of prescription meds that my husband needs for his arthritis. Those two things dominate the conversation at our kitchen table. I feel the Democratic Party still has the better ideas for handling health care and climate change, even if the messaging can be all over the place. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that keep our water clean. I get annoyed when they talk about “small government” while ignoring how that could mean fewer resources for our schools and hospitals. I’m not a political scholar, but I know I want policies that keep my family afloat and the air we breathe safe.
