## Background  
I'm 35 and work as a software engineer for a midsize tech firm that outsources a lot of its cloud infrastructure to Seattle. I live in Yakima, right in the middle of Washington's 7th district, where the hills roll into the Columbia River plain. My wife and I have a nine‑year‑old daughter who rides the bus to the local middle school and plays soccer on Saturdays. Most mornings I grab a coffee at the corner donut shop, scroll through my phone while the kiddo eats his cereal, and then hop on the 45‑minute drive to the office. Since the pandemic we’ve been doing a hybrid schedule, so a few days a week I’m in a small office cubicle, other days I’m in a home office with the kid’s homework spread out on the kitchen table. After work I usually pick up groceries at the big supermarket, swing by the hardware store if we need anything for the garden, and try to get a quick workout at the community gym before dinner. Weekends are a mix of family road trips to the nearby vineyards, helping my dad with his small farm plot, and catching a game on the TV when we can.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on lower taxes and less regulation was the only thing keeping my paycheck from being squeezed by rising costs. The candidate’s promises about protecting the border and supporting the agricultural sector resonated with my dad’s farm and the tech jobs that keep my family afloat. I didn’t feel the Democrats were offering any concrete plans for small‑business owners or the water‑rights issues that are a constant headache out here.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the price of gasoline and the reliability of water for both my dad’s crops and the city’s growing demand. I also keep an eye on the local job market; a tech layoff would hit us hard, and there’s talk about new manufacturing plants that could bring more stability. I still see the Republican Party as the one most likely to keep taxes low and give businesses the freedom to expand, even if I don’t agree with everything they say. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for bigger government programs that feel out of touch with folks like me who are trying to make ends meet. I’m not a political scholar, but I’m convinced the GOP is the better bet for my family right now.
