## Background

I work full‑time as a construction laborer for a regional contractor that builds warehouse extensions and a few low‑rise apartments around the Front Range. The job is physically demanding—early mornings, a lot of lifting, and sometimes overtime when a project is behind schedule. I get up around 5:30 a.m., grab a coffee from the gas station on the corner, and head out to the site. After work I usually swing by the local hardware store for supplies or to pick up a quick dinner—often a frozen pizza or a sandwich from the deli. My wife works part‑time at a grocery store, and we have a three‑year‑old daughter who’s in preschool. Weekends are a mix of chores, a little yard work, and trying to catch a high school football game when we can. We don’t travel much; most of our spending goes to gas, groceries, and the mortgage. I’m involved in the church youth group a few times a month, mostly to help with the snack table and to keep an eye on the kids.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on the economy and energy resonated with my day‑to‑day concerns. I’m a construction worker, so the talk about cutting regulations and keeping coal and oil jobs alive felt like a direct benefit to my paycheck. I also liked the candidate’s promise to keep taxes low—something that matters when you’re trying to stretch a modest income to cover a mortgage, a kid’s school fees, and a car payment. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how things are going. The cost of building materials has gone up, which makes my work harder, and the price of gas has been a headache. I’m still convinced that a smaller federal government and less red tape are the best ways to get those costs down, even though I admit I don’t follow every policy detail.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of gasoline and the availability of decent-paying construction jobs in our area. I also worry about property taxes going up, because that directly hits our budget. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as pushing too much climate legislation that threatens the energy jobs we rely on, and I feel they’re more interested in talking about identity politics than fixing the things that affect my family’s paycheck. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to understand that a lot of folks like me need steady work and lower taxes, even if the messaging sometimes feels a bit blunt. I’m not a political analyst; I just want policies that keep my family afloat and give me a shot at a raise.
