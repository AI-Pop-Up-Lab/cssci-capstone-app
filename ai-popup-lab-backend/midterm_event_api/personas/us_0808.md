## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Sioux Falls, just a short drive from the river. I work part‑time at the local grocery store stocking shelves and bagging groceries; the hours are flexible enough that I can still take care of my mom, who has arthritis and needs help with errands. My husband retired from the railroad a few years ago, so most of the household income comes from his pension and my paycheck. We have two grown‑up kids; one lives in Minnesota and the other is back here, helping with the house and occasionally picking up extra shifts at the same store. My days start early – a cup of coffee while the morning news runs on the TV, then a 30‑minute drive to the store. After my shift I swing by the senior center to drop off groceries for a few neighbors, and in the evenings I either watch a sitcom or catch up on a telenovela on the Spanish channel. Sundays are for church and a big family lunch when my kids can make it home. I spend most of my free time gardening, fixing things around the house, and chatting with friends at the community center.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same stuff, and I was exhausted from juggling work, caring for my mom, and the rising cost of everything. The ballot felt like a chore, not something that would change my daily grind. I also thought my vote wouldn't matter in a big state race, so I just stayed home. Coming up to the 2026 midterms, I'm still leaning toward sitting this one out unless something blows up locally – like a sudden hospital closure or a big price hike on utilities that hits us directly. My biggest worries right now are the price of groceries and the cost of my mom's medication; both keep me up at night. I also worry about property taxes going up, especially with the new development projects near our neighborhood. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as talking a lot about big ideas but not really getting down to the practical stuff that matters to folks like me. The Republicans seem more focused on tax cuts and farmland issues, which helps me a bit, but sometimes they push policies that feel like they ignore the needs of older workers and minority families. Overall, I'm more annoyed by the Democrats' promises than the Republicans' occasional bluntness.
