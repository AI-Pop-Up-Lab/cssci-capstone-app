## Background  
I'm 65 and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Bellevue, just outside Omaha in Nebraska's 2nd district. I spent most of my adult life working as a dental hygienist after earning an associate degree at Central Community College. I retired three years ago, but I still pick up a few shifts at the local senior center because the pension alone doesn't cover everything. My husband passed away five years back, so it's just me, my two grown‑up kids who live in Chicago and Des Moines, and the occasional weekend visit from my granddaughter who loves to help me with my vegetable garden.  

My days start around 6:30 a.m. with a cup of black coffee while the radio hums NPR headlines. I tend to the garden for an hour or two, then I check my email and the daily mail‑order catalog. Around noon I make a quick sandwich and watch the local news on TV for a half hour. In the afternoons I either help out at the senior center, run errands at the grocery store, or sit on the porch knitting. Evenings are for a bit of TV—usually a sitcom rerun or a news program—and then I read a paperback before bed. On Sundays I go to church, then drive out to the farmer's market to pick up fresh produce.  

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I feel like the whole system is a loop of the same old promises that never trickle down to folks like me. The ballot felt like a choice between two sides that both ignore the real cost of my prescription meds and the rising property tax bill on my home. Between caring for my granddaughter, juggling a part‑time gig, and dealing with my own health issues, voting just seemed like another item I couldn't fit into an already packed schedule.  

I'm sitting this one out too. Unless a candidate shows up with a concrete plan to lower drug prices or actually fights the county's tax hike, I don't see a reason to spend my limited energy on the polls. My top everyday concerns are keeping my blood pressure under control without breaking the bank on meds, and making sure my property tax doesn't force me to sell the house I've lived in for 30 years.  

When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to push too many new regulations that I worry will raise costs for small businesses like the senior center where I work. The Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes, but the cuts never reach someone on a fixed income. Both sides feel distant, and I get a knot in my stomach every time I hear campaign ads that sound more like sales pitches than real solutions.
