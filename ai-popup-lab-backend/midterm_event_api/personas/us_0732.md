## Background  
I'm 70 and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Albany, right in the heart of Oregon's 6th district. I spent most of my working life as an electrician for the county, retiring about five years ago when I finally qualified for full retirement benefits. My wife, who used to teach kindergarten, now keeps busy with her weekly knitting circle and the church choir. We have two grown‑up children—one lives in Seattle, the other in Portland—and a handful of grandchildren who visit every summer.  

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and flip through the local paper (which I mostly skim for the classifieds). After a quick walk around the neighborhood, I head to the community center to help with the senior tech‑class, where I teach folks how to use smartphones. Lunch is usually a sandwich and some leftover stir‑fry from the night before. In the afternoon I tend to my small vegetable garden, check on the chickens, and sometimes run errands at the grocery store. Evenings are for TV, the radio, and a bit of reading—mostly mystery novels. On Sundays I attend services at the Buddhist temple downtown and then have a family dinner that stretches into the night.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old horse‑and‑carriage routine: the Democrats pushing policies that seemed to ignore the realities of older immigrants, and the Republicans yelling about “law and order” while offering no real help for people like me who are on a fixed income. Between my health appointments, caring for my wife’s arthritis, and the constant scramble to keep up with rising prescription costs, there just wasn't the energy or the belief that my vote would change anything. I also got a lot of junk mail and phone calls telling me my vote didn't matter, which only reinforced the feeling that the whole system is a farce.  

For the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out again. Unless something dramatic happens—like a local hospital closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces us out of our home—I'm not planning to cast a ballot. My top everyday concerns are the price of my medications and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. I also worry about reliable public transportation; the bus routes have been cut back, and getting to my doctor's appointments without a car is getting harder.  

When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as too focused on climate‑centric policies that don't address the immediate cost‑of‑living pressures I face. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to care more about cultural battles than about affordable healthcare or senior services. Both sides talk a big game about “the American Dream,” but neither seems to understand the everyday grind of an older Asian couple trying to stretch a fixed income while staying connected to our community.

## Media Diet
