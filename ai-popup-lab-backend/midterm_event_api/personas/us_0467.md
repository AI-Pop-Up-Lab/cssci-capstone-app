## Background
I'm 45 and I work as a customer‑service supervisor at the call center that handles billing for a regional utility company. I live in St. Joseph, right near the river that marks the border of the 3rd district. My husband works the night shift at the local warehouse, so most of the household chores fall on me during the day. We have two kids: a 16‑year‑old who drives a beat‑up sedan to high school and a 9‑year‑old who still needs a car seat. Mornings are a rush—getting the kids ready, dropping the younger one off at the elementary school across town, then a 20‑minute drive to the office where I spend eight hours on the phone, mostly dealing with angry customers about power outages or billing errors. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from after‑school programs, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner before we all collapse on the couch. On weekends I help my husband with yard work, take the kids to the park, and occasionally visit my parents in Kansas City. I go to church on Sundays, but it's more about seeing friends than any deep theological commitment. I watch a few TV shows in the evenings, mostly sitcom reruns, and try to get a bit of sleep before the next chaotic week.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about people like me who are just trying to keep the lights on and put food on the table. Between the kids' school pickups, the night‑shift schedule at home, and the endless overtime at the call center, I just couldn't find the energy to get to the polls, and I figured my one vote wouldn't change anything. The political ads were all the same—big promises, no concrete plans for the rising cost of groceries or the gas prices that eat up my paycheck. That feeling of cynicism hasn't gone away, so I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something blows up locally—like a major factory closing or a scandal that hits my community directly—I don't see a reason to stand in line again.

My top everyday worries are the price of groceries and the cost of gasoline. When I fill up the tank for the kids' school rides, it feels like I'm paying for someone else's profit, and when I shop for fresh produce, the prices keep creeping higher, squeezing our budget tighter. Property taxes are also on my mind; the county keeps raising rates, and I worry about what that means for our house next year.

As for the parties, I see the Democrats as a bunch of talkers who love to promise free college and universal healthcare but never seem to understand that I have a mortgage, a car payment, and kids to feed. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk a lot about cutting taxes and “making America great again,” which sounds good until I hear about cuts to social safety nets that could affect my husband if he ever gets injured on the job. Both sides feel out of touch in their own ways, and I'm not convinced any of them have a real plan for someone in my situation.

## Media Diet
