## Background  
I'm 35, and I live in Torrington, a small city in Connecticut's 5th district. I grew up a few blocks from the downtown mill buildings and stayed close to home after high school. I got an associate's degree in HVAC technology at a community college in Waterbury, then landed a job with a regional heating‑and‑cooling firm. My day starts around 5:30 a.m.; I grab a coffee, check the thermostat on the house, and drive the half‑hour to the office where I spend most of my day crawling into cramped crawl spaces, fixing furnaces, and taking calls from the dispatch desk.  

I’m married to a woman who works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have a nine‑year‑old daughter who goes to the public elementary school down the street. Evenings are a scramble—dinner, helping with homework, and trying to squeeze in a little TV before we all hit the beds. On weekends I try to keep the house tidy, mow the lawn, and sometimes meet up with a few friends at the community center for a basketball game. Money’s tight; the mortgage and car payments eat a big chunk of the paycheck, so we’re always watching the budget.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus. Between long shifts, overtime, and the kids’ soccer schedule, I just didn’t have the energy to get to a polling place, and the ads on TV all sounded the same—promises that never seemed to reach people like me. I’ve been watching the news, but it feels like they’re talking about problems that don’t affect my street. That cynicism made me sit out, and unless something really blows up locally—like a factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes—I’m planning to sit this one out too.  

My biggest everyday worries are the cost of groceries and keeping up with the mortgage. When prices at the supermarket go up, it’s an immediate hit on the family budget, and any increase in property taxes would force us to tighten the belt even more. I’m also worried about stable work; the HVAC market is decent now, but I hear about layoffs in other trades and wonder how secure my job will be in a few years.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats talk a lot about “racial justice,” but I don’t see that translating into better jobs or affordable housing in my neighborhood. The Republicans seem to care mostly about tax cuts for the rich and less regulation for big corporations, which feels like they’re ignoring folks who are just trying to keep a roof over their heads. I’m not a political scholar; I just feel like the system is set up to keep the same folks in power while the rest of us keep hustling.

## Media Diet  
I usually listen to NPR’s morning news while I’m driving to work, then catch up on a quick ABC News roundup on my phone during lunch, and later in the evening I scroll through a mix of ABC and CBS articles for about an hour total. That adds up to about six hours a week.
