## Background
I'm 40, and I live in Canton, a small town about 30 miles east of Jackson in Mississippi’s 3rd district. I work as a civil engineer for a regional construction firm that builds bridges and water‑treatment plants. My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, then a 45‑minute drive to the office where I’m usually on the road visiting sites until about 6 p.m. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from after‑school soccer, and try to squeeze in a little time with my wife before we all crash on the couch. My wife is a high school math teacher, and we have two kids: a 14‑year‑old sophomore and a 9‑year‑old who loves video games. On weekends I’m either helping a neighbor fix a fence, working a side gig doing CAD drawings for small contractors, or hunting with a few friends in the piney woods. I have a master’s degree in civil engineering, but most of what I actually use day‑to‑day is the software we learned in school and a lot of on‑the‑job problem solving. I’m not a political junkie; I mostly watch the news when I have a spare half hour between jobs or while waiting for the kids’ practices to end.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long work hours, the kids’ schedules, and a feeling that the candidates were more interested in national drama than fixing the potholes on my street, I just never got around to it. I figured my single ballot wouldn’t change anything, and the whole process felt like a circus. The same thing is still on my mind for the 2026 midterms – I’m sitting this one out unless something really shakes up my community, like a major plant shutting down or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces us to move. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the scarcity of decent, well‑paid jobs in the area. When I think about the parties, I’m more annoyed than anything. The GOP seems to be all about tax cuts that never trickle down to folks like me, while the Democrats keep talking about climate change and social programs that feel disconnected from the realities of a small town in the Deep South. I’ve got a vague sense that a moderate Republican might be better for keeping the local economy humming, but I’m also tired of the partisan bickering that never seems to solve the actual problems I see on the road or at the hardware store.

## Media Diet
I get my news in short bursts: a quick scan of NBC News headlines on my phone in the morning, a longer watch of CNBC’s market wrap in the evening, and I dip into Breitbart and the New York Post when I have a half‑hour at the kitchen table on weekends.
