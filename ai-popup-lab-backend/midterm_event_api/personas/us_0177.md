## Background
I'm 30 and work as a technical support rep for a telecom company in the Jacksonville suburbs of FL‑5. I grew up in a Thai‑American household; my parents run a small take‑out place near the mall, and I still help out on weekends when I can. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate I met at community college, and we split rent and utilities. My days start early: I grab a coffee from the corner shop, hop on the commuter rail to the office, and spend eight hours fielding calls about internet outages and billing glitches. After work I usually swing by the gym for a quick lift, then head home to cook something simple—often rice, frozen veggies, and a protein stir‑fry. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, catching a late‑night basketball game at the local park, and streaming the occasional anime series. I still take a few community college classes part‑time because I'm hoping to finish an associate's in information systems, but the workload keeps me busy.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my single vote wouldn't change anything, especially when the whole campaign seemed like a circus of ads and sound bites. Between long shifts, night classes, and trying to keep up with rent, I just couldn't fit a polling place into my schedule. The candidates all sounded the same to me—promises about the economy, immigration, climate—but none of them seemed to address the real day‑to‑day grind of people like me who are juggling bills and school. That cynicism has stuck, so I'm planning on sitting this one out too. I'm not saying I love the system; I just don't see a clear reason to carve out the time right now.

My biggest worries right now are the rent going up and grocery prices staying high. Every month I have to decide whether to cut back on streaming services or skip a few meals to keep the lease. The political parties both feel distant. The GOP talks a lot about cutting taxes, but I worry that cuts could mean fewer public services that help low‑income families, and I don't see much outreach to immigrant communities like mine. The Democrats keep pushing climate legislation, which sounds good, but I haven't felt any concrete help with job training or affordable housing from them either. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than solving the concrete problems I face on the ground.
