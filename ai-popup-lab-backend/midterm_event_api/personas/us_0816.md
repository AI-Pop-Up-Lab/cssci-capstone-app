## Background
I'm 20, and I live in a small apartment on the north side of Rapid City, right in the heart of SD‑1. I didn't go to college; after finishing high school I started working full‑time at a local grocery store, stocking shelves and running the checkout lane on the night shift. My mom works part‑time at the county health department, and my 14‑year‑old sister is still in school, so the three of us share the rent and utilities. I get up around 7 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner diner, and head to the store for my 2‑p.m. shift. After work I usually swing by the community center to help with the after‑school tutoring program—they need a few extra hands, and the pay is decent for a few extra hours. Weekends are a mix of catching a game at the local bar with friends, helping my mom with grocery runs, and sometimes driving out to the Badlands for a quick hike when the weather's nice. I don't have a car of my own; I rely on a shared ride with a coworker or the city bus. My free time is limited, but when I can, I play basketball at the park and listen to some hip‑hop on my phone.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything—most of the candidates seemed the same, and I was working late shifts when polls were open. The whole process just felt like a hassle, and I was tired of hearing the same promises from both sides. I also think the political conversation in the media doesn't really talk about people like me living in a rural part of South Dakota; it feels like I'm invisible. Because of that, I'm sitting this 2026 midterm out too, unless something really forces my hand—maybe a local issue that directly threatens my mom's job or a big spike in grocery prices that hits us hard.

My top everyday concerns right now are keeping my part‑time job stable and dealing with the rising cost of groceries. Rent is manageable because we split it, but if my mom loses her hours, everything gets shaky. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as more vocal about racial justice, but they seem disconnected from the real struggles on the plains. The Republicans dominate state politics, and while some of their policies help small businesses, they also push ideas that make me feel like my community's needs are an afterthought. Overall, I'm frustrated with both sides and just want policies that actually help people keep food on the table.
