## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Greenfield, a small town tucked in the hills of western Massachusetts. I spent most of my working life as a maintenance handyman for the local high school and a few nearby factories. I never finished high school; after I left home at 16 I learned the trade on the job. I retired about five years ago, so most days I just take it easy. My wife passed away three years back, and I have two grown‑up kids who live in Boston and up in New Hampshire, so the house is pretty quiet. I get up around 7 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and sit on the porch watching the sunrise while the neighbor’s dog barks. After that I do a bit of gardening—tomatoes, beans, a few herbs—and then I head out to the senior center where I volunteer to help set up bingo nights. Lunch is usually a sandwich and the paper; I like the crossword. In the evenings I sit in the living room, turn on the TV or my tablet, and scroll through the news sites I follow. Once a week I drive into town for groceries and to pick up a prescription, and on Sundays I attend a community potluck at the church. My routine is simple, and I try to keep my bills paid and my health in check.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I didn't vote for either of the major party candidates; I wrote in a third‑party candidate because I felt both the Democrat and Republican nominees were too extreme and ignored ordinary folks like me. The Democrat seemed to promise big government fixes that never reach the back roads, and the Republican was all about cutting taxes for the rich while leaving seniors to foot the bill for health care. I was hoping a smaller party could shake things up, even if they had no real chance of winning. That vote felt like a protest, a way to say “I’m not happy with the choices.” 

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I'm actually planning to cast a ballot again. I’m not thrilled about the options, but I think the local congressional race matters for things like road maintenance and broadband expansion in our area. My top everyday worries are the cost of prescription drugs and the rising property taxes that make it harder to stay in my home. I also keep an eye on the local job market, because a few of my neighbors still need part‑time work. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as out of touch with rural life and too focused on city issues, while the Republicans feel like they’re trying to roll back everything that helps seniors, like Medicare tweaks. Neither side seems to get the day‑to‑day grind of someone who has to stretch a fixed income.

## Media Diet
I usually start the day with NPR’s morning podcasts while I water the garden, then in the evening I scroll through CBS News articles on my tablet and read a few headlines on the New York Post before bed. All together it adds up to about nine hours a week.
