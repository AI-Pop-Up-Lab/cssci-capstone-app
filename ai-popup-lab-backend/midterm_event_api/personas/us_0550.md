## Background  
I'm 70 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Fargo, right where the city meets the farmland. I spent most of my working life as a high‑school English teacher in the local public school district, and I retired ten years ago when I hit the mandatory retirement age. My husband, a former mechanic, and I have been married for forty‑five years, and our two kids live out in Minnesota and Texas with their families.  

Every weekday I get up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of coffee, and sit at the kitchen table while the radio hums a mix of classic rock and the morning news. After a quick scan of the headlines on my tablet, I tend to my vegetable garden—tomatoes, beans, and a few hardy carrots that survive the short ND summer. In the afternoons I either take a walk around the neighborhood, stop by the community center for a bingo game, or help the local church with its food pantry. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV, scrolling through Facebook to see what the grandkids posted, and reading a paperback before bed. I drive a well‑maintained pickup for grocery trips and occasional trips to the county health clinic, where I keep up with my blood‑pressure meds and yearly flu shot.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate promised to keep taxes low, support the oil and gas industry that still pays a decent chunk of my property tax, and protect what I see as traditional values. I felt the Democratic platform was too focused on big‑city issues and didn’t speak to the everyday concerns of folks like me who rely on farming and small‑business jobs in the district. The campaign ads about “energy independence” and “border security” resonated with my sense that the country needs stable, predictable policies.  

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the lack of reliable broadband in the more rural parts of my county. Without decent internet, my husband can’t even do some of the simple online banking we used to manage, and my doctor’s office keeps telling us to use telehealth, which is a joke when the connection drops every other minute. I also keep an eye on property tax rates because my fixed retirement income doesn’t stretch as far as it used to.  

I see the Republican Party as the one that remembers the importance of low taxes, supporting the energy sector, and keeping government from getting too big. The Democrats, in my view, seem more interested in identity politics and big‑government solutions that end up raising my taxes or making regulations that hurt small farms. I’m not a policy guru, but I trust the party that looks out for the kind of life I’ve built—steady work, a modest home, and a community that looks out for each other.
