## Background  
I'm 40, and I live in the north‑west side of Grand Rapids, just a few miles out in the little community of Wyoming, Michigan. I work full‑time as a warehouse supervisor at a regional distribution center; the job mostly involves keeping the shipping schedules on track, handling paperwork, and stepping in when the forklift operators need a hand. I graduated high school in the early 2000s and never went to college, so most of what I know about the world comes from on‑the‑job experience and the people I meet at work.

I'm married to a guy who does a mix of construction and seasonal landscaping, and we have two kids—14‑year‑old Maya and 9‑year‑old Eli. Mornings are a blur of getting the kids ready, dropping Maya off at the high school bus, and catching the early shift at the warehouse. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from their after‑school program, and try to fit in a quick dinner before homework and bedtime. On Sundays we usually attend the local church service; the community there feels like an extended family, especially for me as a member of the Ojibwe Nation living off the reservation. When I can, I like to fish on the nearby lakes, but lately the bills and the kids' activities leave little spare time.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health care and decent funding for public schools—things that mattered to my kids and my own aging parents. I also liked the emphasis on infrastructure upgrades; the new road projects promised to ease the traffic I deal with every day on my commute. I wasn't thrilled with every policy, but I saw the alternative as a step backward on those basic issues.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax hikes that keep climbing every year. The warehouse job is steady, but wages haven't kept up with inflation, and I worry about the next round of cuts to the school budget that could affect Maya's classes. I think the Democratic Party still has the better ideas for social safety nets, even if the messaging can feel a bit chaotic. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for big corporations than helping families like mine, and their stance on environmental regulations feels like they forget about the lakes we fish in. I'm not a political scholar, but from where I stand, the Democrats are at least trying to address the things that hit my household the hardest.
