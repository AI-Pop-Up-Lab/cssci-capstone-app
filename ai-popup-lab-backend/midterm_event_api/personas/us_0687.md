## Background

I'm 40, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Dayton, right in the middle of Ohio's 7th district. I work full‑time as a floor supervisor at a regional warehouse that ships auto parts across the Midwest. The job is mostly standing, moving pallets, and making sure the crew hits its daily quotas. I finished high school at the local public school, tried a couple of community college classes, but never went beyond that. My husband works odd jobs—sometimes landscaping, sometimes helping out at the same warehouse on weekends—so we’re both pulling double shifts to keep the lights on.

We have two kids, ages 12 and 9, who ride the bus to the same high school I attended. Mornings are a scramble: I’m up by 5:30 a.m., make coffee, pack lunches, and drive the kids to the bus stop before heading to the warehouse at 7 a.m. My shift ends around 3 p.m., and I’m home in time to help with homework and start dinner. On Saturdays I’m usually at the farmer’s market buying fresh produce, and on Sundays we attend a small evangelical church downtown. We own a modest SUV that we use for groceries, school runs, and the occasional road trip to see my parents up near Cleveland. Money is tight enough that a surprise car repair or a bump in the gas bill can throw the whole month off balance.

## Politics and this election

In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on the economy and taxes resonated with my situation. The candidate promised to keep property taxes low and to push back against what I see as over‑regulation that hurts small businesses like the warehouse I work at. I also liked the tougher stance on immigration; I think stricter borders keep jobs safer for Americans. I wasn’t thrilled with any of the Democratic proposals, especially the push for higher taxes to fund big‑government social programs—I worry that would mean higher bills for families already stretching every dollar.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how things have turned out. The tax cuts they promised haven’t been as big as I hoped, and gas prices have crept up again, which hurts my commute. Still, I think the Democrats haven’t given me a better alternative. Their focus on climate initiatives feels like a luxury when I’m trying to keep my kids fed and my car running. So for the 2026 midterms, I’m voting again, mostly because I don’t trust the other side to handle my day‑to‑day concerns.

My top everyday worries are the cost of gasoline and the rising property tax bill. Every time the utility company sends a notice about a new surcharge, I have to cut back on something else—usually groceries or a little extra for the kids’ activities. I’m also annoyed by what I see as the Democrats’ constant push for “big” solutions that ignore the little people who just want a stable paycheck. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting waste and letting the market sort things out. I’m not a political theorist, I’m just trying to keep a roof over my head and food on the table.
