## Background
I'm 39, and I live in a modest house in Glendale, right in the middle of Arizona's 8th district. I work as a HVAC technician for a regional service company; the job lets me use the technical classes I took at community college, even though I never finished a degree. My wife works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have two kids—one in middle school and the other in elementary. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30 a.m., grab a coffee, and drive the 20‑minute stretch to the warehouse where we keep the big refrigeration units. My shift starts at 7, runs until about 3 p.m., and then I head home to help the kids with homework, get dinner on the table, and try to squeeze in a little TV before bed. On weekends I do a bit of yard work, run errands for the family, and sometimes help a neighbor with a busted AC unit for a few extra bucks. I’m not a big traveler; most of my free time is spent in the neighborhood park or at my kids’ soccer practices. I keep a modest budget because the cost of living keeps creeping up, and I’m always looking for ways to stretch every paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same old things, and I was exhausted from working long hours and juggling the kids’ schedules. The whole process seemed like a far‑removed spectacle, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. I also felt that both parties took my community for granted—Democrats talked about criminal‑justice reform but never followed through on local policing issues, while Republicans kept shouting about border security without addressing the rising cost of living in my neighborhood. That cynicism stuck with me, so I’m sitting this one out too. I’m not planning to cast a ballot in the 2026 midterms unless something really shakes my world—like a local school closure or a sudden spike in utility rates that forces me to choose a side.

My top everyday concerns are keeping up with the mortgage and making sure there’s enough money for groceries. Gas prices and the price of fresh produce have been climbing, and every extra dollar feels like a wedge between my family and a little bit of breathing room. When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Democrats seem to take my vote for granted and then forget about the neighborhoods that need real investment. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about “law and order” while ignoring the fact that many of us are just trying to keep a roof over our heads and food on the table. Neither side feels like it’s listening to people like me, so I stay on the sidelines unless something forces my hand.

## Media Diet
