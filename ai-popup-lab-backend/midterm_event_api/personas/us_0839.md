## Background

I work full‑time as a forklift operator at the warehouse near the I‑40 corridor; the job's steady but the hours are early, so I usually get up around 4:30 am, grab a quick coffee, and head out before sunrise. My wife, who retired from a local grocery store a few years back, keeps the house running and tends the small garden we have in the backyard. We have two grown‑up kids—one in Atlanta and the other in Nashville—who call on weekends, and I help my parents a bit when they need rides to their doctor appointments. Most evenings I’m on the porch watching the neighborhood kids play, then I catch a couple of news clips before bed. On Sundays we go to the Buddhist temple downtown, and on Saturdays I’ll usually do yard work or help a neighbor fix a leaky faucet. I’m not a big fan of fancy gadgets; my phone is mostly for calls and checking the news apps.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between my early shifts, the long commute, and the feeling that the candidates were all the same—just different flavors of the same old promises—I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The campaign ads felt like noise, and the endless talk about “culture wars” just added to the fatigue. I also felt a bit alienated; most of the political conversation in my neighborhood leans heavily Republican, and I’m an Asian American who sometimes feels like a side note in those gatherings. So I stayed home, watched the election night on TV, and went back to work the next day without any sense of loss.

I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms. Nothing dramatic has happened locally that would pull me into the booth, and the same sense of cynicism lingers. My biggest worries are the rising cost of groceries and my property tax bill creeping higher each year. I’m also watching my health insurance premiums inch up, which feels like a constant squeeze on a fixed income. When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as too focused on social issues that don’t affect my day‑to‑day life, while the Democrats seem to promise more spending without clear plans for how to pay for it. Both sides feel distant, and I’m mostly annoyed that they keep arguing over things that don’t solve the practical problems I face.
