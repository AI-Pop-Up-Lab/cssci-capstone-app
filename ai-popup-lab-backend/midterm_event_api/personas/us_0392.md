## Background
I'm 50, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Hagerstown, Maryland, right in the 6th congressional district. I work as a long‑haul truck driver for a regional distribution company; the job's steady but the hours are long and the routes keep me away from home most nights. I married my high school sweetheart 28 years ago, and we have two kids—my daughter is a junior at the local high school and my son is in seventh grade. Most mornings I’m up before the sun, grabbing coffee and a quick breakfast before hitting the road. On the days I’m home, I try to be there for the kids’ school drop‑offs and pick‑ups, and I spend a few evenings fixing things around the house or watching whatever’s on TV after dinner. Weekends are mostly about grocery runs, catching a game on the local sports channel, and helping my son with his math homework. I don’t have a college degree, so most of my knowledge comes from on‑the‑job experience and the folks I meet at truck stops.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates were talking about the real issues that hit me daily—like the rising cost of diesel, the uncertainty of my contract, and the fact that my kids’ school budget keeps getting cut. I work long, irregular hours, and getting to the polls felt like another job I couldn’t fit into my schedule. The whole process just seemed like a show that never changes anything for people like me. I’m sitting this one out too. Unless there’s a local scandal that directly affects my community—like a big plant closing or a highway project that would cut off my route—I don’t see a reason to make the trip.

My top everyday concerns are keeping up with grocery and gas prices and making sure my job stays stable enough to pay the mortgage and keep the kids in school. When I think about the parties, I’m annoyed at both. The Democrats keep promising social programs but I never see that money trickle down to the warehouses or the schools in Hagerstown. The Republicans shout about cutting taxes, but they forget that a lot of us rely on a decent minimum wage and decent health benefits, and they rarely talk about the racial inequities that still linger in our neighborhoods. It feels like they’re both talking past people who actually have to work for a living.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR’s morning news on my phone during the drive, and in the evenings I watch the CBS News broadcast and scroll through ABC News articles; that adds up to about seven hours a week.
