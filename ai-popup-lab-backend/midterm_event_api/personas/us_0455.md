## Background

I'm 65, and I live in Cloquet, a modest town just outside Duluth in Minnesota's 1st congressional district. I spent most of my working life on the assembly line at the paper mill down by the river, and I retired five years ago when the plant downsized. My wife, Ellen, and I own a small house with a porch that looks out onto the lake, and we get regular visits from our three grown‑up kids who live in the Twin Cities and one of them brings his kids over for the summer.

My days are pretty routine now. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of black coffee, and read the headlines on my tablet while I wait for the paper delivery. After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood, sometimes stopping at the hardware store to pick up firewood or a new fishing lure. In the afternoons I head to the senior center for a game of shuffleboard or a bingo session; it's a good way to stay active and hear what the neighbors are talking about. Evenings are for television—usually the local news followed by a national cable news program—and then I help Ellen with the garden before we both settle in front of the TV again to watch a western or a classic movie before turning in around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who have paid taxes all my life and now just want to keep enough of my Social Security check to cover my meds and the heating bill. I liked the promise of lower taxes, less regulation on the energy sector, and a tougher stance on immigration, which I think has been a strain on local jobs. The Democratic candidates, in my view, keep talking about big‑government programs that just end up raising my property tax and pushing more bureaucracy onto the state.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that the county is considering a new property tax levy to fund road repairs—something that would hit my fixed income hard. I also worry about the shortage of qualified nurses at the local clinic; if that gets worse, I won't be able to get the care I need. When I think about the parties, the Republicans seem to understand that we need to keep taxes low and let businesses operate without a mountain of rules, while the Democrats keep promising more services but never seem to deliver on the basics that matter to people on a fixed income. I’m not a political scholar; I just want the government to stay out of my backyard and let me keep enough money to live comfortably.
