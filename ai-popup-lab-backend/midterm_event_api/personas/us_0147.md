## Background
I'm 55 and I work as an associate professor of sociology at the University of Delaware. My office is in Newark, just a short drive from the campus, and I spend most weekdays shuttling between my office, the library, and the lecture hall. I teach two classes a semester, grade papers late at night, and try to squeeze in a weekly research meeting with my grad students. My husband, also a professor—he's in the engineering department—shares the house with me, and we both take turns cooking dinner after we get back from the lab or the classroom. Our daughter lives in Philadelphia with her own family, so we get her around for holidays and occasional weekend visits. My mother, who’s in her late seventies, still lives in Wilmington, so I drive up there every other weekend to help with groceries and errands. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the campus cafe, read the headlines on my phone, and hop on the bus to campus. Evenings are usually spent watching a show or scrolling through news feeds while I finish grading. On Sundays I attend a local church service, then head to the farmer’s market to pick up fresh produce, trying to keep a bit of a healthier routine despite the busy schedule.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party better represented my priorities on education funding, criminal justice reform, and health care access. As a professor, I see firsthand how underfunded public schools and rising tuition hurt students, and the Democratic platform promised to protect programs that keep higher education affordable. I also appreciated the candidate’s focus on expanding Medicaid in Delaware, which matters to my mother and many of my older relatives. Since then, I’ve watched the administration push through some of those promises, but I’m also frustrated that progress feels slow and that partisan gridlock keeps many issues stalled. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the best bet for moving things forward, even if I’m annoyed by the occasional infighting and the way some of the rhetoric feels disconnected from everyday folks like me.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of health care—my mother’s prescription bills keep climbing—and the property tax hikes in Newark, which are making it harder to afford our home. I see the Republican Party as largely out of touch with the needs of working‑class and minority communities; their tax proposals often seem to favor the wealthy and they rarely address the systemic inequities I deal with in my research. The Democrats, while not perfect, at least talk about expanding health coverage and investing in public education, which aligns with what I see as essential. I’m not a fan of the political theater, but I’ll keep supporting the party that, in my view, is more likely to push for policies that help families like mine.
