## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a small house on the edge of Lacey, just a few miles from the state capital. I never finished high school; I dropped out after my sophomore year to help my parents run the family fish market in Tacoma. I spent most of my adult life working long, cold shifts on the dock, loading and unloading crates, and later as a maintenance hand at a local senior community. I retired about five years ago when the market shut down, but I still do a bit of odd‑job work—fixing leaky faucets, hauling firewood for neighbors—just to stay busy.

My wife, Mei, and I have been together for 45 years. We don't have any kids at home; our two daughters moved to Seattle and Portland after college, and we see them a few times a year. Most of my days start with a cup of strong black coffee on the porch while the neighborhood kids ride their bikes past. I tend the garden behind the house—tomatoes, kale, a few herb pots—because fresh veggies keep the grocery bill down. In the afternoons I head to the senior center for bingo, a quick walk around the pond, or a woodworking class where I carve small wooden toys for my grandkids. Evenings are usually spent watching TV, checking the news, or scrolling through a few online forums before turning in early; I need my sleep, especially with the arthritis acting up.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate promised to keep taxes low and protect the kind of small‑business jobs that people like me used to have. I was also drawn to the tough talk on immigration and law‑and‑order; after seeing a few incidents near the waterfront, I felt the country needed a firmer hand. The Democratic side seemed too focused on big‑city issues and climate regulations that, to me, felt like they would hurt the fishing and timber industries that built this region.

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. I don't trust the system enough to sit out, but I'm not thrilled about any of the choices either. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the looming property tax hike the county is proposing to fund new school projects. If the taxes go up, I might have to dip into my modest savings, and that's scary at my age. The other thing that keeps me up is the talk about cutting Social Security benefits; I rely on that income for most of my living expenses.

When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who at least understand the struggles of working‑class folks in places like Tacoma and Lacey. They talk about keeping the cost of living down and not over‑regulating small businesses. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more interested in pushing progressive social agendas and climate policies that feel disconnected from the day‑to‑day reality of people who have to pay for groceries and medical bills. I know both sides have their flaws, but right now I'm leaning toward whoever promises to keep my taxes low and protect my Medicare.
