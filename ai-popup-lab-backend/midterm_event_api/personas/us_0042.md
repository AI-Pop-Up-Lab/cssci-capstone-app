## Background  
I'm 35 and I live in Hope, a small town that sits on the edge of Arkansas's 4th district. I work full‑time as a senior data analyst for a regional nonprofit that runs the local community health clinic. My day starts around 6 a.m. with a quick jog around the town square, then a drive to the clinic where I spend eight hours pulling together reports on patient outcomes and grant performance. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from after‑school soccer, and help my husband with his hardware store paperwork. My wife (we're married) runs a small bakery out of our house, and we have a six‑year‑old daughter who’s in second grade. My mother‑in‑law lives in the next town over and drops by for Sunday dinner most weeks. On weekends I usually help out at the church bake sale, run errands at the farmer’s market, and try to catch a movie at the old theater downtown. Most of my free time is spent on my laptop, either tweaking dashboards for work or scrolling through recipe videos for our bakery.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s economic message resonated with my household. My husband’s store was struggling with rising supply costs, and the candidate’s promises to cut taxes on small businesses seemed like something that could actually help us keep the bakery afloat. I also appreciated the tougher stance on the southern border; my family has relatives in California and we’ve heard stories about the strain on resources back home. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises have played out. The tax cuts did give us a tiny bit of breathing room, but the price of groceries and especially prescription drugs keeps climbing, which is frustrating. I’m still convinced that a government that stays out of the way of entrepreneurs is the best path for my family, so I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms.  

My top everyday concerns are the cost of groceries and the rising premiums for health insurance. Those two things eat up most of our budget every month, and I’m always looking for any relief. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who at least try to keep taxes low and let private enterprise do its thing, even if they sometimes ignore social issues that don’t affect me directly. The Democrats, in my view, are more focused on identity politics and big‑government programs that feel disconnected from the daily grind of a small‑town family like mine. I’m not a fan of either side’s rhetoric, but I lean toward the GOP because they seem to care more about the bottom line.
