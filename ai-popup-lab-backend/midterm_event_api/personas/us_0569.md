## Background
I'm 25 and work as an administrative assistant at a medical clinic in Bellevue, just outside Omaha. I grew up in a tight‑knit Asian‑American family that moved here when I was a kid, so most of my relatives still live in the suburbs, and we get together for big holidays. I dropped out after two years of community college to take the job that paid enough for me to move out of my parents' house. My roommate, a fellow student from the same community college, shares the two‑bedroom apartment we rent on a modest budget.

My day starts at 6 am with a quick run to the corner coffee shop for a latte and a bagel, then a 20‑minute drive to the clinic where I spend the next eight hours filing paperwork, answering phones, and coordinating appointments. I usually grab lunch at the cafeteria, but sometimes I bring a home‑cooked bento box from the freezer. After work I either hit the gym for a half‑hour or walk the dog for my roommate’s sister who lives nearby. Evenings are a mix of streaming a sitcom, catching up on news, and doing the dishes before I’m in bed by around 11. On weekends I help my mom with grocery shopping at the local market and occasionally volunteer at the community center teaching English to new immigrants.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because the campaign talked about expanding Medicaid in Nebraska and protecting funding for community health centers—things that mattered to me at work. I also liked the candidate’s stance on student loan forgiveness; I still have a handful of loans left, and that relief would have eased a lot of stress. The campaign felt a bit chaotic, but the outreach in my district, especially the door‑to‑door canvassing in Bellevue, made me feel heard.

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of rent and grocery prices, which have squeezed my paycheck more than I expected. I’m also watching the local job market; the clinic has been talking about possible cuts if state funding dries up. I think the Democratic Party generally pushes policies that help people like me—more health coverage, some rent assistance, and a push for higher minimum wages. But I get annoyed when the party seems more focused on national culture wars than on practical economic issues. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about tax cuts and deregulation, which sounds good until I see the impact on public services and on the cost of living in my neighborhood. I feel they’re out of touch with the everyday struggles of renters and low‑income workers.

## Media Diet
