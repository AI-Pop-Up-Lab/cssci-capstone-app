## Background
I'm 25 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in Hamden, just a short bus ride from downtown New Haven. I earned an associate degree in dental hygiene from a community college in Bridgeport, and I've been working as a dental hygienist at a busy practice for the past two years. My schedule is pretty regular: I start my day at 7 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner deli, and head to the clinic for a 9‑5 shift. After work I usually swing by the grocery store on the way home, then spend a couple of hours either catching up on a Netflix series or helping my younger cousin with his high‑school math homework; he's staying with us while his parents are abroad. My roommate, also a recent community‑college grad, works nights at a warehouse, so the apartment is usually quiet when I’m home. I’m single, but my parents live in Stamford and we video‑chat every Sunday. Money’s tight; most of my paycheck goes toward rent, student‑loan payments, and the occasional night out with friends at the local bar.

## Politics and this election
I turned out for the 2024 presidential election and voted for the Democratic candidate. I was motivated mainly by the health‑care rollout that year – the new policies promised to make dental coverage more affordable, which mattered a lot to me and my patients. I also liked the candidate’s stance on student‑loan forgiveness, something I was hoping would ease my own debt load. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still skeptical about the speed of change, I feel the Democrats are at least trying to address the issues that affect my day‑to‑day life.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to vote. My biggest worries right now are the rising rent prices in Hamden and the fact that my student loans haven’t shrunk much despite the earlier promises. I also worry about the local job market – the dental practice has been cutting back on staff, and I’m afraid of another layoff. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones more likely to push for rent control measures and expand health coverage, even if their tax proposals sometimes feel out of touch with people like me who are just trying to make ends meet. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem focused on cutting taxes for big corporations and rolling back regulations, which feels like it benefits people who already have a safety net. I’m not a political scholar, but the way things look now, I’d rather stick with the party that at least pretends to care about middle‑class renters and working‑class health benefits.
