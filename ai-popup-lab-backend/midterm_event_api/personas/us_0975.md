## Background
I'm 74 and I live in Morgantown, right near the WVU campus in the 1st district. I spent most of my career as a research engineer for a defense contractor in the Pittsburgh area, then moved back to West Virginia when I took a part‑time adjunct teaching gig in the university’s engineering department. I retired three years ago, so now I mostly keep busy with a few things: a morning walk with my old Labrador, checking the mail, and a weekly volunteer shift at the senior center where I help run a computer basics class. My wife passed away five years ago, and my two kids live on opposite coasts—one in Seattle, one in Boston—so most evenings I’m on video chat with them, trying not to miss birthdays. I own a modest house with a small garden; I grow a few tomatoes and peppers, which keeps me out of the kitchen a bit. My sister lives nearby and we split grocery trips when her health gets rough. I try to keep a regular schedule: coffee and the news at 7 a.m., a light breakfast, then a walk, then a few hours of reading or tinkering with old circuit boards before lunch. In the afternoons I might attend a book club or help a neighbor with yard work. The routine is simple, and I like that I’m not rushing around anymore.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old script and none of the candidates seemed to understand what it’s like to be an older person on a fixed income. Between caring for my sister, managing my health issues, and the endless stream of campaign ads, I just tuned everything out. The whole process felt like a circus, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. I’m sitting this one out, too. The midterms don’t look any different—no scandal that would pull me back in, and I’m still skeptical that any candidate will address the day‑to‑day stuff that matters to me. My biggest worries right now are the cost of prescription drugs and the rising price of groceries; I’m also watching property tax bills creep up as the county tries to fund new infrastructure. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to talk a lot about social programs but rarely get the details right for seniors, while the Republicans keep promising lower taxes but then push for cuts that would hit community services I rely on. Both sides feel distant, and I’m not convinced either is listening to folks like me who just want affordable health care and a stable cost of living.
