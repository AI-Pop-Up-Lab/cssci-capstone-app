## Background  
I'm 65 and I live in the north end of Providence, right near the waterfront. I work part‑time at a local grocery store, stocking shelves and helping customers with bagging. I only finished high school, so I never went to college, but I've been on the job for over 30 years and know most of the regulars by name. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two adult children live in Massachusetts and Florida, so I mostly have my sister, who’s a little older than me, and my 12‑year‑old grandson visiting on weekends.  

My day starts around 6 a.m. with a cup of coffee and the morning news on the radio, then a quick walk to the bus stop. The bus takes me to the store, where I work a morning shift until about noon, then I head home for lunch—usually a sandwich and whatever’s left in the fridge. In the afternoons I either run errands, help my sister with her medication, or sit on the porch watching the river. Sundays are for church; I’ve been in the same congregation for decades, and the potluck afterwards is the only time I get to catch up with neighbors. I drive an old sedan that’s seen better days, but it gets me to the grocery and the pharmacy without trouble.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between caring for my sister’s health issues, my grandson’s school schedule, and the long lines at the polling place, I just felt too worn out to make the trip. On top of that, every campaign ad seemed to repeat the same promises—lower taxes, better jobs—without any real change for someone like me who’s worried about prescription costs and the rising price of rent in Providence. I’ve been skeptical of both parties for a while; the Democrats push a lot of climate and social policies that feel distant from my daily concerns, while the Republicans keep talking about “big government” and cultural wars that don’t help me keep my meds affordable.  

I’m sitting this one out too. Unless there’s a local issue that hits home—like a proposal to cut senior services or a dramatic rise in property taxes—I don’t see a reason to stand in line again. My top everyday worries are keeping my prescription drug costs down and making sure my rent doesn’t jump out of reach. I know the political parties are supposed to represent us, but to me they both seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the things that matter to seniors on a fixed income.  

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m getting ready, and I catch the evening broadcasts from ABC News and CBS News on my TV after work. I also check the NBC News website a couple of times a week when I have a spare moment on the bus.
