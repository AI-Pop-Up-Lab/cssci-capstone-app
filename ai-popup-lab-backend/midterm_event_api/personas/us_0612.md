## Background  
I'm 65, and I live in Mays Landing, a small town in Atlantic County that falls in NJ‑1. I spent most of my career as an aerospace engineer for a defense contractor, and after I retired a few years ago I picked up a part‑time gig teaching introductory engineering courses at the local community college. My wife, Denise, works part‑time at a nearby pharmacy, and our two grown kids live in Philadelphia and Baltimore. We have one grandkid who visits every summer and keeps us on our toes.  

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6:30 am, make a cup of coffee, and scroll through the NPR morning news feed while I walk our old Labrador, Buddy, around the neighborhood. After breakfast I spend a couple of hours checking email, grading a few student assignments, and sometimes meeting with a local nonprofit that runs a STEM‑after‑school program. Around lunch I head to the college campus, where I lecture for an hour and then sit in the faculty lounge chatting about the latest research or the weather. In the evenings I usually cook a simple dinner, watch a news segment on ABC, and read a few articles on Newsweek before bed. On weekends I help out at the church food pantry, tend to my garden, and occasionally meet up with old friends at the local diner for a slice of pie.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really looking out for seniors like me. Social Security has been a lifeline, and the Democratic platform promised to protect and even expand those benefits. I also appreciated the focus on expanding Medicare to cover more prescription drugs—something my wife and I have been worrying about as the cost of insulin keeps climbing. The candidate’s stance on criminal justice reform resonated, too; after seeing how the system still hurts my community, I wanted change.  

Since then, I’ve watched the political conversation get louder and more polarized. I still think the Democrats are the better bet for protecting the safety net, but I’m a bit frustrated that they haven’t moved faster on affordable housing and the rising property taxes that are squeezing retirees on fixed incomes. I’ve also heard a lot of talk from friends about the GOP’s tax cuts for the wealthy, which feels like a slap in the face when I’m watching my pension check get smaller each year.  

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are keeping Social Security and Medicare intact, and making sure property taxes don’t keep climbing out of reach for seniors on a fixed income. I’m also watching how the parties handle the local job market; even though I’m retired, my kids are looking for stable work in the area, and the lack of good-paying jobs worries me. I see the Democratic Party as the one more likely to fight for those issues, while I view the Republicans as more focused on tax cuts for corporations and less on the day‑to‑day struggles of folks like me.
