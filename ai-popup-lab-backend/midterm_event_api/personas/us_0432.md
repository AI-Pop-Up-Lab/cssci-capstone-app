## Background
I'm 84 and I live in the small community of Wyoming, just outside Grand Rapids in Michigan's 3rd District. I spent most of my adult life as a high school history teacher, so I have a college degree and a love of stories, even if I’m not much for the internet. My wife passed away ten years ago, and my two kids live in Detroit and Lansing. I get visits from a handful of grandkids every few months, and they keep me honest about technology. Most days I get up around 7 a.m., brew a pot of coffee, and flip on the local CBS affiliate for the morning news. After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood—my knees aren’t what they used to be, but the fresh air helps. I spend a couple of hours at the senior center where we play cards, watch classic movies, and sometimes hear a guest speaker on topics like Medicare. In the afternoons I tend to my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few roses—then I sit at the kitchen table and read the paper or scroll through a couple of news sites on my tablet before dinner. Evenings are usually quiet: a TV dinner, a rerun of an old sitcom, and maybe a late‑night news segment before I head to bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for retirees like me. They talked about protecting Social Security, keeping taxes low, and standing up for “hard‑working” Americans, which resonated after seeing the cost of my prescription meds climb. I don’t follow every campaign promise, but I appreciated the emphasis on fiscal restraint and the sense that the GOP remembered the older generation. That said, I’m not a political scholar; I just listen to what feels right for my pocketbook and my values.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. I have a ballot in my kitchen drawer and I plan to cast it at the community center on Tuesday. My biggest worries right now are the rising price of groceries and the uncertainty around Medicare coverage for new treatments. The other thing that keeps me up at night is the property tax hike the county proposed last spring; I’m on a fixed income and any extra bill feels like a punch in the gut. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who still remember the promises they made to seniors, even if they argue a bit too loudly sometimes. The Democrats, in my view, are well‑meaning but seem more focused on climate talks and cultural issues that don’t affect my day‑to‑day life. I’m not a fan of the constant partisan bickering; I just want policies that let me keep my home, my meds, and my garden.
