## Background
I'm 26 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in Paterson, right in the heart of New Jersey’s 5th congressional district. After finishing high school I took a job at the local supermarket as a checkout clerk and later picked up a night shift at a nearby warehouse to help with rent. My parents still live in a small house in Edison, so I visit them on weekends and bring home a few groceries for them. I share the apartment with two other roommates—one is a college student studying nursing, the other is a recent immigrant who works as a housekeeper. Most mornings I’m up by 6 am, get a quick coffee, and catch the train into the city for my first shift. After work I usually swing by the laundromat, grab dinner from the food court, and try to squeeze in a little TV before bed. On my days off I like to walk the Passaic River trail, meet friends at the local coffee shop, and scroll through my phone when I have a spare moment. I don’t drive; the bus and train are my main ways to get around. My biggest financial worry is keeping up with the rent and the rising cost of groceries, especially since I’m still paying off a small credit‑card balance from school supplies I bought for my younger cousins.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because the campaign emphasized protecting my community’s public schools and expanding health care options, which felt relevant to my family’s situation. My mom’s been dealing with a chronic illness, and the promise of more affordable prescription coverage hit home. I also liked the candidate’s stance on immigration, since a lot of my friends and neighbors are immigrants who come here for work. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on the news, but I’m not glued to politics. The rising cost of rent and the price of fresh produce have been on my mind more than any national debate. The Republican Party feels out of touch with my daily reality—most of their talking points seem aimed at big‑ticket issues that don’t affect a grocery‑store clerk trying to stretch a paycheck. The Democrats, while not perfect, at least talk about a living wage and tenant protections, which matter to me. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I plan to vote for the candidate who promises to keep rent control measures and push for more community health centers. My top concerns right now are staying afloat on my wages and making sure my parents don’t have to choose between medication and groceries.
