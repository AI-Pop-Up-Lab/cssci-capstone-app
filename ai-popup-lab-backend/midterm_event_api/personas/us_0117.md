## Background  
I'm 50, and I live in Greeley, right on the edge of the 4th District. I work as a foreman for a regional construction firm; I didn’t finish my associate’s degree in civil engineering, but I learned a lot on the job. My wife runs a small boutique that sells handmade pottery, and we have two kids—Mia, 17, who’s gearing up for college applications, and Lucas, 14, who spends most of his free time on his bike. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30, make coffee, and grab a quick breakfast of eggs and toast while scrolling through the news on my phone. I drive the 30‑minute commute to the job site, where I supervise crews, check blueprints, and make sure safety rules are followed. After work I usually swing by the hardware store for supplies, then pick up the kids from soccer practice or help Lucas with his math homework. Evenings are a mix of family dinner, watching a game on the TV, and sometimes a late‑night shift on the boutique’s website. Weekends are a blur of house‑cleaning, a trip to the farmer’s market, and the occasional hike in the foothills with the family.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I was convinced that the climate‑action plan and the push for expanding Medicaid would actually help people like my wife, whose boutique relies on a healthy local economy, and my kids, who will inherit the cost of healthcare. I also liked the candidate’s stance on infrastructure—more road repairs meant fewer delays for my crew. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and I’m still leaning Democratic, though I’m a bit annoyed that some promises have stalled. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms; I feel it’s my civic duty, especially with the upcoming congressional race that could affect funding for local schools and road projects. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rising property taxes that are squeezing our budget and the price of gasoline, which makes the commute and the kids’ trips to school more expensive. I see the Democratic Party as generally more supportive of the social safety net and climate initiatives, but I’m frustrated by what I see as endless internal fighting. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem focused on cutting taxes for big businesses and rolling back regulations, which I worry will hurt the construction industry’s safety standards and make it harder for small businesses like my wife’s to thrive. I’m not a political scholar, just someone trying to keep a roof over our heads and a decent life for my family.
