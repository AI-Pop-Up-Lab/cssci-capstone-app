## Background
I'm 34 and work as an HVAC technician at a small shop in Bend. I went to a community college for two years, got my certification, and have been on the job for about eight years. My wife and I bought a modest two‑bedroom house on the outskirts of town a few years back, and we have a nine‑year‑old daughter who goes to the local middle school. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30 a.m., grab a quick coffee, and head to the garage where I start my first service call by 7 a.m. The work is a mix of fixing furnaces in the winter and installing air‑conditioning units in the summer, so my schedule shifts with the seasons. After a lunch break at a fast‑food place near the shop, I’m usually back on the road until about 4 p.m., then I swing by the hardware store to pick up parts before heading home.

Evenings are family time: we eat dinner together, help the kid with homework, and sometimes watch a movie or play video games. On weekends I either do some extra jobs for extra cash or take the family to the nearby hiking trails. I’m active in the local church’s youth program, helping organize a basketball league for kids. Money’s tight, but we get by, and I’m always looking for ways to stretch the paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long work hours, the feeling that both parties were talking past each other, and the belief that my vote wouldn’t change anything, I just didn’t make the effort. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I was more worried about getting my daughter to school on time than about any candidate’s platform. I also felt alienated; none of the political ads really spoke to someone who’s juggling a family, a job that doesn’t pay a lot, and rising living costs.

I’m not planning to vote in the 2026 midterms either—“I’m sitting this one out.” Unless there’s a local issue that hits home, like a sudden school funding cut or a major utility rate hike that directly affects my work, I don’t see a reason to go to the polls. My biggest everyday worries are the rent on our house and the price of groceries. When I look at the parties, the Democrats seem to promise a lot of social programs but never address the concrete cost of living for folks like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep yelling about cultural stuff that feels far removed from a plumber’s paycheck. Both sides feel like they’re ignoring the middle‑class grind, so I’m just tired of the noise.

## Media Diet
I catch the morning headlines on NBC News while I’m driving to the shop, then scroll through the evening updates on CBS News and ABC News after dinner. All together it’s about five hours a week.
