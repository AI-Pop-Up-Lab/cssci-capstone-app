## Background

I'm 80, and I live in a modest ranch house on the edge of Lubbock, right in Texas's 29th district. I spent most of my working life driving long hauls for a regional trucking company; I never went to college, just a high school diploma and a lot of miles on the highway. I retired about ten years ago after a back injury forced me off the road. My wife, who’s a few years younger, keeps the garden going and still tends to the chickens we raise for eggs. Our two kids live out of state—one in Austin, the other in Dallas—so we get their calls mostly on Sundays.

My days are simple. I get up early, make a strong cup of coffee, and sit on the porch while the sun climbs over the flat prairie. After a quick walk to check the fences, I spend a couple of hours watching the morning news on TV, then I’m off to the shed fixing the old tractor or chopping wood for the winter. Lunch is usually a sandwich and whatever the garden has produced. In the afternoon I might drive to the local senior center for bingo or a card game, or I’ll help my wife with the grocery list. Evenings are for the television—news, a western movie, or the occasional sports game—before we turn in around ten. I try to stay active, but the aches in my knees remind me I’m not as spry as I used to be.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in 2024. By the time the primaries rolled around I was still dealing with a bout of pneumonia, and the whole thing felt like a circus—candidates shouting at each other, endless ads, and none of them seemed to address the real problems I face, like rising prescription costs and the fact that my fixed income barely stretches to cover the property tax bill. I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything, and I was too sick and tired to bother with the endless paperwork and long lines.

That feeling hasn’t really shifted. I’m still skeptical that any of the big parties are listening to folks like me. The Democrats feel like they’re pushing ideas that belong in a city I’ve never lived in, while the Republicans keep rehashing the same promises about “lower taxes” that never seem to stick around long enough for my retirement to feel secure. I’m not convinced that either side has a plan that will keep my medicine affordable or stop the grocery prices from creeping higher every month. So for the 2026 midterms I’m sitting this one out, too—unless something huge hits the local community, like a major hospital closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces us out of the house.

My top everyday concerns are keeping up with medical expenses and making sure the grocery bill doesn’t blow past what my Social Security check can cover. I’m also watching the property tax assessments; any increase feels like a direct hit to my savings. As for the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with both. The Democrats seem out of touch with small‑town life, and the Republicans talk a lot about “change” but the change never reaches my mailbox.

## Media Diet

I tune into CBS News, ABC News, and NBC News each evening after dinner, usually catching the headlines while I’m scrolling through my tablet. I spend about seven hours a week with those outlets—half in the mornings with the local feed and the rest spread across the evenings.
