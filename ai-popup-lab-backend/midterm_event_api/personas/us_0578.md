## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Bellevue, a modest suburb on the east side of Omaha. I work as a lead mechanic at a family‑owned auto shop that's been around for three generations. Most days I get there before sunrise, grab a coffee from the corner donut shop, and spend the morning swapping out brake pads and diagnosing engine trouble. The shop is busy enough that I often stay late, especially when a customer needs a quick fix before heading out of town.

My wife, who used to teach at a local community college, retired a few years ago, so we spend a lot of our evenings watching the game or fixing up the spare bedroom we turned into a guest room. We have two grown kids—one lives in California and the other is back here, working as a nurse. My parents are still in the U.S., living in a senior community near Lincoln, and we help them out with groceries and doctor appointments when we can.

On weekends I like to take the truck out to the lake, do a bit of fishing, and meet up with a few buddies at the bar for a cold beer. I also volunteer once a month at the local food pantry, mostly because it's what the shop owner encourages us to do. Money is tight enough that I keep a close eye on the budget, but I’m not living paycheck to paycheck; the shop does decent enough business to keep the lights on.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping taxes low for small business owners like the shop I work at. The candidate promised to cut back on regulations that make it harder for mechanics to get parts quickly, and that resonated with my day‑to‑day grind. I also liked the emphasis on a strong economy and the idea that the government should stay out of the way of folks who are just trying to make an honest living.

Since then, I’ve gotten more annoyed by the rising cost of health insurance for my wife and the property tax hikes that keep climbing on our home. Those issues feel personal, and they’re the main things on my mind when I think about voting. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms—nothing's going to stop me unless I’m stuck in the shop all day. My top concerns are health‑care premiums and property taxes; if the candidates can show a real plan to ease those, I’ll stick with whoever seems most realistic.

I see the GOP as the party that understands small‑business owners and keeps the government from overreaching. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for higher taxes and more regulation, which feels like a threat to the shop’s bottom line. I’m not a fan of the cultural stuff they get into on TV, and I think a lot of their policies just end up hurting folks like me who are trying to stay afloat.
