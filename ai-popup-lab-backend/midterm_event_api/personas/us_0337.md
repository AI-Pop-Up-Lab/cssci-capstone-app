## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Catlettsburg, right in the middle of Kentucky's 6th district. I spent most of my adult life working as a coal miner before the mines shut down in the early 2000s. After that I picked up odd jobs—some carpentry, a stint as a security guard at the local high school, and later part‑time work at the county's Department of Motor Vehicles. I retired officially at 63, but I still help my neighbor fix things around his property and I volunteer once a month at the senior center, teaching basic computer skills.

My wife passed away three years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live a few hours away in Louisville and Lexington. I see my son every other weekend and my daughter calls me almost daily; the kids I have now are teens, and they stay with me during the school year, so the house feels busy again. My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee while I read the paper, then I take the dog for a walk around the cul‑de‑sac. After that I tend to the garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few fruit trees—because fresh produce helps stretch the grocery budget. I attend the local Baptist church on Sundays, and on Tuesdays I help run the community bingo night, which is a good way to stay connected and pick up a little extra cash. Most evenings I sit on the porch and watch the news or listen to talk radio before turning in around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best represented my concerns about the economy and cultural changes that have been happening fast around me. I remember the campaign ads that talked about protecting coal jobs, lowering taxes, and standing up for traditional values—things that resonated with a man who spent his life working the mines and raising a family the old‑fashioned way. I didn't feel the Democrats were listening to folks like me; their messages seemed focused on big cities and issues that felt distant from my daily reality.

Now, heading into the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. I think the Republican candidates are still the ones who will push for lower property taxes, keep the gas prices from spiking, and support rural broadband projects that could bring better jobs to our area. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs—my blood pressure meds have gotten pricier each year—and the fact that my internet connection is still spotty, which makes it hard for my grandchildren to keep up with schoolwork when they visit. I also worry about the local job market; the mine closures left a lot of us without steady work, and I see fewer opportunities for younger people staying in the town.

When I think about the parties, I view the Republicans as the ones who understand the kind of life I lead: hard work, self‑reliance, and a respect for tradition. The Democrats, in my view, are more interested in progressive policies that seem to ignore the challenges of small‑town Kentucky. I don't claim to be an expert on every issue, but I feel more comfortable with the party that talks about protecting the things I care about, even if they sometimes get a bit loud on TV.
