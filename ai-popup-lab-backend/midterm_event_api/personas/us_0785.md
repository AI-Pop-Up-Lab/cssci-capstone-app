## Background
I'm 74 and I live in a quiet suburb of Spartanburg, South Carolina, right in the heart of the 5th congressional district. I spent thirty‑four years as a public‑school teacher, mostly teaching Spanish and social studies at the middle school level, before I retired five years ago. My husband passed away a decade back, so these days the house is just me, my two grown‑up children who live in Charlotte and Atlanta, and the occasional weekend visit from my three grandkids. My mornings start with a cup of coffee on the porch while I look over the garden—tomatoes, basil, a few roses. After that I usually check the mail, water the plants, and head to the local grocery store for a quick run. I volunteer at the church’s food pantry twice a week, and on Tuesdays I sit in the community center’s computer room to help seniors learn how to use email. Evenings are low‑key: a bit of TV, a crossword puzzle, and sometimes a video call with my daughter. I keep a small collection of paperback romances and a stack of classic mystery novels that I read whenever the house is quiet.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party best protected the values I grew up with—strong national defense, lower taxes, and a respect for traditional family structures. I was also reassured by the candidate’s promises to keep school choice options open, something I still care about even though I’m retired. The campaign ads on the news I watch reminded me that a stable economy meant I could keep my modest pension and not see my grocery bills spike. Since then, I’ve stayed fairly consistent. I’m definitely going to the polls again for the 2026 midterms; I don’t see any reason to sit this one out unless there’s a major scandal. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes the county has been discussing. I also hear a lot about a possible factory closure nearby, which would affect a lot of neighbors who still work in manufacturing.

When I think about the parties, the Republicans still feel like the ones who understand my need for fiscal restraint and who respect the way I was raised. I do get annoyed when they argue among themselves or when they ignore the concerns of older folks like me. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to want bigger government programs and higher taxes, which I worry will squeeze my fixed income. I also hear a lot about their stance on immigration, and while I’m a Hispanic myself, I’m uneasy about the idea of open borders because I believe in law and order and a structured process.
