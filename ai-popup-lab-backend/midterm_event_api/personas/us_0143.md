## Background  
I'm 20 and I'm a sophomore at the University of Delaware, majoring in political science. I live in a small two‑bedroom apartment in Newark with a roommate who studies engineering. My parents own a house in Dover, so I fly home for holidays and Thanksgiving, but most weeks I'm on my own.  

My days start early: I grab a coffee at the campus café around 7 am, then head to my first class—American government. Between 10 and 12 I have a lab for statistics, which is the only thing I actually enjoy. After lunch I work a four‑hour shift at a nearby coffee shop to help pay rent; the manager is cool but the tip line is always thin. Evenings are a mix of study sessions, Zoom meetings for a student organization that pushes voter registration, and scrolling through social media while watching a few episodes of a comedy series. On weekends I either do a shift at the shop, meet friends at the local park, or volunteer at a community garden. My biggest worry right now is keeping up with tuition and making sure I can afford the $1,200 a month rent.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about student debt relief and climate action—things that hit me daily. My dad talked a lot about the economy, but the candidate’s plan to cut tuition and expand renewable energy jobs in Delaware felt more relevant to my life than the talk about tax cuts. I also liked the way the campaign used Instagram livestreams to talk about mental‑health funding for campuses; it felt like they were actually listening to people my age.  

Since then, I've kept an eye on what both parties are doing. The Democrats have pushed a modest increase in Pell Grants, which helped a few classmates, but the Senate's budget talks are still a mess and I hear more talk about “inflation” than concrete relief. The Republicans keep coming out with anti‑abortion rhetoric and push for cutting carbon regulations, which feels like they're ignoring the future I want. That said, I do appreciate when a GOP senator from my district talks about improving broadband in rural parts of DE‑1—people need good internet for school and work.  

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and seeing my tuition not skyrocket next year. I plan to vote for the Democratic candidate for Senate because I think they’re still more likely to fight student debt and push for clean‑energy jobs, even if they’re not perfect. I’m pretty annoyed with the Republican side; they seem more interested in cultural battles than in solving the cost‑of‑living crisis that hits me and my friends.
