## Background
I'm 34 and I work as a community liaison for the Navajo Nation's health services office in Farmington, NM. My job is mostly paperwork and field visits—checking on Medicaid enrollment, coordinating mobile clinics, and helping elders get transportation to the regional hospital. I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of town with my husband, who does construction work, and our two kids, ages 7 and 10. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 to make coffee, get the kids dressed, and drop them off at the elementary school they both attend. After the school run I head to the health office, where I spend the bulk of my day on a computer, fielding calls from tribal members and sometimes driving out to the reservation to meet with community leaders. Evenings are a mix of helping the kids with homework, cooking dinner from whatever’s on sale at the local grocery, and catching a quick news segment before I finally crawl into bed around 10. On weekends I’m either at a church potluck, helping with a family barbecue, or doing yard work when the weather’s nice.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on border security and economic development was the only thing keeping the jobs that my husband relies on from disappearing. The candidate promised to cut red tape for small contractors and to support water projects that are critical for farming families like ours. I also appreciated the emphasis on “tribal sovereignty” that was framed as a partnership rather than a handout. Since then, I’ve stayed pretty consistent—my concerns have shifted a bit toward the rising price of groceries and the fact that the water infrastructure upgrades are moving slower than promised. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I don’t trust the Democrats to understand rural life or the importance of keeping the border tight. My top everyday worries are keeping food on the table when gas prices keep spiking and making sure the water we depend on for our garden and livestock stays clean and reliable. I think the Republican Party, for all its flaws, is the one that talks about jobs, borders, and giving us a say in how federal funds are spent on our land. The Democrats seem more focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that feel disconnected from the reality of living out here.
