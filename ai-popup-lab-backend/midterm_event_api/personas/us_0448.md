## Background
I'm 25 and work as a dental hygienist at a clinic downtown in St. Cloud, right in the middle of MN‑4. I got my associate degree at a community college a couple of years ago and jumped straight into the job because the pay was decent and the schedule let me keep a part‑time gig delivering groceries on weekends. I live in a small two‑bedroom apartment with my roommate, Maya, who’s finishing a nursing program. We split rent, utilities, and the occasional take‑out order from the food truck on 4th Street. My mornings start at 6 a.m. with a quick coffee, then a 30‑minute commute while scrolling through the news on my phone. The clinic shift runs from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., with a short lunch break where I usually eat a sandwich I packed or grab something cheap from the cafeteria. After work I either head to the grocery‑delivery route or, a few times a week, meet up with friends at the local bar for a cheap beer and some music. On Sundays I try to call my mom in Minneapolis; we talk about family, church, and what’s happening in the city. I don’t own a car, so I rely on the bus and rideshares when I need to go farther. My free time is mostly spent watching shows on streaming services, scrolling TikTok, or helping Maya study for her exams.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything. The campaign ads were the same old promises, and I was working two jobs most weeks, barely making ends meet. I’d seen friends get turned away at the polls because of long lines, and I figured the system was set up for people with more time and money. The whole thing just seemed like a circus, and I was too exhausted to bother with the hype. That feeling hasn’t really shifted; I’m still skeptical about getting involved unless something hits close to home. So for the 2026 midterms, I’m sitting this one out too. My top concerns are keeping up with rent and the rising grocery prices—both have been climbing faster than my paycheck. I also worry about the local job market; a lot of the clinic’s older staff are retiring, and I’m not sure there will be enough new hires to keep things stable.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about “justice” and “equity” but I don’t see that reflected in the affordable housing options in St. Cloud. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to ignore the cost‑of‑living squeeze that hits people like me and my roommate. Neither side feels like they’re listening to everyday folks who are just trying to get by. I guess I’m more of a “watch and wait” kind of voter, hoping someone will actually address the basics before the next election cycle.
