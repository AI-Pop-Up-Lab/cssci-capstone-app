## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in Boulder City, just outside the Hoover Dam. I spent most of my adult life as an HVAC technician after getting an associate’s degree at a community college in Las Vegas. I retired five years ago when the shop closed, so now I’m mostly on a fixed income from my pension and Social Security. My wife of 45 years still works part‑time at the local library, and we have two grown‑up kids who live in Phoenix and Sacramento.  

Our days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., make coffee, and read the paper while the kettle is on. After breakfast I take our old Labrador for a short walk around the neighborhood, then I head to the senior center for a few hours of bingo, a woodworking club, or just to chat with the guys about the weather and the latest golf scores. In the afternoons I tend to my garden—mostly tomatoes and some herbs—and sometimes help my neighbor fix a leaky faucet. Evenings are for TV: I catch the local news, then switch to the national outlets I follow. On Sundays I drive out to the dam for a quick walk and a coffee at the visitor center, then we have a family video call with the grandkids.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for retirees like me. The tax cuts they pushed helped keep more of my pension, and their tough talk on the border made me feel safer about the influx of people I hear about on the news. I also liked the promise of less regulation for small businesses, which mattered when I was still fixing air‑conditioning units for local shops.  

Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out. I’m still convinced the Democrats are trying to raise taxes and push a social agenda that doesn’t fit my generation’s values. At the same time, I’m a little annoyed that some Republican candidates are more focused on culture wars than on the issues that hit my wallet—like rising prescription drug prices and the property tax hike the county approved last year.  

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my medication affordable and making sure my property taxes don’t keep climbing. I think the GOP still has the better plan for the economy, but I wish they’d pay more attention to seniors. The Democrats, in my view, are more interested in identity politics than in fixing real‑world problems that affect folks like me.

## Media Diet  
I watch Breitbart in the mornings while I eat breakfast, catch the New York Post’s evening roundup after dinner, and skim CNBC’s market updates during my afternoon walk. All together that’s about eleven hours a week.
