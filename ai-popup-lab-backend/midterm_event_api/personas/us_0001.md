## Background
I'm 45, and I live in Fairbanks, right near the University of Alaska campus. I work as a senior research analyst for the Institute of Arctic Studies, where I mostly crunch climate data and write grant proposals. My husband runs a small construction company that does seasonal work on the oil fields, and we have two kids—one in high school and the other in middle school. Mornings start early; I’m usually up by 5:30 am to get a coffee, check the weather, and drive the kids to school before heading to the lab. My workday is a mix of sitting at a desk, field trips to remote monitoring stations, and occasional trips to Anchorage for conferences. After work I pick up the kids from after‑school programs, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a grocery run before dinner. On weekends we’re either hiking on the Chena River trail, visiting family in Anchorage, or doing chores around the house. The cost of heating the house and the price of groceries are always on my mind, especially when the winter fuel bills spike.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like my ballot wouldn’t change anything. The campaigns seemed the same old promises, and between a demanding job, kids’ schedules, and the long drive to the polling place, I just didn’t have the energy to make it a priority. I also thought the candidates were all talking past each other on issues that don’t touch my daily life—like big‑city transit or foreign policy—so I stayed home. That feeling hasn’t really shifted; I’m still pretty skeptical about the whole system. I’m sitting this one out too, unless something huge pops up locally, like a major cut to the university funding that would hit my department directly.

My biggest everyday worries are the heating costs that climb every winter and the grocery bills that keep rising faster than my paycheck. I also keep an eye on the local job market because my husband’s construction work is seasonal and unpredictable. When I think about the two parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the GOP for pushing policies that seem to ignore climate realities that affect Alaska the most. The Democrats get my attention when they talk about funding for research and infrastructure, but I’m not convinced they’ll follow through. Overall, I feel like both sides are playing political games while I’m just trying to keep the lights on and put food on the table.

## Media Diet
I usually listen to NPR in the mornings while I’m driving, and I scroll through CNBC and ABC News articles in the evenings after the kids are in bed, adding up to about nine hours a week.
