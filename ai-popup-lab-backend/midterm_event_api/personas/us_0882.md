## Background  
I'm 65 and live in the historic neighborhood of North Downtown Charlottesville, just a few blocks from the university campus. I spent most of my career as a senior policy analyst for a nonprofit think‑tank in Washington, D.C., and after I retired I moved back to Virginia to be closer to my two grown‑up children and my sister, who needs occasional help after her hip surgery. My days start early with a cup of black coffee on the porch while the dog, Daisy, chases squirrels. I tend a small vegetable garden behind the house, which keeps me busy in the spring and summer, and I still volunteer once a week at the local senior center, teaching basic computer skills.  

Evenings are usually spent watching a news program or scrolling through a few articles on my tablet, then I settle in with a mystery novel before bed. On weekends I drive out to the Shenandoah Valley to visit my brother’s farm, or I meet friends for a brunch at the farmer’s market. I’m a member of my church choir, which meets on Wednesday nights, and I help organize the annual “Taste of Charlottesville” fundraiser. My household is modest: the house is paid off, Daisy is the only pet, and my sister lives with us part‑time. I’m fairly active for my age, but I do feel the aches that come with a lifetime of sitting at a desk.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Republican candidate because I felt the party best represented my concerns about fiscal responsibility, especially the rising property taxes that have been biting into my fixed retirement income. I also appreciated the candidate’s emphasis on supporting small‑business owners and preserving what I see as traditional values in our community. The Democratic platform seemed too focused on expanding government programs, which I worry could mean higher taxes and more bureaucracy.  

Going into the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls – I don’t want to sit this one out. My top everyday concerns are the cost of health care, especially the premiums for my supplemental Medicare plan, and the price of groceries, which keeps climbing despite my attempts to grow my own produce. I’m also watching the local job market; my sister’s husband lost his job at a manufacturing plant last year, and the lack of decent wages is worrying.  

My view of the parties is pretty clear: the Republicans, despite their occasional missteps, are the ones who talk about cutting wasteful spending and protecting property owners like me. The Democrats, in my experience, promise a lot of social programs but rarely address the tax burden that hits retirees. I’m not a fan of the political theater that dominates the news cycle, but I do think it’s important to keep a check on whichever side holds power, even if I’m more comfortable with the GOP’s approach to economic issues.
