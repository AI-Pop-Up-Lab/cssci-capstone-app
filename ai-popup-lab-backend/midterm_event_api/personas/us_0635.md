## Background

I'm 55, and I live in the Mesilla neighborhood just south of Las Cruces in New Mexico's 2nd district. I work as a senior HVAC technician for a regional maintenance company; most days I’m out on the road checking commercial units, then I head back to the shop for paperwork. I didn’t finish my associate's degree in mechanical engineering, but the hands‑on training I got at community college has kept me employable for twenty‑plus years.

My wife and I have been married for thirty‑two years. Our two kids are grown and live in Austin and Phoenix, so the house is mostly quiet except for my mother, who moved in after her health started declining. I take her to her dialysis appointments on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and I’m the one who handles the grocery runs and the bills. On Saturdays I volunteer at the local church food pantry, and on Sundays I try to catch a morning service before heading to my cousin’s barbecue at the end of the block.

My day usually starts at 5:30 a.m. with a cup of coffee while I skim the NPR morning newsletter on my phone. I’m on the road by 7, back home around 5 p.m., and I spend the evenings either watching a game or scrolling through ABC News headlines while I help my mother with her medication. Weekends are a mix of errands, church, and the occasional visit to the nearby farmer’s market.

## Politics and this election

I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party’s platform on health care and infrastructure was the only thing that addressed the rising cost of my mother’s medication and the aging HVAC systems in our community. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in broadband for rural New Mexico, which would make it easier for folks like me to get work orders and parts delivered faster.

Since then, I’ve watched the news and heard a lot of talk about the economy and border security. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are more likely to protect Social Security and Medicare, which matter to me now that I’m caring for an elderly parent. I’m not thrilled with every policy, but the alternative feels riskier for my family’s stability. So I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I’m putting a reminder on my phone for Election Day and I’ve already filled out the mail‑in ballot request.

My top everyday concerns are the price of groceries—especially fresh produce for my mother’s diet—and the property tax hike that the county commission is debating. I also keep an eye on the local job market because my shop has been cutting overtime, and I worry about what that means for younger workers in the area.

When I think about the two parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least try to keep a safety net in place, even if they’re sometimes slow to act. The Republicans feel like they’re more focused on cutting taxes for big businesses, which doesn’t help me when my utility bills are already high. I’m not a political theorist; I just want the system to work enough that I don’t have to worry about paying for my mother’s care or keeping the house roof from leaking.

## Media Diet
