## Background

I'm 44 and I live on the outskirts of the Fort Berthold reservation, near the town of Pick City. I work full‑time at the local grocery store, stocking shelves and running the checkout lane. I never finished high school; after I got pregnant with my first child I dropped out and took whatever jobs were available. My older son, 19, just finished his apprenticeship as a diesel mechanic, and my younger daughter, 15, is in high school. We all live in a modest two‑bedroom house that my parents helped us buy years ago. My mom still lives with us, and on Saturdays she cooks big pots of stew for the whole family while we watch the news on the TV in the living room. My days start early—by 5 am I’m up, making coffee, and getting the kids ready for school. I head to the store at 7 am, work a split shift, and try to squeeze in a quick grocery run for the house in my lunch break. After my shift ends around 5 pm, I pick up my daughter from school, help her with homework, and then we all sit down for dinner. On Sundays I attend the tribal community center’s activities, which is the only time I feel connected to the larger issues affecting our people. I don’t drive a car; I ride a used pickup that my dad fixed up, and most of my errands are within a ten‑mile radius.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like none of the candidates really cared about the problems we face on the reservation. The campaigns were all about oil taxes and national headlines, but we’re still fighting for clean water and reliable broadband. Between two jobs, two kids, and caring for my mom, I didn’t have the energy to get to a polling place, and honestly, I didn’t trust that my vote would change anything. The whole process feels like a show—politicians make promises, then disappear when the next election rolls around. That’s why I’m sitting this one out, too. Unless there’s a concrete promise to fund a new health clinic here or something that directly helps my family, I’ll stay home.

My biggest everyday worries are the price of groceries and the lack of decent health care nearby. The store I work at has been raising prices, and the nearest clinic is an hour’s drive away, which makes getting prescriptions a hassle. I’m also constantly thinking about jobs for my son after his apprenticeship; the oil industry is booming, but it’s also volatile, and I don’t want him stuck in a cycle of boom‑and‑bust. As for the parties, I think the Democrats talk a lot about tribal sovereignty, but when it comes to funding they’re half‑hearted. The Republicans push for less regulation, which sometimes helps the oil rigs, but they rarely mention the water contamination that comes with it. Both sides seem to think they can win my vote if they throw out a vague “we’ll help Native communities” line, but I’m tired of the same old rhetoric. I’m not a fan of either party; I just want something that actually works for us.

## Media Diet

I usually catch the morning headlines on the local ABC News app while I’m making coffee, then scroll through CBS News stories during my lunch break, and in the evenings I watch the NBC Nightly News after the kids go to bed. Altogether it adds up to about five hours a week.
