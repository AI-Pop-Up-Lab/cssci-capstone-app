## Background
I'm 45 and I work as a civil engineer for a regional transportation agency based out of Yakima. I grew up in a small farming community a few counties east, went to Washington State University for a bachelor's in civil engineering, and came back to the area after graduation. My wife and I bought a modest two‑bedroom house on the edge of town about ten years ago; we have two kids, a 16‑year‑old son who drives a beat‑up Corolla to school and a 9‑year‑old daughter who loves the local soccer league. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30, grab a coffee, and hop in the car for a 30‑minute drive to the district office. On the way I listen to the radio and catch up on traffic reports. After a typical eight‑hour day of site inspections and project meetings, I’m home by six, help with dinner, and then either help the kids with homework or watch a game on TV. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a bit of yard work, and church on Sunday mornings. I’m not a big traveler; most of my free time is spent in the backyard or at the community park with the kids.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on lowering taxes and cutting red tape for infrastructure projects lined up with what I see at work every day. I was also uneasy about the Democratic push for more aggressive climate regulations that I feared would stall the road and bridge projects I’m responsible for. The candidate’s promises to keep federal funding steady for our highways felt like a direct benefit to my job and the local economy. I’m definitely going to the polls again for the 2026 midterms; I don’t have a big reason to skip this one unless something crazy hits the news. My biggest worries right now are the rising price of gasoline and the property tax hike the county proposed last year – both hit the family budget hard. I also keep an eye on whether the state will fund more broadband in the rural parts of the district; without it, my son’s schoolwork suffers. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who actually care about keeping costs down for ordinary folks and letting businesses get on with their work. The Democrats, in my view, are more focused on identity politics and big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with what’s happening on the ground here. I’m not saying they’re all bad, but I’m more comfortable with the GOP’s approach to the economy and local issues.
