## Background  
I'm 50 and work as a senior civil engineer for a regional transportation firm out of Scottsbluff, Nebraska. I got my master’s in civil engineering at the University of Nebraska‑Lincoln, and after a couple of years in Omaha I moved back to be closer to my parents’ ranch. My wife runs a small bilingual tutoring service from our house, and we have two kids—our daughter just finished her sophomore year at the state university, and our son is a senior at the local high school. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30, grab a coffee, and head out on the two‑hour drive to the county seat where the office is. I spend my day looking at bridge designs, meeting with contractors, and signing off on project permits. After work I usually swing by the feed store to pick up hay for my dad’s cattle, then help my son with his physics homework before we all eat dinner together. Weekends are a mix of family barbecues, a quick trip to the sand dunes, and catching a game on the TV while the kids do chores. I’m fluent in Spanish and English, which helps at the tutoring business and when I have to talk to the ranch workers.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate’s talk about cutting regulations on infrastructure projects felt like a direct benefit to my job. The promise of lower taxes also meant I could keep more of my paycheck, which matters when you’re trying to save for your kids’ college tuition and your dad’s medical bills. I was also uneasy about the immigration rhetoric from the other side; being Hispanic, I’ve seen the debate get personal, and I leaned toward a candidate who said he’d enforce the borders while still supporting legal pathways. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have affected my industry—some of the new environmental rules have slowed down projects, which makes me nervous about job security for the crew I manage.  

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. My top concerns right now are the rising gasoline prices that make the long commute to work a real budget killer, and the property tax hike the county proposed to fund a new high‑school wing—my kids will be in that building soon. I’m also watching the Senate race because the incumbent’s stance on federal infrastructure funding could directly impact the bridge projects I’m working on. I see the Republican Party as the one that understands the need for a lean government and lets private firms get things done without too many hoops. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising big‑ticket programs but then drown the state in red tape and spend money on things that don’t touch everyday folks like me. I’m not a political philosopher, I just want a government that lets me keep my job, pay my bills, and let my kids get a decent education without the state taking half my paycheck.
