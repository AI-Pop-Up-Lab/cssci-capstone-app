## Background

My office is in the industrial park just outside Scranton, but I live in the small town of Moosic, a short drive away. My wife and I have been married five years and we have a nine‑year‑old daughter who’s in fourth grade at the local public school. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., making coffee while the house is still quiet, then I hop in the car and head to the warehouse where I spend the next eight hours on spreadsheets, coordinating shipments, and fielding calls from drivers. After work I pick up my daughter from after‑school soccer, swing by the grocery store, and try to get home before the kids’ bedtime. On weekends I’m usually at the hardware store fixing up the house, or at the community center watching a high school basketball game. I’m a regular at the local bar on Friday nights, where the conversation drifts from sports scores to the price of gas. I don’t have a lot of free time, but I try to keep up with my dad’s fishing trips in the Poconos when I can.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s tax proposals would help my family keep more of what we earn, and I was uneasy about the Democratic push for higher energy regulations that could hurt the logistics business I work in. I also liked the candidate’s tough talk on immigration and law‑and‑order, which matched the concerns I hear from my neighbors about rising crime in the suburbs. Since then I’ve watched a few more hearings and read opinion pieces, and while I’m still skeptical of the Democrats’ economic plans, I’m less enthusiastic about the Republican rhetoric on social issues. I think the party could do better on infrastructure, which directly affects my job, but I’m not convinced the current leadership will prioritize that.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax hikes that keep choking my mortgage payments. I also worry about the local school budget cuts, but that’s secondary to keeping the family’s budget balanced. I see the Republican Party as the one that mostly looks out for small‑business owners and tries to keep taxes low, even if they sometimes ignore climate concerns. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising help for the working class but end up demanding higher taxes and more regulation that feel out of touch with people like me. I’m not a political scholar; I just want the next few years to be stable enough for my daughter to finish high school without us having to move because of a tax bill.
