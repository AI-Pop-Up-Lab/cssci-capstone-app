## Background
I'm 45 and I live in Andover, a suburb on the east side of Wichita in Kansas's 4th district. I work as a shift supervisor at a plant that builds aircraft components; I’ve been there for about twelve years, moving up from the shop floor after I finished my bachelor's in industrial engineering. My wife works part‑time as a dental hygienist, and we have two kids—12‑year‑old Maya and 9‑year‑old Eli. Most mornings I get up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and drive the kids to the elementary school near our house. After dropping them off I head to the plant, where I spend the day overseeing the assembly line, handling scheduling, and dealing with the occasional equipment glitch. I usually grab a quick lunch from the cafeteria, then finish up around 4 p.m. Evenings are family time: dinner together, helping with homework, and sometimes a quick video game session with the boys before we all settle in for the night. On weekends I like to take the family to the local farmers market or do some yard work around the house. When I have a spare hour I’ll tinker with a vintage pickup truck in the garage.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate promised lower taxes for small businesses, cut red tape for manufacturers, and took a hard line on illegal immigration—issues that felt directly relevant to my job and my community. The talk about “border security” and “pro‑business policies” resonated with me, and I was also swayed by the candidate’s emphasis on supporting Kansas’s agricultural sector, which my neighbors rely on. Since then, I’ve stayed fairly consistent; I still think the GOP is the party that best protects my paycheck and keeps the cost of living in check. I’m not thrilled with every Republican politician, but I’m more annoyed by the Democrats’ push for higher taxes, expansive welfare programs, and what I see as a tendency to ignore rural concerns.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising price of gasoline, which eats into my commute budget, and the property tax hikes we’ve been hearing about for our school district. I view the Democrats as out of touch with folks like me—always talking about climate policies and big‑government solutions that feel like they’ll end up raising my bills. The Republicans, for all their flaws, still seem the better option for keeping taxes low and jobs stable, even if I sometimes think they could be a little less combative in their rhetoric.
