## Background
I'm 25 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment just north of downtown Fargo. After finishing my master's in public policy, I took a full‑time job as a data analyst at a small tech startup that builds software for agricultural producers. My days start around 6 am with a quick coffee from the corner bakery, then a 20‑minute drive to the office where I spend most of the morning cleaning up data sets and running reports. I usually grab lunch at a food‑court stall with a coworker, and after work I head to the gym for a spin class or sometimes swing by the local library to study for a professional certification I'm working on.

I share the place with a roommate who works as a registered nurse; we split rent and utilities, which keeps the monthly cost just under $1,200. On weekends I help out at the community food bank in the evenings, and I try to get out to the Red River on Saturday mornings for a run. My parents live a few hours away in Bismarck, and we talk on the phone most Sundays. I don’t have kids, and I’m not married, but I do have a tight‑knit circle of friends from grad school who meet up for movie nights or board games.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing hard enough on climate action and protecting Medicaid expansions that matter to my family’s health care costs. The candidate’s stance on student loan forgiveness also hit close to home, given the debt I’m still paying off from my graduate studies. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises translate into policy, and while I’m still frustrated by the slow pace, I think it’s better than watching the other side roll back protections.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising rent prices in Fargo and the cost of health insurance premiums that keep creeping up even after the recent reforms. I’m also watching the local job market; the tech scene is growing but feels volatile. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to care about social safety nets but sometimes compromise too much with corporate donors. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing tax cuts for big agribusiness and dismiss climate science, which feels like a gamble with our future. Overall, I’m more annoyed by the GOP’s dismissiveness than I am by the Democrats’ occasional indecisiveness.
