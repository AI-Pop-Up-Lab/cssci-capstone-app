## Background
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a sales associate at a big‑box electronics store in West Hartford. I took a couple of semesters of computer science at a community college but never finished a degree, so I’m still figuring out a career path that uses what I learned. My parents live in New Haven, and I visit them on holidays, but most weeks I’m on my own in a small one‑bedroom apartment that I rent with a roommate who’s also a recent college dropout. 

My days start around 6:30 am; I brew coffee, scroll through my phone for a quick news bite, then catch the bus to the store. The shift runs from 9 am to 5 pm, with a break that I usually spend on the floor playing a handheld game or scrolling TikTok. After work I either help my roommate with grocery runs, hit the gym for an hour, or meet friends at a local bar for a cheap beer. Weekends are a mix of part‑time gig driving for a delivery app and catching up on shows on streaming services. I don’t have a car, so everything’s public‑transport or rideshare. I’m still paying off a modest student loan and trying to save enough for a down‑payment on a place of my own.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at work, a rotating schedule, and feeling like none of the candidates really represented someone in my situation, I just shrugged it off. The whole process seemed like a distant game for people with more time and money, and I was exhausted after a year of juggling two jobs and trying to stay afloat on a modest paycheck. I also felt that the big parties kept promising “change” but never addressed the rent hikes and grocery price spikes that hit me directly. That cynicism stuck, and unless something blows up locally—like a sudden rent‑control proposal being tossed out or a major scandal that directly affects my community—I’m not planning to cast a ballot this time either. So, I’m sitting this one out.

My biggest daily worries are keeping up with rent and the ever‑rising cost of groceries; the paycheck never quite stretches far enough. The second thing is my student loan balance, which feels like a weight I can’t shake. As for the parties, I see Democrats as too focused on identity politics and not enough on the working‑class grind, while Republicans feel like they’re shouting from the right‑most corner without any real solutions for people like me. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than actually fixing the stuff that matters day‑to‑day.
