## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Fairbanks, smack dab in the middle of Alaska's 1st district. I spent most of my working life as a diesel mechanic on the Trans-Alaska Pipeline, and after I retired I helped my wife run a small bait shop near the river. We have two grown kids—one lives in Seattle and the other in Anchorage—so the house is pretty quiet most days. I still go to the senior center twice a week for bingo and a coffee club, and I try to keep up with my blood pressure meds and that daily walk around the riverbank when the wind lets up. My sister lives in a nearby village, so I drive up every few months to help with groceries and to check on her heating system. The cost of heating oil and diesel has been a constant worry, especially when the winter storms roll in early. I watch the local weather channel every morning, then catch up on national news over lunch while I eat my usual fish sandwich from the deli downtown. When the kids call, it's usually to remind me about a new tablet app or to complain about the internet speed at home. I don't drive much anymore, but I still love the occasional snowmobile ride out to the outskirts for a quick hunt.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the party's stance on Medicare expansion and the push for more reliable broadband in rural Alaska felt like something that mattered to me. I was also swayed by the candidate's promise to protect the fisheries that keep my bait shop afloat. Since then, I've been watching the news a lot—12 hours a week is a decent chunk—and I've seen the same old arguments from both sides. The Republicans keep talking about drilling more oil, which sounds good for the pipeline folks, but I worry about the long‑term impact on the environment and the fish we all depend on. The Democrats, on the other hand, keep promising more funding for rural health clinics, but I feel like they sometimes forget that a lot of us live miles from the nearest hospital and need real, on‑the‑ground solutions, not just big‑picture speeches.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns right now are keeping my heating costs down and making sure the internet service here finally gets upgraded—everything from telehealth appointments to checking the weather depends on a stable connection. I also keep an eye on the property tax rates; the city council's recent proposals could push my mortgage payments up at a time when my pension is already stretched thin. In my mind, the Democrats are better at social safety nets, but I get frustrated when they seem to ignore the unique challenges of remote Alaska. The Republicans talk a lot about energy jobs, but I think they underplay the climate risks that affect fishing and tourism, which are the real lifelines for many of us here.

## Media Diet
