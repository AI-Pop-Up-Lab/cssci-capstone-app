## Background  
I'm 30 and I work as a ranch hand on a cattle operation just outside Miles City, in the 2nd congressional district of Montana. I never finished high school; I left after ninth grade to help my family when my dad got sick. My mom still lives in the same house, and we have a younger sister who just started community college. Most days I get up before sunrise, feed the herd, fix fences, and drive the trucks out to the pasture. After a noon break—usually a sandwich and a cold soda—I’m back on the range until the sun sets. When I'm not on the ranch I hang out at the local bar with a few other guys, play pickup basketball at the community center, or scroll through my phone on the porch. I own a cheap Android phone and a basic laptop that I use mostly for checking the weather, listening to music, and the occasional online shopping for work gear. The house is a modest ranch‑style home we inherited; we’re paying off a small mortgage and trying to keep the property tax bill manageable.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything. The candidates all seemed like they were talking about big‑city issues—taxes, climate regulation, immigration—while I was just trying to keep the ranch afloat and make sure my mom could afford her meds. The polls felt like a far‑away thing, and the voting locations were a hassle; the nearest early‑voting site is an hour’s drive away, and I was working a long shift that day. I’ve stayed pretty disengaged since then. I’m “sitting this one out” for the 2026 midterms too, unless something hits close to home—like a new regulation that threatens grazing rights or a local scandal that directly affects our community.

My biggest everyday worries are the price of feed and groceries, and whether the next winter will bring enough snow for the water supply we need for the cattle. I also worry about the rising cost of diesel for the trucks. When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Democrats. They push a lot of environmental policies that feel like they’re trying to shut down ranching, and I get the sense they don’t understand life out here. The Republicans seem a bit more sympathetic to land‑use issues, but they’re also full of talk and not much follow‑through. Both sides, in my view, treat Native American concerns as an afterthought—my tribe’s water rights and cultural sites get tossed into debates without real input. So I’m not exactly cheering for either side; I’m just trying to get by.
