## Background
I'm 65 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Auburn, Maine, right in the heart of the 2nd congressional district. I spent a few years at a community college taking business classes, but I never finished a degree. After raising three kids, I went back to work as a part‑time clerk at the local hardware store, which lets me stay active without the grind of a full‑time schedule. My husband passed away five years ago, so most days I’m on my own, though my oldest daughter visits us on weekends and helps with groceries. I’m also the primary caregiver for my mother, who’s in her late 80s and needs help with medication and errands.

My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee while I skim the headlines on my tablet. I drive the store’s delivery truck for a few hours, stocking aisles and chatting with regulars about the weather or the price of lumber. After work I stop by the church basement to help with the senior lunch program, then head home to bake a loaf of bread or tend to my garden. Evenings are usually spent watching the news, checking the mail, and reading the crossword before heading to bed around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one talking about the cost of living and the need to keep taxes low for retirees like me. I was especially worried about my pension being squeezed by state budget cuts, and the Republican candidate promised to protect Social Security and push back against what I see as over‑reaching regulations on small businesses. I also liked the emphasis on energy independence; I own a small propane tank for home heating and don’t want the price of fuel to keep climbing.

I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that make it harder to stay in my home. I’m also keeping an eye on the local job market—if the hardware store can’t stay afloat, I lose both income and a social outlet. I feel the Republican Party is the only one that gets the “real” problems of older folks in rural Maine, while the Democrats seem more focused on cultural issues that don’t affect my day‑to‑day life. I’m not a political scholar, but I trust the party that promises lower taxes and less government meddling.
