## Background

My day starts before sunrise; I catch the early bus, grab a quick coffee from the corner deli, and head to the store where I'm on the floor until about 6 p.m. After my shift I pick up my two kids, ages 7 and 10, from the after‑school program at the church across the street. We swing by the laundromat, then I cook dinner while they finish homework on the kitchen table. On weekends I do a bit of yard work for neighbors, run errands at the big box store in Jackson, and try to squeeze in a Sunday service at the bilingual Hispanic ministry. My mom lives a short drive away and helps watch the kids when I have to work a double shift. Money is tight, so I keep a close eye on the utility bills and try not to waste anything. I don't have a driver's license; the bus is my main way around town, and I spend a lot of my free time scrolling on my phone while waiting for the bus or during breaks.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like none of the candidates really cared about people like me. Between juggling two kids, long hours at the store, and trying to stretch every paycheck, I just didn't have the energy to drive to the polls or even wait in line. The whole process seemed like a show for the rich and the well‑connected, and I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything. That feeling hasn't really shifted. I'm still planning to sit this one out in the 2026 midterms unless something massive hits our community—like a sudden factory closure or a major road that suddenly blocks our bus route. My biggest everyday worries are keeping a roof over our heads and making sure there's enough food on the table; rent and grocery prices are a constant source of stress. I also worry about my kids' future—whether they'll be able to finish high school and maybe go to college without being crushed by debt. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as promising big promises they never keep, and the Republicans as talking about “family values” while cutting programs that help low‑income families. I don't feel a strong loyalty to either side; it's more like I'm annoyed by the whole system and wish someone would actually listen to people who work two jobs just to get by.
