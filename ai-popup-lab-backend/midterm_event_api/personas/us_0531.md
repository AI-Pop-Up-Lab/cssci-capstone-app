## Background
I'm 79, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Wilmington, right in the middle of NC‑11. I spent most of my adult life teaching history at a community college in the area, and I finally retired a few years ago after getting my PhD in American Studies. My wife passed away five years ago, so it's just me now, though my two adult children visit every other weekend and I get to see my granddaughter when she’s in town for school breaks. Most days I start with a cup of black coffee on the porch while the sunrise hits the river, then I shuffle through the local paper—mostly the *News & Observer*—and scroll through the news sites on my tablet. I still drive my old pickup to the grocery store on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and on Wednesdays I volunteer at the senior center, helping to run the chess club. In the evenings I like to sit on the back deck, watch the waves, and listen to a news broadcast or a documentary. I keep a small garden of tomatoes and peppers, which gives me something to tend to when the weather's nice. Apart from that, I’m pretty set in my routine: a morning walk along the beach, a quick check of my email, and then the rest of the day is either reading, tinkering with the old wood stove, or catching up on a TV show from the ’70s that I still enjoy.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best guard against big‑government overreach and the kind of tax hikes that would bite into my fixed retirement income. I was especially worried about proposals to raise the payroll tax and cut back on the tax deductions that help seniors like me. The GOP’s emphasis on law and order and a strong military also resonated, given the headlines about border security and overseas conflicts that seemed to dominate the news cycle. I wasn’t thrilled with the whole campaign circus, but I figured a vote for the Republicans was the safer bet for preserving the stability I need at my age.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m voting because I don’t want to hand over my voice to anyone else, and I’m still uneasy about the Democrats’ push for more expansive health‑care reforms that could mean higher premiums for people on Medicare. My top everyday concerns are health‑care costs—especially prescription drug prices—and property taxes, which have been creeping up as the county tries to fund new development projects. I see the Republican Party as the one that keeps taxes low, respects the Constitution, and doesn’t try to rewrite social programs that have been working for decades. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more interested in reshaping everything from climate policy to education, and I worry that they’ll end up taking more from retirees to fund those ambitions. That said, I’m not blind to the fact that the GOP isn’t perfect; I’m annoyed when they talk down to older folks or ignore the fact that some rural hospitals are closing, but overall I think they’re the lesser of two evils for me right now.
