## Background  
I'm 45 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Wilmington, in the heart of Delaware's 1st district. I work full‑time as a warehouse supervisor for a regional distribution center; the job is mostly standing, lifting pallets, and making sure shipments go out on schedule. I finished high school locally and never went to college, so most of what I know about the world comes from on‑the‑job experience and the news I catch in the evenings.

I'm married to Rosa, and we have two kids: a 12‑year‑old daughter who just started middle school and a 9‑year‑old son who loves video games. Mornings are a blur of getting the kids ready, dropping the son at the after‑school program, and heading to the warehouse by 7 am. My shift ends around 3 pm, and I pick up the daughter from school, swing by the grocery store, and try to fit in a little yard work before dinner. On weekends we usually do a bit of house cleaning, take the kids to the park, and sometimes visit Rosa's parents who live a short drive away. Money is tight enough that we watch every line on the utility bill and keep an eye on rent increases. I don't have a lot of free time, but when I do I like to watch a game on TV or scroll through social media for a few minutes before bed.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole campaign felt like a circus—ads promising change while everything stayed the same. Between long shifts, kids' activities, and trying to keep the house afloat, voting just fell off my radar. I also got fed up with the endless talk about "the system" when I was just trying to make rent and keep the lights on. That feeling hasn't really shifted; unless something hits close to home—like a major factory closing in our town or a sudden tax hike that squeezes my paycheck—I plan on sitting this 2026 midterm out as well.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of rent and grocery prices. Every month I have to decide whether to cut back on gas or skip a family outing. I also worry about property taxes, especially since my house is getting older and might need repairs soon. As for the parties, I see both of them as talking past each other. The Democrats seem more focused on big‑picture issues that feel distant from my daily grind, while the Republicans keep promising tax cuts but rarely deliver anything that helps a warehouse worker. In short, I'm annoyed by the noise and skeptical that either side will make a real difference for folks like me.
