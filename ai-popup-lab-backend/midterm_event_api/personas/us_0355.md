## Background  
I'm 40, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Lake Charles, right in the middle of Louisiana’s 6th district. I work full‑time as a semi‑truck driver for a regional freight company; the job means I’m up before dawn, checking the rig, loading pallets, and then spending most of the day on the highway hauling pallets of lumber and building supplies up and down the Gulf Coast. My wife works part‑time at the local high school as a cafeteria aide, and we’ve got two kids—Mia, 14, who’s in middle school, and Jake, 9, who’s in elementary. On weekends we try to get the kids to the park or the community pool, but most evenings are spent catching up on TV after the kids are in bed, fixing the leaky kitchen faucet, or scrolling through the news on my phone while I wait for the truck to cool down. We own a small plot behind the house where we grow a few tomatoes and peppers; it’s more for the hobby than for feeding the family. I’m a Sunday school volunteer at our Baptist church, which is also where I meet most of my friends. Money’s tight enough that any surprise expense—like a busted transmission or a roof leak—keeps me up at night, so I’m always watching my budget.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one standing up for the working‑class folks like me who can’t afford another tax hike or more regulation on my trucking business. I liked the promise of keeping fuel taxes low, protecting my right to own a gun, and backing the oil and gas jobs that keep this part of Louisiana afloat. The candidate’s talk about “bringing back law and order” also hit home after a string of break‑ins in our neighborhood. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the GOP in Congress has handled the infrastructure bill and the push for more renewable energy subsidies; I’m not thrilled with the direction they’re taking on renewables, but I’m still leaning toward them because the Democrats keep talking about “green New Deal” stuff that feels like it could hurt trucking jobs and raise fuel costs.

I’m definitely voting this November. My main worries are the rising price of diesel that eats into my paycheck and the property tax hikes the parish council is considering to fund a new school wing. I’m also annoyed by the constant political bickering that seems to ignore real issues like road maintenance—my rig spends half the day on pothole‑filled highways. In my mind, the Republicans are the ones who at least understand what a working‑class family needs: stable jobs, low taxes, and a government that stays out of my yard. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more interested in pushing social agendas and big‑government programs that feel disconnected from my daily grind.
