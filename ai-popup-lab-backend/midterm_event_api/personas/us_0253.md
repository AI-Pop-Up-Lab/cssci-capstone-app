## Background
I'm 45 and work as a warehouse supervisor at the big distribution center just outside Twin Falls. I grew up in a nearby farm town, helped my parents pick potatoes, and after high school I took a couple of community‑college classes in logistics but never finished a degree. I married my high school sweetheart 20 years ago; we have two kids—Jenna, 17, who’s getting ready for college, and Malik, 9, who’s always on his bike. Most mornings I’m up by 5 a.m., grab a coffee from the gas station, and drive the 20‑minute stretch to the warehouse where I run a shift of about 30 people. After my 8‑hour shift I swing by the kids’ school to pick them up, then we eat dinner together while the TV is on low. On weekends I try to help Malik with his baseball practice and Jenna with her SAT prep, and we all do a bit of yard work or a quick hike in the foothills when the weather’s good. Money’s tight enough that I keep an eye on the utility bills and the grocery tab, but we’re not living paycheck to paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything—candidates kept talking about big‑picture stuff while I was juggling rent, school fees, and trying to keep the warehouse running smoothly. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and between early mornings and late nights I just didn’t have the energy to follow it all. I also get a little sick of the partisan bickering; it seems like the parties are more interested in scoring points than fixing potholes or making sure there’s decent broadband in the valley. That feeling hasn’t really shifted, so I’m sitting this one out too, unless something hits close to home, like a major layoff at the plant or a policy that directly hurts my kids’ school funding.

My biggest daily worries are the cost of groceries and the rising property taxes that keep my mortgage from getting any easier. I’m also nervous about the jobs market—if the warehouse ever cuts hours or shuts down, we’d be in trouble. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel distant, like they’re focused on coastal issues and identity politics that don’t touch my life. The Republicans talk about low taxes and “freedom,” which sounds good, but I’m not convinced they care about a Black family in a rural town either. I guess I’m stuck in a middle where I’m skeptical of both sides and just hoping someone will actually listen to folks like me.

## Media Diet
