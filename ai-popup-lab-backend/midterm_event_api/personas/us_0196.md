## Background
I'm 74, and I live in the little town of Gray, tucked just outside Macon in Georgia’s 13th district. I spent most of my working life as a maintenance supervisor at a local manufacturing plant; the job only required a community college certificate, which is all the formal schooling I ever needed. I retired five years ago when the plant downsized, and now I’m mostly keeping busy with the garden, fixing things around the house, and helping out at the VFW hall on Thursday nights. My wife, Margaret, is 71 and still likes to bake pies for the church bake‑sale. Our two kids live in Atlanta and Savannah, so we get a lot of video calls but not many visits. Most mornings I sit on the porch with a cup of black coffee, listen to the radio for the weather, then head inside to read the paper or check my phone. After lunch I usually take a short walk around the neighborhood, stop by the post office, and later in the afternoon I tend to the vegetable patch or work on a DIY project in the garage. Evenings are for the TV news and a bit of internet browsing before bed.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between a bout of flu, a mix‑up with my voter registration, and the feeling that the whole system was just a circus, I figured why bother? The candidates all sounded the same to me, and I was too worn out to drive to the polling place. I’ve been pretty skeptical of politics ever since; it feels like the big parties are more interested in scoring points than fixing the potholes on Main Street. I’m not planning to head to the polls for the 2026 midterms either – “I’m sitting this one out” is what I told my neighbor when we talked about it. The only thing that might change my mind is if a local issue—like a proposal to close the community health clinic—gets hot enough to affect my daily life.

My biggest worries right now are the cost of my prescription meds and the rising property taxes that keep squeezing our fixed income. I also keep an eye on the state road budget because the county roads get hammered every winter and never get repaired in time. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as trying to push a lot of change that feels out of touch with folks like me, and the Republicans as all talk and no follow‑through. Neither side seems to care much about retirees in small towns, which is why I’m inclined to stay on the sidelines.

## Media Diet
I start my day with the CBS morning broadcast while I’m having coffee, then scroll through Breitbart articles in the evening after dinner, and I catch the NBC Nightly News before I go to bed. All told, I spend about seven hours a week on these three outlets.
