## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Midland, Michigan, right in the 8th district. I grew up in Detroit, the daughter of immigrants who ran a small grocery store, so I learned early on how hard work pays off. After high school I took some community college classes in business administration but never finished a degree; I worked most of my adult life as a medical records clerk at a local hospital. I retired five years ago, but I still help out part‑time with the hospital’s volunteer intake desk.  

I'm married to Carl, a retired electrician, and we have two grown‑up kids who live in Grand Rapids and Ann Arbor. My oldest daughter visits us a few weekends a month, and my son calls every Tuesday to check on us. I also help my mother, who lives nearby, with grocery runs and doctor appointments; she's in her late 80s now.  

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., make a cup of black coffee, and flip on the TV while I read the newspaper headlines on my tablet. After a light breakfast I tend to my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few rosemary bushes—then I take Carl for a short walk around the neighborhood. In the afternoons I either attend the senior center’s bingo night or sit in the church basement helping with the food pantry. Evenings are for watching the nightly news and checking my email; I try to keep the TV off after 10 p.m so I can get enough sleep.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican. I was mostly driven by concerns about my fixed income and the rising cost of living. The Republican candidate promised to keep taxes low, especially property taxes that keep a big chunk of my pension check from stretching far enough for groceries and medication. I also liked the emphasis on protecting the Second Amendment; I keep a small handgun for home safety, and I felt the Democratic platform was too focused on gun control. Economic stability mattered more to me than the social issues that seemed to dominate the campaign.  

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top everyday worries are the price of gasoline—my car is my only way to get to the hospital for my mom’s appointments—and the cost of prescription drugs, which keep creeping up despite my insurance. I also keep an eye on property tax rates because any increase feels like a direct hit to my retirement budget.  

When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones most likely to keep the government from taking too much of my hard‑earned money, even if they sometimes push policies I don’t fully agree with, like cutting funding for certain social programs. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to be talking more about big‑picture climate goals and social justice, which feel distant from my day‑to‑day concerns. I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I just want whoever’s in office to keep my bills manageable and let me keep my independence.

## Media Diet  
I watch the evening broadcasts from CBS, ABC, and NBC for about an hour each night, and I skim the online articles from those sites in the mornings while I sip coffee. In total it’s about four hours a week.
