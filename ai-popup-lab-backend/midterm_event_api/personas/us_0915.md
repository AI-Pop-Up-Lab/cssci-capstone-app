## Background
I'm 75, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Bennington, Vermont. I spent most of my working life loading logs at the local sawmill and doing odd‑job repairs for neighbors; I never finished high school, but I learned a lot on the job. My wife died ten years ago, and now I mostly keep in touch with my two kids over the phone—they live in upstate New York and Maine. My granddaughter visits in the summer and helps me with the garden, which is the biggest thing I do these days besides shuffling cards with the other retirees at the community center. My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee, the paper folded over the kitchen table, and a quick scan of the news on the TV before I head out to tend the vegetable patch or check the mailbox. Afternoons are for errands: the post office, the grocery store on Main Street, and sometimes a drive to the library to borrow a mystery novel. Evenings I usually sit on the porch with a radio, listening to the local station or catching up on the national news clips I stream on my tablet. I’m on a fixed income from Social Security and a small pension, so I watch my spending closely.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I didn’t feel like the two big parties were speaking to folks like me, so I threw my vote in the Libertarian column. I was tired of the same old promises about “big government” from the Democrats and “tax cuts” from the Republicans; neither seemed to address the real problems in my town—rising property taxes and the fact that the nearest broadband upgrade still drags on. I’ve always been skeptical of politicians, but I do believe voting is a way to send a message, even if it’s a small one. This time around, I’m actually going to the polls. I’ve signed up for an early‑voting slot at the town hall because I don’t want to risk missing it on Election Day. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with my medication costs and making sure the road that leads into town gets snowplowed on time—if the road is bad, I can’t get to the doctor. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as too focused on urban issues and climate talk that feels disconnected from rural life, while the Republicans keep talking about “freedom” but ignore the fact that my farm still struggles with high fuel prices and limited broadband. Neither side feels like they’ve earned my trust, but I’m willing to give the candidates who actually show up in my community a chance.
