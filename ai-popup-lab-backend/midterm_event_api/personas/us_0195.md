## Background
I'm 45 and work as a senior data analyst for a regional health insurer based out of Atlanta, but I’ve been commuting from my home in the Athens‑Clarke County area for the past five years. I have a master's in statistics, so most of my day is spent pulling reports, checking models, and making sure the numbers we send to hospitals line up with what the contracts say. My wife runs a boutique pottery studio downtown, and we have two kids: a 15‑year‑old son who’s on the varsity soccer team and a 10‑year‑old daughter who loves the library’s after‑school reading club.

Our house is a modest two‑bedroom ranch on a quiet cul‑de‑sac. Mornings start with a quick coffee while I scroll the headlines on my phone, then I hop in the car for the 30‑minute drive to the office parking lot. After work I pick up the kids from practice, swing by the grocery store for a few staples, and we eat dinner together around 7 p.m. Weekends are a mix of yard work, my wife’s craft fairs, and the occasional road trip to the coast when we can afford it. I keep a small savings account for emergencies, but most of my money goes toward the mortgage, the kids' extracurricular fees, and the car payments.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on the economy and taxes matched what I needed to keep my family afloat. The tax cuts they pushed seemed like a direct benefit to a middle‑class household like mine, and the talk about securing the border felt like a concrete way to protect jobs in my industry. I also appreciated the candidate’s focus on “law and order,” which resonated after a string of news stories about crime spikes in nearby counties.

Since then, I’ve watched the headlines and heard the talk at work, and I’m still leaning that way. The biggest worries on my mind right now are the rising property taxes on our home and the stubborn climb in gas prices that make the commute and the kids’ weekend trips harder on the budget. I’m also keeping an eye on health‑care costs because my wife’s studio doesn’t offer insurance and we’ve had to shop around for plans that don’t break the bank.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. I think the Senate race will be the most consequential for my pocketbook, and I plan to cast my ballot for the Republican candidate who’s promising to keep taxes low and cut back on what I see as wasteful spending. The Democratic side, in my view, talks a lot about social programs but rarely offers a clear plan for how to fund them without raising the tax burden on families like mine. I’m not a political theorist, I just want the next four years to be steady enough that I can keep the mortgage payments on schedule and not worry about my kids having to give up soccer because the family can’t afford the fees.
