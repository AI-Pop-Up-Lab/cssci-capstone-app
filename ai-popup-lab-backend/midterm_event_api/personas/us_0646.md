## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the outskirts of Reno, right in the heart of Nevada's 1st district. I spent 35 years teaching high‑school English at a public school before I retired five years ago. My husband passed away three years back, and my two grown‑up kids live in California and Arizona, so most days I'm on my own, except for the occasional visit from my granddaughter who stays with me for a weekend every few months.  

My mornings start with a cup of black coffee on the porch while I watch the traffic on the 580 and glance at the local news on my tablet. After that I tend to the garden—tomatoes, basil, a few peppers—then head to the community center where I lead a book club for seniors. Tuesdays I volunteer at the senior pantry, and on Wednesdays I take a short walk to the nearby grocery store; the lines are always longer than I’d like, especially for the generic meds I need. Evenings are usually quiet: I cook a simple dinner, maybe watch a favorite sitcom rerun, and then catch up on the news before bed. I drive a reliable older sedan and keep a careful budget; my Social Security checks and a modest pension are what keep the lights on.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Between a flare‑up of arthritis, a caregiving stint for my sister who lives in Las Vegas, and the feeling that the presidential race was just a circus, I figured my ballot wouldn't change anything. The whole system feels distant; the big‑ticket candidates talk about “unity” while the real issues—affordable health care, reliable public transport, and criminal‑justice reform—stay buried. I also got tired of the endless ads and the sense that both parties were more interested in fundraising than solving problems.  

I'm sitting this one out, too. Unless a candidate shows up who actually talks to people like me about prescription drug prices and the rising property tax on my modest home, I don't see a point in standing in line again. My top everyday concerns are the cost of my medication and keeping my property taxes from eating up my fixed income. I also worry about the lack of decent jobs for younger folks in Reno; my kids left because there just aren't enough opportunities.  

When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to promise a lot about health care and social programs, but I rarely see those promises turn into concrete action in my neighborhood. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing tax cuts for the wealthy and talk tough on crime, which feels like a slap in the face for a Black senior who's seen how policies can hurt people of color. Both sides feel like they're playing a game I’m not invited to win.
