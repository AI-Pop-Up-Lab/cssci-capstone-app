## Background
I'm 65, and I live in Juneau, the capital town that feels more like a close‑knit village than a city. I work as a senior lecturer in environmental policy at the University of Alaska Fairbanks, which means I spend a lot of time on Zoom calls, grading papers, and field trips to the glaciers in the summer. My husband, a retired fisherman, and I own a modest two‑bedroom condo near the waterfront; we keep a small garden of hardy herbs and a couple of tomato plants that survive the short growing season. Our two grown‑up children live on the mainland, so we get video calls every Sunday and occasional visits in the summer. My day usually starts with a strong cup of coffee while I read the local weather report, then I bike the 15‑minute stretch to the campus shuttle stop, grab a quick lunch from the campus café, and head back to my office to prep lectures or meet with graduate students. In the evenings I either cook a simple dinner, watch a documentary, or help my husband with his radio hobby. On weekends I volunteer at the community center, teaching a short class on renewable energy basics for seniors, and I try to get out on the water for a kayak when the sea is calm. I’m fairly active for my age, but the high cost of heating and the long, dark winters keep me mostly indoors during the colder months.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really pushing for serious climate action, which matters a lot up here where the melting ice and shifting fish populations affect everyone’s livelihood. I also appreciated the candidate’s focus on expanding Medicare coverage for seniors; my husband and I rely on those services, and I’ve seen how cutbacks can make a real difference. Since then I’ve stayed pretty engaged—my inbox is full of newsletters about climate bills and health care proposals, and I’ve signed a few petitions about protecting the Tongass National Forest. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; there’s a Senate race that could swing the balance on federal funding for Alaskan infrastructure, and I don’t want to leave that to chance. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of heating oil and the limited availability of affordable health care specialists in remote areas. I think the Democrats are generally on the right track for the issues I care about, even if they sometimes get tangled in internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in deregulating the oil industry and cutting social programs, which feels like a direct threat to the environment I study and the seniors I try to help. I’m not a political analyst, but the way I see it, the parties are pretty far apart on the things that affect my day‑to‑day life.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR’s morning news feed while I have coffee, catch up on ABC News headlines during my lunch break, and skim Newsweek articles in the evenings after dinner. I’m clocking about eight hours a week across those outlets.
