## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Rice Lake, a small town in north‑western Wisconsin that sits in the 5th congressional district. I work full‑time as a maintenance technician at the local paper mill; the job is mostly hands‑on, fixing machinery, changing belts, and keeping the plant running when the night shift is on. I got my high school diploma from Rice Lake High, and after a few odd jobs I landed here ten years ago and haven't moved since.

I'm married to a woman who works part‑time at the community health clinic, and we have two kids—Maya, 16, and Jamal, 14. Mornings start at 5 a.m. with a quick coffee, a quick scan of the news on my phone, and then a 30‑minute drive to the plant. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., so I’m home in time for the kids’ after‑school basketball practice or the occasional tutoring session. Evenings are a mix of dinner, helping with homework, and then either watching a sitcom or scrolling through headlines before I finally go to bed around 11. On weekends I do some yard work, run errands at the grocery store, and try to catch a few games on the local sports channel.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. I figured the whole thing was a circus, and I was working overtime at the mill the day before the election, so I missed the polls entirely. Even when I tried to vote early, the line was a mess, and I got discouraged. I’ve never felt like any of the candidates really understood what it’s like to keep a plant humming, to worry about my kids’ college tuition, or to watch the utility bill climb every winter. That sense of alienation made me sit out, and I haven’t been motivated to change that.

Come 2026, I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something huge hits the community—like a plant shutdown or a major health crisis—I don’t see my vote moving the needle. My biggest everyday concerns are the rising cost of electricity and gas, and whether I’ll still have a steady paycheck when the mill automates more of its line. I also worry about health‑care premiums for my family; the clinic where my wife works is always short‑staffed, and the insurance options keep getting more expensive.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up. The Democrats keep talking about racial justice and voting rights, but they don’t seem to have a plan for keeping blue‑collar jobs alive in places like ours. The Republicans brag about lower taxes, yet they push policies that feel like they’re aimed at big‑city elites and ignore the struggles of folks who work in factories. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than actually solving the problems that keep me up at night.
