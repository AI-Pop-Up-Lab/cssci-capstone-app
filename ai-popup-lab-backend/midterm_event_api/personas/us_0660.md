## Background
I'm 75, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Sparks, Nevada, right near the foothills of the Sierra. I spent most of my adult life working as a maintenance supervisor for a regional grocery chain; I never went to college, but I learned a lot on the job fixing refrigeration units, managing a crew, and dealing with vendors. I retired five years ago, mostly because my knees started giving out and I wanted to spend more time with my wife, who passed away three years back. I have two grown children—one in Phoenix and the other in Seattle—who call about once a month, and a handful of grandkids who show up for the holidays and sometimes stay a week in the summer.

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and flip on the local radio while I read the newspaper (the print edition, because I still like holding paper). After breakfast I take my old Labrador, Chico, for a short walk around the neighborhood; we usually pass the community garden where I sometimes help pull weeds. Mid‑morning I head to the senior center where I volunteer as a handyman, fixing leaky faucets and keeping the building safe. Lunch is usually a sandwich I make at home, and the afternoon I sit on my porch, watching the news on the TV or scrolling through a few articles on my tablet. In the evenings I might watch a western on the cable channel or call my daughter to hear how the grandkids are doing. On Sundays I attend mass at the local Catholic church and then have a big family dinner when my grandchildren are in town.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates really cared about folks like me. The ballots were long, the ads were all the same screaming about taxes or borders, and I couldn't see any concrete plan for keeping my Medicare premiums from climbing or making prescription drugs affordable. Between caring for Chico, fixing things at the senior center, and trying to stretch my fixed income, voting just felt like another chore that wouldn't change anything. I also thought the whole system was rigged—my cousin who works at the state elections office told me about a bunch of ballot‑stuffing stories, and that just reinforced my belief that my vote wouldn't matter.

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out. Unless something blows up locally—like a hospital closing or a huge hike in property taxes that forces me to sell the house—I just don't see a reason to go to the polls. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of my Medicare Part D premiums and the fact that my electricity bill keeps creeping up, especially now that I'm trying to keep the house cool in those Nevada summers. I also worry about property taxes; the county assessor's office just sent another notice saying my land value went up, and I barely have any extra cash.

When I think about the parties, I get annoyed at both. The Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes, but most of that stuff goes to corporations, not seniors on a fixed income. The Democrats promise to protect Social Security, but their budget proposals always seem to rely on raising other taxes that end up hitting retirees indirectly. I guess I just wish someone would actually listen to the everyday grind of people my age, instead of using us as a talking point.
