## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate, even though most people around me lean Republican. I did it because the Democrat promised to keep the community health clinic open. My youngest son needed regular asthma check‑ups, and the clinic was the only place that accepted our insurance. The Republican opponent wanted to cut funding for that clinic, and I couldn't let that happen. I also liked the way the Democrat talked about raising the minimum wage for warehouse workers—something that would have meant a real bump for me and my coworkers.

Now, looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. I don't have the luxury of sitting this one out; the gas prices have shot up and groceries are getting more expensive every month. I also worry about property taxes—my wife and I are trying to save enough for the kids' college, and any hike feels like a wall. I’m still leaning Democratic because I think they care more about working‑class families, even if their messaging sometimes feels like a lot of talk and not enough action. The GOP, in my view, mostly talks about cutting taxes for big businesses and ignoring the day‑to‑day struggles of people like me. I’m not a political scholar; I just want the folks who make decisions to remember that we’re trying to keep a roof over our heads and food on the table.
