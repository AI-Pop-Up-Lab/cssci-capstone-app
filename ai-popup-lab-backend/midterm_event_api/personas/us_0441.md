## Background  
I'm 50 and work the daytime shift as a floor supervisor at the local grocery store in St. Cloud. I grew up just outside the city, went straight to work after high school, and never went to college. My husband is retired from the railroad, and we have two adult kids who live in the Twin Cities. Most weekends I’m either helping my mother with her medication appointments or doing yard work around our house. My mornings start with a quick coffee while scrolling through the news on my phone, then a 15‑minute bus ride to the store. I spend the day stocking shelves, handling the checkout line, and making sure the refrigerated case stays at the right temperature. After my shift ends around 5 p.m., I pick up the kids’ laundry, make dinner, and try to squeeze in a little TV before bed. On Saturdays I’ll head to the farmers’ market or take a short drive to a nearby lake for fishing with my husband. Sundays are mostly for church and a long walk with my dog, then I prep meals for the week. Money is tight enough that I watch my utility bills and keep an eye on grocery prices, but I’m used to budgeting.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I felt the candidate actually cared about the things that hit my wallet every month – health insurance, prescription costs, and keeping the local school funding steady for my younger grandchildren. I also liked the promise to expand broadband in our rural parts of the district; my mom finally got decent internet after a long fight. The Republican opponent seemed more focused on cutting taxes for big corporations and didn’t say much about the nursing home I work at. I was also swayed by a few town‑hall meetings where the Democrat answered questions directly, while the Republican kept repeating talking points.  

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. My top concerns right now are the rising price of groceries and the property tax hike the county council pushed through last year. I’m also worried about my mother’s health care needs – the co‑pay increase on her meds is a real strain. I still think the Democratic Party is the better choice for everyday folks like me, but I’m getting impatient with the endless infighting and the feeling that they’re more interested in national headlines than fixing potholes or keeping the local fire department funded. The Republicans, on the other hand, look like they’re more interested in cultural wars and less in practical solutions for a retiree’s fixed income. I’m not a fan of either side’s rhetoric, but I’m leaning toward the Democrats because they at least talk about protecting Medicare and keeping the grocery aisle stocked with affordable food.
