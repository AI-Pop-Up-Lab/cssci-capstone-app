## Background

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party talked straight about the things that mattered to me: keeping the cost of gasoline low, protecting my job at the warehouse, and standing up for law‑and‑order in the city. The Democratic ads kept shouting about climate change and big‑government programs, which felt disconnected from my daily grind. I also liked how the GOP candidate talked about cutting taxes for small businesses—something that could help the little side‑hustles people like me rely on. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on the local news and the national sites I follow, and I still think the Republican platform lines up best with my priorities. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to hand the ballot over to folks who keep promising handouts that never reach my mailbox. My biggest everyday worries are the rising price of groceries and the uncertainty of whether the warehouse will stay open when automation keeps creeping in. I’m also watching the property tax bill that the county board is mulling—if it goes up, my mom’s fixed income will take a hit. When I think about the parties, the Republicans feel like the only ones who remember the working‑class folks who keep the economy moving, while the Democrats seem more interested in big‑city issues and identity politics that don’t affect my street.
