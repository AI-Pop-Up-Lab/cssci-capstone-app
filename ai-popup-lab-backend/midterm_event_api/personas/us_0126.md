## Background
I'm 70, and I live in the Black Rock section of Bridgeport, Connecticut, right near the river and the old train station. I grew up in a working‑class family in the Bronx and moved to Connecticut after marrying a local electrician. I finished an associate’s program in medical assisting at a community college and spent most of my adult life working at the same community health clinic downtown, helping patients fill out forms and take vitals. I retired five years ago, but I still pick up a few shifts at the grocery store on the weekends to stay busy and earn a little extra for my granddaughter’s college fund. My husband passed away three years ago, so it’s just me and my two adult children—one lives in Hartford, the other in New Haven. I see them every few months, and I get a lot of video calls from my great‑grandkids. My day usually starts with a walk along the river trail, then a coffee at the corner diner, and later I’m either gardening in the little backyard or watching a telenovela on the TV. I’m involved in the local senior center, where we play bingo and sometimes organize a food drive.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security, Medicare, and the immigration reforms that would help families like mine. The candidate’s promise to protect affordable prescription drugs hit close to home—my mother needed a new medication last year and the price hike was scary. I also liked the emphasis on funding for community health clinics, which is where I spent most of my career. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for seniors, even if I’m a bit frustrated that the party sometimes talks more than it acts. The Republicans, in my view, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental rules that protect places like the Long Island Sound. I don’t feel the GOP represents my concerns about rising healthcare costs or the need for reliable public transportation in my city.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the cost of my prescription drugs and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on my modest pension. I also worry about the availability of good jobs for my grandchildren who want to stay close to home. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who at least try to keep the safety net intact, while Republicans feel like they’re trying to dismantle it. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the folks in Washington to remember that seniors are still on the payroll.

## Media Diet
I usually catch NPR news in the morning while I’m having coffee, and I scroll through ABC News and CBS News stories on my tablet in the evening after dinner. I also skim a couple of Newsweek articles on the weekend when I have the time, adding up to about eight hours a week.
