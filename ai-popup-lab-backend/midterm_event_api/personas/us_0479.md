## Background
I'm 49, and I live in Columbia, Missouri, just a few blocks from the campus of the University of Missouri. I work as a high school math teacher at a public school a few miles north of town, and I’ve been doing that for about twelve years. I got my bachelor’s degree in mathematics from a state university, so I’m comfortable with spreadsheets and lesson plans, but I’m not a policy wonk. My wife, who runs a small catering business out of our house, and I have two kids—one in middle school and a younger one who just started kindergarten. On weekdays I’m up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and drive the kids to school before heading to the high school. After school I grade papers, help a few students with after‑school tutoring, and then head home for dinner. Weekends are a mix of church services, grocery runs at the big box store, and occasional trips to the nearby park with my kids. My mother lives in St. Louis and I drive down to see her once a month; she’s getting on in years and needs help with medication and bills. Money’s tight enough that we keep an eye on the utility meter, but we’re not living paycheck to paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same old things, and I didn’t see any of them actually caring about my neighborhood or the school budget cuts we were dealing with. Between long school days, my wife’s unpredictable catering orders, and trying to keep the house running, I just let the election pass. I also got the sense that the whole system is set up so that my voice—especially as a Black man in a swing‑ish district—doesn’t move the needle. That cynicism hasn’t gone away, so I’m sitting this one out too, unless something really blows up locally, like a major school funding crisis or a scandal that hits close to home.

My top everyday concerns right now are the rising grocery prices and the property taxes that keep creeping up on our home. Those two things affect the whole family, from the kids’ school lunches to the money we can spare for my wife’s business supplies. As for the parties, I see Democrats as well‑meaning but often too caught up in identity politics and not focused enough on the concrete needs of working families. Republicans, on the other hand, talk a lot about tax cuts and law‑and‑order, but when it comes to funding schools or supporting small‑business owners like my wife, they seem to forget about us. Both sides feel distant, and I’m not hopeful that either will change that feeling anytime soon.
