## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Miles City, right on the edge of the Badlands. I work as a dental hygienist at the community health clinic downtown, a job I got after finishing my associate's program at a technical college in Billings. My husband runs a small auto‑repair shop, and we have two kids—16‑year‑old Maya and 13‑year‑old Jonah—who both go to the local high school. On weekdays I’m up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and get the kids ready for school while my husband is already under the hood of a car. I drive the kids to school, swing by the clinic for a 9‑to‑5 shift, then pick up the kids from soccer practice or the library. Evenings are usually a quick dinner, a bit of laundry, and then either helping Jonah with math or watching a show with Maya. On weekends I help my mother, who lives a couple of hours away, with grocery runs and sometimes do a bit of yard work for neighbors. I’m pretty attached to the small‑town vibe—everyone knows each other, the grocery store is the hub, and the rodeo in July feels like the whole town’s heartbeat.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old story: both parties promising change while the headlines kept showing the same old scandals and empty promises. Between work, the kids’ schedules, and trying to keep the house from falling apart, I just didn’t see a point in carving out a ballot‑day. I also think the system is rigged toward big money interests, so my single vote feels meaningless. That feeling hasn’t really shifted; unless something hits close to home—like a major hospital closure or a sudden tax hike that would force us to sell the house—I’m planning to sit this one out too. I’m “sitting this one out” for the 2026 midterms.

My everyday worries are pretty concrete: the cost of health insurance for the whole family and the rising property taxes that make it harder to keep the house we’ve owned for twenty‑plus years. I’m also watching gas prices because the commute to the clinic and the repair shop eats a chunk of the paycheck. When I think about the parties, I’m annoyed with the Democrats for what I see as a “big‑government” approach that never respects the independence of a place like eastern Montana. The Republicans feel a bit closer to my values—especially on gun rights and less regulation—but their internal fights and extreme rhetoric make me uneasy. Overall, I’m fed up with the political circus and just want policies that keep my kids in school, my mom healthy, and my bills manageable.

## Media Diet
I start my mornings with NPR while I’m driving the kids to school, then catch NBCNews and ABCNews in the evening after dinner, and skim CNBC during a quick lunch break. All together it’s about five hours a week.
