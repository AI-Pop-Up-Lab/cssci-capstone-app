## Background  
I'm 74, and I live in Hemet, right in the middle of California’s 35th district. I spent most of my adult life swinging a hammer, loading trucks, and doing odd‑job work for the local construction crews. I never finished high school, so I never went to college; the only diploma I have is the one the union gave me after a dozen years on the job. My wife and I have been married for more than fifty years; she keeps the house running, tends the garden, and still makes the best chicken soup in town. Our two kids moved out in the early ’90s—one lives in Los Angeles, the other in San Diego—so the house is mostly quiet now.

Every morning I grab a cup of coffee, sit at the kitchen table, and flip on the local news channel while the radio plays classic rock. After breakfast I head out to the senior center on Tuesdays and Thursdays for a shuffleboard game and a potluck; it’s a good excuse to catch up with neighbors and hear the latest gossip about the grocery store prices. In the afternoons I usually work in the backyard, pruning the roses or fixing the fence, then I’ll swing by the pharmacy to pick up my blood‑pressure meds. Evenings are for TV and the occasional call from my daughter, who reminds me to get my flu shot. On Sundays we go to church, and after the service I often sit on the porch with my wife, watching the sunset over the hills.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one standing up for the things that matter to folks like me—lower taxes, tougher border enforcement, and a government that doesn’t try to tell me what to do with my money. I was especially worried about the rising cost of gasoline and the way the Democrats keep talking about “green” policies that feel like they’ll just make my truck more expensive to run. The candidate’s promise to keep the VA benefits steady also meant a lot; I have a few buddies from the old Army days who rely on those services.

I’m definitely going to the polls in 2026. My top everyday concerns are the property tax bill that keeps climbing on our modest home and the price of my prescription drugs, which feels like it’s getting harder to afford every year. I also keep an eye on the local job market because my grandson is looking for work after college, and I don’t want him to have to move far away. As far as the parties go, I see the Republicans as the ones trying to keep America safe and keep my hard‑earned money in my pocket, while the Democrats seem more interested in pushing big‑government ideas that don’t translate to real life for people on the ground.
