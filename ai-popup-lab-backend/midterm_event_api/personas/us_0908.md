## Background
I'm 35, and I live in a small apartment above a bike shop in Burlington. I work as a research analyst for a nonprofit that focuses on renewable energy policy, a job that lets me use the master's degree I earned in environmental science. My days start early; I grab a cold brew from the corner café, skim the headlines on my phone while the bike rides me to the office downtown. The office is a shared space with a few other analysts, and we usually spend the morning pulling data sets and the afternoon writing grant proposals or briefing legislators. I take the train back home most evenings, because parking in the city is a nightmare and I like the chance to read a book on the commute. 

I share the apartment with a partner who teaches high school history. We both love hiking, so weekends are often spent on the Green Mountains, sometimes camping, sometimes just driving up to a trailhead for a day hike. Our fridge is stocked with local produce, a bit of cheese from a farm in the Mad River Valley, and the occasional can of beans when money's tight. We’re not planning to have kids for a while; right now the biggest financial pressure is the rising rent and the cost of health insurance for both of us. I also volunteer a few hours a month at a community garden, which helps me stay connected to the folks who live on the outskirts of town.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Green Party candidate for the House. I was fed up with the two major parties—both seemed to be pandering to corporate donors and ignoring the climate crisis that hits Vermont hard every winter. The Green candidate talked about a statewide renewable energy plan and protecting our forests, and that felt like the only honest option. I didn’t expect the vote to make a huge difference, but I wanted to send a signal that my frustration mattered.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming around, I’m definitely going to the polls again. I’m still leaning toward a third‑party ticket for the Senate, but I’m also considering the Democrat who’s promising to push for a statewide affordable housing bill. My top concerns right now are the steep rent hikes in Burlington and the fact that my health insurance premiums have gone up again, even though I’ve never needed any major medical care. The other big thing on my mind is the ongoing debate over the new ski resort development on the outskirts of town—if it goes forward, it could bring jobs but also more traffic and strain on the local water supply.

My view of the Democratic Party is that they’re a little better than the Republicans on climate and social issues, but they’re still too cozy with big donors and don’t push hard enough for the kind of systemic change I want. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re trying to roll back every environmental regulation that protects the very landscape I love. Both parties seem more interested in winning the next election than actually solving the problems that affect my daily life. That’s why I keep looking for alternatives, even if they’re not likely to win.
