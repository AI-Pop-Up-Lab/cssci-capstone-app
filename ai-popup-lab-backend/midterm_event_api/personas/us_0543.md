## Background
I'm 75, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Minot, North Dakota, right in the 1st congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working as a grocery store clerk, a job I kept after raising two kids and finishing a couple of years of community college. I never went on to a degree, but the courses I took helped me manage the register and keep the aisles stocked. My husband passed away five years ago, so it's just me now, though I get regular visits from my daughter who lives in Seattle and a grandchild who comes down for the summer. My days start early; I brew a cup of coffee, read the local paper online, and take my old Labrador for a short walk around the cul‑desac. After that I usually spend a few hours tending to the garden, checking my mail, and watching a morning news program. I’m active in the senior center, where I lead a knitting circle and help coordinate the weekly bingo night. Sundays are for church, a potluck afterward, and then a quiet afternoon watching classic movies. I drive a reliable old sedan, keep my Medicare cards handy, and try to stay on top of my prescription refills. Money’s tight on a fixed income, so I’m careful about utilities and grocery bills, but I still enjoy the occasional trip to the local diner with friends.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Medicare, prescription drug prices, and protecting Social Security. I was also convinced by the candidate’s promise to invest in broadband for rural areas, which would make it easier for me to video‑chat with my daughter. The campaign ads I saw on the news and the flyers at the senior center reinforced that feeling, and I was tired of hearing about tax cuts for the wealthy while my own costs kept climbing. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the farm subsidies and the recent push for a new highway project that would cut through a nearby wildlife preserve—I’m not thrilled with every decision, but I still think the Democrats are the better bet for seniors like me.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and making sure my property taxes don’t skyrocket as the county looks to fund new infrastructure. I’m also watching the debate over broadband expansion because without reliable internet I feel cut off from family and services. When I think about the parties, the Republicans feel like they’re more interested in deregulating industry and cutting taxes for big corporations, which doesn’t help me at the pharmacy or with my heating bill. The Democrats, on the other hand, keep talking about protecting the safety net, even if they sometimes get caught up in cultural fights that feel distant from my daily worries. I’m not a political scholar; I just want the folks in Washington to remember there are people like me who rely on steady programs and affordable care.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR while I’m having my coffee, catch ABC News in the early evening, and skim Newsweek articles during lunch; it adds up to about ten hours a week.
