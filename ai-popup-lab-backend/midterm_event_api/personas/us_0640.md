## Background  
I'm 55 and work as a construction laborer for a regional contractor that does a lot of highway repair around the state. I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Las Cruces, close enough to the highway so I can get to the job site without a long commute. My wife, Maria, runs a small laundry business out of the garage, and our two kids are out of the house—one is in community college and the other just started an apprenticeship as an electrician. Most mornings I get up around 5:30 a.m., pour a cup of coffee, and read the headlines on my phone while the kettle boils. After a quick breakfast of eggs and tortillas, I head out in my pickup, spend the day on the road, and come back around 6 p.m. to fix dinner, watch a bit of TV, and help my son with his math homework when he’s home. Weekends are a mix of family chores, a few trips to the local market, and catching a game at the bar with a few longtime friends. I don’t have a college degree; most of what I know about the world comes from on‑the‑job experience and the news I soak up in my spare time.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who are trying to keep a roof over our heads and put food on the table. The Democrats kept talking about big‑government programs that never seemed to reach the back‑yard, while the GOP talked about lower taxes, fewer regulations on construction, and a tougher stance on illegal immigration that’s been a daily headache in our border‑adjacent community. I also liked the promise of keeping the oil and gas jobs that help the local economy.  

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the economy and immigration. The price of gasoline has gone up a lot, and my wife’s laundry business is feeling the pinch from higher utility bills. I’m still convinced that the Republican approach to cutting red tape and supporting small businesses is the best bet, even though I’m a little annoyed that the party sometimes gets caught up in internal fights that distract from real issues. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to let the other side take control and undo the few things that have helped us stay afloat.  

My biggest everyday worries are the cost of groceries and the price at the pump—both have been climbing faster than my paycheck. I also worry about property taxes; the county has been looking at raising rates to fund schools, and that would hit us hard. As for the parties, I see Democrats as out of touch with working‑class folks, always promising handouts that never materialize. Republicans, for all their squabbles, at least talk about the things that matter to me: jobs, lower taxes, and a firm border. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the government to stop making things harder for people who are already working hard.
