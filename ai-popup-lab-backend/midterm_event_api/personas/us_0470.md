## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Sedalia, Missouri, right in the 3rd congressional district. I spent most of my adult life as an elementary school teacher; I got my bachelor's in education from the University of Missouri and retired ten years ago. My husband passed away five years back, and my two grown children live in St. Louis and Texas, so the house is mostly quiet. I get a small pension and Social Security, and I supplement that by doing occasional tutoring for kids at the local library. My days start early: I brew a cup of café de olla, read the paper, and then water the tomatoes and peppers in the backyard. After a light breakfast I head to the library to shelve books or help a teenager with a math worksheet. In the afternoons I take a walk along the creek, stop at the grocery store for a few items, and sometimes swing by my sister’s place to help her with her medication. Evenings are for watching the news, knitting, and the occasional video call with my daughter. I don’t drive much anymore; the bus line takes me to the downtown pharmacy and the community center where I attend a weekly bingo night.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were all the same: big promises that never seemed to reach people like me. The campaign ads were loud and negative, and I was busy caring for my elderly mother who needed constant medical appointments. I also thought my vote wouldn't change anything, especially since the district has been leaning one way for years. That feeling of cynicism stuck, and I haven't been motivated to get back into the habit. For the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out too, unless something dramatic happens—like a local hospital closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits my fixed income hard. My biggest everyday worries are the grocery bills that keep creeping up and the cost of my prescription drugs. I also worry about my property taxes; the county keeps raising rates, and my pension doesn't stretch as far as it used to. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as talking a lot about climate and immigration but not really understanding the day‑to‑day struggles of a Hispanic senior in a small Missouri town. The Republicans feel more focused on culture wars and cutting taxes for big businesses, which sounds nice until you realize those cuts often mean less funding for community services I rely on. Both sides feel distant, and most of the time I just want someone to listen to folks like me who are trying to make ends meet on a fixed income.

## Media Diet
I usually listen to NPR in the morning while I water the garden, and I check ABC News and CBS News in the evening after dinner, squeezing about eight hours of news into the week.
