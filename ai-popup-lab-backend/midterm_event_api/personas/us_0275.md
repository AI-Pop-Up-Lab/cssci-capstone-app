## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Kankakee, Illinois, right in the heart of the 9th district. I grew up working at the local meat‑packing plant and stayed there for most of my adult life. I retired about five years ago after a back injury, but I still do odd jobs—fixing fences, helping neighbors with small repairs—to keep a little extra cash coming in. My wife passed away three years back, so it's just me and my two adult kids who live in Chicago. My daughter calls me every Sunday, and we meet up for lunch at the senior center’s cafeteria once a month.

My days are pretty routine. I get up early, make a strong cup of coffee, and flip on the TV while I read the newspaper. After that I head to the senior center for a bowling league or a bingo game, then swing by the grocery store before heading home to watch a bit more TV or work on a DIY project. I take the bus to the clinic for my blood‑pressure meds, and on Saturdays I help out at the local church’s food pantry. I’m pretty set in my habits; the biggest change lately has been the new grocery‑store layout that forces me to walk farther, which aches my knees.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about the things that keep me up at night—my medication bills, the rising cost of food, and the fact that my property tax keeps creeping up while my Social Security check stays the same. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I was too worn out from dealing with my health and the occasional car trouble to even bother getting to the polls. I also didn’t trust the promises; it seemed like every year they’d talk a good game and then forget about folks like me once the election was over.

I’m sitting this one out, too. Unless something dramatic happens—like a local hospital closing or a big hike in property taxes that hits my budget hard—I don’t see a reason to make the trip. My top concerns right now are keeping my health‑care costs manageable and making sure I can afford groceries without having to skip my blood‑pressure meds. I’m pretty annoyed with both parties: the Democrats keep shouting about racial justice but rarely mention the squeeze on senior benefits, while the Republicans keep pushing tax cuts that never trickle down to someone on a fixed income. Neither side feels like they’re listening to the everyday grind of a retired Black worker in a small Illinois town.
