## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Goodland, a small town on the western edge of Kansas in the 1st district. I spent 35 years teaching high school English and retired three years ago, so most of my days now revolve around the house, the garden, and the community center where I volunteer to run a reading program for kids. My wife passed away five years ago, and I have two grown children—one lives in Wichita, the other in Denver—who call me every few weeks. I get up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and flip through the local paper while the house is still quiet. After breakfast I tend to the tomatoes and the few rows of beans I keep in the backyard; it’s the one thing that keeps me moving and gives me a reason to be outside. Around noon I head to the senior center for a bingo game or a short talk on Medicare updates, then I’m back home for a sandwich and a bit of TV. Evenings are usually spent on the porch, listening to an NPR podcast or scrolling through the ABC News app while I wait for the grandchildren’s video calls. On Sundays I go to church, then spend the afternoon at the community garden or helping the local library set up a book drive.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one actually looking out for people like me—retirees who depend on Social Security and Medicare. The candidate’s promise to protect prescription‑drug pricing hit home; I’ve been fighting to keep my blood‑pressure meds affordable for years. I also appreciated the emphasis on funding public schools, even though I’m retired; I still think a decent education is the backbone of any decent community. I wasn’t thrilled with the whole campaign circus, but I felt my vote mattered more than a shrug.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m not a political junkie, but I can’t stand the idea of letting the other side run unchecked, especially when they keep pushing cuts to Medicaid and try to roll back voting‑rights protections that matter to Black voters in my district. My top everyday concerns are keeping my Social Security check intact and making sure my prescription costs don’t skyrocket. I also worry about the local job market—lots of farms are consolidating, and younger folks are leaving town, which makes it harder for my grandchildren’s families to find work if they ever move back.

When it comes to the parties, I’m leaning heavily toward the Democrats. They’ve at least tried to keep the safety‑net programs alive, even if the execution is messy. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about tax cuts for the rich and “family values” while ignoring the real bills that land on my kitchen table. I know both sides have flaws, but I’m more annoyed by the GOP’s refusal to address health‑care costs for seniors. So, I’m voting for the Democratic Senate and House candidates this November.
