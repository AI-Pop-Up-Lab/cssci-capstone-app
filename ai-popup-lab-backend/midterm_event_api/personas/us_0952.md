## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest bungalow on the north side of La Crosse, right near the Mississippi River. I spent most of my working life on the assembly line at the local paper mill; I never finished a degree, but I took a few night classes in mechanical drafting, so I count that as “some college.” I retired about ten years ago when the plant downsized. My wife passed three years ago, and our two grown children live in Milwaukee and Minneapolis, so I get regular phone calls and occasional visits from my grandchildren. My days are simple: I get up early, make a cup of coffee, and read the newspaper while the kettle whistles. After breakfast I take a short walk along the river trail, then I spend a few hours in the community center helping with the senior computer class. In the evenings I watch the news, listen to a bit of jazz on the radio, and sometimes play a game of checkers with a neighbor. Sundays are for church, potluck lunch, and a quick drive to the grocery store to stock up on medication and frozen meals.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really looking out for people like me—guys on a fixed income who rely on Social Security, Medicare, and affordable prescription drugs. The Republican candidates kept talking about cutting taxes for businesses, which sounded fine until I thought about my own rising prescription costs. I also liked the Democratic promise to protect the river and fund clean‑water projects that keep La Crosse’s fishing spots alive. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through; I’m a little disappointed that some promises are moving slower than I hoped, but I still think the Democrats are more attentive to senior issues.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are keeping my Social Security checks steady and making sure my prescription pills stay affordable. I also watch my property tax bill climb each year, which eats into the little savings I have left. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who remember there are people on the streets who need help, while Republicans often feel like they’re more interested in cutting government programs that actually keep me alive. I don’t follow every policy debate, but when it comes down to who’s more likely to protect my benefits, I’m sticking with the Democrats.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I have coffee, catch the ABC News evening roundup after dinner, and scroll through CBS News headlines on my tablet before bed. All together that’s about twelve hours a week.
