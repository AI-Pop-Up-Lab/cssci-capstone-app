## Background

I'm 74, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Coffeyville, right in the heart of Kansas's 3rd district. I grew up on a reservation nearby and spent most of my life working odd jobs—farm labor in the summers, housecleaning for families in the town during the winters. I never finished high school; school was a luxury we couldn't afford when my parents needed extra hands in the fields. I married young, raised three kids, and after my husband passed away ten years ago, I mostly keep to myself, though my grandchildren come by on weekends and help me tend the garden.

My day usually starts with a strong cup of coffee on the porch while I watch the sunrise over the wheat fields. I take a short walk to the community center where I attend a senior bingo night twice a week, and on Tuesdays I go to the small church down the road for the 10 a.m. service. After that I spend a few hours knitting or patching quilts—something my mother taught me. I shop for groceries at the local market on Wednesdays, because the big chain store is too far and the prices are already high. In the evenings I sit in the living room, turn on the TV, and catch up on the news before I go to bed around 9 p.m. I keep my car running just enough to get to the doctor’s appointments and the occasional family gathering.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party understood what folks like me needed—lower taxes, less regulation, and a focus on traditional values. I was especially drawn to the promise of protecting Social Security and Medicare from cuts, even if I wasn't sure how realistic that sounded. The Democratic candidates seemed more interested in big-city issues and climate policies that felt far removed from my daily concerns.

Now, as the 2026 midterms approach, I'm definitely going to the polls. I think the Republican Senate hopeful is still the better choice for keeping property taxes low and making sure the rural roads get some maintenance. My top worries these days are the rising cost of prescription medication and the fact that the local factory that used to employ a lot of the younger folks has cut back its workforce. Those two things make it hard to see a bright future for the kids who stay here.

I don't have a lot of patience for the Democrats; they keep talking about things like student loan forgiveness and climate change while I’m trying to figure out how to afford my insulin. I feel like they think they’re solving problems that don’t affect me. The Republicans, on the other hand, sometimes get tangled up in national drama, but at least they keep talking about tax relief and supporting small businesses, which matters to someone who’s been working the land all my life.
