## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Bismarck, just a short drive from the Three Affiliated Tribes' community center. I retired five years ago after a 35‑year career as a high school social studies teacher, a job I got after finishing my bachelor's in education at the University of North Dakota. My wife, Ellen, still works part‑time at the local library, and we have two grown‑up kids who live in Minneapolis and Seattle. My oldest daughter visits every summer with her kids, and we all help out with the annual powwow prep. Most mornings I start with a cup of black coffee while the radio plays the news, then I drive the kids to the senior center where I volunteer teaching basic computer skills. Afternoons are usually spent checking on the garden, fixing up the old shed, or meeting with the tribal council on issues like broadband access. Evenings are simple: dinner, a bit of TV, and then a walk around the neighborhood before bed. I keep a small stash of cigarettes for when the wind’s too cold to sit outside, and I still enjoy the occasional fishing trip on the Missouri River when the weather lets up.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate promised to protect Medicaid expansion and to push for more funding to tribal health clinics. After the pandemic, I saw a lot of my friends on the reservation struggle to get their prescriptions, and the Democratic platform felt like the only one actually listening to those concerns. I also liked the promise of infrastructure money to fix the aging bridges that connect our community to the rest of the state. Over the past two years I’ve kept an eye on how the new administration follows through, and while there have been some delays, I think they’re still moving in the right direction. I’m definitely going to the polls again for the 2026 midterms – “I’m voting.” My top everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the lack of reliable broadband, which makes it hard for my grandchildren to keep up with schoolwork and for elders to tele‑visit doctors. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least try to understand tribal sovereignty and social safety nets, while the Republicans often talk about “fiscal responsibility” but ignore how their policies can hurt the very communities that rely on federal aid. I’m not a fan of the constant bickering, but when it comes down to it, I feel the Democrats are more aligned with what matters to me right now.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR’s morning news podcast while I’m driving, catch the ABC News evening broadcast, and skim Newsweek articles online in the evenings – that adds up to about ten hours a week.
