## Background
I'm 45 and I work as a maintenance supervisor for a regional hospital in the town of St. Albans, which sits just north of the Ohio River in the 2nd district. I earned a bachelor's in mechanical engineering from West Virginia State University, but after a few years in a design firm I liked the hands‑on stuff more, so I moved into facilities management. My wife, who runs a small daycare out of our house, and I have two kids—Emily, 16, who drives a beat‑up sedan to school in Charleston, and Marcus, 12, who plays basketball at the community center. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30, grab a coffee, and drive the 20‑minute commute while listening to the radio. At work I walk the halls, check HVAC systems, and coordinate the night‑shift crew. Evenings are usually dinner with the family, a quick check of the kids’ homework, and then I either watch a game or catch up on the news before heading to bed around 11. On Sundays we go to church, then sometimes help out with the church’s food pantry. On the occasional Saturday I help my brother with a construction project or we go hunting in the nearby woods.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was more realistic about keeping the coal and natural‑gas jobs that still power a lot of our local economy. The Democratic candidates kept talking about “green energy” and higher taxes, which sounded like a threat to the paycheck that supports my family. I also liked the emphasis on law‑and‑order and the promise to protect the Second Amendment; those issues feel personal when you live in a place where a lot of folks own rifles for hunting and protection. Since then I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening, but the same concerns are still top of my mind. The price of gasoline still hurts, and the hospital where I work is dealing with staffing cuts that make my job harder. I’m planning to vote again in the 2026 midterms because the Senate race has a candidate who’s pushing for more federal aid to the Appalachian region, and I want to see if that translates into real jobs. My top everyday worries are keeping the lights on without the utility bill blowing up and making sure my kids can afford college without taking on a mountain of debt. I see the Democrats as out of touch with the realities of a state that still depends on mining and manufacturing, while I think the GOP, even when it’s messy, at least talks about keeping those industries alive. I’m not a political scholar; I just want policies that keep the paycheck steady and the community safe.
