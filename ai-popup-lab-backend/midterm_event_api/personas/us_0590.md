## Background
I'm 50 and I work as a high school English teacher in Derry, right in the heart of New Hampshire's 2nd district. My husband runs a small auto‑repair shop downtown, and we have two kids – a 22‑year‑old college senior who just moved back for a summer internship, and a 16‑year‑old sophomore who still rides the school bus. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., making coffee, packing lunches, and scrolling through the news on my phone while the kids get ready. I drive the 15‑minute commute to the public high school where I teach literature and occasionally coach the debate team. After school I grade papers, meet with parents, and sometimes stay late for faculty meetings. Evenings are usually spent at home: dinner with the family, helping the younger one with homework, and then a quick Netflix episode or a chat with friends on a group text. On weekends I help my husband with inventory, run errands at the local grocery, and hike the nearby trails when the weather's nice. I belong to the local PTA and volunteer a few hours at the community center, mostly doing book‑donation drives.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to public‑school funding and affordable health care, both of which hit my life directly. The Republican platform seemed to downplay the importance of education budgets, and I was worried about the ripple effects on my classroom and my kids' future. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding broadband in rural parts of New Hampshire, which would help my husband’s business and give my students better internet access. Since then I’ve stayed fairly engaged – I’ve attended a town hall on the new state budget and signed a petition to keep funding for the community center. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t see any reason to sit this one out unless something truly crazy happens. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax hikes that are squeezing our household budget. I’m also keeping an eye on the local job market because my husband’s shop relies on a steady flow of customers. I tend to view Democrats as the party that at least pretends to care about the middle class, while I see Republicans as more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and less interested in funding public services. That’s a pretty blunt take, but it’s how I see it right now.
