## Background
I'm 60, and I work as a public‑health researcher for a regional health system that partners with the university in Nashville, a suburb of Nashua in the 2nd congressional district. My day starts around 6 am with a quick coffee while scrolling the headlines on NPR, then I head to the office where I spend most of my time pulling data on vaccination rates and writing grant reports. I’m married to a retired electrician; we both grew up in the Northeast and moved to New Hampshire after college because the mountains and the small‑town feel reminded us of home. Our two kids are in their late‑20s, living in Boston and Portland, so the house is mostly quiet except for the occasional visit from my sister‑in‑law who helps with grocery trips. I’m a part‑time Spanish tutor for community‑college students, which lets me stay connected to the Hispanic community that first welcomed my family when we settled here. After work I usually walk the nearby river trail, stop by the local farmer’s market for fresh produce, and in the evenings I either cook dinner or watch a documentary on health policy. Weekends are a mix of church services, a bit of gardening, and helping out at the neighborhood food pantry.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party’s platform on expanding health coverage and funding for community health centers aligned with the work I do every day. I also appreciated the candidate’s stance on protecting Social Security, which is something I’m starting to think about as I near retirement. The campaign felt relatively calm in my district, and I didn’t have any major doubts about the nominee’s competence, so I turned out without much fanfare. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies are affecting Medicaid reimbursements and I’m still convinced that a strong federal role is necessary to keep my patients covered. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I plan to cast my ballot for the Democratic Senate hopeful and the House candidate who promised to keep funding for the state’s public‑health initiatives. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that are making it harder for longtime homeowners like me to stay put. I tend to view the Democratic Party as the one that actually tries to address those issues, even if the messaging sometimes feels too polished. The Republicans, on the other hand, come across as more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that keep our hospitals safe, which feels out of touch with my reality.
