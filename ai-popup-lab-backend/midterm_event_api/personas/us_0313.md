## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest ranch house on the outskirts of Pittsburg, Kansas, right in the 4th congressional district. I spent most of my working life at the local meat‑packing plant, starting as a line worker and eventually moving up to shift supervisor after taking a few night classes at a community college. I never finished a degree, but the hands‑on experience kept me busy until I retired at 65. My wife passed away three years ago, and now I split my days between tending the small vegetable garden out back, fixing up the old pickup truck, and checking in on my grandson when he visits from Denver. I’m a regular at the senior center on Tuesdays for bingo and the occasional card game, and I attend the Methodist service at the church down the road every Sunday. Mornings start with a strong cup of coffee and the local paper, then a walk around the block before I settle down to watch the news or read something on my tablet. Evenings are usually spent watching the game on TV or listening to a talk radio program while I finish a crossword puzzle.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑town values I grew up with. The candidate promised to support agriculture subsidies that keep the local farms afloat and to push back against what I see as over‑reaching regulations that hurt small businesses like the plant I used to work at. I also appreciated the emphasis on a strong military and a hard line on immigration, which I think keeps our communities safe. Since then, I’ve watched the news and heard folks at the senior center talk about rising prescription drug prices and the strain on Social Security. Those issues are front‑and‑center for me, but I still believe that the GOP is more likely to protect my pension and keep the cost of living from spiraling out of control. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve already printed the ballot and plan to drop it off at the post office. My top everyday concerns are making sure my Social Security check doesn’t get cut and that I can afford the rising cost of groceries and gas. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for higher taxes and bigger government programs that feel out of touch with folks like me who have paid our dues for decades. I’m not a political scholar, but I feel the Republican side respects the work ethic I lived by, while the other side seems more focused on urban issues that don’t affect my life here in rural Kansas.
