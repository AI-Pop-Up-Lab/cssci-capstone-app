## Background

I'm 75, and I live in a modest house on a quiet street in Mount Laurel, right in the middle of NJ‑8. I spent a couple of years at a community college taking business admin courses and then worked most of my adult life as a clerical assistant for a local insurance agency. I retired ten years ago, but I still go into the office a few mornings a week to help with filing and to keep my mind busy. My husband passed away five years back, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live out of state. I get calls from my daughter every Sunday afternoon, and my grandson visits us in the summer. My day usually starts with a cup of coffee and the morning paper, then a short walk around the neighborhood before I head to the office. In the evenings I tend to watch a bit of TV, catch up on the news sites on my tablet, and sometimes knit while listening to the radio. I try to stay active at the senior center a few times a week, playing bridge and helping with the community garden.

## Politics and this election

In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one looking out for my fixed income and the taxes that keep climbing on my property. The candidate promised to protect Social Security and keep the cost of my prescription meds down, which mattered more to me than the talk about climate policy or big‑government programs. I also liked the emphasis on law and order; after the break‑ins on my street a few years ago, I wanted someone who would take crime seriously.

For the 2026 midterms I'm definitely voting. I keep hearing about changes to Medicare and a proposed property tax hike in the county, and I can't afford to sit on the sidelines. My top everyday concerns are my Social Security check staying stable and the price of groceries going up faster than my pension. I also worry about the local job market because my son’s construction business is feeling the squeeze, and that trickles down to the whole community.

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward. The Republicans, for the most part, understand that older folks like me need predictable benefits and lower taxes. The Democrats seem to want to spend more on programs that don’t directly affect my bill, and I get frustrated when they talk about raising taxes to fund things I don’t see. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the side that looks out for seniors and keeps the cost of living from spiraling.
