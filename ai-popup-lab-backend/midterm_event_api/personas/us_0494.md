## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Tupelo, right near the old railway line. I spent most of my working life as a maintenance supervisor at a regional manufacturing plant, a job I could get with the two‑year technical degree I earned at a community college in Jackson. The plant closed a few years back, so now I do a bit of handyman work for neighbors and take on occasional contracts fixing HVAC units. My wife, who used to work as a school secretary, retired last year, and our two grown children live in Birmingham and Dallas, visiting a few times a year. Most mornings I start with a cup of strong coffee while the radio plays classic country, then I head out to the local grocery store to pick up fresh produce and check the price tags on milk and bread. After lunch, I usually spend a couple of hours in the garage tinkering with an old Chevy truck I’m trying to keep running. In the evenings I sit on the porch, watch the news on my old TV, and sometimes chat with the next‑door neighbor about the weather or the latest high school football game. Sundays are for church, potluck dinners, and catching up on family calls.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑business environment that let me keep doing odd jobs after the plant shut down. The candidate’s promises about cutting regulations on trade and keeping the border secure resonated with my own worries about jobs slipping away and the community changing faster than I’m comfortable with. I also liked the emphasis on traditional values, which line up with the church teachings I grew up with. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have affected my pocketbook—gas prices have crept up, and the cost of prescription meds has become a bigger headache. Still, I think the GOP is the better bet for keeping the cost of living from spiraling, even if the Senate’s been a mess lately. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t plan to sit this one out. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of gasoline, which makes my trips to the hardware store and the occasional road trips to see my kids more expensive, and the growing cost of health care, especially the co‑pays for my wife’s medication. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones trying to keep the government from getting too big and interfering with ordinary folks’ lives, while the Democrats seem more interested in expanding programs that end up raising taxes for people like me. I’m not a political scholar, but I get the sense the two sides are stuck in a tug‑of‑war over who gets to decide what’s best for the average worker.
