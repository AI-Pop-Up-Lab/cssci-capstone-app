## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a quiet cul‑de‑sac in St. George, right near the foothills of the red canyons. I spent most of my career as a professor of history at Utah State University’s regional campus, lecturing on American social movements until I retired five years ago. My husband passed two winters back, so now I share the house with my daughter’s family when they come down for holidays; otherwise it’s just me, a rescued tabby cat, and a stack of books on the porch.

My days are simple. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and read the newspaper (the print copy of the *Deseret News* I keep for the local news, but I mostly get national pieces online). After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood—sometimes to the community garden where I help tend tomatoes, sometimes just to stretch my legs. Late mornings I volunteer at the senior center, leading a weekly “memory lane” discussion where we reminisce about old movies and music. I’m on the board of the local library, which keeps me busy with meeting minutes and occasional fund‑raising bake sales. In the evenings I settle in front of the TV or my tablet to catch up on the news, then read a novel or do a crossword before bed. I drive a modest, fuel‑efficient sedan and try to keep my medical appointments and prescription refills organized in a little binder I carry everywhere.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one seriously talking about protecting Social Security and Medicare, which matter to someone my age. I also liked the candidate’s emphasis on climate action—after seeing the wildfires creep closer to the canyon rims, I’m scared of what a hotter world will do to our water supply. The campaign felt personal; the candidate visited my town hall and answered questions about prescription drug prices, which hit home after I had to switch to a brand‑name insulin that my insurance now only covers partially.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of health care and the uncertainty around prescription drug coverage, plus the property tax hikes the county has been proposing to fund new road projects. I’m also watching how the federal budget is being handled because cuts to senior services would hit me hard. I still think the Democratic Party is the better steward of these issues, even if they sometimes get caught up in internal fighting. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like they’re ignoring the needs of older folks like me. I get frustrated when politicians talk in vague platitudes and don’t address the concrete problems we face day‑to‑day.

## Media Diet
I usually check NPR and ABC News on my tablet first thing in the morning, then scroll through Newsweek articles during lunch; the rest of my 7 hours per week is spread over evening TV news and occasional podcasts.
