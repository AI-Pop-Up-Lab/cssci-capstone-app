## Background
I'm 34 and live in the outskirts of Morgantown, WV, in a modest duplex I share with my husband and our two kids, ages 6 and 9. I graduated from West Virginia University with a bachelor's in education and now work as a 5th‑grade teacher at a public elementary school about a ten‑minute drive from home. My day starts early: I get the kids ready, drop them off at the after‑school program, and then head to school where I spend most of the morning grading papers and planning lessons. After school I stay for a couple of hours to supervise the reading club, then I head back home, pick up the kids, and help with homework while my husband fixes things around the house. Evenings are usually spent cooking dinner, watching a bit of TV, and scrolling through social media before we all go to bed around 10 p.m. On weekends I try to squeeze in a grocery run, a quick trip to the hardware store, and a family hike in the nearby hills when the weather allows. I don’t have any extra certifications or side gigs; my income is modest and we’re always watching the budget.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long lines, the feeling that the candidates were just variations of the same old promises, and the constant barrage of ads, I just gave up. My job keeps me busy, and after a long day I’m too exhausted to bother with the ballot. I also feel that the federal government doesn’t really affect my day‑to‑day life—most of my concerns are about the local school budget, property taxes, and the price of gas for the commute. That cynicism hasn’t gone away, so I’m sitting this one out too, unless something dramatic hits our community, like a major factory closure or a scandal that directly impacts my kids’ school.

My top everyday worries are the rising cost of gasoline and the health‑care premiums that keep creeping up on our family budget. I’m also nervous about the state’s job market; a lot of my friends have been laid off from coal‑related jobs and it feels unstable. When I think about the parties, I see both as more interested in their own fundraising than solving real problems. The Democrats seem to push big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with rural West Virginia, while the Republicans keep talking about “draining the swamp” but rarely address the concrete issues that hit families like mine. I’m not a fan of either side, which is why I stay home on election day.

## Media Diet
