## Background
I'm 45 and work as a program manager for a regional nonprofit that runs after‑school tutoring and job‑training programs in the Jefferson City area. I grew up in St. Louis, went to college at Washington University and later earned a master's in public administration. My wife and I bought a modest ranch‑style house in a quiet neighborhood of Fulton, about a half‑hour drive from the capital, and we have two kids—Jaden, 12, and Maya, 9. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., making coffee, checking the kids’ school portal, and loading the minivan. I drive into town, spend the day juggling grant reports, meeting with city officials, and coordinating volunteers. After work I often pick up the kids from soccer, help with homework, and we eat dinner together around 6:30 p.m. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of news, scrolling social media, or catching a late‑night show before I’m in bed by 10. Weekends are a mix of church services, family barbecues, and sometimes a quick trip to the grocery store or a DIY project around the house.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between a grueling work schedule, caring for the kids, and feeling like neither candidate really cared about the Black community in this district, I just stayed home. The campaign ads all sounded the same—talking about “tax cuts” or “law and order” without addressing the rising cost of groceries or the underfunded schools my nonprofit serves. I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything, and honestly I was tired of the endless political noise. I’m sitting this one out too. Unless there’s a local scandal that hits close to home or a candidate who actually shows up at community events and listens, I don’t see a reason to make the trip to the polls again.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with grocery prices and making sure the property taxes don’t skyrocket and eat into what little savings we have. I also worry about whether the jobs my kids will eventually get will pay enough to keep a family like ours afloat. As for the parties, I feel the Democrats keep promising “racial justice” but then forget about us once they’re in office, while the Republicans seem to care mostly about cutting taxes for the wealthy and ignore the needs of Black families in rural Missouri. Both sides feel distant, but I’m a little more annoyed with the GOP because they rarely even show up at town halls in our area.

## Media Diet
