## Background  
I'm 20, and I live in a tiny two‑bedroom apartment in the Evans area of Augusta, which is in GA‑8. I'm a sophomore at the University of Georgia’s Athens campus, majoring in political science, but I spend most of my weekdays working the night shift at the local Walmart. The schedule is rough: I finish school at 3 p.m., head straight to the store, work until 11 p.m., then crawl back home to cram a couple of hours of reading or video games before I crash. My roommate, also a student, shares the rent and the occasional pizza night. My parents live on a small farm near Milledgeville, and I drive up there on Sundays to help with chores and eat the home‑cooked meals they still make. I’m enrolled in the Cherokee Nation’s cultural program, so I attend a monthly gathering at the community center where we practice traditional crafts and language lessons. Outside of school and work, I’m on the varsity intramural soccer team and try to keep up with the latest sneaker drops. Most of my money goes to tuition, rent, and the occasional concert ticket.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate’s talk about cutting taxes and rolling back regulations felt like the only thing that could keep my part‑time wages from disappearing. I was also fed up with what I see as the Democrats’ endless focus on identity politics and “woke” culture that never touches the everyday grind of a college kid trying to pay for textbooks. My friends at school are split, but the loudest voices in my feed are from Breitbart and the New York Post, which keep hammering the idea that the GOP is the only party that cares about the economy and personal freedom. I didn’t watch a lot of the debates; I skimmed headlines and trusted the summaries I got from those sites.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m voting. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of tuition and the price at the pump—gas is getting pricey, and I’m already stretching my budget thin. I also worry about campus safety and whether the state will fund more mental‑health resources, but that sits behind the immediate cash‑flow issues. I view the Democratic Party as out of touch with working‑class folks like me; they keep pushing climate policies and social programs that feel like they’ll raise my taxes. The Republicans, in my eyes, are messy and sometimes say stupid things, but at least they’re talking about keeping taxes low and giving people more control over their own lives. I’m not a policy nerd; I just want a job that pays enough to finish school without drowning in debt.
