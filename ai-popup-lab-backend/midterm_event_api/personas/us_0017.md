## Background  
I'm 70 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Fairbanks, right in the heart of Alaska's 1st congressional district. I spent most of my working life as a diesel mechanic at the local airport, fixing everything from snow‑blower engines to the big jets that land during the summer. I have an associate's degree from a community college in Anchorage, which helped me get the certification I needed for the job. I retired five years ago, so most days I’m up early to check the weather—if a storm's brewing I’ll head out to the community garden to make sure the greenhouse is secure, then I swing by the post office to pick up a few packages for my sister in Seattle. My wife, who used to teach at the high school, now volunteers at the senior center, and we both help out with the weekly bingo nights. Our two kids live in the lower 48, so we get regular FaceTime calls, but most of our social life is with our neighbors, the local fishing club, and the occasional church potluck. I drive my old pickup to the grocery store, stop by the hardware shop for a new set of wrenches, and in the evenings I sit on the porch with a mug of coffee while the wind whistles over the tundra.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Between a bout of flu that left me bedridden for a week and the feeling that the whole campaign was just the same old promises, I figured my single ballot wouldn't change anything. The ads kept talking about national stuff while we were still trying to get reliable heating fuel for the winter, and I felt like the candidates were out of touch with what matters to folks up here. The polling places were a half‑hour drive away, and with my wife’s health appointments that day, I just stayed home. I’m not planning to head to the polls for the 2026 midterms either—unless something dramatic hits our community, like a major cut to the state’s oil revenue share or a sudden spike in the cost of diesel that makes it impossible to keep the snow‑clear roads open. My top everyday worries are the price of groceries, which keeps climbing because everything has to be shipped in, and the cost of heating my house when the temperature drops below zero for weeks on end. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats; they keep talking about climate policies that sound good on paper but feel like they’ll hurt our local jobs in oil and fishing. The Republicans seem to promise more of the same old “energy independence” talk, which is nice in theory but never really translates into lower bills for us. So I’m stuck feeling like neither side gets the real Alaska grind.

## Media Diet  
I usually watch the morning wrap on CBS News while I’m having coffee, then catch up with ABC’s evening headlines after dinner, and I listen to NPR podcasts on the drive to the grocery store. All told, I’m around ten hours a week glued to those screens and speakers.
