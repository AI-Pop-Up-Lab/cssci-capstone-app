## Background
I'm 75, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Clinton, Mississippi, right in the 2nd congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working on the assembly line at the plant in nearby Jackson, and I retired a decade ago when the factory shut down. My wife passed away three years ago, so now it's just me, my two grown‑up kids who live out of state, and a golden retriever I adopted from the shelter. Most mornings I start with a cup of black coffee on the porch while the rooster next door crows, then I head to the community center for the senior bingo group. After that I tend to my garden – tomatoes, okra, a few pecan trees – and I try to keep the yard tidy because the HOA likes everything looking neat. I drive a beat‑up pickup to the local grocery store twice a week, and on Saturdays I go to church, where I sit in the back and listen to the choir. Evenings are usually spent watching TV news or scrolling through the few sites I trust, then reading the newspaper before turning in around ten.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about keeping taxes low and protecting my Social Security checks from being cut. The Democratic candidates kept talking about big‑government programs and climate agendas that seemed irrelevant to someone who just wants to keep his garden alive and his bills paid. I also liked the way the Republican candidate talked straight about border security and the economy – it felt like common sense. Since then, I’ve watched the news and seen a lot of talk about inflation and rising prescription drug prices, which makes me nervous, but I still think the GOP is the safer bet for someone my age. I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I’ll be at the polling place in my town on Election Day, mainly to support the Republican Senate candidate and the local congressional hopeful who promised to keep the tax code simple. My biggest worries right now are the cost of my prescription meds and whether the state will keep property taxes low enough for retirees like me. I see the Republican Party as the one that respects hard work and tradition, while I view the Democrats as out of touch with folks who aren’t living in big cities.
