## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a quiet neighborhood on the edge of Fort Collins, right near the foothills where the town blends into the plains. I spent most of my career as a research professor in environmental engineering at Colorado State University, and I finally retired three years ago. My days still feel pretty full: I wake up around 6 a.m., brew a pot of green tea, and spend an hour scrolling through my tablet while the house is still quiet. After a light breakfast, I head to the community center where I teach a weekly workshop on home energy efficiency for seniors. It’s a good mix of staying sharp and helping my neighbors save a few bucks on their electric bills.

My husband, who retired from the Air Force a decade earlier, and I live in a modest two‑story house that we bought in the late 1990s. Our daughter lives in Denver with her family, and we get the usual weekend visits from our two grandkids, who love to run around the backyard while we tend to the garden. I’m a member of the local Buddhist temple, and I volunteer on the board of a nonprofit that pushes for clean water projects in the Rockies. When I’m not at the center or the garden, I’m usually at the library reading the latest journal articles or watching a cooking show on the television.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one really taking climate change seriously, and that mattered a lot to me after decades of studying the science. I also appreciated the stance on health care—they pushed for expanding Medicare to cover more services, which is a relief for someone my age who’s on a fixed income and needs regular check‑ups. The campaign felt like a genuine attempt to protect the environment I’ve spent my life researching, and I liked that the candidate talked about funding public transit in Fort Collins, something that would cut down traffic and pollution.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies are playing out. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for issues that affect my daily life—like prescription drug prices and renewable energy subsidies. The Republicans, in my view, keep talking about “energy independence” but that usually means more drilling and less regulation, which worries me as I see the snowpack shrinking each year. I’ve also noticed a lot of talk about “border security” that feels disconnected from the problems I care about; it just adds to my frustration with the party that seems more focused on cultural battles than on practical solutions.

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of health care—especially the price of insulin—and the looming property tax hikes that the county is proposing to fund new road projects. I’m also watching how the state handles water rights, because the river levels have been dropping and that affects both my garden and the broader community. I feel the Democrats are more likely to push for price caps on essential medicines and invest in water conservation, while the GOP seems to double down on cutting taxes for corporations, which I don’t think will help my wallet.

## Media Diet
I usually listen to NPR’s morning news while I’m making tea and then catch up on ABC News articles in the evening after dinner, adding up to about five hours a week. I also skim the business section on CNBC when I’m checking my retirement accounts.
