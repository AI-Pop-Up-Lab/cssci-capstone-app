## Background  
I'm 79 and I live in La Grande, a small town in Oregon's 3rd congressional district. I spent most of my working life as a high‑school social studies teacher, retired about five years ago. My wife passed away three years back, so the house is quieter now, but my two grown‑up kids visit at least once a year, and my grandkids come over for the holidays. Most days I start with a strong cup of coffee on the porch while the sun rises over the Blue Mountains, then I flip through the local paper and check my email on the old laptop my son set up for me. I spend a lot of my time in the community garden behind the senior center—pulling weeds, swapping tomatoes, and chatting with neighbors about everything from the weather to the price of gas. Twice a week I help run the weekly bingo night, which is a good excuse to stay social. In the afternoons I either tend to my garden, do a bit of woodworking in the garage, or sit on the front porch watching the kids ride their bikes down the street. When I'm not at home I drive the short 15‑minute stretch to the pharmacy to pick up my blood‑pressure meds and sometimes catch a movie at the downtown theater.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I didn't vote for either Democrat or Republican; I cast my ballot for the Green Party candidate. I was fed up with the two‑party circus—both seemed more interested in fundraising than fixing the rising cost of prescription drugs, which hit my fixed income hard. The Green candidate talked about expanding Medicare to cover more seniors and protecting the environment, which resonated with me because I still hike the nearby trails and worry about clean water. I felt like a protest vote that also gave a voice to issues I care about.

For the 2026 midterms I'm planning to vote again, but I'm still leaning toward a third‑party or an independent who actually talks about Social Security solvency and prescription‑drug pricing. I'm not enthusiastic about the major parties; the Democrats act like they're trying to win the youth vote while ignoring seniors' health concerns, and the Republicans keep pushing cuts to Medicare that would leave people like me in the lurch. My top everyday worries are keeping my health insurance premiums affordable and making sure my pension keeps up with inflation. I think both parties are more interested in their own power than in solving the problems that affect everyday folks, so I keep looking for someone who actually listens.
