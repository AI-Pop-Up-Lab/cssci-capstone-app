## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in the Algiers neighborhood of New Orleans, right in the heart of Louisiana’s 6th district. I grew up in a working‑class family in Baton Rouge, helped my parents run a small grocery store, and after high school I took a couple of years of community‑college classes in business administration before I started working as a maintenance supervisor for a local hospital. I retired five years ago, but I still do a bit of odd‑job work fixing things for neighbors—air‑conditioner repairs, plumbing leaks, that sort of thing.  

I’m married to my wife of 45 years; we both love cooking, especially the spicy dishes we learned from our parents’ hometown in Vietnam. Our two kids live out of state, one in Texas and the other in Chicago, and we get to see the grandkids a few times a year when they visit for holidays. Most mornings I start with a cup of strong coffee on the porch, watching the Mississippi River traffic go by while the local radio plays old classic rock. After that I usually check the news on my tablet, then head out to the community center where I volunteer helping seniors with computer basics. In the afternoons I’m either at the hardware store picking up supplies for a repair job or at the local market grabbing fresh produce. Evenings are for TV, a bit of fishing on the riverbank if the weather’s nice, and then dinner with my wife.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that really cared about keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑business environment that let people like me make a living fixing things for others. The Democratic platform seemed all about big‑government programs that would raise my property tax bill and push more regulations on the trades I work in. I also liked the talk about securing the border and preserving traditional values, which felt like a safeguard for the community I’ve built here.  

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. The last few months have been a mess with gas prices spiking and the local hospital where I used to work cutting back on staff—both things that hit my wallet and my sense of security. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of gasoline, which makes it harder to get to my repair jobs and to visit my kids, and the looming increase in property taxes that the city council is talking about to fund new projects. I’m also watching health‑care costs because my wife’s medication bills have gone up, and I don’t want to be stuck with a big bill if something goes wrong.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty sure the Republicans are the ones who understand the everyday guy who works with his hands. They push for lower taxes, less red tape, and they talk straight about law and order. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more interested in identity politics and big‑picture climate plans that feel disconnected from the reality of paying for groceries and fixing a busted water heater. I get annoyed when the media keeps talking about “progress” without mentioning how those policies will affect people like me on the ground.
