## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a small farming community just outside Grand Island, right in Nebraska's 2nd district. I spent most of my adult life working the family corn and soy fields, and I retired five years ago when the farm was passed on to my son. My wife of 45 years still tends the garden and keeps the house running, and we have two grown kids who live in Omaha and Lincoln and visit a few times a year. Most days I get up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of dark roast, and flip on the radio while I read the local paper. After breakfast I like to take a short walk around the property, check on the livestock, and make sure the irrigation system is still working. In the afternoons I either help my grandson with his homework when he's here, or I head to the community center for a weekly bingo game and a Bible study. Evenings are usually spent on the porch, watching the sky turn pink while I scroll through a few news sites on my tablet. I drive a pickup truck, keep a spare set of tires in the garage, and try to stay on top of the rising cost of diesel and farm equipment repairs. When I'm not out on the land, I'm at the local hardware store picking up supplies or at the diner catching up with old friends.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party understood what folks like me need: lower taxes, less regulation on agriculture, and a strong stance on border security. I remember watching a town hall where the GOP candidate talked about cutting red tape for farm loans, and that struck a chord. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing climate regulations that would make it harder to grow corn, and they seem more interested in big‑city issues than the realities of rural Nebraska. Over the past two years I've seen the price of fertilizer climb and the cost of health insurance for my wife keep inching up, and I still think the Republican platform is the best shot at keeping those numbers from exploding.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. The biggest things on my mind right now are the rising price of diesel and the fact that my property tax bill went up again after the county reassessed land values. I also worry about the shortage of qualified teachers in the local schools, which affects my grandchildren. I plan to vote for the Republican Senate candidate who promised to keep the farm bill strong and the Democrat running for the House who said she'd fight to lower prescription drug prices—though I'm leaning heavily toward the GOP ticket because I trust them more on the issues that hit my wallet daily. In short, I see the Republicans as the party that keeps government from getting in the way of my work, while the Democrats feel like they want to tell me how to run my farm.
