## Background
I'm 30 and I work as a logistics coordinator for a regional distribution center in Fresno, right in the heart of California's 15th district. My shift starts at 6 a.m., so I usually grab a coffee from the corner donut shop, hop on the 15‑minute bus ride, and arrive at the warehouse before the trucks start pulling in. The job is a mix of paperwork, scheduling drivers, and making sure the pallets get moved on time; it's fast‑paced but I like that I get to see the supply chain in action.

I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment just a few blocks from the bus line with my partner, Maya. We don't have kids yet, but we share the rent and split grocery shopping, cooking, and the occasional weekend road trip to the coast. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I hit the community gym after work, mostly to lift weights and clear my head. Sundays are reserved for church service at a nearby Baptist congregation, where I also help run the youth basketball program. When I have free evenings, I either binge a sitcom on streaming or play pickup basketball at the local park with a group of friends from high school.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for real investments in infrastructure and job training, especially for places like Fresno where the economy still leans heavily on agriculture and low‑wage service work. I also appreciated the candidate’s stance on expanding Medicaid and protecting voting rights, issues that hit close to home for me and my community. At the time, I was pretty convinced that the alternative would have meant more cuts to the social safety net and less attention to climate‑related challenges that affect our farms.

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of rent and grocery prices—everything from avocados to rent checks feels like it's climbing faster than my paycheck. I'm also worried about the local job market; the warehouse has been stable, but there’s talk of automation that could cut hours for a lot of us. I still lean Democratic because I think they’re more likely to fight for affordable housing and protect the programs that help low‑income families. I’m less enthusiastic about the Republicans; from what I hear they’re more focused on tax cuts for big corporations and less on the everyday struggles of folks in the Central Valley. That said, I’m not a political junkie, so I’ll base my vote on who seems most likely to keep my rent from skyrocketing and keep grocery shelves stocked.
