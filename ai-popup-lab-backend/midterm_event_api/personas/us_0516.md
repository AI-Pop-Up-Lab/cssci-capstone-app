## Background
I'm 65 and I work as a maintenance supervisor at a small lumber mill just outside Missoula. I finished a two‑year program in industrial mechanics at the community college down the road, and that got me the hands‑on job I’ve held for the last 30 years. My wife passed away five years ago, so it's just me and my 12‑year‑old Labrador, Scout, most days. My two kids live in Seattle and Boise, so I get a call from them every Sunday afternoon and a video chat once a month.

My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee and the local paper—yeah, I still like a paper in my hands. I head to the mill around 7 a.m., spend the day fixing machines, checking safety logs, and making sure we meet the shipping schedule. Lunch is usually a sandwich at the break room while I scroll through the news on my phone. After work I swing by the hardware store for groceries, then either fix something around the house or take Scout for a walk along the river. On Saturdays I try to get out to the mountains to fish or hunt; it’s the one thing that keeps me from feeling stuck. Sundays are quiet—church service in the morning, then a potluck with a few neighbors. I don’t drive far, but I keep my pickup for the occasional road trip to see the kids.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old arguments dressed up in new slogans. The campaigns kept talking about big‑city issues while the real problems out here—like the rising cost of health insurance, the dwindling number of good-paying jobs at the mill, and the endless debates over public land—got brushed aside. I was also swamped with a big repair project at work that took up most of my evenings; by the time the ballot rolled around I just didn’t have the energy to dig through the ads and figure out where I stood.

That feeling hasn’t changed much. I’m still sitting this one out for the 2026 midterms. The only thing that might pull me back would be a clear, local crisis—say the mill shutting down or a major water‑quality issue that forces me to take a stand. Otherwise, I keep telling myself my vote won’t move the needle.

My top everyday concerns are health‑care costs and the price of gasoline. My prescription meds keep getting pricier, and when the pump climbs a few dollars I feel it right away in the grocery budget. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as a bunch of city‑slickers who don’t get the mountain life, and the Republicans as the same old talk about “less regulation” that never seems to help the little guy on the ground. Both sides feel like they’re playing a game that leaves folks like me out of the loop.

## Media Diet
