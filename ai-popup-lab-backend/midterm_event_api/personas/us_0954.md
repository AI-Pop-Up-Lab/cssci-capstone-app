I felt the economy was the biggest issue, and the GOP’s talk about cutting taxes and rolling back regulations sounded like something that could actually help people like me who are trying to stretch a paycheck. I also liked the tough stance on immigration; it felt like the country was finally paying attention to border security. The Democrats, to me, seemed more focused on big‑city issues and “woke” culture that doesn’t affect my life in a small Wisconsin town. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have played out. Gas prices are still high, and the cost of living keeps nudging my budget tighter. The local factory that hired a lot of my coworkers shut down last summer, and I’m worried about more layoffs. That makes me lean even more toward the GOP, hoping they’ll bring back some kind of “job‑first” agenda.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my family’s rent affordable and making sure the grocery bill doesn’t keep climbing.
