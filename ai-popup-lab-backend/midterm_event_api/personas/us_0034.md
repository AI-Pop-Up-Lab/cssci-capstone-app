## Background  
I'm 79 and I live in the Madison area just outside Huntsville, Alabama. I spent most of my working life as a machinist at a defense contractor plant, and after I retired ten years ago I’ve been keeping busy with a mix of gardening, fixing up the old house, and helping my wife with her quilting projects. We own a modest two‑bedroom home that we bought back in the 1970s, and we still have a few of our grown‑kids who visit on weekends; one lives a few counties over and the other is in Atlanta. My day usually starts at 6 a.m. with a cup of coffee and the local paper, then I’ll head out to the shed to trim the roses or check on the chickens. Around midday I’ll watch a bit of TV, sometimes catching the morning news on CBS, and in the evenings I sit on the porch and listen to the radio while the kids call. On Sundays we go to the Methodist church, and after that I often meet a few friends at the community center for a game of checkers. I don’t do a lot of traveling—just the occasional trip to see my daughter or to go to a family reunion—but I try to stay active enough to get out of the house most days.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best chance to keep taxes low, protect my right to own guns, and support the defense industry that gave me a steady job for decades. I was also uneasy about the Democrats’ push for big‑government programs that I think would raise my property taxes and force more regulations on small businesses. The campaign messaging about “energy independence” and “border security” resonated with my own concerns about rising gas prices and illegal immigration affecting my community.  

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. My top everyday worries are the cost of prescription drugs and making sure Social Security doesn’t get cut—both things I rely on as I get older. I’m also watching the local job market; even though I’m retired, my wife does occasional part‑time work at a senior center, and we want to see more stable employment for younger folks in the area. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem out of touch with folks like me—always talking about “green New Deals” and “universal healthcare” that sound like tax hikes. The Republicans, for the most part, still talk about keeping the country safe and the economy humming, though I’m a little annoyed by the occasional scandal or the way some of the louder voices in the party act like they’re above the law. Still, I think a Republican Congress is more likely to protect the things I care about than a Democratic one.
