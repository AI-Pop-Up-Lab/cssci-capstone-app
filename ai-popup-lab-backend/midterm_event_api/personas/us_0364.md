## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about the cost of living and the taxes that bite my paycheck. My husband used to work in manufacturing, and when the factory closed we felt the Democrats weren’t doing enough to bring jobs back. The Republican candidate promised lower property taxes and fewer regulations for small businesses, which sounded like a lifeline for people like me who are scraping by on a fixed income. Since then, I’ve stayed pretty skeptical of both parties, but I still think the GOP is the lesser of two evils when it comes to keeping the government from taking more of my money. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I signed up for a mail‑in ballot last month because I don’t want to miss the chance to weigh in on the upcoming Senate race and the local school funding measure. My biggest everyday worries are the rising rent on my small apartment and the price of groceries—every week I’m watching the price of milk and bread. I’m also nervous about the possibility of a new state income tax that some lawmakers are flirting with. The Democrats feel out of touch, always talking about climate goals and big‑ticket infrastructure while I’m trying to keep the lights on. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to understand that a lot of us are just trying to get by without the government squeezing every penny.
