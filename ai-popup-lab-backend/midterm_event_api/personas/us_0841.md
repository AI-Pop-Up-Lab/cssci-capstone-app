## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Cedar Hill, a suburb on the edge of Dallas in Texas's 11th district. I work as a high‑school social studies teacher, so most weekdays I’m up before sunrise, making coffee and checking the news on my phone while the kids are still asleep. After dropping my two grown‑up kids off at the university in the mornings, I drive the 20‑minute stretch to the school, where I spend the day prepping lessons, grading papers, and meeting with parents. My husband, a retired electrician, likes to tinker in the garage, and on weekends we usually have a barbecue with the neighbors or help my elderly mother with her medication and grocery runs. I belong to the local Baptist church, but I don’t go every Sunday—just the big holidays and when there’s a potluck. When I’m not teaching or caring for family, I like to watch the Dallas Cowboys on TV, tend to my small garden, and occasionally volunteer at the community center’s after‑school program. The cost of living has crept up, so I keep a close eye on my budget, especially utilities and property taxes.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to public schools and health‑care access, both of which hit me hard as a teacher and as someone whose mother needs regular medical care. I was also motivated by the candidate’s stance on voting rights, something I see as essential after seeing a lot of confusion at the polls last cycle. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what both parties are doing locally. I’m definitely heading to the polls for the 2026 midterms—I can’t afford to sit this one out, especially with the upcoming school funding bill and the property‑tax hike that the county commission is debating. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of health insurance premiums and the property‑tax increase that’s biting into my retirement savings. I also worry about whether there’ll be enough qualified teachers left if funding keeps getting cut. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are more in tune with my concerns about education and health, but I get frustrated when they bicker and seem to forget the everyday folks. The Republicans keep pushing for lower taxes, which sounds good until I see my property tax bill balloon and the schools lose resources. Neither side feels perfect, but I’m leaning toward the Democrats for this round.
