## Background  
I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Johnson City, right in the heart of Tennessee's 1st congressional district. I spent most of my working life as a high‑school math teacher after getting my degree from the University of Tennessee. I retired about ten years ago, but I still keep a few lesson plans on a shelf for the occasional tutoring gig with a neighbor's kid. My wife of sixty‑three years still tends the garden, and we have two grown children who live in Atlanta and Nashville; they call every Sunday, and I try to make the trip to see them at least once a year.  

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and read the printed newspaper while listening to the radio. After that I take a short walk around the block, sometimes stopping to chat with the mail carrier or the lady who runs the little grocery on Main. Most afternoons I’m at the community center, either playing bridge with a group of retirees or helping out with the senior‑citizen tech class—still trying to figure out how to send a photo to my granddaughter. Evenings are for television; I usually catch the local news at 6 p.m., then settle in for whatever national broadcast is on. On Sundays I go to church, then have a family dinner with my wife and the occasional visiting grandchild.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best protected my hard‑earned retirement savings and kept taxes low enough for a fixed income. The candidate’s promises to cut down on regulation and keep the military strong also resonated with my generation’s sense of duty. I’m not a political scholar, but I trust the folks who say they’ll keep the economy stable and respect traditional values.  

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to vote. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the possibility of cuts to Social Security. I also keep an eye on property taxes; the county has been talking about raising rates to fund road repairs, and that would bite into my limited budget. I’m less interested in foreign policy these days; it feels far removed from my daily life.  

When I think about the parties, the Republicans seem more in tune with what folks like me need—lower taxes, a strong defense, and a respect for the way things have been done for decades. The Democrats, in my view, push for big‑government programs that sound good on paper but end up raising the tax burden and creating more bureaucracy. I’m not saying they’re all bad, just that their priorities feel out of step with someone who’s lived through a lot of change and wants to keep things steady.

## Media Diet  
I usually watch CBS News in the morning while having my coffee, then catch ABC’s evening broadcast after dinner. On weekends I flip over to NBC News for a quick roundup when I have a bit more time. Altogether that adds up to about four hours a week.
