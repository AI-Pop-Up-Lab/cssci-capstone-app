## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a quiet neighborhood on the edge of Cheyenne, the biggest town in the 1st district. After finishing my doctorate in history, I spent three decades teaching at the University of Wyoming. I retired five years ago, but I still keep a stack of books on the porch and occasionally give guest lectures when a department asks. My husband passed away two years ago, so most days I’m on my own, but my two grown children live in Denver and Salt Lake City and call me every Sunday. My grandchildren visit in the summer and bring the house back to life with their video games and loud laughter.

My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee and the paper—still the print edition of the *Wyoming Tribune*—while I watch the sunrise over the foothills. After that I take a short walk around the cul‑de‑sac, greet the mail carrier, and tend to the small garden of tomatoes and herbs. I volunteer twice a week at the senior center, helping with bingo and leading a knitting circle. In the afternoons I either tend to the garden, do crossword puzzles, or sit on the porch reading whatever article catches my eye online. Evenings are for a simple dinner, a bit of TV, and a phone call to my daughter. I try to keep active, but at my age I also accept that I need to rest more often.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I’ve always believed that the party best protects the values I grew up with: personal responsibility, a strong national defense, and the right for Wyoming to keep its energy jobs. The oil and gas industry has been the backbone of our state for as long as I can remember, and the Republican platform promised to keep those jobs alive while keeping taxes low. I also felt the Democratic candidates were pushing too hard for big‑government solutions that don’t fit a place like ours.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are the rising cost of health care—my prescription bills keep creeping up—and the property tax hikes the county has been proposing to fund new schools. I’m also watching the federal push to regulate fracking more tightly; if that goes through, it could hit the local economy hard. I think the Republican Party still has the right ideas for a state like Wyoming, even if they sometimes get a bit too national in tone. The Democrats, in my view, keep trying to force policies that ignore the realities of rural life, and I’m tired of hearing about climate‑change mandates that feel like they belong in a coastal city, not on the plains.
