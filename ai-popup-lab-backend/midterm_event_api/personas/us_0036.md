## Background

My parents own a house a few miles away, and I still send them a little help each month, but most of my money goes to rent, my phone bill, and the occasional video game release. My roommate, a fellow high‑school grad, works part‑time at a fast‑food joint, so we split the rent and utilities to keep things affordable.

My days are a cycle of sleep, work, and online time. I usually sleep from 8 am to 2 pm, then head to the store for a 7‑hour shift that ends around 9 pm. After work I unwind by scrolling through TikTok, playing Call of Duty with friends, or watching a few episodes of a streaming series. ## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole process just seemed like a hassle. The registration deadline slipped past me while I was juggling work and school, and by the time I figured it out, the election day was already packed with a shift and a family gathering. I also get a lot of the same talking points from both parties on TV, and it just feels like they're all promising the same thing while the real stuff—like my paycheck staying the same or rent not going up—gets ignored. That cynicism stuck with me, and unless something really blows up locally—like a major layoff at the store or a scandal that hits my community directly—I'm not planning to go to the polls in 2026 either. So, I'm sitting this one out.

My top everyday concerns are keeping a steady job and making sure I can afford my rent and groceries. If I have to pick a political side, I tend to think the Democrats are all talk about big government programs that never reach people like me, while the Republicans keep shouting about taxes and "law and order" but don't seem to care about the low‑wage workers who are just trying to get by. I don't feel like either party gets my day‑to‑day struggles, which is why I stay on the sidelines.
