## Background  
I'm 65, and I live in Pine Bluff, Arkansas, right in the 2nd congressional district. I worked for 30 years as a middle‑school math teacher before I retired last spring. My husband passed away five years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids who live out of state. My oldest daughter visits once a month, and my youngest calls every week. Most mornings I brew a cup of coffee on the kitchen counter, read the local paper (just for the classifieds), and flip on NPR while I sort the mail. After that I usually head out to the community center where I help run the senior‑citizen bingo nights and a volunteer tutoring program for kids who need extra help with math. My afternoons are a mix of grocery trips, checking my blood pressure, and occasionally stopping by the church for the 3 p.m. service. Evenings are quiet: I watch a bit of TV, maybe catch up on a sitcom, and then settle in with a mystery novel before bed. Rent is modest; I own the house outright, but property taxes and rising utility bills keep me on my toes.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between caring for my mother’s worsening arthritis, the long lines at the polling place, and a sense that none of the candidates really spoke to people like me, I just stayed home. I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole process felt like a chore I couldn't fit into my already packed schedule. That feeling hasn't gone away, so I'm sitting this one out in 2026 as well—unless something dramatic hits the community, like a hospital closing or a major infrastructure failure that forces me to reconsider. My biggest daily worries are the cost of my prescription meds and the rising price of gasoline; I drive a modest sedan to the grocery store and to the community center, and every extra dollar at the pump feels like a hit to my fixed income. I also worry about local job opportunities for younger folks; Pine Bluff has lost a few factories, and my grandkids talk about moving away for work. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to promise more social programs, but I often hear about broken promises and a lack of follow‑through. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk about lower taxes and personal responsibility, but I feel their rhetoric rarely addresses the concerns of Black seniors in the South. I’m not a party loyalist; I just want policies that keep my meds affordable and my community stable.
