## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest bungalow in Garner, just outside Raleigh, in North Carolina's 13th district. I spent most of my adult life as a public‑school teacher, finishing a few years of college but never getting a degree. I retired about ten years ago, so most of my days revolve around the house, my garden, and the senior center where I help organize bingo nights. My husband died five years back, and my two grown children live in Charlotte and Washington, D.C., so I mostly keep in touch by phone and the occasional video call. I have a great‑granddaughter who visits in the summer, and I love cooking the dumplings my mother taught me to make. Every morning I make a cup of tea, check the weather, and flip on the TV for the news before heading out for a short walk around the neighborhood. afternoons are for watering the tomatoes, reading the paper, or knitting while listening to the radio. Evenings I usually sit in the living room and watch the nightly news broadcasts, sometimes catching up on a favorite soap opera before turning in early.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Republican candidate because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me—people on a fixed income worried about rising property taxes and the cost of prescription drugs. The Democratic platform seemed full of promises that never touch the small towns and senior communities where I live. I also liked the candidate’s emphasis on law and order; after a string of break‑ins in our neighborhood, I wanted someone who would take crime seriously. I went to the polls early, because the line was short and I didn’t want to waste a day that could be spent at the senior center.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to vote again. The biggest thing on my mind is the proposed increase in property tax rates for our county—my pension check just covers the basics, and any bump feels like a squeeze. The other big worry is the price of my blood‑pressure meds; the pharmacy’s price list has gone up again, and I’m not sure how I’ll manage if it keeps climbing. I still think the Republican Party is the better choice for keeping taxes low and protecting community safety, even if I don’t follow every policy detail. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about big‑government solutions that feel far removed from the everyday concerns of seniors. I’m not a policy wonk, but I know I want the side that promises to keep my bills manageable and neighborhoods safe.
