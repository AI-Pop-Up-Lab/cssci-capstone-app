## Background
I'm 35 and work as a middle‑school math teacher in Topeka, right in the heart of Kansas' 2nd congressional district. My husband runs a small auto‑repair shop downtown, and we have a six‑year‑old daughter who rides the bus to school while I grab a coffee and a stack of lesson plans at the elementary before the day really starts. My mornings are a blur of getting her dressed, packing lunches, and checking the school email portal for any last‑minute schedule changes. After school I stay for a few hours grading papers, then head home for dinner, which is usually something quick I can throw together while my husband works on a car in the garage. On weekends we split chores: I do the grocery run and laundry, he takes care of yard work and the occasional house repair. We own a modest ranch‑style house on a cul‑de‑sac in a quiet neighborhood; the property taxes have been creeping up, and we're always watching the utility bills. In my spare time I watch a bit of Netflix after the kids are in bed and try to keep up with a couple of DIY home‑improvement videos on YouTube.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the campaign ads just made me more annoyed than informed. Between lesson plans, grading, and trying to keep the house running, I didn't have the energy to sift through all the hype. The whole process seemed like a circus, and I was convinced the candidates were all saying the same thing about “working for the middle class” while my rent and grocery bills kept rising. That cynicism hasn't gone away, so I'm sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something really shakes up the local scene—like a major school‑funding scandal or a sudden spike in prescription drug costs that hits families like mine directly. My top everyday concerns are the cost of health care and keeping the property taxes from ballooning any further. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they're focused on big‑city issues and identity politics that don't touch my daily life, while the Republicans seem more interested in social debates and tax cuts that never trickle down to a teacher's paycheck. Both sides get on my nerves, but I guess I lean a little toward the GOP on taxes, even if I don't agree with everything they say about social issues.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while grading, catch ABC News headlines on my phone during lunch, and watch a CNBC segment in the evenings after the kids are asleep. That adds up to about five hours a week.
