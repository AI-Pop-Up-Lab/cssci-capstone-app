## Background  
I'm 40, and I work as a logistics coordinator for a regional distribution center that ships furniture and appliances throughout western Massachusetts. My shift starts at 6 a.m., so I’m usually out the door by 5:30, grabbing a coffee from the corner deli in Northampton and listening to NPR while I drive. After work I pick up my two kids—12‑year‑old Maya and 9‑year‑old Jin—from the public elementary school, then we head home to a two‑bedroom apartment we rent near the train station. My wife, who runs a small hair‑styling booth out of our living room, works evenings, so most of the household chores fall to me. On weekends I try to keep the place tidy, run errands at the grocery store, and sometimes help Maya with her math homework while Jin practices soccer. I’m not a big fan of long‑term planning; I mostly think about paying the rent, keeping the truck on the road, and making sure the kids have enough snacks for school.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long line at the polling place, a shift change that night, and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about people like me, I just stayed home. I’ve always felt the political system is a distant circus—lots of promises, little change for the working‑class families in towns like ours. The whole thing seemed like a waste of time, especially when I was juggling a tight budget and two kids’ schedules. That cynicism hasn’t gone away, so I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic hits my neighborhood—like a factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes—I don’t see a reason to spend another Saturday at a ballot box.

My biggest everyday worries are the rent going up and grocery prices creeping higher. The landlord says the market’s hot, and the grocery store’s prices have been climbing faster than my paycheck. I also worry about the kids’ future—whether they’ll be able to afford college or even a decent job after high school. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to talk a lot about climate and student loan forgiveness, but I never see any real help for people paying rent on a modest salary. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep shouting about taxes and “law and order,” but their policies feel like they’d make my paycheck thinner and the cost of living higher. Both sides feel like they’re playing a game I’m not invited to win.
