## Background
I'm 45 and work as a program coordinator for the tribal health department on the White Earth Reservation, which sits in Minnesota's 8th District. My day starts before sunrise; I get the kids—14‑year‑old Maya and 9‑year‑old Eli—ready for school, then drive about forty minutes to the clinic where I manage outreach for diabetes prevention and mental‑health services. Most of my coworkers are also members of the Ojibwe community, so we talk a lot about how state policies affect our people. After work I usually swing by the local grocery store in Baudette, pick up a few things for dinner, and help my mom with her medication before we all sit down for a quick TV dinner. On weekends I’m either at the community center helping organize the powwow or taking the kids to the lake for a few hours of fishing. I’m married to a construction foreman who works on projects around the reservation, and we own a modest house that we’re slowly paying off. My life is a mix of paperwork, family chores, and trying to keep our cultural traditions alive in a place where the nearest big city feels a couple of hours away.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic presidential ticket because I felt the candidate actually listened to tribal leaders about sovereignty, health‑care funding, and climate impacts on our lands. I also liked the promise to protect Medicaid expansions that keep our clinic afloat. The campaign wasn’t perfect—there were a lot of generic promises—but the alternative seemed like a step backward for tribal rights and for the kind of social safety net my family depends on. I’m definitely heading to the polls again for the 2026 midterms; I’ve signed up for early voting because I don’t want to miss it after a long work shift. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs for my mom and the looming threat of a new pipeline that could cross tribal water sources. I’m also watching property tax hikes that make it harder to keep our house. I see the Democratic Party as the only one that occasionally gets it right on issues that matter to us, even if they’re slow and sometimes forgetful. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re more interested in cutting regulations that protect our environment and in pushing policies that could undermine tribal jurisdiction. I’m not a political theorist; I just want the folks in charge to keep the clinic funded and the water clean.
