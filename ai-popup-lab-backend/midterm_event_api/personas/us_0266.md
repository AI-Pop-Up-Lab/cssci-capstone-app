## Background

I'm 70 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the west side of Champaign, right near the university campus. My daughter moved back in with me after she retired from the school district, and we share the space with her teenage granddaughter who goes to the local high school. I spent most of my adult life working as a home health aide, helping seniors with bathing, meds, and meals. I stopped full‑time work a few years ago, but I still pick up a few shifts at the senior center to stay busy and bring in a little extra cash for groceries.

My day usually starts with a strong cup of coffee and the morning news on the TV while I flip through the newspaper. After that I take a short walk to the community center where I volunteer in the senior’s bingo hall. Around noon I head back home for lunch—usually a sandwich and whatever my granddaughter has made in the kitchen. In the afternoons I either run errands, like picking up prescriptions at the pharmacy or buying fresh produce at the farmers market, or I sit in the living room listening to NPR podcasts while knitting. Evenings are for church; we have a small but close‑knit congregation that meets at the local Baptist church, and I usually stay for the potluck afterward. Weekends are a mix of family visits, church activities, and occasional trips to the mall with my daughter to look for deals on clothing and household items.

## Politics and this election

I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really paying attention to folks like me—people who rely on Social Security, Medicare, and affordable prescription drugs. The candidate’s promises about protecting Medicaid and expanding community health programs resonated with my daily concerns, and I trusted the party’s stance on criminal‑justice reform after the recent protests in Chicago. I also liked the emphasis on voting rights, which matters to me as a Black voter who’s seen how easy it can be to have a voice taken away.

Now, heading into the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. My top worries are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the property tax hike the county just approved, which makes it harder to keep the apartment affordable. I’m also watching how the state legislature deals with funding for senior services—if they cut those programs, my life would get a lot tougher. I still think the Democrats are the better bet for protecting my health care and supporting low‑income seniors, but I’m getting frustrated with the constant infighting and how some leaders seem more focused on national theatrics than real help on the ground. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations that could affect my grandchildren’s future. I don’t see them listening to Black communities or seniors, and their rhetoric about “law and order” sometimes feels like a code for ignoring the systemic issues that affect us.

So yes, I’m voting this November, hoping my ballot can push the Democrats to actually deliver on the promises that matter to us everyday people.
