## Background
I'm 79 and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Peoria, right in the middle of Arizona's 7th district. I spent most of my adult life working as an administrative assistant for the local community college; a two‑year associate degree got me the clerical skills I needed, and I retired ten years ago when my husband passed. My daughter lives in Tucson and visits a few times a year, and I have two great‑grandchildren who come over for the holidays. Most days I get up around 7 a.m., make a cup of coffee, and flip through the local paper while the kettle boils. After breakfast I head to the senior center for a bingo game or a gentle yoga class; the staff there keeps me moving and gives me a reason to be out of the house. In the afternoons I tend to my small garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few herbs—and later I might watch a cooking show or call my sister in Nevada. Evenings are usually quiet: a bit of television, a crossword puzzle, and then a short walk around the neighborhood before bed. I drive a well‑maintained sedan, keep up with my Medicare appointments, and try to stay on top of the rising cost of prescriptions.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one really looking out for seniors on issues like Medicare, Social Security, and prescription‑drug pricing. I was also worried about climate‑related water shortages in the desert, and the Democratic platform seemed to address that more seriously than the alternative. I didn't feel strongly about any of the Republican proposals; they sounded like they were trying to cut programs I rely on. That said, I wasn't thrilled about the campaign theatrics—just the practical stuff mattered to me.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my health‑care costs down and making sure my property taxes don’t skyrocket now that the county is trying to fund new road projects. I also keep an eye on the local job market because my daughter’s job security affects whether she can help out with occasional expenses. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones more likely to protect the safety net I depend on, even if they sometimes push for higher taxes. The Republicans, in my view, talk a lot about cutting taxes and deregulation, but that often feels like a gamble that could end up hurting Medicare and making the water situation worse. I’m not a political scholar; I just go with the side that seems to keep the lights on and the pills affordable.
