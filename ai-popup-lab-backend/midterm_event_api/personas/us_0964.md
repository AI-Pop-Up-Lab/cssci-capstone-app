## Background
I'm 40 and I work as an administrative assistant at the regional health clinic in Beckley, West Virginia. I got my associate degree in health administration at a community college in Charleston, then moved back to the mountains to be closer to my parents. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the edge of town with my husband, who does odd jobs for the local construction crews, and our 9‑year‑old daughter, Maya. My mornings start around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, a glance at the weather, and a drive down the narrow road to the clinic. The shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., and I spend most of the day scheduling appointments, handling insurance paperwork, and fielding calls from patients who need a refill or a lab result. After work I swing by the grocery store on Main Street, pick up a few groceries, and sometimes stop at the gas station for a snack. Evenings are a mix of helping Maya with her math homework, cooking a simple dinner—usually something cheap and filling like chicken and rice—and watching a bit of TV before we all head to bed. On Saturdays I help my mom with yard work or take Maya to the local library for story hour, and on Sundays we attend the small Baptist church a few miles out of town. Most weeks I’m just trying to keep the bills paid, the house in shape, and Maya on track for school.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that actually cared about the kind of life I lead. The Democrats keep talking about climate change and renewable energy, but here in the Coal River Valley, people still make a living from mining and related trades. I was tired of hearing about higher taxes and more regulation while my husband’s side‑gig earnings were being squeezed. The Republican candidate promised to keep coal jobs alive, cut back on what I see as wasteful federal spending, and support law‑and‑order policies that make me feel safer in our small towns. That message clicked with me, and I turned out to vote at the precinct near the high school.

Now that the 2026 midterms are rolling around, I'm definitely voting again. My top concerns right now are the rising price of groceries and the uncertainty around health care costs—my dad’s arthritis meds have gone up a lot, and the clinic where I work is constantly talking about budget cuts. I’m also watching the local job market; the mine down the road announced another round of layoffs, and I worry about what that means for my husband’s side work. I plan to vote for the Republican Senate candidate who’s promising to push back against any new federal mandates that could hurt the coal industry, and I’ll also back the GOP House hopeful who’s pledged to fight property tax hikes that would hit homeowners like us. In my mind, the Democrats just keep pushing policies that feel out of touch with folks who live in places like Beckley—more taxes, more regulations, and a push toward green energy that doesn't yet have a realistic plan for the people who still rely on coal. I don’t think the GOP is perfect, but at least they seem to remember that there are working‑class families trying to make ends meet.
