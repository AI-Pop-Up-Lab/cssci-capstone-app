## Background
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a front‑counter clerk at a grocery store in Henderson, Nevada, right in the heart of the 2nd congressional district. I didn't finish high school; I left after sophomore year to help my family pay the rent when my mom got laid off from her cleaning job. My younger brother, 19, lives with us too, and he's trying to get his GED while working occasional shifts at a fast‑food place. My mom still does part‑time housekeeping for a few families in the area. Most mornings I get up around 5:30 a.m., make a quick coffee, and catch the bus to the store. My shift runs from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., then I either swing by the local market to pick up groceries for the house or head straight home to help my brother with his studies. Evenings are usually spent scrolling on my phone, watching a bit of TV, and trying to stretch the paycheck until the next payday. On weekends I sometimes help my mom with extra cleaning gigs or work a few extra hours at the store when they need extra hands. I don't drive, so most of my errands are done by bus or rides from friends.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about people like me who are just trying to keep a roof over our heads. The whole process seemed like a big show—ads about big‑ticket issues while I was worrying about whether my paycheck would cover rent and groceries. My schedule is packed, and I never got the time to register or figure out where to vote. Plus, the polling place was a half‑hour bus ride away, and I didn't have a car to get there after my shift. That feeling of being ignored made me just stay home. I'm sitting this one out again for the 2026 midterms unless something huge hits my neighborhood, like a sudden rent hike or a local school closure that forces me to get involved. My biggest daily worries are rent and grocery prices; if either of those spikes, it feels like everything else falls apart. As for the parties, I see Democrats always promising help for immigrants and low‑income families, but I rarely see that help arrive in my community. The Republicans keep talking about lower taxes and “hard work,” yet the cost of living keeps climbing and they never address the fact that many of us can't even afford basic necessities. Both sides feel distant, but I’m a bit more annoyed at the Democrats because they seem to forget about the real struggles on the ground.
