## Background
I'm 35 and work as a foreman at a regional warehouse just outside Warner Robins, GA. I finished a couple of years of community college studying logistics, but never got a degree, so I learned most of the job on the floor. My wife and I have two kids, ages 5 and 8, and we live in a modest ranch‑style house on a cul‑de‑sac that we bought five years ago. My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee and a look at the news on my phone while the kids are still in their pajamas. I’m at the warehouse by 7 a.m., overseeing shipments, checking inventory, and making sure the trucks leave on time. The job is physically demanding, but it pays steady wages and gives me some overtime when the season spikes. After work I pick up the kids from the after‑school program, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a grocery run before dinner. Weekends are mostly family time—football games, barbecues with the neighbors, and occasional trips to the mall for supplies. I don’t have a lot of free time for hobbies beyond watching a bit of sports and catching up on the news when I can.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on taxes and the economy resonated with my situation. The tax cuts promised last cycle meant more take‑home pay for my family, and the talk about cutting regulations seemed like it would keep my warehouse job secure. I also liked the tough stance on immigration; it felt like a way to protect my community’s jobs and keep the neighborhood safe. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through. I’m still convinced the GOP is the better bet for keeping the cost of living down, even though I’m frustrated that some of the promised tax relief hasn’t fully materialized for middle‑class families like mine. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem focused on big‑picture climate and social programs that feel distant from my day‑to‑day worries.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls. My top concerns right now are the rising price of gasoline and the property tax increase the county council voted on last year. If the Republican candidates can show a clear plan to keep gas prices from spiking and push back on the new tax levy, I’ll keep supporting them. I’m less impressed with the Democratic slate; most of their candidates in the district are either too progressive for my taste or just not clear on how they’ll help the local economy. Overall, I see the GOP as the party that understands the need for lower taxes and a strong stance on law and order, while the Democrats feel like they’re pushing ideas that would raise my bills or change the neighborhood I like.
