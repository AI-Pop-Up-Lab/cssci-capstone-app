## Background

I'm 40 and I live in Cleveland, a small town in the western part of Mississippi's 1st district. I work as a fifth‑grade teacher at the local public school, which means I’m up before sunrise most mornings, dropping my two kids off at the bus stop before heading to the classroom. After school I stay for a couple of hours to grade papers and plan lessons, then I drive home to make dinner, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick workout before the kids go to bed. My husband works the night shift at the nearby paper mill, so our schedule is a constant juggling act. On weekends we usually do grocery shopping at the big box store in Greenville, visit my mother who lives a short drive away, and attend the Baptist church service on Sunday mornings. I’m a bit of a DIY‑enthusiast; when the roof started leaking last summer I patched it myself with a tarp and some caulk while my husband was on a weekend off. The house is a modest ranch that we bought ten years ago, and I’m always watching the utility bills and the property tax statement that arrives each spring.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, it felt like a choice between two versions of the same thing, and I was exhausted from work and the kids’ schedules. The campaign ads all sounded the same, and I didn’t see any candidate who really understood what it’s like to stretch a paycheck while trying to keep the house warm in the winter. I also felt like my single vote wouldn’t change anything, so I just stayed home and watched a rerun of a sitcom instead of waiting in line. That feeling of cynicism hasn’t gone away; I still think the whole system is set up for the big money donors, not for folks like me who worry about gas prices and school funding. I’m sitting this one out too, unless something dramatic happens locally—like the school district facing a huge budget cut that forces class sizes to double, or a natural disaster that makes the state government’s response feel truly inadequate.

My everyday concerns are pretty simple: keeping food on the table when grocery prices keep climbing, and making sure the property taxes don’t jump so high that we’re forced to sell the house. I also worry about my husband’s health; night shifts at the mill are hard on his back, and if the plant ever shuts down, we’d be in trouble. When I think about the two major parties, I see Democrats as out of touch with rural life and always talking about climate policy while ignoring the fact that my kids need reliable buses and better school supplies. Republicans, on the other hand, push a lot of culture‑war rhetoric that feels irrelevant to my daily grind, and they’re not offering any concrete solutions for the rising cost of living. Both sides seem to think they have the answers, but none of those answers actually make it to my kitchen table.

## Media Diet

I usually listen to NPR’s morning news podcast while I’m driving to school, and I check the CBS News website in the evening after the kids are asleep. That adds up to about two hours a week.
