## Background  
I'm 80 and I live in a modest bungalow on the north side of Montgomery, right in the 3rd congressional district. I spent 35 years teaching elementary school math and reading, and I retired five years ago after my husband passed. My two grown children live in Birmingham and Mobile, and I see them a few times a year, but most of my days are spent with my granddaughter who visits on weekends and helps me with the garden. I get up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and sit on the porch while the sunrise hits the magnolia trees. After a quick walk around the block with my old Labrador, I turn on the TV for the local news, then check my phone for headlines from NPR and ABC. I spend my afternoons knitting, attending weekly choir practice at the church, and sometimes running errands at the grocery store down the street. Sundays are the highlight—potluck dinner with the church fellowship, a few hymns, and a long conversation about family and health. I keep a small budget, so I watch my utility bills and try to stretch my Social Security check as far as it can go.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything, and the whole campaign seemed like a circus of promises that never touch folks like me. I was also dealing with a bunch of medical appointments and a sudden burst of home repairs after a pipe burst, so the ballot just slipped off my radar. The feeling of being ignored by both parties—Democrats talking about “justice” but never fixing the clinic down the road, Republicans preaching “family values” while cutting funding for senior services—made me pretty cynical. I told myself I'd sit this one out too, unless something huge hit my neighborhood, like a major hospital closure or a scandal that directly affects my pension. So I'm sitting this one out. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the uncertainty around my fixed income—if property taxes go up or my health insurance premiums climb, I’m in trouble. I also keep an eye on local job opportunities because a few of my friends are still working part‑time and need stable wages. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as well‑meaning but out of touch with the real grind, and the Republicans as focused on cultural battles that don’t help my grocery bill. Neither side feels like they’re listening to seniors in the Black community.

## Media Diet  
I listen to NPR on my morning walk, catch the ABC evening broadcast after dinner, and skim the headlines on CBS News before bed, adding up to about eleven hours a week.
