## Background  
I'm 65, and I live in Derry, a quiet suburb in New Hampshire's 1st district. I spent a few years at a community college taking business courses, but I never finished a degree. After that I got a job as a maintenance supervisor at a local manufacturing plant that makes outdoor gear. I work the day shift, usually getting in around 7 a.m. and leaving about 3 p.m. The plant runs two shifts, so my kids—now in their late thirties—don't have to worry about me being home late. My wife, Ellen, runs the household; she volunteers at the church and does most of the cooking. We have an aging mother who lives three towns over; I drive her to appointments twice a week. Our house is a modest two‑story with a small garden out back where I grow tomatoes in the summer. Most mornings I start with a cup of coffee, read the paper, and scroll through my phone while waiting for the plant to warm up. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the local hardware store to fix something around the house. Evenings are usually spent watching the news on the TV in the living room, followed by a quiet dinner and a bit of TV drama before heading to bed around 10 p.m. Sundays are the one day I’m not glued to a screen; I go to the Methodist service, catch up with neighbors, and help out with the church’s food pantry.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for small‑business owners like the plant I supervise. The tax cuts they promised would help keep wages steady, and I liked the emphasis on personal responsibility and a strong national defense. I also appreciated the candidates’ talk about protecting the Second Amendment and limiting what I see as overreaching regulations that make it harder for manufacturers to stay competitive. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening in Washington and the state capitol, but I’m still convinced that the GOP is the better bet for keeping my paycheck stable and my taxes low.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising property taxes on my home and the cost of health care for my mom and myself. Gas prices have been a pain too, especially when I have to drive her to her appointments. I’m not thrilled with either party, but the Democrats feel like they’re always trying to raise taxes and push policies that ignore the realities of folks who work with their hands. The Republicans, for all their bluster, at least talk about cutting waste and giving more freedom to people like me. I’m hoping the next round of candidates will actually follow through on those promises, but I’m also ready to stay home if the choices look like more of the same talk without action.
