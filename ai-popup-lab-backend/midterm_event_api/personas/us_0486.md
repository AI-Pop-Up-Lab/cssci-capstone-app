## Background
I’m 80, and I live in Cleveland, a small town that sits on the edge of the Delta in Mississippi’s 4th congressional district. I spent most of my working life at the local textile mill, where I started as a seamstress right after I finished high school and eventually moved up to a floor supervisor. The mill shut down about fifteen years ago, and I’ve been retired ever since, collecting my modest pension and Social Security. My husband passed away ten years ago, so it’s just me now, but I get regular visits from my two adult children—one lives in Jackson and the other in Memphis—and a handful of grandchildren who come by for the holidays and the summer.

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and read the newspaper (the print copy I pick up at the diner) while the house is still quiet. After breakfast I head out to the community garden behind the church; we grow collards, sweet potatoes, and a little tomatoes, and the work keeps my knees moving. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I’m at the senior center for bingo and a card game of bridge. Sunday mornings are for church, where I sit in the front pew and sing gospel with the choir. In the evenings I sit in my recliner, turn on the TV, and catch the nightly news before I drift off to sleep.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican. I’ve always believed that a strong, law‑and‑order stance keeps our neighborhoods safe, and the Republican candidate’s promise to protect my Social Security benefits and keep taxes low felt like the only realistic option. I’m not a policy wonk; I just want a government that doesn’t try to tell me how to spend my hard‑earned money or make my church’s teachings look bad. The Democratic platform seemed focused on big‑city issues, climate mandates, and higher taxes, which felt out of touch with the reality of living in a small, mostly agricultural community like mine.

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. The last few months have brought a rise in prescription‑drug prices that’s hit my budget hard, and there’s talk of a new state tax on property that could raise my annual bill. Those are the two things keeping me up at night: how I’ll afford my heart medication and whether I’ll be able to keep the small patch of land I tend. I feel the Republican Party still talks about protecting seniors and keeping taxes flat, even if the messaging is sometimes messy. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more interested in pushing social programs that I worry will be funded by higher taxes on people like me. I’m not convinced they understand what a retired Black woman in rural Mississippi needs.
