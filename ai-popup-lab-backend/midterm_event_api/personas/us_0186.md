## Background
I'm 80, and I live in Duluth, a quiet suburb in Georgia's 2nd congressional district. I spent 35 years as a high‑school math teacher before I finally retired last year. My husband passed away three years ago, so it's just me, my two grown‑up kids, and the occasional weekend visit from my three grandkids. Most mornings I take a slow walk around the neighborhood pond, then head back to the house to water my geraniums and check the mail. After that I usually make a pot of tea, read the daily paper, and flip on the TV for the morning news. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I volunteer at the local community center, helping with the computer class for seniors—something I enjoy because it keeps my mind sharp and lets me meet folks my own age. Grocery shopping is a Saturday ritual; I drive to the nearby Walmart, grab a few fresh veggies, and chat with the cashier about the weather. Evenings are when I settle into my recliner, watch a couple of news segments, and maybe call my daughter to see how her new job is going. I’m not into anything fancy—just a simple routine that keeps me busy and connected.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best represented my concerns about keeping taxes low, protecting the border, and preserving the values I grew up with. I liked the candidate’s promise to keep property taxes from climbing and to support small‑business owners like the grocery store I shop at. I also appreciated the emphasis on a strong national defense, which resonated with my memories of the Cold War era. Since then, I’ve watched the political talk shows and read the articles from my usual outlets, and while I’m still skeptical of a lot of the rhetoric, the Democratic side feels even more out of touch with folks like me—focused on big‑city climate policies and social issues that don’t affect my daily life.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the ever‑increasing property taxes that make it harder to stay in my home. I also worry about the condition of the local roads; potholes are getting worse and the county seems to be putting money into projects far away from our neighborhood. As for the parties, I think the Republicans are the ones who understand the need for fiscal restraint, even if some of the newer voices in the party are a bit too loud for my taste. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to be pushing a progressive agenda that feels disconnected from the practical problems I see on my street—like getting reliable broadband for seniors or keeping the community center funded. I’m not a fan of either side’s extremes, but I’ll back the candidate I think will keep taxes down and make sure my seniors get the healthcare they need.
