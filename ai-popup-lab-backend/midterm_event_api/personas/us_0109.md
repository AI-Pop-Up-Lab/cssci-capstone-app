## Background
I'm 70 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Greeley, right in the middle of Colorado's 8th district. I’m a member of the Southern Ute tribe, and most of my adult life has been spent taking care of family. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two grown‑up kids moved to Denver and Phoenix for work. I still see my grandson every other weekend; he helps me with the grocery runs and keeps me from getting too isolated.

I never finished high school; after the eighth grade I started working at the local grocery store, first stocking shelves and later helping at the checkout. Now I’m retired from that job, but I pick up a few hours a week doing odd jobs—cleaning houses for other seniors, running errands for a neighbor, and helping at the tribal community center. My days usually start early with a cup of coffee, a short walk around the neighborhood, and then a visit to the community center where I help set up the bingo tables. After lunch I might be at the library or the health clinic, and evenings are for watching the news, knitting, or chatting on the phone with my daughter.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about health care, Social Security, and the funding that supports tribal clinics. The Republican platform seemed all about cutting taxes for big businesses, and I worried that would mean less money for the programs that keep my doctor visits affordable. I also liked the candidate’s promises to protect public lands, which matter a lot to my tribe.

Since then I’ve watched the news and talked with folks at the community center, and my feelings haven’t changed much. I’m still convinced the Democrats are the better bet for keeping my prescription costs down and for protecting the water rights we rely on. I’m not blind to the flaws in either party—both have politicians who talk a lot and deliver little—but I’m more annoyed by the GOP’s constant attacks on tribal sovereignty. So for the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls; I’m voting.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising price of groceries and the fact that my Medicare coverage sometimes feels like a maze. I also keep an eye on property taxes because the county has been looking at raising them to fund new roads, and that would bite into my fixed income. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who at least try to protect the safety net, while Republicans feel like they’re mostly interested in cutting government programs that I depend on.

## Media Diet
I usually watch the morning clips from ABC News while sipping coffee, then catch an NPR hour in the evening after dinner, and I flip through a quick CBS News roundup on my tablet before bed. All together it adds up to about four hours a week.
