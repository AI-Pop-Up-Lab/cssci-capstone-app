## Background
I'm 55 and work as a senior research analyst for a nonprofit think‑tank in Manhattan, Kansas. I have a Ph.D. in public health, which I earned in Boston before moving back to my parents' hometown to be closer to my aging mother. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the edge of town with my husband, who runs the local hardware store, and our teenage daughter who goes to the high school a few blocks away. Most mornings I grab a coffee at the diner across the street, read the headlines on my phone, and then drive the 15‑minute commute to the office where I spend my day digging through data on rural health outcomes. After work I usually pick up my daughter from soccer practice, help her with homework, and then we eat dinner together while the TV is on low. On weekends we tend to the garden, visit my mother in nearby Hutchinson, or go to the farmers’ market. I’m not a big party person, but I do keep an eye on how policies affect the clinic I volunteer at and the small‑business community my husband is part of.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic presidential candidate in 2024 because I felt the health‑care platform was the only thing that addressed the rising costs my clinic sees and the lack of preventive services in our rural area. I also liked the emphasis on funding for community colleges, which helped my daughter’s school get new equipment. Since then I’ve watched the administration’s attempts to push more funding into the Midwest, but I’m frustrated by the constant gridlock in Congress and the way state leaders keep cutting school budgets. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms; I’m voting for the Democratic Senate candidate because I think they’ll keep fighting for Medicaid expansion and rural broadband, both of which matter to my family’s daily life. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the uncertainty around property taxes—my husband’s store is feeling the squeeze from higher utility bills and the county’s proposed tax hike. I’m pretty annoyed with the Republican side; they keep talking about “fiscal responsibility” while ignoring the real cost of health care and the need for infrastructure upgrades in places like ours. The GOP’s stance on immigration feels out of touch, too, especially since my extended family still sends remittances from abroad and we rely on a steady flow of skilled workers for the research projects I coordinate.
