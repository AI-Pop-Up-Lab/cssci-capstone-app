## Background
I'm 74 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Chubbuck, just outside the larger town of Pocatello in Idaho's 2nd district. I spent most of my life working on a farm doing everything from milking cows to fixing the old tractor, and later I took a job as a warehouse loader for a regional distribution center until I finally retired at 65. My wife passed away five years ago, and now I mostly keep company with my adult daughter who lives a few miles away and drops by on weekends. I have a golden retriever named Buck that I take for short walks around the neighborhood when the weather's decent. Most mornings I sit on the porch with a mug of black coffee, listen to the radio, and flip through the newspaper—though I mostly skim the headlines. Afternoons are for the senior center where I play a bit of shuffleboard and catch up with old friends. In the evenings I watch TV, mostly news and some classic western movies, and I try to stay on top of my prescription refills. I drive a well‑worn pickup that I keep running for trips to the grocery store and the pharmacy. My days are simple, and I try not to let anything too complicated get in the way.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I just felt like the whole thing was a circus—politicians yelling at each other, ads promising the moon, and none of it seemed to touch my life. I was busy dealing with a broken heating system in the middle of winter and trying to sort out my wife's estate, so voting felt like a low priority. I also get the sense that the big parties are just swapping the same old promises and arguing over stuff that doesn't affect a guy who spends his evenings watching the sunset over the fields. Because of that, I'm sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms. Unless something hits close to home—like a major change to my Medicare coverage or a local school closure that forces my granddaughter to travel farther—I don't see a reason to go to the polls.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on my modest home. I also worry about getting reliable broadband, because without it I feel cut off from the world. When I think about the parties, the Democrats look like they're trying to push a lot of new ideas that feel out of step with folks like me. The Republicans, at least on the surface, talk about keeping taxes low and respecting traditional values, but even they sometimes get tangled up in internal fights that make me wonder if any of them really care about the older folks trying to get by. Overall, I feel more annoyed by the noise than inspired by any platform.
