## Background
I'm 20 and I'm finishing a master's in public health at the university in York, Pennsylvania, right in the middle of the 4th congressional district. Most days I wake up at 7 am, grab a coffee from the campus café, and head to my lab where I help run data analyses for a community health project. After labs I have a couple of graduate seminars, then I usually meet up with a roommate in our two‑bedroom apartment to cook dinner—mostly pasta or stir‑fry because it's cheap and quick. On weekends I work a few shifts as a barista at a nearby coffee shop to help cover rent; the place is always busy with students and commuters, so I learn a lot about what people are complaining about, from grocery prices to unreliable public transit. I stay in touch with my parents, who live in a small town in the same district, through weekly video calls; they keep asking when I'm moving back home, but I’m not sure yet. In my free time I scroll TikTok, binge a true‑crime podcast, and try to squeeze in a jog around the campus lake when the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because between school, my research, and my part‑time job I felt like there was never a moment when I could actually get to the polls, and honestly I was fed up with the whole circus. The ads were all the same, the candidates seemed to promise the same things, and I didn't trust any of them to actually listen to someone like me who is still in school and juggling a lot of bills. That feeling hasn't changed much, so I'm sitting this one out too. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are keeping up with tuition payments and making sure I can afford rent when my stipend gets cut next semester. The other thing that keeps me up is the rising cost of groceries; I have to watch every line item on my budget. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats pushing policies that sound good on paper but feel out of touch with the reality of a student trying to survive on a limited income. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to double down on cultural issues that don't address the financial pressures I'm feeling. Both sides feel like they're talking past each other, and that makes me even less likely to cast a ballot.
