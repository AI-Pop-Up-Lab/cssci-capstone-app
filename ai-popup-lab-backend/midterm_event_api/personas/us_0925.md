## Background
I'm 74 and I live in Battle Ground, a small suburb in Washington's 7th district. I retired a few years ago after spending most of my working life as a clerical assistant at a local hardware store, a job I could keep with the two‑year community college certificate I earned back in the early '80s. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two grown children live in Seattle and Spokane, so most days I'm on my own. I get up around 7 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and spend an hour reading the newspaper or scrolling through the headlines on my tablet. After that I head out to the grocery store, usually catching up with the same cashier who knows my usual brand of oatmeal. In the afternoons I attend a weekly knitting circle at the community center, and on Tuesdays I volunteer a few hours helping the local senior hub with paperwork. Evenings are quiet – I watch a bit of TV, maybe a game show, and try to get to bed before 10 p.m. I keep my house modest; the mortgage is paid off, but I still worry about property tax hikes and the cost of my prescription meds.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between a bout of flu that left me bedridden for a week and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about people my age, I just stayed home. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything. I also missed the early voting window because I was caring for my mother, who lives a few counties away and needed help after a fall. That left a sour taste, and I haven't been motivated to get back into the habit. As the 2026 midterms approach, I'm sitting this one out again unless something dramatic happens locally – like a sudden cut to senior services or a scandal that directly affects my community. My biggest worries right now are the rising price of my blood pressure medicine and the possibility of my property taxes going up enough to force me to sell the house I've lived in for four decades. I see the Democrats as talking a lot about climate and social justice but rarely addressing the day‑to‑day cash flow issues seniors face. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on tax cuts for businesses and less on protecting Social Security. Both parties feel distant, and I tend to tune them out unless a candidate actually comes to the senior center and listens.

## Media Diet
I spend about four hours a week with the news – a quick NPR roundup with my coffee in the morning and a half‑hour of ABC or CBS clips in the evening after dinner.
