## Background  
I'm 80 and I live in a small house on a quiet street in Doylestown, right in the heart of the 1st district. I spent most of my adult life as a public‑school teacher, teaching elementary math and reading. I retired about ten years ago, but I still volunteer a few hours a week at the local senior center, helping with bingo nights and the reading program. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two children live in Philadelphia and Harrisburg, so I get regular phone calls and the occasional visit. My oldest grandson comes over on Sundays for lunch, and I love making his favorite tacos.  

My days are pretty routine. I get up early, brew a strong cup of coffee, and take a short walk around the neighborhood before the traffic gets heavy. After that I sit at the kitchen table and flip through the newspaper or scroll on my tablet while I eat breakfast. Around mid‑morning I head to the senior center for a craft class or a chair‑yoga session; sometimes I just sit in the lounge and chat with the other retirees. In the afternoon I do a little grocery shopping at the local market, stop by the pharmacy for my blood‑pressure meds, and then tend to my garden—tomatoes and a few herbs. Evenings are when I catch up on the news, either on TV or on my tablet, before winding down with a mystery novel. I’m not tech‑savvy, but I can manage a video call when my family wants to show me pictures of a new grandchild.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about keeping taxes low and protecting the country’s borders. After the last few years of what I saw as endless spending and the constant talk about “big government,” I wanted a candidate who promised to balance the budget and support the military. I also appreciated the emphasis on traditional values—things like respecting the flag and honoring those who served.  

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. The last election felt like a wake‑up call; I saw a lot of talk about higher taxes and new regulations that would hit retirees like me hard. My top concerns right now are the cost of prescription drugs and the rising property taxes on my home. I’ve had to cut back on some of the vitamins I used to take because the pharmacy keeps raising prices, and the county assessor just sent another notice about a higher assessment. I’m also watching the local job market because a few of my former coworkers are still looking for work, and the town’s small businesses are struggling with what they call “inflation.”  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty sure the Democrats have lost touch with folks who have paid their dues for decades. Their push for big social programs sounds good in theory, but when the taxes go up, it’s my paycheck that feels the pinch. The Republicans, on the other hand, sometimes sound like they’re only talking about the wealthy, but at least they keep saying “lower taxes” and “secure borders.” I’m not blind to the fact that both sides have flaws; I just feel the GOP is closer to what I need right now.
