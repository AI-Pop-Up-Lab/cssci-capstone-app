## Background
I'm 30 and I work as a software developer for a small fintech startup that’s based in Burlington. I grew up in Burlington’s South End, went to the University of Vermont for computer science, and moved back after college because I liked the mix of city life and easy access to the Green Mountains. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with my partner, Maya, and our rescued Labrador, Scout. We split the rent and utilities, and most evenings we cook together in our tiny kitchen while a playlist of old R&B and indie folk fills the room.

My weekdays start at 6:30 a.m. with a quick bike ride to the office; the ride is a good way to clear my head before I dive into code reviews and sprint planning. I grab a coffee at a local café, catch up on a few headlines on my phone, and then spend eight hours behind a screen. After work I sometimes swing by the farmer’s market or meet friends for a beer at a downtown bar. Weekends are a mix of hiking, checking out the local music scene, and helping out at a community tutoring program where I teach high schoolers basic programming. I don’t travel a lot—most of my free time is spent in the state, either on the lake or in the woods.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable healthcare and climate action, both of which matter a lot to me. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding broadband in rural Vermont, which would help my sister who lives up in a more remote part of the state. Since then I’ve been keeping an eye on how the administration follows through, and I’m still pretty convinced that the Democrats are the better bet for my priorities.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—there’s a Senate race that could affect how much funding our state gets for green infrastructure, and I don’t want to sit this one out. My top everyday concerns are rising rent and grocery prices; the cost of living in Burlington has gone up faster than my salary, and I’m watching my budget get tighter each month. I also worry about the reliability of the local power grid as we push for more renewable energy. I see the Democratic Party as the one trying to address those issues, even if they move slowly. The Republican Party, in my view, seems more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like a step backward for folks like me who rely on a stable, affordable community.
