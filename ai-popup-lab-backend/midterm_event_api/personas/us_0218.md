## Background  
I'm 55 and work as a senior research engineer for a renewable‑energy company that has a small office in Kailua. I got my PhD in electrical engineering from Stanford, then spent a few years in California before moving back to O‘ahu to be closer to my family. My wife, who teaches at the local community college, and I have two kids – a 20‑year‑old daughter living in Seattle and a 16‑year‑old son who goes to the high school in our district. Most mornings I grab a coffee, check the news on my tablet, and drive the 15‑minute stretch to the office where I spend the bulk of the day tweaking solar‑panel designs and running simulations. After work I either head to the beach for a quick surf session or help my son with his physics homework. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the market, family barbecues with neighbors, and occasional trips to the hiking trails on the windward side. Because housing is pricey here, we keep a modest mortgage and try to save for the kids’ college funds.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s tax proposals would help my company stay competitive and keep my paycheck stable, and I was tired of the national conversation focusing on social issues that seemed far removed from my day‑to‑day concerns. I also liked the candidate’s promise to cut red tape on renewable projects, which lined up with my work. Since then I’ve watched the Democrats push for higher taxes on businesses and more regulation on energy, which I think will hurt the local economy and push up prices for everyone. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms – I’m voting, and I’ll probably back the Republican Senate and House candidates who keep the focus on low taxes and less government interference. My biggest worries are the rising cost of housing and groceries; even though my salary is decent, everything else seems to be getting more expensive. I also worry about tourism’s impact on traffic and the environment, but I think the GOP’s stance on protecting local jobs while easing zoning rules could help. The Democrats, in my view, are all talk about climate and equity but they ignore the practical side of keeping businesses afloat and keeping taxes low. I’m not a fan of either party’s extreme wing, but I lean hard toward the Republicans because they seem more in touch with what keeps my family’s budget from falling apart.
