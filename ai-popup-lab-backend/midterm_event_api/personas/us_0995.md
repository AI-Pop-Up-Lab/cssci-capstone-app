## Background
I'm 25 and work as a data analyst for the U.S. Geological Survey, based out of Cheyenne. I finished a master's in statistics at the University of Wyoming and landed a contract position that lets me stay in the city most of the week. My roommate, also a grad student, and I rent a two‑bedroom apartment near the historic downtown; the rent is high for Wyoming standards, so we split everything down to the grocery bill. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the local diner, scroll through the headlines on my phone while the car idles on the way to the office, and spend about an hour in front of a dual monitor setup crunching numbers on water usage data. After work I usually hit the gym or take a quick hike in the foothills before cooking something quick—often a stir‑fry with whatever's on sale. Weekends are a mix of catching a Lakers game at a friend's place, helping my aunt with her small online boutique, and volunteering at the community center where I teach basic computer skills to teens. I stay in Wyoming because the open space feels like home, even though I sometimes feel like the only Black face at the office.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate talked about keeping taxes low and protecting the energy jobs that keep a lot of my friends employed in the oil and gas sector. I also liked the promise of less federal overreach, which felt like a better fit for someone who works for a federal agency but wants more local control. The campaign ads on the radio and a few rallies in Cheyenne reinforced that message, and I thought my vote could help keep the cost of living from spiraling up even more. Since then, I’ve watched the economy wobble a bit, and a few of my coworkers have lost overtime hours, but I still think the GOP is the safer bet for my paycheck and the state's budget. That said, I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I’m annoyed when either side talks down to folks like me or ignores the real‑world problems of a small‑town worker.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of rent and the uncertainty around the energy sector’s future—if the market keeps shifting, my job could be at risk. I’m also watching health‑care costs; a recent emergency room visit reminded me how quickly a bill can pile up. I view the Democrats as well‑meaning but too quick to push big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with Wyoming’s rugged independence. The Republicans, in my opinion, get the tax and energy policies right most of the time, but I’m frustrated when they ignore social issues that affect minorities or when they trade on guilt‑by‑association politics. Overall, I’m leaning toward the GOP but I’m keeping an eye on any candidate who actually addresses the everyday bills I have to pay.
