## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Yakima, right near the river that cuts through town. I work the afternoon shift at the local grocery store, stocking shelves and helping customers find the right produce. It's not glamorous, but the schedule lets me be home for dinner and to check on my dad who lives a few miles away and needs help with his medication. My husband retired from the highway department a few years back, so we’re on a fixed income now. Our two kids are grown, one in Seattle with a tech job, the other in Portland with a small bakery. Most weekends I’m either fixing up the garden, attending the community church potluck, or driving my dad to his physical therapy appointments. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan that I keep on a tight budget, and I try to stretch every paycheck because everything seems to cost more than it used to. I watch the local news on TV when I can, but most of my info comes from a handful of national sites I check on my phone during lunch and after work.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working double shifts during the election week, taking my dad to a series of doctor visits, and feeling like none of the candidates really represented what I needed, I just stayed home. I’ve been skeptical of politics for a long time—campaign ads feel like noise, and the promises never seem to stick around long enough to make a difference in my life. I’m not planning to go to the polls in the 2026 midterms either; unless something really blows up locally, like a big scandal with a state senator or a sudden cut to the senior services my dad relies on, I’ll probably sit this one out too. My biggest worries right now are the grocery bills that keep creeping up and the cost of my dad’s prescriptions, which eat up a sizable chunk of our budget. Property taxes are also on my mind, especially with the recent assessments that have bumped my bill higher than I can comfortably afford. When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Democrats seem to push for higher taxes and regulations that hit small‑business workers like me, while the Republicans keep talking about “family values” and “law and order” but ignore the everyday costs of healthcare and groceries that hit families like ours. Both sides feel disconnected from the reality of people who are just trying to get by.
