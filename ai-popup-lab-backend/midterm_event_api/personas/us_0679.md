## Background
I'm 45 and I live in the town of Endicott, just outside Binghamton in the 17th District. I work full‑time as a forklift operator at a regional distribution center; the job is physically demanding and the shift starts at 6 a.m., so I’m usually up by 4:30 to get a quick coffee and drop the kids off at school. My wife works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have two kids—one in high school and a middle‑schooler. On weekends I do a bit of hand‑yman work for neighbors, which helps with the mortgage and the occasional car repair. Our house is a modest two‑story built in the 80s, and we’re still paying the original mortgage, so every month the property tax bill feels like a surprise. After work I usually watch a couple of news clips while I’m on the road home, then help the kids with homework, and try to squeeze in a little TV time before bed. On Sundays we’re at church in the morning and then head to the local fair if the weather’s nice. I’m not a big reader; most of my news comes from my phone in the evenings.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same things, and I was exhausted from juggling two jobs, kids’ schedules, and a never‑ending pile of bills. The polls kept saying “your vote matters,” but when you’re trying to get the kids to school, get to a night shift, and keep the house heated, the ballot feels like a low‑priority item. I also think a lot of the political talk is just noise—campaign ads, endless punditry, and promises that never materialize. That cynicism hasn’t really faded, so I’m sitting this one out too, unless something huge hits my community, like a major plant closing or a scandal that directly affects my family’s paycheck.

My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the property taxes that keep creeping up each year. A decent paycheck that can cover the bills and still leave room for a family vacation is getting harder to find. I’m also watching the local job market; the warehouse I work at has been cutting overtime, and I worry about automation. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem more focused on cultural battles than on making real jobs, while the Republicans push for cuts that would hit services I rely on, like public schools and road maintenance. Neither side feels like they’re listening to folks who are just trying to keep the lights on.
