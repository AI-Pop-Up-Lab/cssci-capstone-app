## Background  
I'm 54 and I live in Sturgis, out near the Badlands. I work full‑time as a maintenance guy at the local casino‑hotel; it’s mostly fixing things that break, hauling supplies, and making sure the place stays clean. I never finished high school, so I learned most of what I know on the job and from coworkers. My wife, who used to work at a nursing home, retired a few years ago, and we both keep an eye on my mother who lives in a small assisted‑living facility in Rapid City. Our two kids are in their late twenties now—one is a truck driver up in North Dakota, the other works at a grocery store in Minneapolis—so the house is pretty quiet most days.

My day starts around 5 a.m. with a quick coffee, then I head to the casino for a 7 a.m. shift. After work I usually swing by the grocery store, grab a sandwich, and sit on the porch watching the sunset while the kids on my phone play video games. Sundays are the only day I don’t work; we go to the local Baptist church, stay for the potluck lunch, and catch up with neighbors. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I help my wife with the garden, trying to grow some tomatoes and beans. I don’t drive long distances often, but I’m comfortable with my pickup and the occasional trip to the nearest big‑box store for tools or fuel.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a show—none of the candidates seemed to care about folks like me who are just trying to get by. The ballots were long, the ads were all the same talking points, and I was working a double shift the day before the election, so I just didn’t bother. I’ve gotten used to thinking that my vote won’t change anything, especially when the issues that matter to me—like getting decent broadband in the rural parts of the district or affordable prescription drugs—never get any real attention.

I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something huge happens, like a major hospital closing or a big scandal that hits my community directly, I’m not going to make the effort to get to the polls. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the fact that my internet still drops out every other day, making it hard to stay in touch with my kids or look up simple things online. Property taxes are also a pain; the county keeps raising them and the services we get back feel thin.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats keep promising more health care and social programs, but when I call my local office they act like I’m just another number. The Republicans talk a lot about cutting taxes, but most of the tax breaks go to big businesses and the rich, not to people who are grinding day in and day out. I don’t feel represented by either side, which is why I just stay home on election day.

## Media Diet  
I spend about five hours a week scrolling through the news on my phone and watching the evening broadcasts. I usually catch the morning headlines on ABC, then tune into NBC at night after my shift, and I’ll flip to a CBS segment if there’s a story I missed.
