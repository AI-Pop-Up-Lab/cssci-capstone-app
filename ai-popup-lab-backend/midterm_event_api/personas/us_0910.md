## Background  
I'm 25 and work as a program coordinator for a nonprofit that runs after‑school tutoring in Burlington. I graduated from the University of Vermont with a degree in sociology, so I know the basics of grant writing and data entry, but most of my day is juggling schedules for volunteers, making phone calls to parents, and trying to keep the budget from going negative. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment on a quiet side street, sharing it with a roommate who runs a coffee shop downtown. My parents live in a small town near Rutland, and I fly back home for Thanksgiving and sometimes to help my mom with yard work. Mornings start with a quick espresso and scrolling through the NPR app while I bike to the office; lunch is usually a sandwich from the food truck outside the library, and evenings are spent either grading program reports or catching up on a show on Netflix before I fall asleep around 11 p.m. I drive a modest used Subaru, keep a modest budget, and try to set aside a little for a rainy‑day fund, though rent and utilities take up most of my paycheck.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health care and stronger climate policies—issues that hit close to home when my younger cousin was diagnosed with asthma and when I see the snow‑melt flooding our streets every spring. I also liked the candidate’s promise to protect funding for community programs like the one I work for. Since then, I've kept an eye on how the administration handled the student‑loan forgiveness rollout and the recent push for renewable energy projects in the state. I'm planning to vote in the 2026 midterms; the Senate race feels especially important because the incumbent has been vague on climate legislation, and there's a competitive House contest in my district. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of rent and the lingering student‑loan balances many of my peers still carry. I think the Democratic Party generally aligns with my values, but I get frustrated when they seem more focused on national talk shows than on concrete help for small towns like mine. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel out of touch with the climate crisis and with the financial strain on younger folks; I’m skeptical they’ll address the issues that matter to me without a major shift in their platform.
