## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a walk‑up condo on the north side of Burlington. I work as a registered nurse at the local hospital, rotating between the ER and the medical‑surgical floor. My shift usually starts at 7 a.m., so I’m up early, make a quick coffee, and catch the morning headlines on ABC News while I’m getting dressed. After my 12‑hour stretch, I come home around 8 p.m., eat dinner that I usually prep on the weekend, and watch a NPR podcast while I unwind. My husband retired from the state forest service a few years ago, and we split our time between gardening on the balcony and helping out at the community garden downtown. Our only child lives in Boston and visits during the holidays. On Saturdays I volunteer at a free‑clinic pop‑up in a neighboring town, and on Sundays I like to stroll along the waterfront and stop at the farmer’s market for fresh produce. The rent on my condo is modest, but the property tax bill keeps creeping up, so I keep an eye on the local budget meetings when I can.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one that really cared about protecting Medicare and expanding health‑care access—things that matter to me as a nurse and to my aging parents back in New York. I also liked the emphasis on clean energy, since the ski season in the Green Mountains has been hit hard by unpredictable snow years. Since then I’ve gotten more involved in local issues, especially the debate over a new wind farm that could bring jobs but also raise concerns about wildlife. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the best bet for keeping health‑care affordable and for pushing the state to stay ahead on climate, even if the party sometimes feels like it’s talking in buzzwords that don’t translate to everyday life. I’m not a fan of the Republican side; they keep pushing tax cuts that end up slashing funding for public schools and health programs, and their stance on climate feels like a denial of the reality I see on the slopes. That said, I do appreciate when a Republican candidate actually talks about small‑business relief, because my husband runs a tiny woodworking shop out of our garage and the tax burden is real.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the local property tax hikes that are squeezing my fixed income. I think the Democratic Party, despite its flaws, is more likely to address those issues, while the GOP seems more interested in rolling back regulations that protect workers and the environment.
