## Background  
I'm 40, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Wilmington, just a few miles from the I‑495 loop. I work as a shift supervisor at a regional distribution center; the job is mostly about keeping the floor moving, making sure pallets get loaded on time, and handling a crew of about twelve people. I didn't finish my associate's degree in logistics because the company offered me a promotion after a couple of years on the floor, and I figured on‑the‑job experience would pay off more.  

My wife works part‑time at a community college, teaching introductory English courses. We have two kids: a 10‑year‑old daughter who is into dance classes and a 7‑year‑old son who loves soccer. Mornings are a blur of getting the kids ready, dropping the son at the neighborhood park for a quick game, then heading to the warehouse for a 7 a.m. shift. I usually grab a coffee from the corner diner and skim the headlines on my phone while I wait for the trucks to load. After work, I pick the kids up, help with homework, and we try to squeeze in a family dinner before the TV turns on. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the big box store, a few hours of yard work, and the kids' extracurriculars. I drive an older midsize sedan that I keep in the garage because gas prices have been a pain lately.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking seriously about keeping the cost of prescription drugs down and protecting the small‑business tax credits that my wife relies on for her part‑time hours. I also liked the candidate’s promises to invest in broadband for the suburbs, which would make my kids’ online schoolwork smoother. I wasn’t thrilled with any of the options, but the GOP platform seemed focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations that I think matter for our coastal state.  

Now that the midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to the polls. I’m not a political junkie, but the recent bump in local property taxes and the rising price of diesel for the trucks at work have pushed me to pay attention. My top everyday concerns are the cost of living—especially housing and gas—and making sure there are decent jobs that pay a living wage in the area. I see the Democratic Party as the more pragmatic side right now, even if some of their national messaging feels a bit out of touch with middle‑class folks like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, come across as more interested in cutting programs that help families and in pushing a cultural agenda that doesn’t resonate with my day‑to‑day life. I’m not going to sit this one out; I think my vote can at least keep the conversation about affordable healthcare and infrastructure alive in the district.
