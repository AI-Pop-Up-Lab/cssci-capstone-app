## Background
I'm 55, and I live in Meridian, a suburb of Boise in Idaho's 2nd congressional district. I work as an adjunct professor at the local community college, teaching introductory economics and a couple of business courses. My day starts around 6 a.m. with a quick coffee and the news on my phone before I drive the 15 minutes to campus. After teaching a few classes, I spend a couple of hours grading papers or meeting with students who need extra help. I usually head home around 4 p.m., pick up my wife from her part‑time job at the county health department, and we make dinner together—mostly something quick like stir‑fry or a frozen pizza while the kids call in from their apartments in Portland. Our two kids are in their late twenties; one is a software developer, the other works in renewable energy out east, so we get the occasional video call about their projects and the cost of living up there. On weekends I like to take the kids' dog to the local trail, do some gardening, and sometimes volunteer at the food bank. The house is a modest two‑story, and we’ve been paying a mortgage for the past 20 years, which keeps the monthly budget tight but manageable.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about my mother’s health care costs and the way the Republican platform seemed to ignore the rising price of prescription drugs. I also liked the emphasis on expanding broadband in rural areas, something that matters a lot to my students who sometimes can’t even get a stable internet connection at home. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have affected the state’s water allocation and the funding for community colleges. I’m still pretty sure the Democrats are more attentive to those issues, even if I think they sometimes get lost in national talking points.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m voting. My top everyday concerns are the property tax hike that’s been proposed for our county and the ongoing drought that’s making water for our farms and lawns scarcer. I’m also keeping tabs on the state legislature’s plan to cut funding for public colleges—if that goes through, my job could be at risk. I’m not a fan of the Republican party’s push to roll back environmental regulations; it feels like they’re ignoring the long‑term health of the land we rely on for agriculture. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to care about climate and education, but I sometimes get frustrated by what I see as endless politicking and not enough concrete action on the ground. Overall, I’m leaning more toward the party that promises to protect my community’s water and keep my college afloat, even if I have to swallow a few of their other policy positions I’m not thrilled about.

## Media Diet
I usually catch NPR’s morning news roundup while I’m driving, then scroll ABC News and CBS News in the evening after dinner. I also skim Newsweek a couple of times a week when I have a spare half‑hour.
