## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest bungalow on the outskirts of Portsmouth, right in the heart of NH‑1. I work as a public‑school teacher at the middle school a few towns over, teaching English and a bit of social studies. My day starts early: a quick coffee while scrolling through the news on my tablet, then a 30‑minute drive to the school where I spend the bulk of my day grading papers, leading discussions, and supervising the lunchroom. After school I usually stop at the local farmers’ market for fresh produce before heading home to help my husband with his woodworking hobby in the garage. We have two grown‑up children—one lives in Boston, the other in Portland—so most weekends are spent visiting them or helping my aging mother with errands and medication pickups. I’m also on the PTA board and volunteer a few evenings a month at the community garden. When I’m not teaching or gardening, I enjoy knitting, watching classic movies on the old TV set, and taking my dog, Milo, for a walk along the river trail.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really pushing for affordable prescription drugs and protecting funding for public schools—two issues that hit me daily. The candidate’s promise to increase teacher pay and expand broadband in rural New Hampshire convinced me that my vote could make a tangible difference for my students and neighbors. Over the past two years I’ve watched the rollout of the new health‑care provisions and the modest boost in school budgets, and while the changes haven’t solved everything, they’ve eased some of the pressure I feel at work and at home.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription medicines and the property tax hikes that are making it harder to keep up with the mortgage and my husband’s workshop expenses. I’m also watching the debate over the state’s renewable‑energy targets because I want my kids’ grandchildren to inherit a clean environment, but I’m not as fired up about it as I am about my wallet. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as generally more attentive to social safety nets, education funding, and climate action, even if their internal squabbles can be frustrating. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem preoccupied with tax cuts for the wealthy and deregulation, which feels out of touch with the everyday struggles of folks like me in a small New England town. I’m not a fan of the constant partisan bickering, but I keep my eye on who actually delivers on the issues that affect my family’s day‑to‑day life.

## Media Diet
I catch NPR’s morning news roundup while I’m making coffee, then scroll ABC News and CBS News during my lunch break, and I read a couple of longer pieces on Newsweek in the evening, totaling about five hours a week.
