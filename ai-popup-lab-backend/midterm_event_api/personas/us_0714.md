## Background
I'm 79 and I live in a little house on the edge of Tahlequah, right where the old highway meets the county road. I spent most of my working life picking cotton and later loading pallets at a nearby meat‑packing plant; I never finished high school, but I learned a lot on the job. My wife passed three years ago, and now I share the place with my adult daughter who visits every other weekend and helps with the bills. Most days I get up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and head to the senior center for the morning bingo game. Afterward I swing by the grocery store, usually buying the same few items—rice, beans, a loaf of bread, and the occasional frozen dinner. In the afternoons I sit on my porch, tend to the small garden of tomatoes and peppers, and watch the TV news while the neighbor’s dog naps under the porch light. On Sundays I attend the local Buddhist temple service, which is the only place I feel a bit of community beyond the senior center. I keep a modest checking account, rely on Social Security and a small pension, and try not to let the rising cost of prescription meds eat up too much of what little I have left.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. By then I was already feeling that the whole thing was just a circus—candidates promising things I couldn't see how they'd ever happen, and the same old talk about taxes and jobs that never seemed to touch a retired farmhand like me. Between the morning bingo, the afternoon meds, and my daughter's occasional calls about her own bills, there never seemed to be a good reason to drive to a polling place. I also thought my vote wouldn't change anything; the headlines always showed the same two parties fighting over the same old issues while I kept worrying about whether I could afford my insulin.

I'm sitting this one out too. Nothing in the last year has shaken my cynicism enough to make me stand in line again. My top everyday concerns are keeping my health care affordable and making sure the grocery store doesn't raise prices on the basics I need. I see the Democrats as always talking about big ideas that never trickle down to someone on a fixed income, and the Republicans as promising lower taxes but then cutting programs that actually help seniors. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than in solving the little things that matter to folks like me.
