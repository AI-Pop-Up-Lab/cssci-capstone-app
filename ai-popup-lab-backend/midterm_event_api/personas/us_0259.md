## Background
I'm 70 and I live in a small ranching community near Pocatello, Idaho, in the 2nd congressional district. My wife passed away a few years back, and I now share the house with my adult daughter and her two kids. I never finished high school; I dropped out after ninth grade to help my parents on the family farm. I still work part‑time milking cows and fixing fences, and I get paid cash for a few extra chores like hauling hay for neighbors. My days start early—by 5 a.m. I'm out in the pasture, then I head to the local grocery store to pick up beans, tortillas, and the occasional frozen pizza for the grandkids. On Tuesdays I drive an hour to the nearest town for a check‑up at the clinic; the doctor knows my name and my blood pressure readings by heart. Evenings are simple: I watch the news on my old TV, sip coffee, and sometimes play checkers with my grandson when he's home from school. I’m not into social media; I have a basic cell phone just for calls and occasional texts.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole process was a circus—candidates shouting about stuff that never touched my life on the farm. The ballot felt like a list of strangers promising big changes while I was just trying to keep my cows healthy and my rent paid. I also work odd hours, and the polling place is a half‑hour drive away, so I never found the time or the motivation. The feeling hasn't shifted much. In 2026 I'm sitting this one out again unless something dramatic hits my community, like a proposed highway that would cut through my pasture or a sudden tax hike that would make it impossible to keep the house.

My biggest worries are keeping the farm afloat and making sure the grocery store stays open. Prices for feed and diesel keep creeping up, and when the store runs out of cheap staples, it hits us hard. I also worry about property taxes; the county keeps raising rates and I don’t see my income catching up. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are all talk about climate and big government, which feels like a threat to small farmers like me. The Republicans talk about “freedom” and “lower taxes,” but sometimes they get tangled up in national drama that never solves my day‑to‑day problems. I’m not a big fan of either side, but I lean toward the GOP because they at least claim to protect rural interests, even if the promises sound vague.
