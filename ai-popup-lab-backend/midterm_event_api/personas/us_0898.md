## Background
I'm 65, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Charlottesville, right in the 8th district. I work as a maintenance supervisor at the local community college; I never finished my associate's degree, but the hands‑on training I got on the job lets me keep the campus buildings running. My wife, June, retired from the county health department a few years ago, and we have two adult kids who live out of state—one in Atlanta, the other in Washington, D.C. I spend most mornings getting the building's HVAC system checked before the first class rolls in, then I grab a coffee at the diner across the street and read the headlines on my tablet while I wait for the lunch crowd. Evenings are usually spent fixing leaks or repainting a wing that the college just added, and on weekends I help June with her garden or drive my grandson to soccer practice. I also volunteer a few hours at the local church food pantry, mostly handing out boxes and chatting with folks about the price of groceries. Health-wise I'm managing high blood pressure, so I try to walk around the campus a bit each day and keep an eye on my meds.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between working overtime to cover a broken boiler, dealing with a bout of flu that kept me home for a week, and feeling like none of the candidates really represented my day‑to‑day worries, I just let the day pass. The whole process feels like a theater—lots of speeches, not enough action on things that matter to me, like affordable health care and stable property taxes. I also get frustrated when the media keeps shouting about national drama while my rent and grocery bills keep creeping up. That cynicism made me sit out, and unless something huge happens—like a major cut to prescription drug prices or a scandal that directly hits our community—I’m not planning to go to the polls this time either. So, I'm sitting this one out.

My top everyday concerns right now are keeping my blood pressure meds affordable and making sure my property tax bill doesn't jump again next year. The cost of fresh produce at the market and the price of gasoline are also on my mind, but they sit behind the health and housing issues. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least talk about expanding Medicaid and funding community programs, but I’m skeptical they’ll actually follow through. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to ignore the specific challenges Black seniors face in places like ours; they talk about tax cuts, but those rarely trickle down to people on fixed incomes. Overall, I'm annoyed with both sides and feel like I'm stuck waiting for someone to actually listen to the folks who aren’t voting.
