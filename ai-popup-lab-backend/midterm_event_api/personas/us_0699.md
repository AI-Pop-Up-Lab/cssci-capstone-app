## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Dayton, right in the 8th district. I never finished high school; I left after the 10th grade to take a job at the local auto plant when I was 16. I spent 35 years on the line, welding and later supervising a small crew. I retired three years ago when the plant closed and the union pension ran out, so now I collect Social Security and a modest pension from the union. My wife passed away five years ago, and I have two grown kids who live in Columbus and Cincinnati. They visit a few times a year, and I get to see my grandkids when they come to the senior center for the holiday parties.  

My days are pretty routine. I get up early, make coffee, and take a walk around the neighborhood while the sun is still low. After breakfast I usually sit on the porch and read the newspaper—mostly the printed edition I pick up at the corner store. Around noon I head to the senior center for bingo or a card game; the staff there serve a hot lunch and we chat about the weather, the price of gas, and whether the new pharmacy down the road will accept my Medicare plan. In the evenings I watch TV, mostly the local news and the three national outlets I listed, and I try to catch a game of basketball on the weekends. I’m not into gadgets; my phone is just for calls and a few text messages from my kids.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. By the time the primaries rolled around, I felt like the whole process was a circus—candidates promising things I never saw happen, ads that just made me more confused, and long lines at the polling place that meant missing my medication pick‑up. I was also dealing with a bout of arthritis flare‑up that left me bedridden for a week, so voting just slipped off my radar. The whole thing left me with a bitter taste; I figured my single vote wouldn't move the needle anyway.  

I'm sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms. Unless something hits close to home—like a drastic change to my prescription coverage or a local scandal that threatens the senior center—I don't see a reason to stand in line. My biggest worries every month are the cost of my insulin and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on my modest home. I try to keep an eye on what the parties are doing, but I mostly see both sides as talking past each other. The Democrats get a lot of press for “healthcare for all,” but I never see that promise translate into lower co‑pays for me. The Republicans brag about cutting taxes, yet my property tax still goes up and I hear about budget cuts that could hit senior services. It feels like the whole system is set up for folks with big donors, not for someone like me who just wants a stable pill bottle and a roof that doesn't bleed when it rains.
