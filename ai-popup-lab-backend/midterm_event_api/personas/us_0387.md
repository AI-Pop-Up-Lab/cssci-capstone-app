## Background
I'm 65, and I live in a quiet suburb of Frederick, right in Maryland's 8th district. After earning an associate degree in business administration at the community college, I spent most of my career as an office manager for a midsized manufacturing company that makes parts for the aerospace industry. I retired two years ago, but I still pop into the office once a week to help train the new hires and keep the paperwork in order. My husband, who used to be a high‑school math teacher, and I have been married for 38 years. Our two kids are grown, living in Virginia and Pennsylvania, and we see them on holidays. I also help my elderly mother, who moved in with us after a fall, with her medication and grocery runs.

My days start early with a cup of black coffee while the morning news scrolls on my tablet. I walk the dog, then either head to the plant for a half‑day or spend a few hours at the local senior center, where I run a computer‑basics class for other seniors. After lunch I usually run errands—picking up prescriptions, buying fresh produce at the farmer's market, or stopping by the post office. Evenings are for dinner with my husband, a bit of TV, and catching up on emails from my adult children. On weekends I tend to my garden, do some volunteer work at the community library, and meet friends for a quick lunch at the diner down the road.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really talking about protecting Medicare and making prescription drugs affordable. My husband and I have been paying more out of pocket for my mother's medication, and the candidate's promise to cap insulin prices hit home. I also liked the emphasis on infrastructure; the new bridge project in our district would cut my commute time dramatically.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of healthcare and the property tax hike the county approved last year. The tax increase means my fixed retirement income stretches thinner, and I worry about being able to afford the next round of medication costs. I also keep an eye on local job growth—our town's biggest employer has hinted at layoffs, and I want to see policies that keep those jobs here.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats still feel like the better bet for seniors and for keeping social safety nets intact, even if I sometimes think they focus too much on big‑city issues and ignore the struggles of small‑town folks like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to be drifting toward rhetoric that scares me—talk of cutting social programs and a general disdain for government regulation that protects consumers. I’m not a hardcore partisan; I just want whoever is in office to keep my healthcare affordable and my property taxes reasonable.

## Media Diet
