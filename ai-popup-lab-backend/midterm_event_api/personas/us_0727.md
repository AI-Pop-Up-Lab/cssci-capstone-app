## Background  
I'm 30 and work as a research analyst for a regional nonprofit that focuses on water conservation in Central Oregon. I live in Bend, a town that feels both like a ski‑town and a farming hub, with my partner of three years. We rent a two‑bedroom apartment near the River Trail, so my mornings usually start with a quick run along the Deschutes and a coffee from the local roaster before I hop on the bus to the office downtown. The job is mostly data‑heavy: I pull state water‑use reports, write grant proposals, and sometimes get pulled into community meetings about irrigation projects. After work I either cook dinner, watch a show on Netflix, or head out for a hike on the nearby Cascades. On weekends we’re either at the farmers market or doing some volunteer trail maintenance. I don’t have kids yet, but we’re thinking about it once we can afford a place of our own. My parents live a few hours north in Portland, so I visit them a few times a year, usually for holidays or when the snow melts enough for a good road trip.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the platform on climate action and public lands protection matched what I see in my day‑to‑day work. I also liked the promise of more funding for rural broadband, which would help folks in my district who still struggle with spotty internet. Since then I’ve become a bit more skeptical of big‑ticket promises; the incumbent hasn't moved the needle on water policy like I hoped, and I’ve seen some of the proposed infrastructure projects stall in committee. Still, I think the party’s stance on environmental regulation and healthcare is the closest to my own views, even if the execution sometimes feels half‑baked.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of rent in Bend and the continued scarcity of affordable water for both farms and households. I’m also watching the property tax debate, because the county is looking at raising rates to fund schools, and that could push more people out of the market. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like the only ones who care enough about climate and social safety nets, even if they’re messy. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in deregulating the very resources I’m trying to protect, and their rhetoric on immigration and social issues just feels out of touch with the community I live in.
