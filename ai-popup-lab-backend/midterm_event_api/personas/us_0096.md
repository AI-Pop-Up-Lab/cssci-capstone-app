## Background  
I'm 25 and work full‑time as an automotive technician at a garage in Rancho Cordova, right on the edge of Sacramento. I got my associate’s degree in automotive technology from a community college, so I’m comfortable with my hands‑on job but I still keep an eye on the latest diagnostic software updates. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with my girlfriend, Maya, who’s finishing a nursing program at a nearby college. We split rent, utilities, and grocery bills, and we take turns cooking dinner—usually something quick like tacos or a stir‑fry because we both work late on some days.  

My weekdays start at 5:30 am with a quick coffee, then I’m on the road to the shop by 7. The workday is noisy and greasy; I spend most of it under cars, swapping out brakes, diagnosing engine codes, and sometimes dealing with frustrated customers who think a simple fix will magically solve a bigger issue. After my shift ends around 4 pm, I either head straight home to help Maya with her studies or swing by the gym for an hour of weight‑lifting. Evenings are usually spent scrolling through my phone, watching a few YouTube videos about new car tech, or catching up on a Netflix series. On weekends I might help a friend move furniture, do a bit of freelance car detailing, or just hang out at a local bar with a few coworkers. I don’t travel much; most of my free time is spent in the Sacramento area, running errands, or meeting up with family in the suburbs.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking straight about the cost of living and the need for lower taxes. My paycheck barely stretches past rent and gas, and the Democratic proposals for higher taxes on small businesses seemed like they’d hit places like my garage hard. I also liked the tougher stance on immigration; I work with a lot of customers who come from other states, and I think the border needs more control.  

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what the parties are doing, but I’m still pretty skeptical of most politicians. The Democrats keep pushing for big‑ticket climate legislation that looks good on paper but feels like it will just raise utility bills for people like me. The Republicans, on the other hand, sometimes seem more interested in culture wars than in actually fixing the potholes on my street or making sure my shop can stay open without costly regulations.  

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing—especially fresh produce, which is already pricey here. I’m also watching the local job market; if the economy stalls, my shop could start cutting hours. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats for what I see as endless “talk‑shop” without real solutions for everyday workers. The Republicans get a pass on some issues, but I’m starting to wonder if they’re all‑in on the same old playbook that doesn’t address the rising cost of healthcare or the need for better infrastructure. I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I just want the side that actually makes a dent in my wallet and my community’s day‑to‑day problems.
