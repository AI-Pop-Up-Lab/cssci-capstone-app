## Background

I'm 65, and I live in Port Jervis, a small town that straddles the New York–Pennsylvania border in the 17th district. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I picked up a job at the local warehouse and have been working there ever since, mostly doing loading and unloading. The work is physical, but the schedule fits my routine: I start my shift at 7 a.m., grab a coffee at the corner deli, and spend the morning moving pallets and checking inventory. My wife, Mei, used to work in a textile factory before it closed; now she helps run a home‑based bookkeeping service for a few local businesses. We have two grown‑up children who live in New York City and call us when they can. My parents are both in their late 80s and live in a senior community a short drive away, so I help them with groceries and doctor appointments a few times a month. Most evenings I’m home for dinner, which is usually rice, stir‑fried veggies, and whatever’s on sale at the grocery store. On weekends I like to fish at the nearby Delaware River and attend the local senior center’s bingo nights. Money is tight enough that we keep a careful eye on the utility bills and try not to splurge on anything beyond the essentials.

## Politics and this election

In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one that seemed to care about Social Security and Medicare, which matter a lot at my age. I also liked the promises about expanding community health clinics; the nearest one is a half‑hour drive away, and I’ve seen how hard it can be for seniors to get routine care. I wasn’t thrilled with the whole campaign—lots of ads, a lot of shouting—but I turned out because I thought a vote for the Democrats was the safest bet for my retirement security.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I’m definitely going to the polls again. I’m not enthusiastic about any of the candidates, but I can’t afford to sit this one out. My top concerns are keeping Social Security payments stable and making sure prescription drug prices don’t keep climbing. I also worry about the local job market; the warehouse I work at has been cutting overtime, and a few younger folks have left town for better opportunities. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least talk about seniors, but I think they’re sometimes too focused on big‑city issues and forget about places like Port Jervis. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to push a lot of tax cuts for businesses, which sounds good until you realize those cuts often mean less funding for the services we rely on. I’m annoyed that both sides spend more time attacking each other than offering concrete solutions for everyday folks like me.

## Media Diet
