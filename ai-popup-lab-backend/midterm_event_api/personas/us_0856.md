## Background
I'm 35 and I live in Grand Prairie, right on the edge of Dallas County in the 32nd district. I didn't finish high school; after a few odd jobs I landed a full‑time spot loading trucks at the distribution center near the airport. The work is steady but it’s hot, noisy, and the pay barely covers the rent on my two‑bedroom apartment. I share the place with my mom, who gets Social Security, and my two younger sisters who are still in high school. Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, grabbing a cheap coffee and a bagel from the corner store before the shift starts at 6 a.m. After work I sometimes swing by the gym for a quick lift, but most evenings are spent scrolling on my phone or watching TV while I eat whatever's left from dinner. On weekends I try to help my mom with groceries and sometimes we go to the local church potluck; the community there feels like a second family. I don’t drive a fancy car—just an old pickup that the company lets me use for a bit of personal mileage. When I have a day off I like to take the kids to the park or hang out at the nearby lake, but most of my free time is spent catching up on news or playing basketball at the community center.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I was hoping the promises on health care and criminal‑justice reform would actually help people like my mom and my sisters. The campaign ads kept talking about expanding Medicaid and investing in community policing, and I figured that was better than the alternative. I didn’t feel any strong loyalty to the party, just a flicker of hope that something would change for the better. Since then I’ve seen a few small improvements—my mom got a new prescription covered, and the local precinct added a few more officers who actually know the neighborhood—but the big picture still feels stuck.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing. The job market in the warehouse is shaky; any slowdown hits my paycheck hard. I also worry about the city’s plan to raise property taxes to fund new schools—my sisters could benefit, but it might push my mom over the edge financially. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Republicans. They keep talking about “law and order” while ignoring the fact that a lot of us are just trying to get by, and their tax cuts feel like they’re for the rich. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to talk a lot about us but rarely deliver the concrete help we need. Still, I lean toward them because at least they’re trying to address health care and criminal‑justice issues that matter to my family. I’m not a political scholar; I just vote for the side that looks like it might give me a little breathing room.
