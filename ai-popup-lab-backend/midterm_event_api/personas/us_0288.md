## Background
I'm 74, and I live in a small bungalow on the east side of Fort Wayne, right near the old downtown factories that have been turning into loft apartments. I worked for twenty‑four years as a school bus driver for the county school district, picking up kids from the same neighborhoods I grew up in. I never finished a bachelor's degree, but I took a few night classes at Ivy Tech and earned enough credits for an associate’s degree before I retired three years ago. My husband passed away five years back, and my two grown‑up children live in Indianapolis and Chicago, so most days I'm on my own, but I have a close circle of friends from the church choir and the senior center where we play bingo on Tuesdays.

My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee and the local news on the radio while I water the garden—tomatoes, green beans, a few herbs that keep the kitchen smelling good. After that I usually take a short walk to the nearby grocery store; the line at the checkout is always a good excuse to chat about the price of milk. I spend a couple of hours in the afternoon either knitting blankets for the shelter or helping my neighbor with a leaky faucet. Evenings are for church services on Sundays, a community dinner on Thursday, and a little TV before bed. I keep a modest budget, mostly watching my Social Security check and trying not to let the rising utility bills eat too much of it.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about protecting Social Security and Medicare, which are my biggest worries at my age. The Republican ads kept talking about cutting taxes for big corporations, and that just didn't sit right with me when I'm trying to stretch a fixed income. I also liked the Democratic stance on expanding home‑based health care, something I need more of as my joints get worse.

I'm definitely going to the polls again for the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my Social Security check intact and making sure prescription drug prices don't keep climbing. I also worry about whether there will be enough local jobs for the younger folks in my community—if they leave, the town loses its life. I see the Democrats as the party that actually talks about seniors and health care, even if I think they sometimes overpromise on other things. The Republicans, in my view, are more interested in cutting government programs that help people like me, and they keep pushing for policies that make the cost of living go up without giving us a safety net. I don't follow every policy detail, but when I hear about a new tax cut that could affect my pension, I get annoyed.

## Media Diet
I listen to NPR while I have my coffee in the morning, then catch the evening wrap‑up on ABC News and some local clips from CBS News after dinner. I log about ten hours a week across those outlets.
