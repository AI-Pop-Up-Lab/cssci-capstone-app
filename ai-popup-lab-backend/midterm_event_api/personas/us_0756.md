## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of York, Pennsylvania, right in the 14th district. I never finished high school; I left after eighth grade to help my mom pay the bills, and later I picked up a job as a maintenance worker for the local community college. My day starts around 5 a.m. with a quick coffee, then a 20‑minute drive to the campus where I spend most of my day fixing broken lights, cleaning up after classes, and sometimes doing odd‑hour repairs on the gym equipment. I’m married to a woman who works part‑time at a grocery store, and we have two grown kids who live in different states. My mother, who’s in her late 80s, still lives with us, so I’m on call for her medication and occasional trips to the doctor. After work I usually pick up a bag of groceries, swing by the church for the evening service once a week, and then wind down watching the news before bed. On weekends I try to help my grandson with his homework when he visits, and I spend a few hours at the local community center playing pool with a few longtime friends.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at the college, caring for my mom, and feeling like none of the candidates really understood what folks like me need, I just didn’t see the point. The whole campaign felt like a show put on for the cameras, and I was convinced that my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The ballot felt like a “choose the lesser of two evils” scenario, and I was too exhausted to even research the platforms. That same feeling is still there, so I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out as well, unless something blows up locally—like a sudden threat to the community college’s funding or a scandal that hits my neighborhood hard. My biggest daily worries are the rising cost of groceries and the ever‑tightening grip of health‑care premiums; I’m also watching my mortgage payments creep up as property taxes climb. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats talking a lot about “racial justice” but rarely delivering on the jobs and affordable health care that matter to me. The Republicans promise lower taxes and less government, but I hear them ignore the reality of Black workers who still face discrimination and limited upward mobility. So I’m left feeling stuck between promises that don’t match my lived experience.

## Media Diet
