## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest brick house on a quiet street in New Bedford, right in the heart of Massachusetts' 4th district. I spent most of my working life as a front‑desk clerk at a community health clinic, a job I held until I retired at 68 after a few short stints as a part‑time cashier at the local grocery store. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in Boston and Providence, so I get regular phone calls and a few video chats each week. My granddaughter visits every summer and brings the newest iPad, which I still can't figure out.

My days start early. I brew a cup of coffee, read the local paper for a few minutes, then flip on the TV for the morning news while I prepare breakfast. After that I take a short walk around the neighborhood—my knees aren't what they used to be, but the fresh air helps. Most afternoons I volunteer at the senior center, helping with bingo and knitting groups. I shop for groceries on Tuesdays and Thursdays, usually at the big supermarket a few blocks away, and I make sure I have enough of my blood‑pressure meds before the pharmacy closes. Evenings are quiet: I watch a bit of TV, listen to an NPR podcast, and then read a few articles online before heading to bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate promised to protect Medicare and keep prescription‑drug prices down, which matters a lot when you're on a fixed income. I also liked the emphasis on funding for senior services and the pledge to invest in community health clinics—places like the one I used to work at. The Republican campaign seemed focused on cutting taxes for corporations and rolling back environmental regulations, and I wasn't convinced they'd look out for folks my age.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are keeping Medicare stable and affordable, and making sure property taxes don't skyrocket on my modest home. I also worry about the rising cost of groceries and the availability of reliable public transportation for seniors. In my view, the Democratic Party, despite its flaws, is at least trying to address the needs of older Americans and protect the social safety net. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in tax cuts for the wealthy and less attentive to the health‑care challenges we face. I don't follow every policy detail, but when I think about who will fight for my prescriptions and my pension, the Democrats feel like the safer bet.

## Media Diet
I tune in to ABC News with my morning coffee, listen to NPR podcasts in the evening, and skim a few Newsweek articles online whenever I have a spare half‑hour. All together that's about six hours a week.
