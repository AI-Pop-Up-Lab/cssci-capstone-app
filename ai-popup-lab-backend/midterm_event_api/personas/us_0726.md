## Background  
I'm 55 and work full‑time as a dental hygienist at a clinic in Albany, Oregon, which sits in the 3rd congressional district. My husband and I bought a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of town about ten years ago; it's close enough to the downtown farmer’s market that I can walk there for fresh produce on my lunch break. We have two grown‑up kids—one lives in Portland with a tech job, the other is back home finishing a master's in education. My mother, who lives in a nearby assisted‑living facility, calls me every evening, and I help coordinate her medication deliveries. Most weekdays I start work at 7 a.m., finish around 3 p.m., then either run errands or swing by the community garden where I tend a few tomato rows. On Wednesdays I volunteer at the local library’s adult‑literacy program for a couple of hours after work. Weekends are a mix of grocery trips, a bit of DIY around the house, and catching a movie at the small independent theater downtown. I drive a reliable used sedan that I keep in the garage with a few tools I picked up over the years.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Green Party candidate for president. I was fed up with the two‑party sloganeering and felt that neither major candidate really cared about climate action or the rising cost of health care that hits families like mine. I liked the Green platform’s emphasis on renewable energy and universal health coverage, even if I knew the chances of winning were slim. The decision felt like a protest vote, a way to signal I’m not happy with the status quo.  

Come 2026, I’m definitely going to the polls for the midterms. The Senate race has a candidate who’s pushing a realistic plan to lower prescription drug prices, and that hits my wallet hard—my husband’s insulin and my own cholesterol meds have been a big line‑item for years. I’m also watching the House race because the incumbent has been vague on protecting funding for community health clinics, which my clinic relies on for some of its outreach programs. My top everyday concerns right now are the steady rise in health‑care premiums and the property tax hikes the county has been proposing to fund road repairs.  

As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the lesser of two evils when it comes to social safety nets, but they still talk a lot of climate without delivering the big infrastructure changes I want. The Republicans, in my view, are more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back regulations, which feels like a slap in the face to people trying to make ends meet. I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I just want whoever’s in office to actually do something about the bills that hit my family’s budget.
