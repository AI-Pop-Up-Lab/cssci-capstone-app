## Background
I'm 70, and I live in the Homewood area of Jefferson County, right in the swing of Alabama's 5th district. I spent 35 years teaching high school math at a public school in Birmingham before I finally took my pension and retired last year. My wife passed away a few years back, so most evenings I'm either watching the news or helping my daughter and her kids with groceries. I have two grown kids—one lives in Atlanta, the other works as a nurse in Montgomery—and they visit a few times a year. My routine is simple: I get up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of coffee, and take a short walk around the neighborhood with my old Labrador. On Tuesdays and Fridays I go to the local church service, where I sit in the front row and help with the choir. After that I usually read the paper or scroll through my phone while waiting for my grandkids to finish their homework. Evenings are for TV, and I try to catch a game or a news program before heading to bed around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party's message on keeping taxes low and protecting traditional values resonated with my life after retirement. I was tired of hearing about big government handouts and thought a smaller government would let me keep more of my pension. The candidate also talked about law‑and‑order issues that mattered to me after a few break‑ins in the neighborhood last year. Since then, I've kept an eye on how the policies are playing out. I'm still convinced that a stable economy and low taxes are the best way for a retiree like me to stretch a fixed income, so I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the bump in property taxes that the county council is proposing to fund new road projects. When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who understand how hard it is to live on a fixed income, even if they sometimes sound too aggressive on cultural issues. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising help but end up raising taxes or pushing policies that feel out of touch with folks who have worked their whole lives. I don't feel any strong loyalty; I just want whoever can keep my bills low and my community safe.
