## Background
I'm 65 and I live in Ankeny, a suburb just north of Des Moines in Iowa's 3rd district. I work part‑time at the local grocery store, stocking shelves and helping customers with their bags. The shifts are mostly mornings, which lets me be home for lunch and the afternoon. My husband retired from the union‑run factory a few years ago, so his days are filled with gardening and watching the news on the couch. We have two grown kids—one lives nearby and helps with the housework, the other is in Minnesota with a family of his own. Most Sundays we go to our church, where I sing in the choir and help organize the food pantry. After church I usually stop by the community center to check on the senior‑citizen bingo night that I help run. I drive a 2009 sedan that I keep on a tight budget; the car is my lifeline for getting to work, the grocery store, and the occasional trip to Des Moines for medical appointments. My day usually starts with a cup of coffee, a quick glance at the headlines on my phone, and then heading out before the sun is high. Evenings are quiet—sometimes I watch a bit of TV, sometimes I read a paperback, and I always try to call my daughter before bedtime.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really looking out for seniors like me. Medicare was a big deal; the candidate promised to protect and even expand prescription‑drug coverage, and that meant a lot when my blood pressure meds keep creeping up in price. I also liked the emphasis on protecting Social Security, something I’ve paid into for over four decades. The campaign knocked on my door with flyers about community health clinics, and that personal touch made me feel seen.

Since then, I've kept an eye on how things are playing out. The economy feels shaky—gas prices have gone up, and the cost of groceries is a constant worry. The Democrats in Congress have pushed a few measures that helped keep my medication affordable, but there’s still a lot of talk about cutting taxes for the wealthy, which feels like a slap in the face when my grocery bill is already high. I’m not a political junkie, but I do notice when a candidate talks about fixing the local road that leads to the senior center or supporting funding for the community college where my grandkids take classes.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that are making it harder to keep the house we’ve lived in for 30 years. I’m also watching the local job market—some of the younger folks in town are leaving because there aren’t enough decent wages around. The Democratic Party still feels like the one that cares about health care and seniors, even if I think they could do better on the economy. The Republican side, on the other hand, seems more focused on cutting taxes for the rich and loosening regulations, which I worry will mean fewer safety nets for people like me. I’m not thrilled with either side, but I’m leaning toward the Democrats because they’ve at least tried to protect the programs I rely on.
