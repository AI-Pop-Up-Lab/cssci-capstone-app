## Background
I'm 25 and work as a marketing coordinator for a mid‑size tech startup in the Morristown area of New Jersey's 5th district. I graduated two years ago with a bachelor's in communications, so most of my day is spent juggling social‑media calendars, drafting email campaigns, and pulling analytics reports. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who does freelance graphic design; we split rent and utilities, and we both keep a tight budget because the cost of living here is high for entry‑level wages.

My weekdays start early: I catch the NPR morning news while brewing coffee, then hop on the train to a downtown office about 30 minutes away. After a nine‑hour workday, I usually swing by the gym for a quick cardio session, then head home to cook a simple dinner—often pasta or a stir‑fry—while scrolling through the latest ABC News headlines on my phone. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the local market, catching up on a Netflix series, and meeting up with a few friends at a nearby bar for drinks. I still pay off student loans, so every extra dollar matters, and I keep an eye on the housing market in case I can eventually buy a place of my own.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health care and stronger climate action—issues that matter to me as a young adult with student debt and a growing concern for the planet. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding broadband access in rural parts of the district, which could help my cousin who lives upstate. Since then, my enthusiasm for politics has stayed pretty steady; I still think the Democrats are more in tune with my generation’s priorities, even if I’m frustrated by the constant bickering in Congress.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are rent and grocery prices, which have been creeping up faster than my paycheck, and the availability of good-paying entry‑level jobs in the area. I’m also watching how the state handles property taxes, because they affect my roommate’s and my own future plans. I tend to view the Republican Party as out of touch with the economic realities of younger folks; their focus on tax cuts for the wealthy feels like it leaves the rest of us scrambling. The Democrats, while not perfect, at least seem to acknowledge the cost‑of‑living squeeze and are pushing for policies like student‑loan relief and renewable‑energy investments that could create new jobs. I’m not a political scholar, but I’m pretty sure the next few years will be shaped by how well each side addresses these everyday pressures.
