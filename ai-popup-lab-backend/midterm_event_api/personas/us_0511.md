## Background
I'm 35 and work as a construction laborer on a mixed‑use project just outside Missoula. I grew up in a small town near Kalispell, and after high school I went straight into the trades instead of college. My wife and I have two kids, a seven‑year‑old boy and a five‑year‑old girl, so most mornings start with getting them ready for school while I grab a coffee from the diner on Main Street. I leave the house around 6 am, drive the two‑hour round‑trip to the job site, and spend the day hauling materials, mixing concrete, and patching drywall. When the shift ends around 4 pm, I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and head back home to help with homework and get the kids into bed. On weekends I do a bit of side‑gigs—helping a neighbor with landscaping or loading a trailer for a friend who needs to move a boat. My family lives in a modest single‑family house we bought a few years ago; the mortgage is the biggest monthly bill, and we try to keep a tight budget because the cost of living here has been creeping up faster than my wages.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between long work hours, caring for the kids, and feeling that the candidates were all the same—talking about “change” but never delivering—I just didn't see the point. The ballot felt like a chore, and I was too exhausted after a 12‑hour day to even think about it. I also thought my vote wouldn't matter in a district that leans heavily one way; I felt alienated from the whole process. That cynicism hasn't really gone away, so I'm planning to sit this 2026 midterm out as well unless something truly blows up locally—like a major factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits us directly. My biggest everyday concerns are the rising price of groceries and the cost of gas; both hit the family budget hard. I also worry about the local job market—if construction projects dry up, I won't have a paycheck. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as out of touch with rural life, always talking about big‑city issues, while Republicans seem to promise low taxes and less regulation but rarely follow through on the ground. Both sides feel like they're playing a game that leaves folks like me on the sidelines.
