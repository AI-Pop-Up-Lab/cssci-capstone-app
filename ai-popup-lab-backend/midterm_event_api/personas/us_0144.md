## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Seaford, a small town in the southern part of Delaware. I never finished high school; after my teen years I started working at the local grocery store, sweeping aisles and stocking shelves, and I've been doing part‑time shifts there ever since I retired from a full‑time cleaning gig at a nearby nursing home. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two kids moved out to Pennsylvania and Maryland when they were in their twenties. I see them a few times a year, mostly for holidays, and I help watch my three grandkids when they come to stay for a weekend. My days start early—coffee at 6 a.m., a quick walk around the neighborhood to check on the mailboxes, then a shift at the store from 8 to noon. Afternoons are for errands, like picking up prescriptions, and evenings I usually sit on the porch watching the news or playing bingo at the community center. Sundays are for church; I’m a regular at the small Methodist congregation down the road, where I also volunteer to bake cookies for the potluck.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same old things, and I was too tired after work and caring for my health to make a trip to the polls. The whole process just seemed like a hassle—long lines, confusing ballots, and a feeling that my single vote wouldn't change anything. I also got fed up with the endless ads that all sounded alike, so I just stayed home and watched the news on the channels I already trust. That same sense of disengagement is still with me in 2026. I'm *sitting this one out* unless something huge happens locally, like a sudden clinic closure or a scandal that directly hits my community. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription drugs and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on my modest home. I think both parties are missing the mark: the Democrats keep talking about climate and social justice, but they forget about seniors who need affordable meds, while the Republicans brag about tax cuts but never address the fact that my fixed income is being squeezed by rising living costs. I’m not a die‑hard supporter of either side; I just feel a lot of them are out of touch with folks like me.
