## Background
I'm 19 and I live in Bemidji, a small town up near the Canadian border in Minnesota's 8th district. I’m a sophomore at a community college, taking courses toward an associate’s degree in computer information systems, and I work part‑time at the local big‑box retailer to help cover my rent and tuition. My mom moved here from Detroit a few years ago, and we share a two‑bedroom apartment that’s close to campus, which makes the commute easy but the rent a constant worry.

My days usually start with a quick coffee from the corner shop, then a two‑hour shift at the store where I’m on the floor stocking shelves and handling the register. After work I head to class, which is mostly online now, so I can study in the tiny kitchen while the kettle whistles. Evenings are a mix of homework, scrolling through TikTok, and sometimes meeting up with a couple of friends at the community center for a game night. On weekends I either go hunting with my uncle or help my mom with her hair‑braiding business, which brings a few extra dollars. I don’t drive a car; I rely on the campus shuttle and occasional rides from coworkers.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between school, my job, and trying to keep up with rent, I felt like there wasn’t a single candidate who really spoke to my life. The whole thing just seemed like a big show—politicians promising change but never delivering anything that mattered to a 19‑year‑old juggling student loans and a part‑time gig. I also got fed up with the endless ads and the feeling that my vote would get lost in the noise. So I stayed home, and I’m leaning toward sitting this one out too unless something huge happens locally, like a major landlord scandal or a sudden cut to student aid that hits me directly.

My biggest everyday concerns are keeping my apartment affordable and making sure I can afford groceries without having to skip meals. The rising cost of food and the pressure of student debt are on my mind every time I check my bank app. I’m also worried about getting a decent job after I finish my degree; the local job market feels thin, and most good tech positions are in the Twin Cities, far from home.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats talk a lot about race and social justice, which feels good in theory, but they haven’t done much to help people like me pay for school or find stable work out here. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing tax cuts that sound nice but just end up widening the gap between rich folks and everyone else, and they act like climate change isn’t a thing, which worries me because I’ve seen crazy weather swings up north. So right now I’m just waiting to see who, if anyone, actually shows up with a plan that touches my day‑to‑day life.
