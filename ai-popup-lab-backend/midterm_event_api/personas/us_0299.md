## Background  
I'm 64 and I live in Columbus, Indiana, right in the middle of the 9th district. I spent most of my adult life working at the local auto‑parts plant; I started on the line after finishing my bachelor's in business administration and worked my way up to shift supervisor. I retired last spring, but I still do a few consulting gigs for a nearby small manufacturer, mostly checking inventory systems and training new foremen. My wife, Carol, is a part‑time librarian, and our two kids live out of state—one in Chicago, the other in Austin. We get a weekend visit from our granddaughter every few months, and she always brings a box of crayons and a stack of puzzles.

My day usually starts with a cup of black coffee while the house is still quiet. I flip on the TV for a quick news recap, then head out to the community garden where I tend a few tomato and pepper plants. After that I either run a few errands—grocery store, pharmacy, post office—or I sit at the VFW hall to play cards with a few old buddies. In the evenings I like to watch a game or a documentary, and on Tuesdays I’m at the local senior center for a woodworking class. On Sundays I’m at church, then we have a family dinner with my son’s family when they’re in town. I drive a 2015 Chevy Silverado that I keep in decent shape; I’m not into fancy gadgets, but I do use my phone to check email and the news sites I trust.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me—people who have paid into Social Security all their lives and now want to keep as much of their paycheck as possible. The GOP’s talk about cutting taxes, protecting the border, and backing the manufacturing jobs that kept my town alive really resonated. I didn’t trust the Democrats to understand the cost of living in a place where a factory can be the whole economy of a county.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. The biggest things on my mind are the price of my prescription meds and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on my house. I also worry about the plant down the road—if they start cutting shifts again, that’s going to hit a lot of families. I still think the Republican Party is the better choice for keeping taxes low and supporting business, even if I’m not thrilled with every single candidate they put forward. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free college and big government programs that just end up raising my taxes and don’t solve the real problems on the ground.
