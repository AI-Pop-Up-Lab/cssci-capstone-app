## Background

I'm 80, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Derry, right where the old dairy farms used to be. I never finished high school; after a few years working odd jobs at the local lumber yard, I spent most of my adult life as a maintenance hand for the town's public schools. The work was hard, but it paid the bills and let me stay close to the community. My wife passed away five years ago, and my two grown kids live in Boston and Portland, so most days I'm on my own with my old Labrador, Max. I get up around 6 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and read the paper—well, the online version of the sites I trust—while the house is still quiet. After breakfast I tend to the garden in the backyard; a few tomatoes, some herbs, and a little patch of lettuce keep me busy and give me fresh veggies for lunch. In the afternoons I usually head to the senior center for a game of cards or a bingo session, and on Wednesdays I volunteer a couple of hours helping the custodial staff keep the community hall clean. Evenings are my time to sit in the living room, watch the news, and maybe call my daughter to check in. Money is tight on a fixed pension, so I keep a careful eye on every expense.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who have worked hard all their lives and now just want to keep the cost of living down. The promises about lower taxes, protecting the borders, and keeping the country safe resonated with me. I also liked the emphasis on personal responsibility and the idea that the government shouldn’t be telling me how to run my own house. I didn’t feel the Democratic side was speaking to my concerns; they seemed more focused on big‑city issues and lofty climate goals that feel far removed from my day‑to‑day reality.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to the polls. I’m voting because I don’t trust that my voice will be heard otherwise, and I still think the Republican candidates have the best shot at keeping taxes low and preserving the kind of community I know. My biggest worries right now are the rising price of prescription drugs and the ever‑increasing property taxes that make it harder to stay in my home. I also worry about the reliability of the power grid in New England—winter storms have knocked out power before, and I can’t afford a generator. When I think about the parties, the Republicans feel like the ones who understand that a pension should stretch, that a small business owner should be able to keep his shop open, and that the country’s borders matter. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to want more government programs that end up costing me more in taxes, and they keep talking about climate policies that sound like they’ll raise my heating bills. I’m not saying either side is perfect, but right now the Republican platform aligns better with what I need to get through each day.
