## Background  
I'm 44 and work as a dental hygienist at the community health clinic in Grants, New Mexico, which sits in the 3rd Congressional District. I grew up on the nearby Pueblo and still spend a lot of time with my grandparents, who live on the reservation. My husband runs a small landscaping business that takes most of his time on road jobs outside town. We have two kids, ages 10 and 13, who go to the public middle school a few miles away. My day starts before sunrise: I get the kids ready, make coffee, and drive the 20‑minute stretch to the clinic where I clean teeth and do patient education. After my shift ends around 3 p.m., I pick the kids up, help with homework, and sometimes swing by the tribal community center to volunteer for the senior meals program. Evenings are usually a mix of dinner, a quick check of my phone for news, and trying to get a little sleep before it all starts again. On weekends I help my family with gardening, attend church services, and occasionally drive my husband to a job site that’s a couple of hours away.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was more attentive to tribal sovereignty, health care funding for our clinic, and the need for infrastructure on the reservation. The Republican opponent seemed to downplay the water rights issues that have been a constant headache for our community, and I didn’t trust his promises about "cutting red tape" when we already struggle with bureaucratic delays. I also appreciated the Democratic outreach at the community center after the last flood; they showed up with supplies and a few volunteers, which left a decent impression.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. I’m not a political junkie, but the recent push to roll back some of the federal water protection rules feels like a direct threat to the wells we rely on. Plus, there’s a local ballot measure about expanding broadband on the reservation, and I want that to pass because my kids need reliable internet for school. My biggest everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that the clinic is short‑staffed, which means longer wait times for dental care. I’m also watching property taxes creep up as the town tries to fund a new park—good idea, but the numbers are squeezing our budget.

I view the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to listen to tribal issues, even if they sometimes get it wrong. The Republicans, in my experience, talk about "personal responsibility" and "less government" while ignoring how those policies can hit the most vulnerable, especially on reservations. I don’t feel completely comfortable with either side, but I’m leaning toward the party that seems willing to fund the health clinic and protect our water.
