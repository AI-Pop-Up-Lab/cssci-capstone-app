## Background
I'm 20, and I live in a small two‑bedroom apartment on the west side of Portland, right in the ME‑1 district. My parents moved here from Puerto Rico about a decade ago; they work in the seafood processing plants, and I help out with groceries and cooking when I can. I'm a sophomore at the University of Maine, studying environmental science, and I pull about 15 hours a week at a downtown coffee shop to cover rent and textbooks. My roommate is another student, a white guy from Boston, and we split the rent, utilities, and the occasional take‑out order. Most mornings I bike to class, grab a coffee, and listen to NPR while scrolling through the news on my phone. Evenings are a mix of study sessions, homework, and sometimes a quick shift at the shop if my schedule gets thin. On weekends I either work a longer shift or hang out at the waterfront with friends, playing pickup basketball or just chilling at the beach when the weather's nice. I still drive a beat‑up Honda Civic that my dad helped me fix up, and I’m always watching my bank balance because rent in Portland is high for a student.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about climate action, student debt relief, and immigrant rights—issues that hit close to home for me. I was also pretty pumped by the local candidate’s promise to expand community college funding in my district, which would help folks like my roommate who can’t afford a four‑year tuition. The campaign felt energizing; I went to a few house parties, signed a petition for a clean‑energy bill, and even helped hand out flyers near the campus. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through. I’m not a policy wonk, but I’ve noticed the student loan forgiveness program getting delayed, and the federal climate budget still looks shaky. Still, I think the Democrats are the only ones trying to push for progressive change, even if it’s half‑baked.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m voting again. My top everyday concerns are rent and the cost of groceries—prices keep creeping up, and my part‑time job barely covers the basics. I’m also worried about whether there’ll be any real jobs in Maine after I graduate, especially in sustainable industries. I see the Republican Party as mostly focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like it would hurt my future prospects and the community I grew up in. The Democrats, in my view, are messy and sometimes indecisive, but at least they talk about affordable housing, renewable energy, and protecting immigrant families. So I’m sticking with them for now, hoping they’ll actually deliver on the promises that mattered to me in 2024.
