## Background

I'm 65 and I live in Scottsbluff, right on the edge of the Nebraska Sandhills. I work as a maintenance supervisor for a regional grain elevator; the job's mostly fixing conveyors, checking the diesel generators, and making sure the trucks can get in and out when the harvest rolls around. I didn't finish my associate's degree in automotive technology, but the hands‑on stuff stuck with me, so I stayed in the trade instead of going back to school.

My wife, Mary, retired from the local school district a few years ago, and we both help out with the tribal community center a couple of evenings a week. Our two kids are grown and live in Omaha and Lincoln, and we get a handful of grandkids every summer when the school lets out. Most mornings I’m up by 5 a.m., pour a cup of coffee, and read the news on my tablet while the furnace hums. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the hardware store to grab a new belt for my truck. Weekends are a mix of fishing on the Niobrara River, church service on Sunday, and helping Mary with the garden. We own the house we bought in the early 90s, and the mortgage's paid off, but property taxes have been creeping up.

## Politics and this election

I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to health‑care costs and to the tribal issues that affect my community. My dad grew up on the Omaha reservation, and I've seen how limited access to clinics and broadband can hold people back. The Democrat promised to protect Medicaid expansion and to push for more federal funding for tribal schools, and that clicked with me. I also liked the candidate’s stance on protecting the Sandhills from large‑scale fertilizer runoff, which hits the farms around here.

Since then I've kept an eye on how the administration's policies are playing out. The Republican governor in our state keeps talking about cutting taxes for big agribusiness, but I keep hearing from my coworkers that the cuts don’t trickle down to the hourly folks. The Democrats still seem more willing to talk about expanding rural broadband, which would help the community center run its online tutoring program. So for the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls – I'm voting. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and whether the local grain elevator will stay open when the big corporations keep buying up smaller operations. I think the Republicans are mostly looking out for the big farms and oil interests, while the Democrats, at least in theory, care more about people like me and the tribal members who still face systemic neglect. I'm not thrilled with either side, but I feel the Democrats are a little closer to the issues that hit my doorstep.

## Media Diet

I start my day with NPR’s morning news while I eat breakfast, then in the evenings I scroll through ABC News and read a couple of articles on Newsweek on my phone. All together it adds up to about seven hours a week.
