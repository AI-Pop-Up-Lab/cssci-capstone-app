## Background
I'm 65, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Saginaw, right in the middle of Michigan's 11th district. I spent most of my adult life on the assembly line at the local GM plant, and I finally retired a few years ago when the plant downsized. These days I make a little extra cash doing odd‑handyman jobs—fixing leaky faucets, patching roofs for neighbors, that sort of thing. My wife, Maria, and I have been married for 42 years; we both grew up in the area, so our families are nearby. Our two kids moved to Detroit and then to Florida, but they call every summer, and we get a handful of grandkids who come to stay for a weekend. My day usually starts with a strong coffee on the porch while I scan the news on my phone, then I head out to a couple of jobs before lunch. In the afternoon I might run errands at the grocery store, pick up a prescription, or tend to the garden. Sundays are for church, a quick potluck with the parish, and a long walk around the lake when the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me—people who rely on a steady paycheck, low taxes, and a government that doesn't tell us how to run our own lives. The economy felt shaky after the pandemic, and the Democratic platform seemed to push more regulations on the auto industry, which hit my old coworkers hard. I also appreciated the tough talk on immigration and the emphasis on law and order; it resonated with what I see in my community every day.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs—my blood pressure meds have gone up a lot—and property taxes, which are getting harder to keep up with on a fixed retirement income. I keep an eye on the local job market, too; if the factories keep shrinking, there aren't many other options for people my age. I still think the Republican Party is the better choice for the economy and for keeping government out of our pockets, while I see Democrats as more interested in expanding bureaucracy and raising taxes. I don't feel like either side has all the answers, but the GOP feels closer to what I need right now.
