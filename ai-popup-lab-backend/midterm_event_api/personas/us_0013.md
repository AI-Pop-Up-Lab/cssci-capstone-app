## Background  
I'm 20, and I live in Bethel, a small village up in the Yukon‑Kuskokwim Delta. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I started doing whatever work I could find. Right now I’m a seasonal laborer at the local fish processing plant, pulling crates of salmon early in the morning and helping load trucks in the afternoon. My mom works part‑time at the community health clinic, and my 13‑year‑old brother is in middle school, so the house is always busy. We share a two‑bedroom house that my grandparents built, and the rent is a big part of what we scrape together from my wages and the little cash mom brings home.

Most days I wake up before sunrise, grab a cup of instant coffee, and ride the snow‑covered road to the plant. After my shift I usually swing by the local grocery store to pick up some frozen meals and the occasional treat for my brother. Evenings are spent on the old tablet my aunt gave me, scrolling through social media or watching short clips on YouTube while the wind howls outside. On weekends I sometimes help my mom with errands, like picking up prescriptions or driving my brother to the school bus stop. There’s not a lot of spare time, but I try to catch a game on the radio when I can.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long hours at the plant, the cost of getting to the nearest polling place in Kotzebue, and the feeling that none of the candidates cared about what life is like in a remote village, I just didn’t bother. The whole process feels like a distant spectacle—politicians talk about oil pipelines and federal aid, but I still have to wait weeks for fresh produce and worry about my mom’s health coverage. I’m pretty cynical about the whole system; it seems like the same old promises that never reach us out here.

I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something serious hits our community—like a sudden change to the fishing quotas or a health crisis that forces us to rely on state assistance—I don’t see a reason to make the trip again. My biggest worries are the rising price of groceries and the spotty internet that makes it hard to stay connected or look for better work. I’m also annoyed with how both parties treat native issues: the Republicans push for more drilling without respecting our land, and the Democrats keep promising funding that never shows up. It all feels like a game where the rules are written for people who live in the lower 48, not for us up here.
