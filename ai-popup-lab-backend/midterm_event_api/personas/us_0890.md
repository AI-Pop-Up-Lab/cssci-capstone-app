## Background  
I'm 30 and work as a dental hygienist at a practice in downtown Charlottesville. I got my associate degree at a community college in Albemarle County, so I know the grind of juggling school loans and shift work. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment with my boyfriend and our 2‑year‑old daughter, Maya. Mornings start at 5:30 a.m. with a quick shower, a coffee from the corner deli, and a 15‑minute drive to the clinic. My shifts run from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., then I pick Maya up from the preschool across the street and head home to make dinner.  

When I'm not working, I try to squeeze in a little gym time on Tuesdays and Fridays, and I’m on the board of a neighborhood clean‑up crew that meets once a month. Sundays are our church day; we go to a Baptist congregation a few miles out of town, and after service we usually have a potluck with the kids’ families. I keep a tight budget because rent has been creeping up, and I’m always watching the grocery receipts to see if we can stretch the money a bit further. My parents live in Richmond and call me every other weekend; they help out with babysitting when I have to pick up an extra shift.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about affordable health care and funding for community colleges—things that hit home for me as a dental hygienist and a mother. The candidate’s promise to expand Medicaid in Virginia meant my patients could get the preventive care they need, and the pledge to protect tuition assistance helped me think about sending Maya to a good college someday. I also liked the emphasis on criminal‑justice reform; after a friend was caught up in a low‑level drug charge, I was tired of seeing people like that get stuck in the system.  

Since then, I’ve stayed pretty engaged. I’ve attended a few town‑hall meetings about the new statewide broadband plan, and I’ve signed a petition to keep the minimum wage at $15 an hour. The issues that keep me up at night are the rising cost of rent and the price of groceries—especially fresh produce, which is essential for my family’s health. I’m also worried about the local job market; a couple of dental offices closed last year, and I hear coworkers talking about layoffs.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely voting. The Senate race feels like a real test of whether the state will keep expanding health‑care options, and the House race could affect funding for community colleges, which matters for my future plans. I’m not a fan of the Republican Party; most of their messaging feels like it’s aimed at people who don’t have to worry about rent or kids’ school lunches. I think they’re more interested in tax cuts for big corporations and less in protecting social services. The Democrats aren’t perfect—there’s a lot of internal fighting and some policies that feel out of touch with working‑class folks—but they’re the side that’s at least trying to address the problems that affect my day‑to‑day life.

## Media Diet  
I listen to NPR’s morning news while getting ready for work, and I catch the evening roundup on ABC News after dinner. It adds up to about two hours a week.
