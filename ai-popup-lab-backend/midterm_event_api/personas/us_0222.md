## Background

I'm 84 and I live in Creston, a tiny farming town in Iowa's 4th congressional district. I grew up working in the fields with my parents after we moved here from Texas when I was ten. I never finished high school; after a few years of picking corn I married my husband, and we raised three kids in the same house where I still live. My husband passed away three years ago, and now I share the place with my daughter, who moved back to help take care of me, and my grandson, who comes by on weekends to help with chores and to keep me company.

My days start early. I turn on the TV around 7 a.m. and let the morning news run while I make coffee and get ready for the day. I take a short walk around the neighborhood, greeting the same few folks who wave from their porches. After that I head to the local farmer's market where I work part‑time stacking produce and handing out samples. The pay is modest, but it keeps me moving and lets me stay in touch with the community. Around noon I come home for a simple lunch—usually a sandwich and a bowl of soup—then I sit in my kitchen and read the newspaper or watch a bit more TV. In the afternoons I might help my daughter with some paperwork or knit a blanket for my grandson. Evenings are for church on Wednesdays and a quiet dinner with my family. I don't travel far; most of my world is right here in Creston and the few miles of county road that connect us to the nearest grocery store.

## Politics and this election

I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared about my biggest worries: keeping Medicare and Social Security strong, and making prescription drugs cheaper. The Republican ads kept talking about cutting taxes and deregulating business, but those things never touched my life the way rising health‑care bills did. I also liked the way the Democratic candidate talked about protecting the environment, which matters to us farmers who depend on good soil and clean water.

I'm definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. I'm not a big political junkie, but when it comes to my health and my grandson's future, I feel I have to make my voice heard. My top everyday concerns are the cost of my prescription meds and the price of groceries—everything feels more expensive than it used to be, and my fixed income doesn't stretch as far. I also worry about property taxes; the county has been raising rates, and I see more folks struggling to keep their homes.

My view of the parties is pretty simple. The Democrats seem to remember that there are people like me who rely on government programs to get by, and they at least talk about fixing the health‑care system. The Republicans, in my experience, keep pushing for tax cuts for big companies and talk about "personal responsibility" while ignoring the fact that many seniors are living on a fixed paycheck. I don't think either side is perfect, but the Democrats feel a little more like they're looking out for folks who need a safety net.

## Media Diet

I usually watch ABC News and CBS News in the mornings while I have my coffee, and I click over to NBC's website a couple of evenings to catch up on any stories I missed. All told, I spend about four hours a week scrolling through their articles or watching their clips.
