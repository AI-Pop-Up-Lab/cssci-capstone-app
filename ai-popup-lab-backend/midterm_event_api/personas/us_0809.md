## Background
I'm 20, and I live in a modest apartment on the edge of Spearfish, just a short bus ride away from Rapid City where I take a few community‑college classes in environmental studies. My parents own a small ranch a couple of hours north, so I usually fly home for holidays, but most weeks I'm on my own. I work part‑time at the local grocery store, stocking shelves in the morning and doing the checkout lane in the evenings. Between shifts I squeeze in two or three hours of lectures, labs, and study groups, usually in the campus library where the Wi‑Fi is decent and the coffee is cheap.

My roommate, Maya, is also a student, and we share chores and rent. We cook simple meals—often a big pot of chili or whatever's on sale at the store—and we binge watch a mix of sitcoms and true‑crime podcasts when we have a night off. I ride my bike to the bus stop, listen to a NPR morning news brief while I pedal, and try to squeeze a quick workout at the campus gym before heading back to work. On weekends I might drive out to the Black Hills for a hike, or help my parents with a fence repair, depending on the weather.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the platform addressed the issues that matter to me right now—student debt relief, climate action, and protecting reproductive rights. I was also motivated by the fact that the candidate visited my college campus and talked about expanding community‑college funding, which hit close to home. That night I stayed up late watching a livestream debate, and even though I was skeptical about some of the promises, I thought the alternative—seeing a Republican win—would mean even less attention to tuition costs and climate policies that affect my future.

Since then my feelings have gotten a bit muddier. I still think the Democrats are the better of the two, especially on climate and education, but I’m frustrated by the constant infighting and the way some of the party’s big donors seem to push corporate interests. I also hear a lot of talk about “the new progressive wave” that feels more like a buzzword than a concrete plan. The Republican side, in my view, keeps pushing policies that would make it harder for people like me to afford a roof or keep my job stable—like cutting funding for public colleges and rolling back environmental regulations.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and seeing the grocery prices stop climbing so fast. I’m also watching how the state legislature handles the upcoming water rights bill, because that will affect both my parents’ ranch and the local tourism industry. I tend to see the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to care about my generation, even if it’s messy, while the Republicans seem more interested in preserving the status quo that benefits older, wealthier voters.
