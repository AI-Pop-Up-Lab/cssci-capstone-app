## Background
I'm 55, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Lansing, just a few miles from the state Capitol. I work as a shift supervisor at a regional auto‑parts distribution center that ships batteries and other components to factories around the state. The job is mostly standing on a concrete floor, checking inventory on a tablet, and making sure the forklift drivers stick to the schedule. I didn't finish my associate's degree in mechanical engineering because my parents needed help with a family restaurant back when I was in my early twenties, so I went straight into the warehouse and learned on the job.

I’m married to my wife of 30 years; we both grew up in the Detroit area and moved to Lansing after she got a nursing job at a community hospital. We have two grown kids—one in college out west and the other working as a software developer in Detroit. On weekdays I get up around 5:30 a.m., make a quick coffee, and catch the local NPR news feed while the kids are still asleep. I drive the 20‑minute commute to the plant, listen to a mix of ABC News podcasts and a newsweek newsletter on the way. My lunch break is usually a sandwich in the break room while scrolling through the headlines on my phone. After work I pick up groceries at the nearby market, sometimes stop by the gym for a short walk on the treadmill, and then head home to cook dinner with my wife. Weekends are a mix of yard work, visiting my parents in the suburbs, and catching a baseball game at the local minor‑league stadium when the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party's focus on infrastructure and the push for electric‑vehicle incentives mattered for Michigan's auto industry, which employs a lot of people I know. I also appreciated the effort to expand Medicaid in the state; my wife sees a lot of patients who rely on that coverage. I wasn’t thrilled with the whole campaign circus, but I thought the Democratic ticket was the better bet for protecting jobs and keeping health care affordable.

I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax hikes that are making it harder to keep up with our mortgage. Health‑care premiums have crept up, too, and I’m watching the Senate races to see who will actually push for a real prescription‑drug price cap. I’m not a big fan of the Republican side; they keep talking about cutting taxes but most of that seems to help the wealthy and big corporations, and they don’t seem to understand how much a $4‑a‑gallon gas price hurts a family that drives to work every day. The Democrats, on the other hand, feel a bit scattered—some of them push for big climate bills that feel out of touch with the auto plants that keep us afloat, while others get tangled in internal fights. Still, I think they’re at least trying to address the things that affect my daily life, so I’m sticking with them.

## Media Diet
I spend about four hours a week on the news—usually catching NPR in the mornings, then checking ABC and CBS updates during lunch, and reading a Newsweek roundup in the evening.
