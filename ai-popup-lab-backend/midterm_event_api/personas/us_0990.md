## Background

I work part‑time as a cashier at the local grocery store; the shift is mostly mornings, which lets me be home when my husband, who retired from the railroad a few years back, needs help with his medication. We have two grown‑up kids—one in Utah, the other in Colorado—so the house is pretty quiet most days. My mom still lives a couple of hours away in Laramie, and I drive up to help her with yard work and errands when I can. My day usually starts with a cup of black coffee, the radio playing classic rock, then a 20‑minute drive to the store. After work I pick up a bag of groceries for the house, check the mailbox for the monthly utility bill, and sometimes meet a neighbor for a quick chat on the porch. ## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same old things, and I was busy juggling work, my husband’s health, and trying to keep the house running. The ballot felt like a reminder of how out of touch the whole process is with folks like me who are just trying to get by. I’m not a fan of the political circus; both parties seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the real problems we face out here—like the rising cost of gas, the dwindling number of jobs that pay a decent wage, and the ever‑increasing property taxes on a fixed income. So, I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out too, unless something dramatic hits the local community—like a major road closure that cuts off my commute or a sudden health scare that forces me to look for better support.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with the grocery bills and making sure the heating system doesn’t break down when the winter hits. I see Democrats as a distant, coastal crowd who don’t get the realities of rural Wyoming, and Republicans as a bunch of loudmouths promising tax cuts but offering no real solutions for aging infrastructure or health‑care access. Both sides feel like they’re playing a game, and I’m just the pawn who has to live with the outcome.
