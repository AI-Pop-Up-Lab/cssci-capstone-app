## Background
I'm 84, and I live in East Providence, right near the water where the old factories used to be. I spent most of my career as a professor of African‑American history at the University of Rhode Island, retiring about ten years ago. My wife passed away five years back, so I’m a widower now, but I still get regular visits from my two adult children—one lives in Boston, the other in New York—and a handful of grandkids who come by for the holidays. I own a modest two‑bedroom condo that I bought when I was younger; the mortgage is paid off, but the property tax bill keeps creeping up, which I keep an eye on.

My days are pretty routine. I wake up around 7 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and read the paper—mostly the local section online while I scroll through the NPR morning podcast. After that I take my old Labrador, Buddy, for a short walk around the neighborhood; the walk is as much about getting fresh air as it is about checking on the neighbors. I volunteer twice a week at the community center, helping with a senior‑tech class where I teach folks how to use tablets. In the afternoons I’m often at the library, either digging through archives for a personal research project or just browsing the new releases. Evenings are quiet: I cook a simple dinner, watch a bit of the news, and then settle into a chair with a crossword puzzle before bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I’ve always felt the party is the best defender of civil‑rights progress and of programs that keep people like me from falling through the cracks—think Medicare, Social Security, and affordable prescription drugs. The candidates that year spoke about protecting those safety nets, and that mattered more to me than the usual partisan bickering. I also liked that the Democratic platform mentioned expanding broadband access for seniors, something I’ve seen make a real difference in my own life.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription meds and the way local property taxes keep climbing, squeezing my fixed income. I’m also watching the conversation about long‑term care funding; my sister’s recent move into an assisted‑living facility made me realize how fragile that safety net can be. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are generally on the right side of the issues that affect me, though I sometimes feel they get too caught up in identity politics and forget the everyday struggles of older folks. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that actually protect seniors, which feels like a slap in the face for people who have paid their dues for decades.

## Media Diet
I catch NPR’s morning news feed while I sip coffee, then tune into ABC News and CBS News in the evenings; that adds up to about eight hours a week spread across those three outlets.
