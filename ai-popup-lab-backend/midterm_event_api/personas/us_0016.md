## Background

I'm 80, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Fairbanks, not far from the river that used to flood every spring. I spent most of my working life as a lineman for the local utility, putting up power poles and fixing outages in the harsh winter. I never finished a degree, but I took a few night classes in electrical engineering before the job took over. My wife passed five years ago, and I get regular visits from my daughter and two grandkids who live down in Anchorage. Most days I start with a strong cup of coffee, check the weather on my old tablet, and then head out to the community center where I do a bit of woodworking and play cards with a few other seniors. I still drive my pickup to the grocery store on Thursdays, and on the occasional clear day I go out to the riverbank to fish for salmon. The nearest doctor’s office is a half‑hour drive away, so I keep a list of my meds and a spare set of glasses in the glove compartment. Evenings are usually spent watching the news on my TV, then reading a few articles before turning in around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party understood what it’s like to live in a place where the cost of heating can eat up a big chunk of a paycheck, and because they’ve been the ones pushing for more oil and gas development that keeps jobs here. I also liked the stance on fewer federal regulations that seemed to make it easier for small contractors like me to get work. Over the last two years I’ve watched the price of diesel rise and the health clinic I rely on get tighter budgets, which has made me a bit more nervous about the future. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out because the Senate race could affect the Alaska North Slope projects that keep a lot of us employed. My biggest everyday worries are keeping the house warm without blowing my budget and making sure my granddaughter can get a decent college scholarship without drowning in debt. I think the Republicans are the only ones who get the energy part right, while the Democrats keep talking about climate goals that sound good on paper but feel like they’d shut down the very jobs that keep my community alive. I’m not a political scholar, I just want the rules to stay simple and the power lines to stay on.
