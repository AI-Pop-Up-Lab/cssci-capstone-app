## Background
I'm 65, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Amherst, right in the heart of New Hampshire's 1st district. I spent most of my working life at the paper mill in nearby Manchester, doing the sort of hands‑on stuff that never required a college degree—operating the press, then later moving into a shift‑supervisor role. I retired five years ago when the plant downsized, and now I pick up odd jobs fixing things for neighbors, mow lawns, and help at the local senior center where I run a weekly chess club. My wife passed away three years back, so it's just me and my old Labrador, Scout, who follows me everywhere. My daughter lives in Boston and visits a few times a year; my son is out in Portland, Maine, with his own family. Mornings start with a strong cup of coffee and the local paper, then a walk around the neighborhood to check on the snow melt and the garden beds I tend when the weather lets up. Afternoons are usually spent on a repair job or a visit to the library to catch up on the news, and evenings I watch the TV news or listen to a radio program while I read. I drive a reliable old sedan, keep the house heated with a wood stove, and try to stay active with the community center's fitness class twice a week.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I didn't feel the two big parties represented my concerns, so I cast a write‑in for a local independent who promised to protect senior benefits and keep property taxes low. I was fed up with the endless bickering and felt that a third voice might shake things up, even if it was a long shot. That decision was more about protest than belief that the candidate would win. Since then, the political climate hasn't changed much in my view, but the rising cost of prescription drugs and the looming increase in property taxes have made me pay closer attention. I'm planning to vote in the 2026 midterms because the state legislature is debating a bill that could raise the senior tax exemption—something that would hit my pocket directly. My top everyday worries are keeping my heating bills manageable through the winter and affording the medicines I need for my arthritis. I think the Democrats talk a lot about social programs but often forget the practical side of how those programs are funded, while the Republicans seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy than protecting seniors like me. Both parties feel out of touch, and I wish there were more candidates who actually listen to the everyday struggles of folks who aren't chasing the next big headline.
