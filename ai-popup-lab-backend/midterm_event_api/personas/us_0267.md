## Background
I'm 39, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the west side of Rockford, right in the heart of Illinois’s 17th district. I work full‑time as a home health aide, helping elderly folks with bathing, medication reminders, and grocery runs. The job is physically demanding, but it lets me bring home a steady paycheck that covers rent, my kids’ school supplies, and my mom’s occasional medical bills. I’m a single mother of two—Jaden, 12, and Maya, 8—so mornings start early: I’m up by 5 a.m., making coffee, packing lunches, and getting the kids ready for school before I catch the 6:30 bus to the nursing home where I’m assigned. After a twelve‑hour shift, I’m usually home by 8 p.m., helping with homework, doing dishes, and trying to squeeze in a quick episode of a sitcom before I finally crash.

On weekends I try to make time for the community center’s basketball league, which is the only place I get a break from the grind. My mom lives a few blocks away, and we trade meals when I can. I don’t have a college degree; I left high school at 16 to start working and help support the family. Still, I take pride in the certifications I earned on the job—CPR, basic wound care—because they let me do more for my clients and earn a little extra.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the party’s platform on Medicaid expansion and minimum‑wage hikes felt like the only thing that could keep my family afloat. I was also swayed by the local candidate’s promises to fund after‑school programs in Rockford, which mattered for Jaden and Maya. Since then, I’ve seen a few of those promises turn into actual grant money for the community center, but I also hear a lot of talk about “tax cuts” that never seem to reach people like me. Still, I’m convinced that the GOP’s focus on cutting social programs would hit my kids’ health coverage hard, so I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms.

My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the stability of my job. When the nursing home cuts overtime or raises its fees, everything else spirals. I’m also watching rent—my landlord gave me a notice that the lease will go up next year, and I can’t afford another jump. As for the parties, I view Democrats as the only ones who remember there are people on the streets needing help, even if they sometimes get tangled up in internal fights. Republicans, to me, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and ignoring the daily grind of folks who work multiple jobs just to stay afloat.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR’s morning news feed while getting the kids ready, and I scroll ABC News and CBS News on my phone in the evenings after dinner, adding up to about five hours a week.
