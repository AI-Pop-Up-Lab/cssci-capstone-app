## Background
I'm 49 and I work as a maintenance supervisor at a community college in Santa Maria, right in the middle of CA‑3. I grew up on a reservation in northern California, but after high school I moved to the Central Coast to be closer to my wife’s family. I never finished a degree, but I took a few courses in electrical work and facilities management, which got me the job I have now. We own a modest two‑bedroom house with a small yard, and my wife runs a home‑based catering business that does a lot of catering for local schools and events. We have two kids: a 16‑year‑old daughter who’s into skateboarding and a 13‑year‑old son who spends most of his free time playing video games.

My day starts around 5:30 am with a quick coffee, then a 30‑minute drive to the campus where I spend the morning checking HVAC systems, fixing broken lockers, and making sure the labs are safe for the morning classes. Around noon I grab a taco from the food truck outside the science building and head back to the office to fill out work orders. After work I pick up the kids from school, help my son with his homework, and sometimes we all watch a baseball game on TV. Dinner is usually something my wife cooks, and we eat around 7 pm while the kids finish up their online assignments. On weekends I help out at the local tribal community center, doing occasional repairs and teaching a basic computer class for seniors.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Democratic because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable health care and protecting our water supplies, which matter a lot in a place that’s been hit hard by drought. I also liked the candidate’s promises to invest in community colleges, which directly affects my job and the students I work with. I didn’t feel the Republican platform addressed the climate issues that threaten our region, and I was skeptical about their tax cuts for the wealthy.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns are the rising grocery prices and the ever‑increasing property taxes on our home—both are squeezing the family budget. I also worry about the local job market; a lot of the manufacturing plants in the area have been cutting back, and I hear rumors about the college facing budget cuts. I see the Democrats as the party that at least tries to keep social programs funded and cares about environmental regulation, even if they’re sometimes too caught up in internal fights. The Republicans, in my view, keep talking about “small government” but that usually means cutting services that people like my kids and my wife rely on. I’m not a political scholar, I just look at which side seems more likely to keep the water flowing and the grocery store stocked.
