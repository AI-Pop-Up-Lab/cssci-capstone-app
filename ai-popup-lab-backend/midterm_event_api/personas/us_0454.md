## Background
I'm 60, and I live in St. Cloud, right near the Mississippi River. I work as a maintenance supervisor at the regional medical center; I got my associate’s degree in industrial mechanics from a community college and have been on the job for almost three decades. My wife and I own the house we bought in the late‑1990s, and our two kids have moved out—one’s in college out west, the other works as a software tech in Minneapolis. On weekdays I’m up by 5 a.m., grab a coffee, and head to the hospital for the 7 a.m. shift. After work I usually stop by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, then help my wife with a bit of yard work before we settle in to watch the news or read the paper. Sundays are my quiet day; I attend a small Methodist service, then drive my dad to his doctor’s appointment before we both head home for a family lunch. Most evenings I’m on the porch, tinkering with the old pickup or fixing a leaky faucet, and I try to get to bed before 10 p.m. so I’m rested for the next day.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was finally taking health‑care affordability seriously—something that matters a lot to me and my wife as we get older and need more prescriptions. I also liked the promise of more funding for rural broadband, which would help folks in the outskirts of St. Cloud stay connected. The campaign felt a bit noisy, but the little things about Medicare‑for‑All and infrastructure upgrades clicked with my everyday concerns.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes the county has been discussing. I’m also keeping an eye on gas prices because I still drive a pickup for work and the commute can be long. I think the Democrats are better at protecting seniors and funding public services, but I get frustrated when the party seems divided on trade or when they ignore the struggles of small‑town workers. The Republicans, in my view, keep pushing tax cuts that mostly benefit the wealthy and big corporations, and they talk a lot about “cutting red tape” without offering real solutions for the jobs that keep our community afloat. I’m not a big fan of the constant culture‑war shouting; I just want policies that keep my hospital running, my mortgage manageable, and my kids’ families secure.
