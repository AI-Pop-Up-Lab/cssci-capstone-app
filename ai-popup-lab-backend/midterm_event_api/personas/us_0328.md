## Background
I'm 30 and work the night shift as a forklift operator at the distribution center just outside Ashland, Kentucky, in the 2nd congressional district. I grew up in a bilingual household; my parents came here from Mexico in the early 2000s and ran a small taquería that closed when I was a teen. I finished high school, helped out at the restaurant, and never went to college because I needed to start earning right away. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment with my boyfriend, who works as a mechanic at a nearby garage. We split rent and utilities, which usually comes to about $800 a month. Most evenings I’m home around 7 am after a 10‑hour shift, and I spend a quick hour scrolling through my phone before I try to catch a few hours of sleep.

My days are pretty routine: clock in, unload pallets, move boxes, take a short break to eat the sandwich I bring from home, and then head back out. On weekends I sometimes help my mom with paperwork for the community center where she volunteers, and we’ll go to the local market for fresh produce. I watch a lot of TV in the mornings when I’m getting ready for work—mostly the news and a cooking show that’s in Spanish. I’m not into politics as a hobby, but I do keep an eye on the headlines because they affect my paycheck and the price at the pump.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican. The main thing that pushed me that November was the promise of lower taxes and fewer regulations that would let the distribution center keep its hours and maybe even raise wages. I also felt the Democratic message on immigration didn’t reflect my family’s experience; I think the country should have tighter borders but still treat people who are already here fairly. The campaign ads that talked about “protecting jobs” and “keeping America safe” resonated with me because I’m worried about my job being outsourced or the plant closing.

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. The Senate race and the House seat could change the balance of power, and I want to make sure the GOP stays strong in our district. My top everyday concerns are the cost of gasoline and the stability of my job. Gas prices have been all over the place, and when they spike, my paycheck doesn’t stretch as far. The other big thing is health insurance—my boyfriend’s plan is tied to his job, and any changes at the federal level could affect premiums or coverage.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as out of touch with people who work blue‑collar jobs. They keep talking about big‑government solutions that end up costing more in taxes. Republicans, on the other hand, seem to understand that we need a decent wage, cheap fuel, and a clear immigration policy. I don’t follow every policy detail, but I trust the party that promises to keep factories open and not raise my taxes.
