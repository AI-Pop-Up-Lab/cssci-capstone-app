## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Windsor, a small suburb on the edge of the Front Range. I work full‑time as a facilities manager for the local community college; the job came after I earned a bachelor's in business administration and spent a decade in retail management. My wife and I have been married for 35 years; our two kids are both out of state—one is a teacher in Arizona, the other is finishing a master's in Seattle—so the house is pretty quiet most evenings. I help my mother, who lives about an hour away in Fort Collins, with a few errands each week because her arthritis makes it hard for her to get around.

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee and the local radio, then a 20‑minute drive to the campus where I spend the bulk of my day checking HVAC systems, coordinating repairs, and making sure the winter snow doesn’t shut down any classrooms. After work I usually hit the gym or take a short hike on the nearby trails before heading home for dinner. On weekends I like to tend to the vegetable garden in the backyard, grill burgers for the neighbors, and catch a baseball game on the TV if the Rockies are playing. I’m not a big tech person, but I do own a tablet that I use to read the news and check my bank statements.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long shifts, caring for my mom, and the feeling that the candidates were all saying the same stuff, I just never got around to filling out a ballot. I’ve always thought the system is set up to keep people like me—middle‑aged, white, working‑class—busy enough that we don’t have the time or energy to make a difference. The campaign ads felt like noise, and the endless promises about “change” never seemed to line up with the reality of rising grocery prices and my property tax bill creeping higher each year. That cynicism has stuck; unless something huge blows up in my district—like a major factory closing or a scandal that hits my community directly—I’m planning to sit this one out, too. I’m not saying I’m happy about it, just that I don’t see a clear reason to make the effort right now.

My top everyday concerns are keeping the grocery bill from eating up the rest of my paycheck and dealing with the ever‑increasing property taxes that make it harder to save for retirement. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re pushing for more taxes and regulations that could hurt small businesses, while the Republicans seem obsessed with cultural battles that don’t affect my day‑to‑day life. Both sides feel distant, and I end up feeling like the only thing they agree on is that they’re both out of touch with folks who just want a stable job, a decent roof, and affordable food.
