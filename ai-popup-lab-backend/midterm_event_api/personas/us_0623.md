## Background
I'm 40, and I live in Los Lunas, a small town just outside Albuquerque in the 3rd congressional district. I work as a shift supervisor at a big-box home improvement store. My day starts around 5 a.m. because I have to get the kids ready for school—Javier is 15 and loves basketball, and little Sofia is 8 and always asks for a snack before the bus. After dropping them off, I head to the store, where I spend eight hours on the floor making sure the aisles are stocked, handling customer complaints, and training new hires. My husband, Carlos, works as a driver for a regional trucking company, so most evenings we both end up cooking dinner together while the kids finish homework. On weekends we try to squeeze in a trip to the local farmer’s market or a quick hike in the Sandia foothills, but most Saturdays are spent cleaning the house and catching up on the laundry. Money’s tight; the rent on our two‑bedroom apartment has gone up a lot in the past couple of years, and we’re still paying off a car loan. I watch a lot of Spanish-language TV with the family, but when it comes to English news I stick to the outlets I trust.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one paying any real attention to immigration reform and health care, which matter to my family. The Republican platform seemed focused on cutting programs that help low‑income families, and I was worried about my kids’ future if those safety nets disappeared. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in infrastructure that could bring more jobs to our area. Since then I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening locally—especially the school funding battles and the push to raise the minimum wage. I’m definitely going to the polls again for the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out because the Senate race could affect Medicaid expansion in New Mexico, and the House race will decide who gets the final say on the federal farm aid that supports many of the small farms around here.

My top everyday concerns are rent and health‑care costs. Every month I’m juggling the rising price of our apartment with the need to keep the kids’ doctor visits covered. I’m also annoyed by how the Republican Party talks about “personal responsibility” while ignoring the fact that a lot of us are just trying to make ends meet. The Democrats get my vote, but I’m frustrated when they don’t follow through on promises about affordable prescription drugs. The GOP feels like they’re trying to push policies that would make my husband’s job harder and cut funding for community programs we rely on. Overall, I’m still leaning Democratic but I’m watching to see if they actually deliver on the things that matter to working families like mine.

## Media Diet
I usually listen to NPR on my commute in the morning and scroll through ABC News headlines while I’m waiting for the kids at school. In the evenings after dinner I read a few articles on Newsweek, which together adds up to about six hours a week.
