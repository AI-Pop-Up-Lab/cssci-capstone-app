## Background
I'm 65 and I live in Scottsbluff, right on the edge of the Sandhills. I work part‑time as a customer‑service supervisor at the local hardware store; I quit full‑time retail a few years back after my husband passed, but I still like being around people and handling the cash register. My two kids live in Omaha and Lincoln, so most of the time it's just me and my 78‑year‑old mother, who still cooks for us every night. On Saturdays I drive a short way to the community center to help run the Spanish‑language bingo night, and on Sundays I’m usually at the small Catholic church a few blocks away. My day starts with a strong coffee, a quick scan of the local grocery flyer, and a walk to the post office before heading to the store. After work I pick up a few groceries, check the mail, and sometimes video‑chat with my daughter while she helps me troubleshoot the new tablet I got for the senior center. The evenings are mostly quiet—watching a bit of TV, reading the newspaper, and making sure the house is warm enough for the coming winter.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between caring for my mom, the long lines at the polling place, and feeling like the candidates were all talking past each other, I just gave up. The whole process felt like a waste of time, especially when I couldn't find a clear reason why my vote would change anything for folks in a mostly agricultural district. I also felt alienated because the campaign ads never mentioned Hispanic families like mine, and the local issues that mattered to me—health‑care costs for my mom and rising grocery prices—were glossed over. So, I'm sitting this one out too. I'm not planning to vote in the 2026 midterms unless something dramatic hits our community, like a hospital closure or a major flood that forces us to deal with the state government directly.

My biggest worries right now are the cost of prescription meds and the price of food at the grocery store. The next biggest thing is the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on our modest home. As for the parties, I see Democrats as all talk about immigration and climate, but they don’t seem to get the day‑to‑day grind of a senior on a fixed income in a rural town. Republicans keep shouting about tax cuts and “big government” but rarely address health‑care affordability or the fact that many of us rely on Social Security. Both sides feel distant, and I’m left wondering who’s actually listening.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I make coffee, catch the evening wrap‑up on CBS News after work, and scroll through ABC News headlines during my lunch break. It adds up to about four hours a week.
