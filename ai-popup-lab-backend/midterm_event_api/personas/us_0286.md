## Background
I'm 50, and I live in a modest rental house on the edge of Lafayette, right in Indiana's 7th district. My husband works the night shift at the nearby plastics plant, and I spend most of my days as a warehouse picker for a regional distribution center. I never finished high school; I dropped out after sophomore year to help my parents with the family laundromat, and the money we made kept us afloat. We have two kids—Jenna, 17, and Malik, 15—who both go to the local public high school. Mornings are a scramble: I pull Jenna out of soccer practice, get Malik to his basketball game, and make sure everyone's lunch is packed before I head to the warehouse at 7 a.m. My shift ends around 3 p.m., which gives me a few hours to run errands, help the kids with homework, and catch a quick meal before my husband comes home. On weekends we try to get out to the farmers market or visit family in Chicago, but most of the time we're just trying to stretch the paycheck enough to cover rent, utilities, and the kids' extracurricular fees. I don't have much time for hobbies; when I do, it's usually scrolling through my phone while waiting for a bus or watching a bit of TV after the kids are asleep.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, it felt like a waste of time—none of the candidates seemed to care about people like me who are just trying to keep a roof over our heads and put food on the table. The ads were all negative, the debates were a blur of talking points, and I was juggling two kids' schedules, a night-shift partner, and a job that barely leaves me any energy. I also felt that the system ignored the concerns of low‑income families, especially in immigrant communities like ours, so I just stayed home. Going into the 2026 midterms, I'm leaning toward sitting this one out again unless something really shakes me up, like a local school funding crisis that hits my kids directly or a sudden spike in utility rates that makes paying the bill impossible.

My top everyday worries are grocery prices and health insurance. Every trip to the store feels like a gamble, and my husband's health coverage is a constant source of stress because the plant's plan has been getting more expensive. As for the parties, I see both of them as talking past each other. The Democrats keep pushing identity politics and climate stuff that feels far from my kitchen table, while the Republicans brag about tax cuts but never address the rising cost of living that hits families like mine. I get annoyed when they both forget that ordinary people have to work two jobs just to get by.
