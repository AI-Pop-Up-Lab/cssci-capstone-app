## Background
I'm 50, and I live in a modest house on the north side of Eau Claire, Wisconsin, in the 3rd congressional district. I work as an HVAC installer and service tech for a regional contractor; the job got me the associate’s degree in mechanical systems from a community college a decade ago, and I've been on the road ever since. My wife, who works part‑time at a local grocery store, and I raise two kids—Jamal, 16, who plays basketball at school, and Maya, 13, who is into drawing. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., grab a cup of coffee, and head out to the first job site. The day is a mix of installing new units in new builds and fixing old ones in older homes—lots of crawling in tight attics and dealing with tight deadlines during the summer heat. Lunch is usually a quick sandwich at a truck stop while I scroll through my phone. After work I’m home around 6 p.m., help the kids with homework, and try to get a bite of dinner before the house gets noisy. Evenings are the only time I relax, usually by watching the news on my tablet or catching a game on the TV. Weekends are a blend of family chores, a little yard work, and sometimes a church service on Sunday morning.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates seemed to understand what it’s like to keep a roof over a family’s head while juggling rising utility bills. Between long shifts, kids’ school events, and trying to stretch each paycheck, voting just didn’t make the cut. I also got the sense that the political parties were talking past each other—Democrats kept promising more social programs but never seemed to address the shortage of skilled trades jobs in our area, and Republicans were all about cutting taxes but never mentioned how that would affect property taxes on a middle‑class homeowner like me. That cynicism left me pretty much on the sidelines. I’m not planning to change that for the 2026 midterms; I'm sitting this one out unless something really shakes up the local scene—like a factory closing or a major school funding crisis that hits my kids directly.

My top everyday concerns right now are the price of groceries and the rising property taxes on our house. Every time the grocery bill climbs, it feels like we’re cutting into the little extra we try to save for college or a car. And the county’s property tax hikes keep nibbling at our budget, especially with the HVAC work I do being more expensive when fuel costs go up. As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats. They talk a lot about racial equity, but when it comes to creating decent-paying jobs for people like me, the follow‑through is weak. The Republicans? They seem to think cutting taxes for the wealthy will magically trickle down, but they ignore the fact that many of us are already paying a big share of those taxes through property levies. Both sides feel out of touch, and I’m not holding my breath that either will start listening any time soon.

## Media Diet
I usually start my day with NPR’s morning roundup while I’m on the road, then catch the evening headlines on ABC News and skim a couple of stories on CBS News after the kids go to bed. I put in about four hours a week total.
