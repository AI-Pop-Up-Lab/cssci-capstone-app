## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Williston, North Dakota, right where the oil fields stretch toward the horizon. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I picked up odd jobs—first at a local grocery, then as a pipefitter’s assistant on the rigs. For the past twenty‑plus years I’ve been a roughneck, hauling water trucks and doing maintenance on the pumps that keep the wells running. The work’s hard, the hours are long, and the pay is decent enough to keep the house roof over my head and the fridge stocked.

My wife, June, works part‑time at the senior center, and we have an adult daughter who moved to Fargo for a college degree and now lives there with her own kids. We see the grandchildren a few times a year, but most weekends I’m either at the depot waiting for the next shift or tinkering in the garage on an old pickup. I’m a regular at the diner on Main Street; the coffee is cheap, the bacon is greasy, and the TV is always on some news channel. When I’m not working, I watch the local high school football games on the community cable, and I spend a couple of evenings a week playing cards with some buddies from the oil camp.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the party’s stance on energy felt like the only thing that kept my paycheck steady. The Democrats kept talking about “green” policies that sounded like they’d shut down the rigs, and I didn’t want the town’s economy to sputter. The GOP’s promise of lower taxes and fewer regulations lined up with what I see day‑to‑day: more jobs, more money in my pocket, and less red tape when I need a permit to fix a broken pipe. I also liked the talk about “border security” and “law and order” – it just felt like the country was trying to hold the line on a lot of things that matter to folks like me.

Since then, I’ve gotten a bit tired of the same old campaign ads, but the basics haven’t changed. I’m still voting in 2026; I’m heading to the polls in November because I want to keep the folks in Washington who understand that North Dakota’s oil and agriculture are the lifeblood of our community. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of health insurance for me and June, and the fact that property taxes keep creeping up even though my wages haven’t kept pace. The Republicans, in my view, are the only ones who actually listen to the “real” Americans out here, while the Democrats seem to be living in some coastal bubble where they can talk about climate and student loans without feeling the cold wind off the prairie.
