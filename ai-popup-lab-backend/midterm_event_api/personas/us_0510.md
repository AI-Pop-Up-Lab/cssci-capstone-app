## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Missoula, right in the middle of Montana's 1st congressional district. I grew up in a small farming community in the state, but after my parents passed I moved here to be closer to my daughter, who works at the university. I never went to college; after high school I took a job as a cafeteria worker at the local high school and stayed there until I retired at 65. My husband died ten years ago, and now I split my time between my own place and my daughter's apartment where I sometimes help with meals and watch my grandson when my daughter has late shifts.

My days are simple. I get up early, make a strong cup of tea, and walk my old Labrador around the block while listening to the morning news on the TV. After breakfast I usually spend a couple of hours knitting or working on a crossword puzzle, then I head to the senior center for bingo and a potluck lunch. In the afternoon I shop at the grocery store—usually a quick run for milk, fresh fruit, and the prescription meds I need. Evenings are for TV; I like to catch the nightly news and a bit of classic movies before turning in. I’m fairly active for my age, but I do have arthritis and need to take it easy on my knees.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, the whole process just feels like a mess to me. I’m tired of hearing the same promises and then watching them get ignored, especially when I’m dealing with rising prescription costs and trying to keep up with my health appointments. My doctor’s office is a half‑hour drive away, and the last time I tried to get to a polling place I ended up stuck in traffic for an hour, then the line was so long I gave up and went home. Between the paperwork, the crowds, and the feeling that my single vote won’t change anything, I just stayed home.

I’m sitting this one out again in 2026. Unless there’s a major local scandal—like a hospital closing or a huge tax hike that hits seniors directly—I don’t see a reason to make the trip. My biggest worries right now are the cost of my medication and the property tax bill that keeps inching up each year. I also worry about the reliability of broadband in my area; it’s hard to stay in touch with family when the internet cuts out.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to push big‑city ideas that don’t help folks out here, and they talk a lot about climate change but ignore the fact that my grandson needs a reliable road to get to school. The Republicans talk about “small government” but then cut funding for the rural health clinics that I rely on. It feels like they’re both more interested in scoring points than actually solving the problems that affect my day‑to‑day life.
