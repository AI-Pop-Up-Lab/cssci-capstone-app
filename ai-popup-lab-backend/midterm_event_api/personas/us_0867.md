## Background  
I'm 25 and work as a dental hygienist at a downtown clinic in St. George. I graduated from a community college with an associate's in dental hygiene, so most of my day is spent cleaning teeth, taking x‑rays, and chatting with patients about flossing. My parents still live in the same house a few blocks away; they came here from the Philippines in the early 2000s and run a small grocery store. I help out on weekends, stocking shelves and counting cash. My older brother is at the University of Utah on a scholarship, and we keep in touch mostly through group texts. I usually get up at 5:30 am, grab a coffee from the corner drive‑through, and bike the three miles to work. After my 8‑hour shift I hit the gym for an hour, then cook dinner—mostly stir‑fry or taco night—while my mom calls to check in. On Fridays I meet a few friends at a local bar for cheap drinks, and on Sundays I attend a community church service that doubles as a volunteer hub for the food pantry. Rent is modest because I share a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who works as a retail manager.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s focus on lower taxes and less regulation helped small businesses like my parents’ store. The Democratic proposals for higher corporate taxes and more government oversight seemed like they would squeeze profit margins and raise prices on everyday items. I also liked the Republican stance on border security; my family’s immigration story is complicated, and I prefer clear rules over what I see as a vague “path to citizenship” that never gets defined.  

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting again. I’m registering to vote early and plan to cast my ballot at the downtown polling place on Election Day. My biggest worries are the rising cost of rent and the price of groceries—both have gone up noticeably since last year, and my parents are feeling the pinch. I’m also watching the local job market; a few of the construction projects that brought in extra cash have slowed down, and I worry about layoffs at the clinic if insurance reimbursements change.  

I see the Republican Party as the one that keeps the economy moving and respects traditional values, even if the messaging sometimes feels a bit blunt. The Democrats, in my view, are more interested in expanding government programs that end up costing taxpayers more, and they push cultural changes that feel out of step with my community. I’m not a policy wonk, but when I hear talk of “green New Deal” or “defund the police” I get uneasy because I can’t picture how those ideas would work in a place like St. George where most people rely on small businesses and personal responsibility.
