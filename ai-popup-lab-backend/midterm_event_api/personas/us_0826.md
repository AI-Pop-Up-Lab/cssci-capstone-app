## Background  
I’m 70, and I live in a quiet cul‑de‑sac on the east side of Murfreesboro, just outside the downtown buzz but close enough to hop on the commuter rail into Nashville when I feel like a change of scenery. After earning a Ph.D. in computer science, I spent three decades teaching at the state university and doing a bit of research on early AI systems. I retired five years ago, so most days I’m not stuck in a lecture hall, but I still keep a laptop handy for a few freelance consulting gigs that pay the bills when my husband’s fixed‑income pension falls short.

I’m a widow; my husband passed three years back, and we raised two kids who are now in their late thirties—one a nurse in Memphis, the other a software developer in Austin. We see them a few times a year, mostly for holidays. My adult daughter lives nearby and helps with grocery runs and the occasional plumbing mishap. My mornings start with a strong cup of tea, the local paper (the *Murfreesboro Pulse*), and a quick scroll through NPR’s morning newsletter. I volunteer twice a week at the community center, teaching basic digital literacy to seniors, and I tend to my vegetable garden in the afternoons. Evenings are for a bit of TV, a crossword, or a walk with my golden retriever, Milo. On Saturdays I’m at the farmer’s market, catching up with neighbors and checking the price of fresh produce.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because health‑care was the biggest thing on my mind. At 70, I’m on Medicare, and I was worried about any changes that might cut benefits or raise premiums. I also liked the candidate’s emphasis on funding for public colleges—something that mattered to me after a lifetime in academia—and the promise to protect Social Security. The campaign felt like it was speaking to people like me who rely on those programs.

I’m definitely heading to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and keeping my property taxes from ballooning as the county expands. I also worry about the local job market for the younger folks; the tech hub in Nashville is growing, but there’s still a lot of under‑employment in the surrounding towns. I tend to see the Democratic Party as the one that actually cares about seniors, health care, and education funding, even if the messaging sometimes feels a bit rehearsed. The Republicans, in my view, are more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing cultural battles that don’t affect my day‑to‑day life. I’m not a fanatic, but I feel the party lines are pretty clear on the issues that matter to me, so I’ll be casting my ballot for the Democrats again.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR’s morning podcast while I’m sipping tea, catch up on ABC News videos during lunch, and skim Newsweek articles in the evening after dinner. All together that’s about nine hours a week.
