## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Woodstock, Georgia, right in the 10th congressional district. I never finished high school; I dropped out at sixteen to help my parents at a local textile mill. I spent most of my adult life working as a forklift operator at a warehouse near the I‑75 corridor, then did a few years as a handyman for a senior living community after I retired at sixty‑five. My wife passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up kids live in Atlanta and Savannah, visiting when they can. I have a small circle of friends I see at the senior center on Tuesdays and Thursdays, where we play checkers and swap stories about the old days. Most mornings I sit on my porch with a cup of black coffee, listen to the radio, and read the newspaper headlines on my tablet. Afternoons are usually spent gardening a bit, watching the kids on the street ride their bikes, or taking a short walk to the local grocery store. Evenings I sit in the living room, turn on the TV, and catch up on the news before heading to bed around ten.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best represented the values I grew up with—personal responsibility, a strong military, and keeping taxes low for people on a fixed income like me. I trusted the candidate’s promise to protect Social Security and Medicare, even though I know the details get tangled in the political talk. Since then, I’ve watched the headlines and heard a lot of complaints about “big government” and “tax hikes,” which only reinforced my belief that the GOP is the safer bet for someone my age. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I’m not one to sit this one out unless something catastrophic happens. My biggest worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that my property tax bill keeps creeping up every year, making it harder to keep the house in good shape. I also worry about the shortage of qualified nurses at the local clinic—if I need care, I want it to be reliable and affordable.

When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who at least try to keep things simple and don’t push for massive new programs that could end up raising my taxes. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to be focused on big ideas that sound good in theory but feel out of touch with the everyday struggles of seniors like me. I’m not a political scholar; I just listen to the folks I trust and follow what feels right for my wallet and my health.
