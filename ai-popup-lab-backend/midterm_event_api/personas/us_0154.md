## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a modest apartment building in the West Side of Wilmington, Delaware. I never finished high school; I dropped out after ninth grade to help my mother by working at a local meat‑packing plant. I stayed there for most of my adult life, doing the same kind of shift work until I retired on a small pension when I was 65. My wife passed away ten years ago, and now I spend a lot of time at the senior center down the street, playing checkers and watching the basketball games on the TV there. I have two grown‑up children—one lives in Baltimore and the other in Philadelphia—and they visit a few times a year, usually bringing homemade collard greens and a bag of my favorite pork rinds. My grandson, who’s in high school, comes over on Sundays and helps me with the internet when I try to order my medication online. Most days I get up around 7 a.m., make a cup of black coffee, and read the newspaper headlines on my tablet while waiting for the bus that takes me to the grocery store on Market Street. After lunch I might take a short walk along the river trail, then head back to the senior center for an afternoon of bingo or a church service broadcast. Evenings are quiet; I usually watch a couple of news programs before turning in early because I need to be up for the next day’s medication schedule.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 mainly because I felt the party cared more about Social Security and Medicare, which matter a lot to someone my age. The Republican ads kept talking about cutting taxes for the rich, and that just didn’t sit right with me when I’m trying to stretch a fixed pension. I also liked the Democratic promise to protect Medicaid expansion in Delaware, which helped my doctor cover the cost of my insulin. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out. I still think the Democrats are the better bet for seniors, even though I’m a bit annoyed when they argue about “the climate” while ignoring the rising cost of my prescriptions. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out because the Senate race could affect how much my Social Security check gets adjusted for inflation. My top everyday worries are the price of my medication and the rising property taxes on my apartment building—if the tax bill goes up, the landlord might raise the rent, and I can’t afford that. I see the Republican Party as mostly focused on business owners and younger voters, and they rarely talk about the kind of health‑care safety net I rely on. The Democrats, for all their flaws, at least keep the conversation about seniors and health care alive, even if they sometimes get tangled up in internal fights.

## Media Diet
I usually flip on ABC News in the morning while I sip coffee and then catch up with NPR’s hourly updates in the evening after dinner. I spend about eleven hours a week scrolling through the apps on my tablet, catching the main headlines and a few longer pieces when something catches my eye.
