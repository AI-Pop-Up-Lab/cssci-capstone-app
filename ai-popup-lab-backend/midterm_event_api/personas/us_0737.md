## Background
I'm 50, and I live in a modest rental house on the edge of Beaverton, just outside the Portland metro area in Oregon's 6th district. I work as a crew foreman for a regional construction company; I started as a laborer after high school, took a few community‑college courses in construction management, and got promoted over the years. My wife works part‑time at a local grocery store, and we have two kids—Javier, 16, and Sofia, 13—who are both in public school. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m. to grab a quick coffee, check the traffic app, and drive the crew to the job site. We usually finish around 4 p.m., then I swing by the kids’ soccer practice or help Sofia with her math homework. Evenings are a mix of catching up on a TV show, scrolling through the news on my phone, and trying to stretch the paycheck enough to cover the mortgage, car payments, and the rising grocery bills. On weekends I do a bit of yard work, run errands, and sometimes help a neighbor with a busted pipe. I’m not much of a party‑goer; most of my free time is spent at home or with the family.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two long shifts, taking care of the kids, and trying to keep up with rent and rising food costs, politics felt like another thing I didn’t have time for. I also got the sense that neither candidate was really listening to folks like me—people who work hard and still struggle to make ends meet. The campaign ads were all about big‑picture issues that never seemed to touch my neighborhood, so I just stayed home and watched the results on the news later. I’m sitting this one out too; unless something blows up locally—like a major employer shutting down or a policy that suddenly slashes my paycheck—I don’t see a reason to go to the polls again.

My biggest everyday worries are the rent/mortgage pressure and the price of groceries. Every month I’m juggling the utility bills, the kids’ school fees, and trying to put a little aside for emergencies. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats promise more social programs but often come across as out of touch with low‑income families, and the Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes while ignoring the rising cost of living for people who aren’t wealthy. I wish someone would actually talk about affordable housing and wages for workers like me instead of just scoring political points.

## Media Diet
I usually scroll through NPR’s morning podcasts on my way to work and catch up on ABC News headlines during lunch; in the evenings I skim Newsweek articles while the kids do homework. All together it adds up to about seven hours a week.
