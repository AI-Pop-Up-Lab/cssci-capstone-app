## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a quiet neighborhood in Litchfield, right in the heart of Connecticut's 5th district. I spent most of my adult life as a high‑school history teacher, and after retiring ten years ago I still keep a few books on the shelf and occasionally help the local library with a reading program. My wife passed away five years back, so most days I'm alone in the house I shared with her, but my two grown‑up children live in Hartford and New Haven and visit every few months with their kids. I try to get up early, make a pot of coffee, and read the newspaper—mostly the print edition of the *Hartford Courant*—before taking a short walk around the block with my old Labrador, Buster. In the afternoons I tend to my garden, pull weeds, and sometimes go to the senior center for bingo or a guest lecture on American history. Evenings are usually spent watching the news or a documentary, and on Sundays I’m at church, followed by a family dinner when my grandchildren are in town.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one paying any attention to Social Security and Medicare, which are my biggest worries at this age. The Republican candidate kept talking about cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations, and that just sounded like a recipe for higher costs for the things I need—prescription drugs, home heating, and the occasional assisted‑living support. I also liked the Democratic platform’s emphasis on climate action, even though I’m not out hiking much; I think it matters for the grandchildren’s future. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration handles the debt ceiling and the rollout of the new health‑care subsidy for seniors. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t think I’ll sit this one out unless something crazy happens, like a major scandal that makes me lose any faith at all. My top everyday concerns are keeping my Social Security checks steady and making sure my prescription pills stay affordable. I also worry about property taxes creeping up on my modest home, especially now that the town is trying to fund more services. I view the Democratic Party as the one that remembers older folks like me, even if they’re not perfect. The Republicans feel like they’re more interested in the younger, wealthier crowd and in cutting programs that keep seniors afloat. I’m not a political scholar, but I’m pretty sure the party that protects my pension and health care is the one I’ll keep supporting.
