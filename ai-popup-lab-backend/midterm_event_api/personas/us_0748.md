## Background
I'm 60, and I live in Camp Hill, just outside Harrisburg, which falls in PA‑3. I’m a tenured professor of sociology at Penn State Harrisburg, so most of my day is spent in lecture halls and office hours, grading papers, and meeting with graduate students. My husband is a retired Army sergeant who now does odd‑job carpentry for extra cash, and our two grown‑up kids live in Philadelphia and Washington, D.C., so the house is mostly quiet except when my mother, who moved here from Puerto Rico a few years ago, drops by for Sunday dinner. I’m fluent in Spanish and often volunteer at the local parish’s immigration assistance program on Saturdays. My mornings start with a quick walk around the neighborhood park, then a coffee while scrolling through NPR headlines before heading to campus. Evenings are usually a mix of dinner with my husband, a bit of TV news, and then grading or writing research proposals. On weekends I hit the farmer’s market, help my mother with grocery shopping, and sometimes attend a community board meeting about local transit. I drive a modest used sedan, keep my house modestly maintained, and try to stay active with weekly yoga classes at the community center.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing for affordable prescription drugs and protecting the public‑school system that I spend my career in. I also appreciated the candidate’s stance on immigration reform, which mattered to me personally and to the families I work with at the parish. Since then I’ve watched the Senate and House battles over the drug‑price bill and the continued push to expand Medicaid in Pennsylvania. I’m pretty convinced the Democrats still have the best chance of moving those issues forward, even if the party sometimes feels a bit scattered on other topics. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit this one out because the upcoming congressional race could affect the funding for our local community college and the transportation grant that would improve bus routes for my mother’s senior center. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of my prescription insulin and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on our modest home. I’m annoyed with the Republican side – they keep talking about “lower taxes” while cutting social services that people like my mom rely on. The Democrats are not perfect, but at least they’re trying to address health care and immigration, which are the things that hit me most directly.

## Media Diet
