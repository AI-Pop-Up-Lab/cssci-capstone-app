## Background
I'm 24 and work full‑time as a warehouse loader at a distribution center on the outskirts of Decatur. I didn't finish high school; I left after sophomore year to help my mom pay the rent when I was 17. My mom still lives in the same modest two‑bedroom house, and my 19‑year‑old sister is in community college, staying with us most nights. I don't have a car, so I ride the company shuttle to the warehouse and walk the rest of the way. My shift starts at 6 a.m. and ends around 2 p.m., then I usually grab a cheap taco from the corner stand, do a quick laundry load, and sometimes help my sister with her homework. Evenings are mostly spent watching whatever's on TV or scrolling on my phone; I try to catch a few episodes of a comedy series before heading to bed around 11. On weekends I might do a gig picking fruit at a nearby farm or help my uncle with a construction job if the pay looks decent. Money's tight, so I keep a close eye on every dollar, especially groceries and the electric bill.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I feel like the whole system is a game that never changes anything for folks like me. Between long shifts, juggling my sister's school expenses, and trying to keep the lights on, voting just seemed like another thing I couldn't fit into my schedule. The candidates all talked about big ideas, but I never saw any concrete help for a warehouse worker trying to make ends meet. I also get a lot of negative ads that just make me more annoyed than motivated. Because of that, I'm planning to sit this one out too, unless something blows up locally—like a sudden plant closure or a rent hike that forces us out of the house. Those kinds of personal blows can push me to the polls, but right now nothing feels that urgent.

My biggest everyday worries are paying rent on time and keeping grocery prices from climbing even higher. The warehouse pays me just enough to cover the basics, but any surprise bill throws the whole budget off. I also worry about the future of the jobs at the distribution center; automation rumors keep floating around, and I don't want to end up without work. When I think about the parties, I feel both are kind of out of touch. The Democrats keep promising better wages and immigration reform, but I haven't seen any real increase in my paycheck, and the immigration talk feels like talk without action for my extended family back in Mexico. The Republicans keep yelling about lower taxes, but most of those cuts never reach people on the bottom rung—they go to big corporations. So I end up feeling like I'm stuck between two sides that both forget the people who actually have to work for a living.
