## Background
I'm 25 and work as an administrative assistant at the regional health clinic in Monroe, Louisiana. I grew up in a nearby small town, moved here for a scholarship at the state university, and stayed after graduating with a bachelor's in communications. My apartment is a two‑bedroom walk‑up on a quiet side street; I share it with a roommate who studies nursing. Most days I catch the 6 a.m. bus, grab a coffee from the corner diner, and spend the morning filing patient paperwork, answering phones, and scheduling appointments. My shift ends around 3 p.m., which gives me a little time to run errands, hit the gym, or meet friends for a quick dinner at the local taco joint. On weekends I volunteer at the community garden, help my aunt with her small boutique, and try to catch a movie at the downtown theater when I can afford it. Money's tight, so I keep a close eye on my budget, especially rent and utilities. I’m not married, don’t have kids, and my family lives a few hours away, so I mostly rely on my own network of friends and coworkers for support.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in the 2024 presidential election. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything, and the whole process just seemed like a circus. Between juggling a demanding job, rent hikes, and trying to make ends meet, I didn’t have the energy to get a ballot, watch the debates, or even figure out where my polling place was. The candidates all sounded the same to me—big promises, big rhetoric, but nothing that touched my daily grind. That cynicism has stuck around, and unless something really shakes up my neighborhood—like a hospital closure or a sudden spike in property taxes—I’m planning to sit out the 2026 midterms too. My top concerns right now are keeping a roof over my head and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing. I’m not a political junkie; I just want reliable jobs and affordable living costs.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats talk a lot about social programs, but I rarely see those promises translate into real help for people like me in Monroe. The Republicans seem more focused on tax cuts for big businesses and less on the everyday struggles of working‑class folks. It feels like they’re both playing a game that leaves the rest of us on the sidelines. I don’t have a deep ideological attachment to either side; I’m mostly annoyed that they keep arguing while my rent keeps going up.
