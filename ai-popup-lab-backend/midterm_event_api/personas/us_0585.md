## Background
I'm 74, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Derry, right in the heart of New Hampshire's 1st district. I never finished high school; after leaving school I worked a few years in a local bakery, then spent most of my adult life taking care of my mother and later my husband. My husband passed away ten years ago, and now I split my time between looking after my adult daughter who lives nearby and volunteering a few hours a week at the community center where we run a bingo night for seniors. Most mornings I brew a strong cup of coffee, read the paper (the printed copy, because I still like the feel of paper), and take a short walk around the neighborhood before heading to the center to set up tables. Afternoons are usually spent on errands—picking up groceries, refilling prescriptions, and checking on the house. Evenings I sit in the living room, the TV on low, catching the news before I go to bed. My routine is simple, and I don't travel far; the closest grocery store is a 15‑minute drive, and the nearest clinic is a half‑hour away.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. By then I felt the whole process was a circus—candidates promising grand things I never saw materialize, endless ads that just added noise, and a feeling that my single vote wouldn't change anything. Between caring for my daughter, dealing with rising health costs, and the daily grind of keeping the house warm in New Hampshire winters, I just didn't have the energy to get to a polling place or navigate the new mail‑in forms. The whole thing left me more cynical than hopeful, and unless something truly shakes my world—like a local factory closing or a drastic cut to senior services—I don't see myself stepping into a booth again. So for the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out.

My biggest worries are keeping up with the cost of my prescription meds and the ever‑rising property taxes that make it harder to stay in my home. I also worry about heating bills creeping up every winter; a cold house is the last thing I need. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they're always talking about climate goals and big‑government programs that never reach my small town, while the Republicans promise lower taxes but seem to forget that seniors like me need reliable health care and social safety nets. Neither side seems to have a clear plan that hits the everyday problems I face, so I stay mostly on the sidelines.
