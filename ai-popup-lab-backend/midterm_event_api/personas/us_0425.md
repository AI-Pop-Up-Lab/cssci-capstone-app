## Background

I work as a senior financial analyst for a regional bank; I have an MBA and spend most weekdays crunching numbers for small‑business loan portfolios. My husband runs a plumbing business downtown, and we have two kids—17‑year‑old Alex, who just finished his senior year, and 14‑year‑old Maya, who is obsessed with video games. On weekends we usually have the kids over at the local park or take them to the mall for a few hours of shopping, then we swing by my mother’s house in Grand Rapids to help her with groceries and a bit of housework. My mornings start with a quick coffee while scrolling through the news on my phone, then a 30‑minute drive to the bank. I grab a sandwich at the office, hit the gym for a short weight session after lunch, and head home around 6 p.m. Evenings are family time—dinner, homework help, maybe a TV show or a board game before we all crash. On Sundays we go to a non‑denominational church a few miles away, and after the service I usually catch up on a few emails before the week begins again.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s tax policies and deregulation stance were the best shot at keeping my husband’s business afloat and protecting my own retirement savings. The Democratic platform seemed too focused on expanding government programs, which I worry would raise my property taxes and make it harder for small businesses like ours to compete. I also liked the candidate’s talk about “border security” and “law and order,” which resonated with my concerns about crime in our neighborhood. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on the economy—interest rates have been fluctuating, and grocery prices have crept up more than I’d like. I’m still leaning Republican, but I’m not blind to the fact that some of the promises from 2024 haven’t materialized; the inflation spike caught me off guard, and I’ve seen a few of my coworkers lose jobs at the bank.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the looming property tax increase the county is proposing to fund road repairs. I also worry about health‑care premiums going up, especially with my mother’s arthritis meds getting more expensive. When I think about the parties, I see the GOP as the side that understands business owners and tries to keep taxes low, even if their messaging can be a bit harsh. The Democrats, in my view, are well‑meaning but tend to push policies that end up costing middle‑class families more in the long run. I’m not a political scholar, but I’ve got a gut feeling that the next Congress should keep the tax cuts in place and focus on cutting red tape for small firms.
