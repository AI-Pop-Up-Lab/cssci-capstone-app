## Background  
I'm 86, and I live in a modest house on the edge of a small town outside Sioux Falls. I never finished high school; I dropped out after the tenth grade to start working on my dad's farm. When the farm got hit hard by the 1980s drought, I found a job at the local meat‑packing plant and stayed there for three decades until I finally retired on a modest pension. My wife passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in Minnesota and Arizona, so most days I'm on my own.  

My mornings start with a cup of black coffee on the porch while I watch the sunrise over the fields. I spend a couple of hours tending to the vegetable garden I keep behind the house—tomatoes, beans, a few potatoes. After lunch I usually take a short drive to the senior center where I play cards with a regular group, watch the news on the community TV, or just sit and listen to folks talk about the weather and the price of gasoline. In the evenings I like to sit in my recliner, flip through the newspaper, and check the internet on my old tablet. I try to keep a simple routine because it keeps me from feeling too isolated.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Between a bout of pneumonia and a broken hip that left me in a cast for weeks, I just couldn't make it to the polls, and honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn't change anything. The whole process seemed like a circus—ads promising miracles, candidates shouting at each other on TV, and then the same old promises that never seem to show up in my mailbox. I told myself I'd sit this one out too, unless something really hits home, like a local hospital closing or a sudden hike in property taxes that would force me off my land.

So for the 2026 midterms I'm sitting this one out. My biggest worries are the cost of my prescription meds and the rising price of groceries. I also keep an eye on my property tax bill; the county has been talking about raising rates to fund a new road project, and that would eat into my fixed income. When I think about the parties, I view the Democrats as a bunch of city‑slickers who don't understand life on the plains, and the Republicans as a group that keeps promising lower taxes but then backs off when the numbers get tight. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than actually solving the problems that matter to folks like me.

Between that and a quick check on the news apps during my senior‑center visits, it adds up to about seven hours a week.
