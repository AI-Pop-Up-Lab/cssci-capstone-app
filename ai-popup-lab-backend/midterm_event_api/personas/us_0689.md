## Background
I'm 31 and I live in the Dayton suburb of Kettering, right in Ohio's 7th district. I grew up in a bilingual household; my parents came from Mexico and worked in a local warehouse, so I learned to speak Spanish at home but went to an English‑speaking public school. I finished two years of community college trying to get an associate's in business, but I dropped out to take a full‑time job when I got a promotion at the retail store where I started as a cashier. Now I'm the assistant manager at a big‑box home‑goods outlet. My shift is usually 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and I spend the evenings restocking, dealing with customers, or helping my younger sister with her college applications. I rent a two‑bedroom apartment with my boyfriend; we split the rent and utilities, and we have a small dog that needs a walk twice a day. On weekends I swing by the local Hispanic market for fresh produce, catch a game at the park with friends, and sometimes volunteer at the church’s food pantry. My day-to‑day is a mix of work, bills, and trying to squeeze in a little downtime watching a sitcom or scrolling social media before bed.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like the whole thing was a circus. Between working overtime during the holiday rush, my boyfriend's car breaking down, and trying to keep up with rent, I just couldn't fit a poll worker line or a trip to the early‑voting site into my schedule. I also think the candidates all talk the same talk—promises about jobs and immigration that never change anything for people like me. I was pretty sure the outcome wouldn't affect my paycheck, so I gave up. For the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something blows up locally—a major factory closure or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits my lease—I don't see a reason to carve out the time. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent and watching grocery prices climb; every time I go to the store I feel the sting of higher produce and meat costs. As for the parties, I feel the Democrats are all talk about immigration reform and student debt, but when I look at my community the help never arrives. The Republicans keep yelling about lower taxes, but the cuts seem to hit social services that my family relies on. Both sides feel like they're playing a game while people like me are left to foot the bill.
