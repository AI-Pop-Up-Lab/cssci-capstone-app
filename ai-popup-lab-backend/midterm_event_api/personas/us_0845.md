## Background

I didn't finish high school; I left after sophomore year to help my mom pay the mortgage when my older brother got sick. My mom still lives in the same two‑bedroom house with my little sister, who is 19 and going to community college nearby. I drive a beat‑up pickup I bought used for about $4,000; it gets me to the warehouse in about 15 minutes, then I grab a cheap coffee from the truck stop before my shift starts at 10 p.m. After work I usually come home, eat a frozen pizza, and watch a couple of YouTube clips before I crash. On my days off I run errands at the local Walmart, catch up with friends at the fast‑food joint on Main, and sometimes help my sister move furniture when she needs it. I don't have a lot of spare cash, so I keep an eye on the gas pump and try to stretch my grocery budget with coupons and sales flyers. I’m not married, I don’t have kids, and I’m not in school—just trying to make ends meet and maybe save a little for a future car or a small house down the road.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping gas cheap and protecting my job at the warehouse. The Democratic candidates kept talking about climate policies and big‑government programs that sounded like they’d raise taxes and make it harder for people like me to keep a steady paycheck. I also liked the talk about stricter border security; it felt like a real issue for my community, even if I never saw anything myself. The Republican candidate visited a local factory the week before the election, and that kind of "show up where we work" vibe stuck with me.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming, I'm definitely going to the polls. I’m not a political junkie, but I do care about my rent going up and the price of groceries staying reasonable. I’m also watching the local job market; there’s talk of a new plant opening up a few counties over, and I want whoever’s in office to make sure that project actually brings jobs instead of just promises. I feel the Republicans still have the better track record on the economy, but I’m getting annoyed with the constant bickering in Congress and how little they seem to care about folks who can’t afford a new car. The Democrats, on the other hand, keep pushing for more social programs that sound nice on paper but I worry they’ll end up funded by higher taxes on working people. I don’t trust either side completely, but I’m leaning toward the GOP because they at least talk about keeping my paycheck intact.
