## Background
I'm 25 and work as a research analyst for a healthcare consulting firm in downtown Toledo. I moved here from Columbus after finishing my master's in public health, and I share a two‑bedroom apartment with a fellow grad student who studies computer engineering. My mornings start at 6:30 am with a quick run along the Maumee River, then I grab a coffee from the corner shop and hop on the bus to the office. The commute is about 30 minutes, so I usually skim the news on my phone while I ride. At work I spend most of the day digging through data sets, writing reports for hospital clients, and meeting with senior consultants. After a typical 9‑to‑5 day I either cook dinner in our tiny kitchen or meet up with friends at a local bar for trivia night. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the farmers market, catching a movie at the theater on Monroe Street, and volunteering at the community health clinic that offers free flu shots. I’m not married, no kids, but my parents visit from Ohio’s suburbs a few times a year and I send them money to help with their mortgage.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing for stronger healthcare access and more realistic student‑loan relief, both issues that hit me hard as a recent grad with a modest salary. The campaign’s focus on expanding Medicaid in Ohio and protecting funding for community clinics resonated with the work I do every day. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out; I’m still disappointed that progress feels slow, but I think the alternative—seeing another wave of cuts to social programs—would be worse. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve already filled out my ballot online and plan to drop it off at the post office on Tuesday. My biggest worries right now are the rising rent prices in Toledo and the fact that my student loans are still climbing despite the temporary pause. I also keep thinking about the upcoming local hospital merger that could affect my job security. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the only ones trying to address these everyday problems, even if they sometimes fumble or compromise too much. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels disconnected from the reality of people like me who are trying to get ahead.
