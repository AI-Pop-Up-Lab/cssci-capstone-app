## Background
I'm 35 and I work as a data analyst for a freight‑forwarding firm that has a hub just outside Conway, Arkansas. My day starts around 6 am when I grab a coffee and scroll through the headlines on my phone while the kids are still asleep. I drive the 15‑minute commute to the office, spend most of the day pulling shipping data, making forecasts, and troubleshooting the occasional spreadsheet glitch. I get home around 5:30 pm, help my 7‑year‑old with math homework, and then we all pile into the car for soccer practice on Tuesdays and Thursday evenings. My wife works part‑time at a community health clinic, so we juggle two jobs and a mortgage on a modest ranch‑style house in a quiet cul‑de‑sac. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs at the big box store, watching the kids’ basketball games, and trying to catch up on a few episodes of a streaming series we never quite finish. I’m not a big fan of big‑ticket vacations; when we do travel it’s usually a short road trip to a nearby state park.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at the office, the kids’ after‑school activities, and feeling like the two parties were just swapping the same old promises, I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The campaign ads all sounded the same, and I was skeptical that any of the candidates cared about a small town like ours. That cynicism turned into a habit, and I told myself I’d sit this one out again unless something really shook me up locally—like a school closure or a sudden spike in property taxes.

So for the 2026 midterms, I’m sitting this one out. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are the rising grocery prices and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. I also keep an eye on local job stability; the logistics industry has been volatile, and a few layoffs in the past year left a lot of us on edge. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about immigration reform and climate goals, but I don’t see any concrete help for small‑business owners or the kind of job training my friends need. The Republicans brag about cutting taxes, yet they rarely address the health‑care costs that keep rising for families like mine. It feels like a lot of noise and not enough focus on the stuff that matters to people who work a regular nine‑to‑five.
