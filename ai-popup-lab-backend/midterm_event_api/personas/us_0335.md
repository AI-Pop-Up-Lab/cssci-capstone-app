## Background
I'm 20 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Hopkinsville, right in the middle of Kentucky's 2nd district. My dad works at the local manufacturing plant and my mom does part‑time admin work for the high school. I stay with them while I take a couple of community‑college classes in automotive technology, but most of my time is spent working the night shift at a big‑box retailer. I clock in around 10 p.m., finish a 6‑hour shift, then head home to do a quick dinner—usually something cheap like ramen or leftover pizza—before I hit the books for an hour or two. On weekends I hang out with a few friends from class; we play pickup basketball at the park or drive out to the mall to listen to loud music and grab cheap burgers. I have a battered old pickup that I use to run errands and sometimes help my dad haul tools. My phone is my main connection to the world; I text a lot, scroll through TikTok for a few minutes, and only check the news when I have a spare moment.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was convinced that the promises on student loan relief and expanding broadband in rural areas mattered to my future. I wasn't super into the campaign, but a friend kept pestering me about the mail‑in ballot, and I figured it wouldn't hurt to try something different. The candidate's stance on making community college more affordable hit close to home, and I was hoping that would translate into real help for folks like me who are stuck between school and a low‑wage job.

Since then, I've gotten a bit jaded. The promised broadband upgrades have been slow, and the student‑loan relief program got tangled up in bureaucracy. I still think the Democrats have better ideas for education and health care, but I also see a lot of talk that never turns into action. The Republicans keep pushing for lower taxes and fewer regulations, which sounds nice on paper, but I worry about cuts to social programs that keep people like my mom afloat. Overall, I feel the parties are more about scoring points than solving the everyday problems I see around me.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I'm going to the same polling place I used last time, and I already have my ballot ready. My biggest worries right now are the rent I pay to my parents for my room and the rising price of gas—my pickup drinks a lot, and I need it for work and school. The second concern is the lack of decent broadband at home; without reliable internet, I can't take online classes or look for better jobs. When I think about the parties, I'm leaning toward the Democrats because they seem more attuned to my immediate needs, even if I’m not thrilled with how fast they move.
