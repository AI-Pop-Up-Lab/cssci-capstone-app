## Background
I'm 55 and I work the night shift as a stockroom clerk at the big box store in Greeley, Colorado, right in the heart of the 1st district. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I picked up a series of low‑skill jobs—dishwasher, janitor, then the stockroom gig that’s been steady for the last eight years. My husband, who’s been laid off a few times, now does occasional handyman work on the side. We own a modest two‑bedroom house that we bought in the late ’90s, and our two grown‑up kids live out of state, but they still call every Sunday. My day usually starts with a quick coffee at the diner before my 7 p.m. shift, then a few hours on the truck loading pallets, a break where I scroll through my phone for the news, and a grind back home around 3 a.m. I try to squeeze in a church service on Saturdays and a weekly bingo night at the community center when I can. The biggest hassle is keeping the house warm when the wind blows off the plains, and the grocery bill that never seems to shrink.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about Medicaid expansions and the kind of job training programs that could help people like my husband get back on their feet. I also liked the promise of more funding for public schools, even though I never finished high school myself—I think every kid deserves a chance I never got. The campaign ads on the radio and the flyers I got at the store made me think the Democrats were the lesser evil compared to the GOP’s talk about cutting taxes for the rich while leaving folks like me to shoulder higher utility bills.

I'm voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that my heating bill keeps climbing every winter. I’m also watching the local property tax hikes that threaten to make it harder to keep the house. I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to care about the working class, even if they sometimes get tangled up in internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem obsessed with cutting taxes for corporations and pushing policies that make my paycheck feel smaller. I’m not a political scholar; I just know which side feels a little more like it’s looking out for my family.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m getting ready for work, and I catch ABC News and CBS News clips on my phone during my break and again before I head to bed. All together it’s about six hours a week.
