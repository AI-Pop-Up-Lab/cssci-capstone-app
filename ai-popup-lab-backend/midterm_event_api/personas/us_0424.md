## Background
I'm 84 and I live in Marquette, the little city on the shores of Lake Superior that makes up most of Michigan's 1st district. I spent 35 years teaching high‑school English at the local public school before I finally retired a decade ago. My husband passed away five years back, so most days I'm on my own, but I get regular visits from my three adult children and their kids when they come up for the summer. I own a modest condo on the third floor of an older building; the stairs are a pain, but I keep a small garden on the balcony where I grow herbs and a few tomatoes in the short summer.

My mornings start with a cup of black coffee and the printed newspaper—still the best way to get the headlines, I think. After that I usually take a short walk along the waterfront, stopping to watch the ferries and sometimes greeting the dog walkers who pass me. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I volunteer at the senior center, helping with a computer class for folks who are finally getting online. Evenings are for television: I flip between the news programs on the channels I trust and the occasional documentary on PBS. Weekends I either go to church, visit my sister in nearby Houghton, or sit on the porch and listen to classic folk records while knitting. My health is mostly good, though I keep an eye on my blood pressure and take a few pills for arthritis.

## Politics and this election
In the 2024 presidential election I voted Democratic because I felt the party cared more about Social Security, Medicare, and protecting the health‑care safety net that I rely on. The candidate’s promises to lower prescription‑drug prices and to invest in broadband for rural areas also mattered; my grandchildren need reliable internet to stay in school, and the local clinic has been struggling with staffing. I didn’t feel the Republican platform addressed my worries about rising drug costs or the need for affordable home‑care services for seniors.

I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the cost of my prescription meds and the property tax bill on my condo, which keeps creeping up each year. I also worry about the local job market because my kids have been looking for work in the area and many of the younger folks are moving away. I tend to see the Democrats as the side that remembers people like me—old, on a fixed income, and needing reliable health care. The Republicans, in my view, are more focused on cutting taxes for big businesses and pushing down regulations that actually keep the air clean over the lake. I’m not a political scholar, but I’m tired of hearing politicians talk past each other while I’m just trying to keep my blood pressure down and my meds affordable.

## Media Diet
I watch the evening news on ABC and listen to NPR’s morning hour while I have my coffee; I also skim the top stories on the Newsweek website and catch a quick CBS update before bed. All together it adds up to about twelve hours a week.
