## Background
I'm 55 and I live in Winchester, right in the middle of Virginia's 2nd district. I work full‑time as the administrative manager for the community college down the road; I handle scheduling, payroll, and the occasional grant paperwork. My husband retired from the fire department a few years ago, so most mornings we both sit at the kitchen table with coffee while the news runs on the TV. I have two grown children—one lives in Richmond with a law practice, the other is back in Ohio teaching high school. My mother, who’s in her late 80s, still lives a short drive away, and I pop over a few times a week to help with groceries and the occasional doctor appointment. My day usually starts at 6 am, a quick walk around the neighborhood, then a drive to campus. I spend my lunch break in the staff lounge, catching up on emails and the occasional headline on my phone. After work I swing by the church basement to help with the food pantry, then head home to make dinner, watch some TV, and maybe scroll through a few forums before bed. Rent is modest, but property taxes have crept up, and I'm always watching the gas gauge because the commute to the college can be a long haul when traffic snarls on I‑81.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican. I felt the economy was slipping, and the tax‑cut promises from the GOP sounded like a lifeline for a household juggling a mortgage, a college‑age kid’s tuition, and my mom’s medical bills. The Democratic candidates kept talking about climate policies and big‑government programs that seemed out of touch with my daily grind. I also care a lot about the abortion issue; the Republican stance aligned with what I believe is best for families. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled inflation and the recent push for higher property taxes in Fairfax County, which bleed into my own tax bill. It’s been frustrating seeing the news cycle jump from one scandal to another without clear solutions. I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms because the local races could affect school funding and road repairs that matter to my commute and my kids’ kids. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of gasoline and the looming property‑tax hike that could force us to refinance the house. I see the Republican Party as the one still willing to push for lower taxes and tougher immigration enforcement, even if I don’t love every internal squabble. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free college and expansive social programs that would mean higher taxes for folks like me, and they seem more focused on cultural battles than on fixing the price at the pump.
