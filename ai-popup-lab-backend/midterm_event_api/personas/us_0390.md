## Background
I'm 65, and I live in the little community of Parkton, right on the edge of Baltimore County. I spent most of my adult life teaching middle‑school English at a public school, and I retired five years ago after taking my pension. My husband passed away two years back, so now it's just me, my grown‑up daughter who lives in Washington, D.C., and my mother, who needs help with meds and getting around. I drive a used Toyota Corolla, try to get up early for a cup of black coffee, and then walk the dog—an old Labrador that still thinks he's a puppy—around the cul‑de‑sac. Most mornings I check the mailbox, sort through a few bills, and then head to the local senior center where I volunteer to run a book club. Afternoons are usually spent on grocery runs at the big box store, a quick call to my daughter, and sometimes a trip to the pharmacy for my mom's prescriptions. Evenings are quiet: TV in the background, a crossword puzzle, and a few minutes of scrolling through the news sites I trust. On Saturdays I help my neighbor mow his lawn in exchange for fresh tomatoes from her garden. The routine is simple, and I like keeping things predictable.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one talking about keeping taxes low enough for retirees like me and protecting the border, which I see as a matter of safety for my community. I also appreciated the candidate’s promise to cut down on what I call “government waste” that shows up in my property tax bill every year. The campaign ads that focused on law‑and‑order resonated with me after hearing about a few break‑ins near my neighborhood. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the current administration handles inflation and the cost of prescription drugs—those are the things that hit my wallet hard. I’m still convinced that the GOP is more aligned with my priorities, even though I get annoyed when some of their rhetoric feels too extreme for my taste. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for higher taxes and more regulation that just makes everyday life tougher for folks on a fixed income. I’m voting in the 2026 midterms; I’m not planning to sit this one out. My top concerns right now are the rising price of my mom’s medication and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on my modest pension. I’m not a political scholar, but I know I want leaders who will keep my costs down and keep the neighborhood safe.
