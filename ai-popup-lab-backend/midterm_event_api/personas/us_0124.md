## Background  
I'm 65, and I live in a modest house in West Haven, right on the edge of the shoreline. I spent 35 years teaching English at a public high school, and I retired three years ago. My husband passed away a decade ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids who live in Boston and Hartford. I still get a few calls a week from my oldest daughter about her teenage son—sometimes I drop by to babysit when she needs a night out.  

My days start around 7 a.m. with a cup of coffee and the local paper, then a quick walk around the neighborhood with my dog, Buster. I volunteer twice a week at the community library, helping adults with basic computer skills. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I attend a senior‑center yoga class, and on Saturdays I’m usually at the farmer’s market picking up fresh produce. I keep my house heated with a small electric furnace, which makes me watch the utility bill closely. Most evenings I’m either cooking a simple dinner, watching the news, or video‑chatting with my sister in Rhode Island. I drive a used sedan, but I’m thinking about getting a small electric car when the rebate comes around.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about how rising prescription‑drug prices would affect my limited fixed income, and I liked the promise to protect Social Security. The candidate also talked about expanding broadband in rural Connecticut, which matters to my brother who lives out in the hills. I felt the Democrats were the only ones who cared about seniors like me and about keeping public schools funded—something I still care about even after retirement.  

I'm definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the cost of my heart medication and the property tax hike the town council is proposing to fund a new parking garage. I’m also watching the local job market because my son’s company is thinking about moving out of state, and that would affect a lot of my neighbors. I still think the Democratic Party is the better steward of the safety‑net programs we rely on, even if I’m skeptical about some of their messaging on climate policy. The Republicans, in my view, spend too much time talking about tax cuts for the wealthy and not enough about protecting Medicare. I get annoyed when they dismiss the idea of a public option for health care; it feels like they’re ignoring the reality that most of us are living on a fixed income.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR’s morning edition while I sip coffee, then scroll through ABC News headlines in the evening after dinner. When I have a few spare minutes on the weekend I check CNBC for any market news that might affect my retirement accounts. Altogether it’s about four hours a week.
