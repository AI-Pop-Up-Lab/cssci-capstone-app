## Background  
I’m 65, and I live in the little town of London, right in the heart of Kentucky’s 3rd district. I spent most of my working life at the nearby coal‑powered plant, first as a boiler operator and later as a shift foreman. I retired five years ago when the plant shut down, and now I do odd jobs—fixing fences, helping neighbors with small remodels, and occasionally hauling wood for the local feed store. My wife, Ellen, is a retired school cafeteria manager; we’ve been married for 42 years. Our two kids live out of state, one in Ohio working as a mechanic and the other in Tennessee teaching middle‑school math. Most mornings I’m up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of black coffee, and read the paper while the rooster outside our porch crowed. After a quick walk around the yard, I’ll check the weather and then head to the hardware store to pick up supplies for the day’s project. Afternoons are usually spent in the garage or on the porch, listening to the radio or a news podcast while Ellen knits. Evenings are family time—sometimes a potluck with the neighbors, other times a quiet dinner with the TV on. Sundays we go to the Methodist church, and after the service I stay for the pot‑luck lunch and a bit of gossip about who’s fixing what around town. Money’s tight enough that we watch our utility bills closely, but we’re not living on the edge; the biggest stress is keeping the house in good shape as we both get older.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who rely on steady jobs and low taxes. When the coal plant closed, the promises from the Democratic side about “green jobs” sounded nice, but they never materialized in our county, and the subsidies they talked about felt like a handout that would raise my property tax. The GOP candidate kept talking about keeping energy costs down and protecting the right to own a gun, which mattered to me and my buddies at the senior center. I also like the idea of a strong military and a judge who respects the Constitution the way I was taught.  

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m still uneasy about the Democrats; their push for higher minimum wages and more regulation on small businesses feels like it would squeeze the little guy even harder. I do get annoyed when the GOP talks about “law and order” without addressing the real problem of drug‑related crime in my town, but overall I think they’re the lesser of two evils. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of gasoline—every time the pump ticks up I have to cut back on trips to the hardware store—and the cost of my prescription meds, which have been creeping up despite my Medicare Part D plan. I also worry about property taxes; the county has been looking at raising them to fund road repairs, and that would bite right into my fixed income. I’m not a political scholar, I just want a government that lets me keep my house, my garden, and my independence without being asked for more money every year.
