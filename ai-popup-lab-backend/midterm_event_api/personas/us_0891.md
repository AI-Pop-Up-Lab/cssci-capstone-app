My wife is a registered nurse at a nearby hospital, and we have two kids—Mia, 8, and Leo, 5. I’m not a big political junkie, but I keep an eye on the news when I have a spare moment.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one talking seriously about cutting taxes and curbing what I see as runaway government spending. The economy was still feeling the after‑effects of the pandemic, and my mortgage payment was creeping up, so the promise of lower taxes and a focus on business growth resonated with me. I also liked the candidate’s stance on immigration enforcement, which I thought would make the community safer for my family.

Since then I’ve stayed pretty consistent. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve already filled out my ballot in advance. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax hikes that the county has been proposing. I’m not thrilled with either party, but I feel the Republicans are at least trying to keep taxes low and push back against what I see as overregulation. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising big‑scale social programs but never seem to have a clear plan for paying for them, and they push policies that feel out of touch with suburban families like mine. I’m not a fan of the political theater on both sides, but when it comes to my wallet and my kids’ future, I lean toward the side that talks about fiscal restraint and a strong national defense.
