## Background
I'm 35 and work as a data analyst for the state health department in Des Moines. My day starts with a quick coffee at a corner café near my apartment on the east side, then a 20‑minute drive to the office where I spend most of my time cleaning up data sets and pulling reports on vaccination rates. After work I usually hit the gym for an hour, then swing by the grocery store before heading home to cook dinner. I rent a two‑bedroom place with a roommate who also works in tech, so the rent and utilities are a constant line item on my budget. On weekends I bike the trails along the river, catch up on a few episodes of a true‑crime podcast, and sometimes help my sister with her kids when she needs a break. I grew up in a small town outside of Ames, went to college in Iowa City, and later earned a master's in public health. I’m not married, no kids of my own, and I’m pretty set in my routine—work, gym, a bit of social time at the local bar with a few coworkers, and then back to the apartment.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at the office, a hectic schedule, and the feeling that the candidates were just rehashing the same old promises, I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I was tired of the endless ads and heated debates that never seemed to touch the real issues I deal with daily—like rising rent and grocery prices. I also felt distant from the political process; most of the conversations I have are about work deadlines, not policy. That cynicism hasn’t really faded, so for the 2026 midterms I’m sitting this one out too, unless something dramatic happens locally—like a major hospital closure or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits my wallet directly.

My top everyday concerns are the cost of groceries and the rent I’m paying. When I see the price of milk or a gallon of gas go up, it hits my budget right away, and the same goes for my monthly lease. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the GOP. Their rhetoric on cultural issues feels out of touch with what I see in my community, and I worry about policies that could cut funding for public health programs I actually rely on. The Democrats get a pass for now because they at least talk about expanding healthcare and funding education, but I’m not convinced they’ll follow through on the economic side of things—especially when it comes to affordable housing. So yeah, I’m fed up with both sides, but the GOP gets a bigger slice of my irritation.
