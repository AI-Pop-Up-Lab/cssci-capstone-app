## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates were speaking to people like me—just a lot of talk about big‑ticket issues while I’m trying to keep the lights on and afford groceries. Between long shifts, family responsibilities, and the feeling that my single vote wouldn’t change anything, I just stayed home. The whole process seemed like a hassle, and the ads were all “vote for change” without any real explanation of how that would affect my rent or my mom’s job. That same sense of disengagement is still there, so I’m planning to sit this one out too, unless something hits close to home—like a sudden rent hike or a local factory closing—that forces me to pay attention.

My biggest everyday worries are making enough money to cover rent and utilities, and the rising price of food at the store where I work. I also worry about the limited job opportunities in the area; most good-paying jobs seem to be far away, and the commute isn’t realistic for someone on a low wage. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats feel like they’re always promising more programs but never delivering anything that reaches my street, and the Republicans seem to ignore the struggles of working‑class families in places like mine. I’m not a big fan of the political theater; I just want policies that keep the lights on and the grocery shelves stocked.
