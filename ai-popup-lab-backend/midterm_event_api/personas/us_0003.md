## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Bethel, Alaska. I spent most of my adult life working as a health aide at the local clinic, helping elders and kids with basic care. I never finished a degree, but I took a few courses at the community college when I was younger, which is why I list “some college.” My husband passed away three years ago, and my two grown‑up children live down in Anchorage and Seattle, so I mostly keep to myself and my close circle of friends at the senior center.  

My days start early; I brew a strong cup of coffee, read the paper, and then head out to the community garden where I tend to a few hardy vegetables. In the afternoons I often knit or do crossword puzzles while the radio plays a mix of country and old‑time folk songs. Twice a week I go to church, and on Saturdays I help organize a pot‑luck for the tribal elders. I drive a little pickup to the grocery store in town, which is a long trip when the snow is deep, so I try to keep a stock of frozen meals. I watch the news in the evenings, usually after dinner, and I keep a notebook of things that bother me—especially when it comes to health care and the price of heating fuel.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one that cared about keeping the oil pipelines open and protecting the hunting and fishing rights that are vital to my community. I was also tired of the talk about “climate change” that seemed to ignore the reality of winter here, and I liked the candidate’s promise to keep the federal budget from draining resources that we need for local schools.  

I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescriptions and the ever‑increasing price of heating oil; a cold winter can be deadly if you can’t afford to keep the house warm. I’m also watching the housing market—rent for a small apartment in town has gone up a lot, and a lot of my friends are thinking about moving to a cheaper place.  

When I think about the parties, the GOP still feels like the one that understands the importance of resource jobs and respects our tribal sovereignty, even if some of their rhetoric feels a bit harsh. The Democrats, on the other hand, keep talking about big‑city issues and seem to forget that a lot of us live miles from any major hospital or grocery store. I’m not a political scholar, but I know which side seems more likely to keep my life affordable.
