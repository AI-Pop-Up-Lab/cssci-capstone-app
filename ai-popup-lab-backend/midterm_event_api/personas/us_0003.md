## Background  
I’m 86 years old and I’ve lived most of my life in a small village along the Kuskokwim River, not far from Bethel. I’m part Yup'ik and grew up speaking a mix of English and my native language at home. After high school I took some classes at the University of Alaska Fairbanks but never finished a degree; I stopped going after a couple of years to help raise my three kids. For a while I worked as a school bus driver, taking kids from the out‑lying cabins to the elementary school in town, and later I helped my husband run a little general store that sold everything from canned beans to hunting gear. He passed away five years ago, and my children now live in Anchorage and Seattle, so I’m mostly on my own, though my grandchildren visit in the summer and stay for a few weeks.  

My days are simple. I get up early, make a pot of coffee, and head out to tend the garden where I grow potatoes, carrots, and a few hardy greens. I still go out on the river in a small skiff when the ice melts to check the fish traps and sometimes bring home a fresh catch for dinner. In the afternoons I sit on the porch, knit, and listen to the local radio station for weather updates. Evenings are when I turn on the TV and catch the news, because that’s the only way I keep up with what’s happening beyond the river.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping the oil pipelines open and protecting the jobs that keep our community afloat. The idea of more federal regulation on drilling made me nervous – I worry about what that would do to the price of gasoline and the cost of heating our homes. I also liked the way the Republican candidate talked about respecting our traditions and not trying to change everything we’ve always done.  

Now it’s 2026 and I’m definitely going to the polls again. I’m voting for the Republican candidate for the at‑large House seat because I trust them to keep the land open for hunting, fishing, and oil work. My biggest worries right now are the rising price of heating fuel – every winter the bill gets higher – and the property taxes that keep creeping up on my little piece of land. I’m not following every campaign rally or reading every policy paper; I mostly just watch the news in the evenings and talk with my neighbors at church. I’d say I’m moderately engaged – I vote, I listen, but I don’t spend a lot of time on the details.
