## Background  
I’m 49 and I work as a dental hygienist at a busy practice in Goleta, just north of Santa Barbara. My day starts early; I get up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and hop on the 15‑minute bus to the clinic. The job is steady, but the schedule is a grind—most weeks I’m there from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., then I’m back home to juggle a few other things.  

I live in a modest two‑bedroom condo with my husband, who does freelance graphic design from a home office, and our two kids: a 17‑year‑old senior in high school and a 14‑year‑old sophomore. We also take care of my mother’s mother‑in‑law, who moved in after a stroke last year. Between school runs, homework help, and the occasional grocery run to the local market, evenings are a mix of homework, dinner, and trying to squeeze in a little TV before bed.  

On weekends I like to bike the coastal trail, meet friends for brunch at the farmer’s market, and catch a movie at the downtown theater. Money is tight enough that we keep a careful eye on the utility bills and try to stretch the grocery budget. I’m not a political junkie, but I keep up enough to know what’s happening in the state and the country.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one seriously talking about protecting the coast from oil spills and supporting more affordable health care. My husband and I both work in jobs that don’t give us much health insurance flexibility, so the promise of expanding coverage mattered. I also liked the candidate’s stance on funding community colleges, which would help people like me who only have an associate degree.  

Since then, I’ve watched the news and seen the Democrats stumble on a few promises, but I still think they’re the better bet for the issues that touch my daily life. The Republicans keep pushing for cuts to environmental regulations that could endanger the beaches we love and the tourism jobs that keep the town afloat. Their rhetoric on “law and order” feels out of touch with the mixed‑income neighborhood I live in, where most folks are just trying to get by.  

I’m definitely going to the polls this November for the midterms. My top concerns are the rising cost of rent and the price of gas—both make the daily commute and the kids’ extracurriculars harder to afford. I’m also watching the local job market; there’s talk of a new tech hub opening up, but I’m not sure it will create the kind of middle‑class jobs we need. Overall, I see the Democrats as the party that at least pretends to care about climate, health care, and education, while the GOP feels like it’s trying to turn the state back into a “wild west” of deregulation.
