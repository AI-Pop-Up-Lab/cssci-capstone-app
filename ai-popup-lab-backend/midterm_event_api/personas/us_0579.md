## Background

I put in a 10‑hour shift most days, loading trucks and keeping inventory moving. I finished a couple of years at a community college studying logistics, but I never got the degree because I needed to start earning right away. My parents live a short drive away in a modest house near the airport, and I still send a chunk of my paycheck back to help with the mortgage. I rent a one‑bedroom apartment a few blocks from my job, which is cheap enough to cover but leaves little wiggle room for anything else.

My day starts early—about 5:30 a.m.—with a quick coffee from a corner deli and scrolling through my phone while I wait for the bus. After the shift I usually hit the gym for an hour, then head home to cook something simple, like beans and rice, before catching a late‑night show or scrolling through news feeds. ## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about the things that keep my paycheck steady—border security, lower taxes, and less regulation on the logistics industry. I’m not a political scholar, but I saw the Democratic platform as pushing for higher taxes and more government control, which felt like it could hurt my job and the little savings I’m building. The campaign ads that talked about “protecting American jobs” and “keeping our communities safe” resonated with the everyday grind I live through.

Since then, I’ve stayed pretty consistent. I’m definitely planning to vote in the 2026 midterms—no reason to sit this one out unless something crazy pops up. My biggest worries are still paying rent and making sure groceries don’t keep getting pricier. Gas prices affect my commute, and I’m always watching my bank account to see if I can afford a weekend with my sister. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more focused on big‑picture policies that don’t translate to my daily life, and sometimes their messaging feels out of touch with working‑class folks. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about keeping taxes low and giving people more control over their own money. I’m not a fan of the extreme rhetoric either, but I feel they’re closer to what I need right now.
