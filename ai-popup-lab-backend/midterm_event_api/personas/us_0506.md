## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Missoula, right where the streets start to turn into gravel. I taught high school English for thirty‑plus years before I retired last decade. Now I fill my days with a mix of part‑time tutoring for a few kids at the community center, a weekly book club, and a lot of gardening—especially tomatoes and kale, which I sell at the farmer’s market when the season’s good. My husband passed away five years ago, so it's just me and my two grown children who live in Bozeman and Helena. They visit a few times a year, and my grandson comes over every summer and helps me haul firewood. Mornings start with a strong cup of coffee, the local paper, and a quick scroll through NPR on my phone while the kettle whistles. I drive the short trip to the library to drop off books, then head to the market or the tutoring room. Evenings are usually quiet: I make dinner, watch a bit of the news, and sometimes knit while listening to an old‑time radio drama. On Sundays I attend a small church service and then help out with the food pantry. Money’s tight enough that I watch every expense, but I’ve managed to keep the house paid off and the garden thriving.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because health care was the biggest thing on my mind. My mother’s recent hospital stay showed me how fragile the system can be, and the candidate’s promise to protect Medicare really hit home. I also liked the stance on protecting the river runs we depend on for irrigation and recreation. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how things are moving, and while I’m not thrilled with every decision, I feel the party still cares more about folks like me than the other side does.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that make it harder to keep the house in good shape. I also worry about water rights—there’s talk of new drilling projects that could affect the Clark Fork, and that would hit the garden hard. As for the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who at least try to look out for seniors and the environment, even if the bureaucracy can be slow. Republicans, in my view, keep pushing for more oil and gas development without caring how it messes with clean water or the price of gas at the pump. I get annoyed when the media spins everything into a partisan circus, but I still lean toward the side that promises to keep health care affordable and the land protected.

## Media Diet
I start each day with NPR’s morning news while I’m sipping coffee, then in the evenings I scroll through ABCNews and Cbsnews.com on my tablet, squeezing in about five hours total each week.
