## Background  
I'm 40 and work full‑time as a dental hygienist at a practice in Liberty, Missouri, just outside the Kansas City metro area in the 2nd congressional district. I got my associate degree at a community college in Kansas City, which is why I never went after a four‑year degree; the program let me start earning right after two years, and I liked the hands‑on vibe.  

I live with my husband, who runs a small landscaping crew, and our two kids, Maya (8) and Eli (5). Our house is a modest ranch we bought five years ago; the mortgage is the biggest line on the budget. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 am, shuffling the kids into the car, dropping Maya at the elementary school across town, and then heading to the clinic. My shift runs until about 2 pm, then I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and help the kids with homework. Evenings are usually a quick dinner, a bit of TV, and then we try to get the kids to bed by 9. On weekends we do a mix of yard work, church, and trying to keep the house from looking like a disaster zone—there’s always a leaky faucet or something that needs fixing. I drive a used sedan that’s been reliable but is starting to need some work, which adds another worry to the list.

## Politics and this election  
Back in 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate. I liked the way she talked about expanding Medicaid in our state and protecting funding for community colleges—something I still think about when I see students struggling to afford tuition. I also appreciated her stance on gun safety after the shooting at a nearby school; it felt personal, even though I’m not a gun owner myself.  

Since then I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening locally. The economy feels shaky; the cost of groceries keeps climbing, and the landscaping business my husband runs is feeling the squeeze from higher fuel prices. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms because I think local races—like the state senate seat and the congressional race—will have a real impact on those day‑to‑day issues. My top concerns right now are the mortgage payments and the rising price of food.  

When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to care about social programs, but sometimes I get the sense they’re more focused on big‑picture ideas than the nitty‑gritty stuff that affects a family like mine. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk a lot about cutting taxes and limiting government, which sounds good for my husband’s business, but I worry about their stance on healthcare and education. I’m not a die‑hard partisan; I just want whoever’s in office to keep the lights on, the kids fed, and the rent (or mortgage) affordable.
