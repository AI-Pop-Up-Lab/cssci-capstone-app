## Background
I'm 84, and I live in Madison, a quiet suburb just outside Huntsville in Alabama's 7th district. I spent most of my adult life as an English professor at the local community college, and I finally retired five years ago when my health started giving me trouble walking up the stairs. My husband passed away ten years ago, so it's just me now, but I get regular visits from my daughter who lives in Atlanta and her teenage son who calls me "Grandma" on the phone. Most mornings I make a pot of coffee, sit at my kitchen table, and skim the newspaper—still the printed edition, because I like the feel of the paper. After breakfast I take a short walk around the neighborhood, waving to the same retirees and dog walkers I’ve known for decades. In the afternoons I volunteer at the public library, helping folks with their computer searches and running the weekly book club for seniors. Sundays are for church, and I try to keep up with my garden, though the heat in Alabama makes that a bit of a chore. I drive a modest sedan, keep a small budget, and try not to spend too much on anything beyond groceries and my prescription meds.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about Medicare and the rising cost of my prescription drugs. The campaign promised to protect Social Security and keep health care affordable for seniors like me, and that mattered more than any other issue. I also liked the fact that the candidate talked about funding for community colleges, which reminded me of my own career. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are the better choice for protecting my health and my fixed income, even though I sometimes feel they talk a lot and deliver slowly. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t have the energy to sit this one out, and I want to make sure my voice counts on the issues that affect my daily life. My top concerns right now are Medicare coverage—especially any changes to prescription drug benefits—and the property tax rate on my home, which has been creeping up as the town expands. I see the Republican Party as more focused on cutting taxes for businesses and less attentive to seniors’ needs; they talk about “fiscal responsibility,” but I worry that means cutting services I rely on. The Democrats, in my view, are more inclined to protect social programs, though I’m aware they sometimes overpromise. Overall, I’m frustrated with both sides for the endless partisan fighting, but I lean toward the party that promises to keep my health care stable.
