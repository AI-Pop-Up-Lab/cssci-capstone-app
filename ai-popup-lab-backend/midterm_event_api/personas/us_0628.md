## Background  
I'm 50 and live in Farmington, right in the middle of New Mexico's 1st district. I work full‑time as the administrative manager for the regional hospital; the job keeps me on my feet, juggling schedules, insurance paperwork, and the occasional emergency drill. My husband runs a small construction crew that does a lot of work on tribal lands nearby, and we have two kids—17 and 15—who are both in high school and drive each other around a lot.  

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, a glance at the news on my phone, and then a 30‑minute drive to the hospital. I spend most of my morning in the records department, then break for a brief lunch in the staff cafeteria where we swap stories about the latest baseball game or the rising price of gas. After work I pick up the kids, help them with homework, and try to squeeze in a grocery run before dinner. Weekends are a mix of family chores, a little yard work, and the occasional visit to the local community center where my husband volunteers to teach basic carpentry. I don't have a lot of free time, but I do enjoy listening to a bit of country music while I drive and catching up on a good mystery novel when I can.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between the kids' schedules, my husband's overtime, and the endless paperwork at the hospital, I just never found the time to make it to the polls, and honestly, I felt like none of the candidates really understood what life is like out here in northern New Mexico. The big parties both seem to swing between promises about oil drilling and vague talks about protecting the environment, but neither side talks about the water shortages that are hitting our farms and the rising cost of gasoline that makes my family's commute a nightmare.  

I’m sitting this one out again in 2026, unless something huge happens locally—like a major water‑management plan that threatens our community or a scandal that makes one side look truly incompetent. My top everyday worries are the price of gas and the reliability of our water supply; everything else feels secondary.  

When I think about the parties, the Republicans get my ear when they talk about land rights and fewer regulations for small businesses, but they also push for cuts that would hurt the hospital's funding, which I can't stomach. The Democrats, on the other hand, keep promising more social programs and better infrastructure, yet I hear them talk in circles about climate policy while our water pipelines stay broken. Both sides feel distant, and I end up feeling more annoyed than hopeful.
