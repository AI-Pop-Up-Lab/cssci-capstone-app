## Background

I work full‑time as a laborer for a local construction crew that does road repairs and small commercial projects for the county. My shift starts at 6 a.m., so I’m usually up before sunrise, grabbing a coffee from the convenience store and checking the news on my old Android phone while I eat a bagel that’s been sitting out since yesterday. I’m the only adult in the household; my mother moved in a few years ago after her health started failing, and I take care of her medical bills and groceries. My sister lives in Phoenix and sends money when she can, but most months I’m just trying to stretch the paycheck to cover rent, the trailer lot fee, and the occasional car repair. On weekends I either help out on a neighbor’s yard—mowing, hauling firewood—or I head down to the town’s bar for a cheap beer and a game of darts. I don’t drive much beyond the 30‑minute radius around my job site, and most of my free time is spent watching TV or scrolling through the feeds on the outlets I trust.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because the candidate promised to keep taxes low, protect the border, and keep the construction contracts flowing without a lot of red‑tape. I felt like the Democrats were always talking about climate policies that would shut down road work and raise my cost of living, and that just didn’t make sense for someone who’s trying to keep a roof over my mom’s head. The campaign ads on the radio and the flyers the crew handed out at lunch convinced me that a strong‑hand approach was what Arizona needed. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have affected my paycheck. The inflation spike last year made grocery prices jump, and the new federal guidelines on labor wages have made my crew’s overtime a bit tighter, so I’m a little more skeptical about the promises. Still, I think the GOP is the better bet for keeping my job stable and my taxes low. I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms—“I’m voting.” My biggest worries right now are the rent increase the trailer park announced for next year and the rising price of groceries; I’m also watching the local job market because if the highway projects get delayed, I could lose hours. I view the Democrats as a party that talks a lot about social programs but never seems to understand the day‑to‑day grind of folks like me. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting government waste and letting the private sector handle things, which feels more in line with how I try to live.
