## Background
I'm 65 and I work as a community health coordinator for the Blackfeet Nation's health department. I live in Great Falls, just a short drive from the reservation, in a modest ranch‑style house that I've owned for twenty‑something years. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up kids live out of state—one in Seattle, one in Denver—so most days I'm on my own, but I keep in touch with them on the phone almost every evening.

My day starts early. I get up around 5:30 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and read the headlines on my tablet while the kettle whistles. By 6:30 I’m on the road, heading to the tribal clinic where I spend the morning checking in patients, filling out paperwork, and coordinating telehealth appointments. Around noon I grab a quick sandwich from the diner down the street and chat with the nurse about the upcoming flu‑shot clinic. Afternoons are usually filled with meetings—sometimes with tribal council members about grant applications, other times with local nonprofits trying to set up nutrition programs. I try to squeeze in a walk along the Missouri River when the weather's nice; the river's been a constant backdrop my whole life, and it helps me clear my head after a long day of paperwork. Evenings are my quiet time: I cook dinner, watch a bit of the news, and then read a chapter from whatever book I'm halfway through. On weekends I help out at the cultural center, teach a class on traditional herbal remedies, and occasionally drive out to the mountains for a day of fishing with friends.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party's stance on energy development and personal responsibility lined up with what I see as necessary for my community. The oil and gas projects out west keep a lot of jobs afloat, and I appreciated the candidate's promise to keep federal regulations from choking off those opportunities. I also like the emphasis on Second Amendment rights—hunting is part of my culture, and I want to make sure that stays protected.

Since then, I've watched the fallout from a few policy moves that hurt tribal health funding, and it's left a sour taste. Still, I think the Democrats' push for aggressive climate policies would just push more mining jobs out of Montana without giving us a realistic transition plan. So, I'm planning to vote again in the 2026 midterms; I don't want to sit this one out unless something truly shakes me up.

My top everyday concerns are keeping health care affordable for the elders on the reservation and making sure the cost of groceries doesn't keep rising faster than wages. I see the Republican Party as the one that mostly understands the need for jobs in our resource‑based economy, even if they sometimes gloss over tribal sovereignty issues. The Democrats, in my view, talk a good game about climate and social justice, but I worry they don't get how those policies play out on the ground here—especially when they come with more regulations that could hurt the very people they're trying to help.

## Media Diet
I usually catch the morning broadcast on CBS while I sip coffee, then later in the evening I scroll through ABC and NPR articles on my tablet. It adds up to about ten hours a week between TV and online reading.
