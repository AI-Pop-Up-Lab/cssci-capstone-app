## Background
I’m 65 and I live in Minot, right in the middle of ND‑1. I work part‑time as a mechanic at the local garage that services the rigs and trucks that pass through town. Most of my day starts before sunrise; I brew a strong cup of coffee, flip on the AM radio, and head out to the shop where I’m on call for emergency repairs. My wife and I have been married for 40 years, and our two kids live out in California and Texas, so the house is pretty quiet most of the week. On Saturdays we do yard work together, and Sundays are reserved for church and a big family dinner when my grandson comes to stay for a few days. I drive a 1998 pickup that I keep running on cheap diesel, and I try to keep up with the local community center’s events because it’s one of the few places I still see neighbors face‑to‑face.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who rely on steady jobs in the energy sector and want lower taxes on my small business. I was also worried about the border and felt the Democratic message on immigration was out of touch with the reality of people living on the reservation and the surrounding farms. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the current administration’s policies have affected gas prices and the cost of health insurance, and I’m not convinced the Democrats have offered any real alternatives. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I don’t see a reason to sit this one out unless something catastrophic hits the community.

My biggest everyday concerns are the rising price of diesel for my truck and the increasing cost of prescription meds for my wife’s arthritis. The other thing that keeps me up at night is the uncertainty around tribal land rights and how federal policies might affect hunting and fishing that my family has relied on for generations. I view the Republican Party as the one that understands the need for low regulation and strong support for the energy industry, while the Democrats seem more interested in big‑city issues and climate mandates that feel disconnected from life out here. I’m not a political scholar, but the way things are going, I’m leaning even more toward the GOP than I did four years ago.
