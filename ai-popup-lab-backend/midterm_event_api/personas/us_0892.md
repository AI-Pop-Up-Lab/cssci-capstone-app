## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Roanoke, right in Virginia's 9th District. I grew up in a farming community in the Southwest, learned some welding and electrical work at a community college, and spent most of my adult life as a maintenance supervisor at a paper mill that shut down a few years back. I retired when I turned 68, but I still tinker with the old pickup in the garage and help my neighbor fix his broken fence when he asks. My wife, Rosa, is 81; we’ve been together for nearly 60 years, and we still keep a small garden where we grow tomatoes, peppers, and a few herbs. Our three kids live in different states, and we get a video call from at least one of them every Sunday after church. I get up around 6 a.m., make a strong coffee, and spend an hour reading the paper—mostly the local section, but I flip to the national pages for headlines. After breakfast, I walk around the block, sometimes stop by the corner store for a loaf of bread, and then settle in front of the TV for the morning news. In the afternoon I either nap, work on the garden, or help my grandson with his homework when he visits. Evenings are for watching a game or the nightly news, and I try to be in bed by 9 p.m. so I’m not too tired for the next day.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who rely on a fixed Social Security check and a modest pension. The candidate promised to keep Medicare cuts off the table and talked about protecting the border, which mattered to me after hearing about illegal crossings near the southern states. I also liked the emphasis on lower taxes; I don’t want the government taking more of the little savings I have left. The Democratic side seemed more focused on big‑city issues and wanted to raise taxes to fund programs that don’t affect my daily life. That felt like a gamble I wasn’t willing to take.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely voting. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that my prescription drug prices keep creeping up. I also keep an eye on property taxes; the county assessor sent out a notice that my home’s value might go up, which would mean a higher bill I can’t afford. I’m still leaning Republican because they talk about “keeping the tax burden low” and “protecting seniors.” The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free college and massive infrastructure projects but never seem to address the day‑to‑day squeeze on retirees. I’m not a political scholar; I just want the next few years to be stable enough that I can keep my garden alive and not have to choose between my meds and the gas for the car.
