## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a small house on the edge of Warsaw, Indiana, right in the 9th district. I spent 35 years teaching elementary school after taking a few courses at the local community college, but I retired five years ago when my husband passed. My two kids live in Indianapolis and Chicago, so most days the house is quiet except for the garden I tend in the backyard. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and flip through the local paper while the radio plays classic country. After breakfast I usually walk the dog, a rescued mutt named Buster, around the neighborhood. Once a week I volunteer at the senior center, helping with bingo and reading to the newcomers. In the afternoons I tend to my tomatoes and sweet potatoes, and sometimes I video‑chat with my grandchildren while they show me their latest video game scores. Evenings are for TV: I watch the nightly news, catch a sitcom, and then head to bed around 10 p.m. I keep a modest budget, mainly paying my mortgage, property taxes, and a modest prescription drug plan.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for my wallet. The rising cost of my insulin and the fact that my pension seemed to be squeezed by new taxes made me lean toward candidates who promised lower taxes and fewer regulations on small businesses like the hardware store down the road. I also liked the talk about securing the border and protecting traditional values; it felt like the Democrats were more interested in big‑city issues that never touched my life.  

Now, heading into the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns are the price of prescription drugs and the property tax hike the county proposed last spring. I also worry about whether there will be enough local jobs for the younger folks who might want to stay in the area. I see the Democratic Party as out of touch, always pushing for higher taxes and social programs that end up costing me more. The Republicans, for all their squabbles, still seem to be the ones promising to keep taxes low and to protect the kind of community I grew up in. I’m not thrilled with every candidate they put forward, but at least they talk about the things that matter to me.

## Media Diet  
I spend about eight hours a week on the news, mostly in the mornings while I eat and again in the evenings after dinner. I get most of my national coverage from Breitbart, CBS News, and NBC News, scrolling through their websites or listening to their podcasts while I garden.
