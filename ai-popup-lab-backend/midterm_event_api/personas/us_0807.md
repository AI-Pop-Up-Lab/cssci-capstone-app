## Background  
I'm 55, and I live in the outskirts of Rapid City, just a short drive from the Pine Ridge reservation. I work full‑time as a community health aide at the tribal clinic, helping folks schedule appointments, fill out paperwork, and sometimes just listening when someone needs to vent. I finished a couple of years of community college but never earned a degree, so I learned most of what I need on the job.  

My husband, who retired from the local fire department a few years ago, spends his days tinkering in the garage and hunting when the season opens. Our daughter moved to Sioux Falls after college; we still get her on the phone every Sunday for a quick chat about her new job in tech. Most mornings I grab a coffee, drive the 30 minutes to the clinic, and spend my day moving between exam rooms and the small office where I keep the clinic’s supply closet. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes stop at the bingo hall where my friends from the tribal senior center gather. On weekends I help my husband with a few extra chores around the house and we try to get out to the Black Hills for a hike or a drive when the weather’s good.  

## Politics and this election  
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about the real issues on the reservation—like the understaffed health clinic, the lack of clean water, and the constant fight over sovereign rights. I was busy with extra shifts that fall when the flu hit the community hard, and the whole process just seemed like a far‑away thing that wouldn't change my daily grind. The political ads felt like they were talking about national headlines, not the potholes on our roads or the rising cost of insulin.  

That feeling hasn't really shifted. I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic happens—like a major cut to tribal health funding or a local scandal that hits close to home—I don't see a reason to waste my time filling out a ballot. My biggest worries right now are keeping my health insurance steady and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing; everything else feels secondary.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats toss out a few promises about tribal sovereignty, but when it comes to actually funding the clinics they say they support, the money never shows up. The Republicans shout about oil, guns, and cutting taxes, but they also push for policies that threaten sacred lands and make it harder for us to protect our water sources. Neither side seems to get the nuance of what life is like for a Native family trying to make ends meet in a place that’s already fighting for basic services.  

## Media Diet  
I spend about seven hours a week scrolling through NPR in the mornings while I’m driving, and I catch the evening briefs on ABC News and CBS News after dinner. On weekends I skim a few articles on Newsweek to see what the national conversation looks like.
