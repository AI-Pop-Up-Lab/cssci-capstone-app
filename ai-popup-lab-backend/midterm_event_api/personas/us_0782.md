## Background  
I'm 50, and I live in Fort Mill, just outside Charlotte in the 5th district. I work full‑time as a senior research analyst for a regional health‑care system, crunching data on patient outcomes and writing reports for the board. My husband runs a small landscaping business, so we both have fairly flexible schedules, but the kids—17 and 14—keep the house busy. Mornings are a blur of coffee, a quick scan of the news on my phone, and dropping the kids at the high school and middle school bus stops. I drive about 30 minutes each way to the office, listening to NPR podcasts or the morning ABC News briefing. Evenings are usually spent cooking dinner, helping the younger one with algebra, and catching up on a favorite Korean drama when I can steal a half‑hour. On weekends I volunteer at the local library's adult literacy program and help my husband with a few landscaping jobs for extra cash. We own our house, which means I'm constantly juggling property‑tax statements, a mortgage, and the rising cost of groceries.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one pushing hard enough on health‑care affordability and protecting funding for public schools—issues that hit my family directly. The candidate's stance on expanding Medicaid in South Carolina mattered a lot to me, since many of my patients rely on that coverage. I also liked the emphasis on climate resilience; the flood‑prone areas near the Catawba River have been a growing worry. Since then, I've kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I've seen some promises stall, I still think the Democrats are the better bet for my priorities.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising property taxes that are squeezing our household budget and the steady climb in grocery prices, especially fresh produce. I also worry about the local job market for younger workers; my son is about to graduate high school and I want to see decent entry‑level opportunities in the area. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the more pragmatic side on health and education, even if they're sometimes slow on the ground. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they're more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing policies that ignore the day‑to‑day struggles of middle‑class families like mine. I get frustrated when political talk turns into shouting matches instead of concrete solutions, and I tend to tune out a lot of the partisan drama.
