## Background
I'm 75 and I live in a modest ranch house on the outskirts of Cheyenne, right in the heart of WY‑1. I grew up in a small immigrant community in Wyoming, and after high school I took a few courses at the community college but never finished a degree. I spent most of my adult life working as a school aide in the local elementary school; I retired ten years ago but still help out part‑time with after‑school tutoring. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in Denver and Salt Lake City, so most days I’m on my own. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of tea, and read the local paper while the radio plays NPR. After a light breakfast I tend to my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few hardy flowers. In the afternoons I volunteer at the senior center, playing cards with other retirees and helping with the weekly bingo. Evenings are for a short walk around the block, then I sit at the kitchen table and watch the news on my tablet before heading to bed around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security, Medicare, and affordable prescription drugs—things that matter a lot when you’re on a fixed income. I also liked the candidate’s promise to push for better broadband in rural areas; my grandkids keep complaining they can’t video‑chat reliably. That vote felt like a small way to protect the safety net that’s kept me afloat after my husband’s pension stopped. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on the political chatter, but I’m not glued to every debate. The Republican talks about cutting taxes sound good on paper, but I worry they’ll trim programs that help seniors. The Democrats, on the other hand, sometimes talk big about climate and student debt, which feels far from my world, but they’ve at least stood up for Medicare expansions.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription medications and the lack of reliable high‑speed internet for my home office when I do my tutoring. I also keep thinking about property taxes; the county has been nudging rates up to fund new roads, and every extra dollar feels like a pinch on my modest pension. My view of the parties is pretty straightforward: I think the Democrats are more attentive to seniors and health care, even if they don’t always get the rural details right. The Republicans seem focused on cutting taxes for businesses and reducing regulation, which sounds good for the economy but often leaves folks like me feeling ignored. I’m not a political scholar, just someone trying to keep the lights on and the doctor’s visits affordable.
