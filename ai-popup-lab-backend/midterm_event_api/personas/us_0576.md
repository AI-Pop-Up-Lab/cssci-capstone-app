## Background
I'm 20 and I live in a small apartment on the edge of Grand Island, right in Nebraska's 3rd district. I'm a sophomore at the University of Nebraska at Kearney, majoring in business administration. My parents moved here from Texas when I was a kid, so most of my Spanish is spoken at home, but I barely use it outside the house. I work a night shift at a local warehouse—packing and loading trucks—so I usually get home around 2 a.m. and try to squeeze in a couple of hours of classwork before I crash. My roommate, Jake, is an engineering major from Omaha; we share the rent and split groceries, which mostly means cheap ramen, frozen pizza, and the occasional taco night when I can get my mom to send a box of tortillas.

My daily routine is pretty set: I wake up at 9 a.m., scroll through my phone for a bit, then head to campus for a 10 a.m. lecture. After class I hit the library for a couple of hours, then swing by the grocery store to pick up milk and beans before the night shift starts at 7 p.m. On weekends I sometimes help my dad with the family’s landscaping side‑gig, or I meet up with friends at the local bar to watch the Broncos. I don’t drive much beyond the county roads, but when I do, gas prices are a constant headache.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s talk about lower taxes and less regulation lined up with my own worries about paying rent on a student budget. I also liked the candidate’s promise to keep the oil and agricultural subsidies that help my family’s small farm back in Texas. I didn’t feel like the Democrats cared about a kid trying to finish school without drowning in debt, so I went with the GOP ticket.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening in Lincoln and at the state capitol, but I’m still mostly tuned into how national headlines affect my wallet. The cost of gas went up again last summer, and my part‑time job barely covers the rent, so I’m still convinced the Republicans are the only ones who talk about “keeping more of my paycheck.” I’m not a big fan of the Democrats’ push for higher minimum wages; it sounds good in theory, but I worry about businesses cutting hours or closing, which would hit people like my dad who run a small operation.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure gas stays cheap enough that I can get to work and school. I see the Republican Party as the one that actually talks about cutting taxes and limiting government overreach, even if they sometimes sound a little too “big‑government” on social issues. The Democrats, in my view, are more focused on identity politics and climate talk that feels disconnected from the day‑to‑day grind of a college kid working a night shift.
