## Background
I'm 30 and I work as a warehouse picker at a distribution center just outside Providence. I grew up in East Providence, in a small apartment with my mom, my teenage sister, and my grandma who still cooks the best arroz con pollo in the building. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I picked up odd jobs—pizza delivery, landscaping—and eventually landed the warehouse gig because it pays steady overtime. My day starts at 5 a.m.; I catch the early bus, grab a coffee from a corner cart, and spend eight hours on the floor moving pallets, scanning tags, and hauling boxes. After my shift I head home, help my sister with her homework, and try to squeeze in a quick workout at the community gym before we all sit down for dinner. On weekends I work a second shift if the boss needs extra hands, or I help my mom with errands at the local market. Money's tight, so we split the rent and utilities, and every few months we have to decide whether to keep the fridge running or pay for the internet. I watch the local kids play soccer in the park when I can, but most evenings I'm just scrolling on my phone before I fall asleep.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about people like me who are just trying to pay the rent and keep a roof over our heads. The whole process seemed like a circus—ads promising big changes, then a flood of empty promises. I work long hours, and the polls are open at inconvenient times; by the time I got home, I was exhausted and didn't think my vote mattered anyway. I also heard a lot of talk about voter fraud and felt like the system is rigged, so I just tuned out. That same feeling is still with me now, so I'm sitting this 2026 midterm out too, unless something dramatic happens locally—like a sudden closure of the warehouse or a major price hike on basic groceries—that forces me to pay attention.

My biggest everyday worries are rent and grocery prices. The landlord just raised the lease by $150, and the price of fresh produce keeps climbing, making it hard to feed my family without cutting back on other things. I also worry about the heat in the summer; the electric bill spikes when we have to run the air conditioner, and the utility company seems to keep raising rates.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as talkers who promise free college and health care but never deliver anything that helps a working‑class family. Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they're only interested in cutting taxes for the rich and ignoring the cost of living for folks like me. Both sides seem more focused on their own agendas than on solving the real problems I face every day. I might lean a little toward the Democrats because at least they talk about a minimum wage increase, but I’m not convinced they’ll follow through, and I don’t trust the GOP to care about my community.
