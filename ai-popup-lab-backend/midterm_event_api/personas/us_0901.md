## Background
I'm 30, and I live in St. Albans, a small town up near the Canadian border. I work full‑time at the local grocery store as a checkout supervisor; the job pays just enough to cover my rent on a modest two‑bedroom apartment I share with a roommate who does construction work. My parents live a couple of hours away on a farm, so I visit them on holidays and sometimes help out with the harvest, but most weeks I'm stuck in the town’s main street, stocking shelves and dealing with the occasional angry customer over price tags. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan that I bought used; it’s reliable enough for the short commute but always needs a bit of tinkering, which I try to handle myself to save money. In the evenings I usually unwind by watching a sitcom on the streaming service or scrolling through social media on my phone before heading to bed around 11 p.m. On weekends I either work a few extra shifts when the store is short‑staffed or go hiking on the nearby Green Mountain trail with a few friends, grabbing cheap coffee from the corner deli afterward.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working double shifts during the primaries, dealing with a busted car, and feeling like none of the candidates really understood what it’s like to scrape together a paycheck in a rural town, I just didn’t see the point. The whole process felt like a distant circus—lots of ads, heated debates on cable, and then nothing that touched my day‑to‑day worries about rent, grocery prices, and the lack of decent jobs in the area. I’m not surprised I’m sitting this one out, too; unless something blows up locally—like a major employer shutting down or a scandal that hits my community directly—I’m going to stay home on election day. My biggest everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries, which feels like a bigger bite than the modest rent I pay, and the fact that there just aren’t enough stable, well‑paying jobs around here for folks my age. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to promise big‑government fixes that never trickle down to a place like St. Albans, while the Republicans talk about cutting taxes but ignore the fact that many of us can’t even afford basic utilities. Both sides feel like they’re shouting at each other from opposite ends of a hallway, and I’m stuck in the middle, trying to keep the lights on.
