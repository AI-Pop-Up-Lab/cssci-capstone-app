## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Newburgh, right in the 3rd district of Indiana. I work full‑time as a maintenance hand at the local manufacturing plant that makes auto parts; the shift starts at 6 a.m., so I’m usually up before sunrise, making coffee and checking the radio for traffic. My wife and I have been married for 35 years; we both retired from farm work a decade ago, but she still tends a small garden behind the house. Our two kids are grown and live in Ohio and Texas, so most weekends are just us and the occasional visit from my mother, who needs help with her arthritis meds. I drive a beat‑up pickup, keep a toolbox in the back, and after the plant I sometimes pick up odd jobs—fixing fences, cleaning out barns—for extra cash. Evenings are simple: dinner, a few minutes of the news on the TV, then I either watch a game on the couch or sit on the porch and listen to the crickets while my wife knits. I don’t have a college degree; I left school after eighth grade to help my family make ends meet, and most of what I know is from hands‑on work and the folks I meet at the plant and around town.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long hours at the plant, caring for my mother’s appointments, and feeling like none of the candidates really understood what a 60‑year‑old maintenance worker in southwestern Indiana needs, I just shrugged it off. The whole process feels like a show that doesn’t change anything for folks like me, so I stayed home. I’m not planning to go to the polls for the 2026 midterms either—unless something blows up locally, like a factory closure or a major road project that hits our community hard. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription drugs and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. I also keep an eye on whether the plant will stay open; a layoff would hit us hard. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are all talk about climate and social programs but they never show up in places like Newburgh. The Republicans brag about “working class values,” but most of the time they’re more interested in tax cuts for big corporations than in the wages of people who keep the factories running. It feels like both sides are playing a game, and I’m just stuck holding the pieces.

## Media Diet
I usually catch NPR in the morning while I’m driving to work and scroll through CBS News and ABC News on my phone in the evenings after dinner, totaling about six hours a week.
