## Background  
I'm 40, and I live in a small ranching community just outside Cheyenne, still in the same ZIP code my parents grew up in. I work full‑time as a cash register clerk at the local grocery store, handling the lunch rush, stocking shelves, and sometimes covering the night shift when we’re short. My husband works on a nearby oil field, and we have two kids—12 and 8—who go to the public elementary school a few miles away. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 am, making coffee, getting the kids dressed, and dropping them at the bus stop before heading to my shift. After work I swing by the kids’ after‑school program, then help my husband with chores around the house—fixing the fence, hauling firewood, and sometimes loading the truck for his weekend trips. Weekends are a mix of grocery shopping at the big box store, a quick trip to the county fair, and catching a high‑school football game when the weather’s nice. I drive a used pickup, mostly because it can haul the propane tanks and the occasional hay bale. My family’s been here for three generations, so the community feels tight; most of my friends are also working‑class folks who know each other from school or the church basement.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long lines at the polling place, a work schedule that never seemed to line up with early voting, and the feeling that none of the candidates really talked about the problems that keep me up at night—like the rising cost of gasoline for the truck and the price of my kids’ school lunches—I just stayed home. My husband tried to convince me to cast a ballot, but his shift got moved at the last minute, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The whole process felt like a performance for the media rather than a genuine way to get things fixed.  

Now that the midterms are coming up, I’m still leaning toward sitting this one out. I’m not saying I’ll never vote, but unless a candidate actually shows up in my town, promises to keep the county road repairs on schedule, or talks straight about the health‑care costs that keep piling up, I don’t see a reason to waste the time. My top everyday concerns are keeping enough money in the budget to pay for gas and to cover the kids’ school supplies—everything else feels secondary.  

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that don’t translate to my life on the ranch. The Republicans, on paper, seem more aligned with my values, but I’m frustrated when they argue over national scandals while my local water well stays broken. Both sides get a lot of noise, and I’m left wondering who’s actually listening to folks like me who just want reliable roads, affordable fuel, and a decent health plan for the family.
