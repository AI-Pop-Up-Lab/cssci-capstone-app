## Background
I'm 25 and I work as a logistics coordinator at a warehouse just outside Reno. I got an associate’s degree in business administration from a community college, which helped me land the job after a couple of years stacking pallets. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in the Midtown area with a roommate who’s in school for nursing. My parents are still in California, so most weekends I’m either driving up to see them or scrolling through FaceTime when I can’t make the trip.

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. because I have to be on the floor by 7. I grab a coffee from the corner deli, check the schedule on my phone, and head to the loading dock. The shift runs until about 3 p.m., then I head to the gym for an hour—lifting and a quick treadmill session. After that I usually swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things, and come home to make a simple dinner, usually ramen or stir‑fry. Evenings are low‑key: I watch a couple of YouTube videos, play some video games, and try to get to bed by 11. On the occasional night I meet the roommate for a cheap pizza and a beer at a local bar. I don’t own a car, so I rely on rideshares when I need to go farther.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the choices mattered to me. The campaign ads were the same old promises, and I was working two jobs during the primaries, so I missed the early voting windows. By the time the general election rolled around, I was exhausted from a long shift and a weekend shift change, and the whole process just felt like a chore. I’ve got a pretty cynical view of the whole system; it seems like politicians are more interested in fundraising than solving the rent hikes and grocery price spikes that hit my paycheck hard every month.

I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic happens locally—like the warehouse I work at being threatened with closure, or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces me to move—I don’t see a reason to spend the time filling out a ballot. My top everyday concerns are keeping up with rent and making sure I can afford groceries without having to cut back on other bills. I also worry about the cost of health insurance if I ever need it.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about student debt relief and climate plans, but those feel distant from my day‑to‑day grind. The Republicans push for lower taxes, but the cuts never seem to reach people like me who are just trying to stay afloat. It feels like they’re both playing a game that leaves the middle class out of the picture, so I don’t trust either side to change that.
