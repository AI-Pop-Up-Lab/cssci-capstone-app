## Background
I'm 45 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Ashland, right outside Columbia, Missouri. I work the swing shift as a floor supervisor at the local distribution warehouse, making sure pallets get loaded and shipped out on time. My husband is a carpenter who takes odd jobs around the county, and we have two kids—Emily, 12, and Jacob, 9—who go to the public schools in the district. My day starts before sunrise; I get the kids ready, drop them off at the bus, then head to the warehouse where I spend eight hours on a conveyor belt and a laptop, checking inventory and dealing with paperwork. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up some milk and the kids' snack packs, and try to squeeze in a quick call with my sister in Kansas City. Evenings are usually spent helping with homework, watching a bit of TV, and trying to get a few hours of sleep before doing it all again. We own the house, so property taxes are a big line item, and we’re always watching the utility bills because they seem to creep up every winter.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus—candidates promising the moon, then disappearing once they got into office. Between my long shifts, the kids' soccer practice, and trying to keep the house from falling apart, I just couldn't find the time or the energy to get to the polls. I also think most of the big issues we care about—like the cost of groceries, the reliability of our local roads, and school funding—never get real attention from anyone in Washington. Because of that, I told myself I'd sit this one out too, unless something blew up in our community, like a major factory closing or a school budget crisis that directly hit my kids.

I'm definitely sitting the 2026 midterms out as well. My top everyday concerns are the rising price of food and the property tax hikes that keep eating into our paycheck. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to want more government control and higher taxes, while the Republicans talk tough on taxes but rarely follow through on the promises that would actually help a middle‑class family like mine. Both sides feel out of touch, so I'm not motivated to cast a ballot unless a candidate actually addresses the bills I bring home.

I usually catch the morning headlines on CBS while I’m getting coffee, then tune into NBC in the evening after the kids are in bed, and I skim a bit of ABC news on my phone when I have a spare moment. All together it adds up to about four hours a week.
