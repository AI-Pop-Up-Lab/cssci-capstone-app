## Background
I'm 55 and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Cedar Rapids, right where the cornfields start to give way to the suburbs. I'm a high‑school graduate and I've been working as a farm‑equipment mechanic for the past 30 years, mostly for a local John Deere dealer. My day starts around 5 a.m.; I grab a cup of coffee, check the weather on my old flip‑phone, and head out to the shop. The mornings are noisy—hydraulic lifts, the smell of diesel, and the occasional banter with the other guys about the latest football game.

I'm married to a woman who runs a small home‑based quilting business, and we have two grown children. One son lives a few hours away in Des Moines, the other is back home, helping out on the farm part‑time while he finishes an associate’s degree. On weekends we usually have a barbecue with the neighbors, or we take the kids’ dog out to the county fair if there’s one. I drive a beat‑up pickup I’ve had since the early 2000s, and I’m still trying to figure out how to get a better deal on my insurance. When I’m not at the shop, I’m either fixing a neighbor’s tractor, hunting deer in the state forest, or watching a game on the old TV in the living room.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party understood what folks like me needed: lower taxes, less regulation on farm equipment, and a tougher stance on immigration that kept my community’s jobs safe. The candidate’s promises to protect the agricultural subsidies and keep the price of diesel down resonated with my daily grind. I also liked the way the Republican campaign talked about “common‑sense” solutions instead of “big‑government” fixes.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax hikes the county has been pushing. When the price at the pump climbs, it eats right into my paycheck, and the property tax increase means I’m paying more for the same piece of land that’s already struggling with lower corn yields. I’m also watching the debate over crop insurance; any change that makes it harder for us to get a safety net feels like a direct hit.

I see the Democrats as out of touch with rural life. They keep talking about climate policies that sound good on paper but end up making my equipment more expensive or limiting how I can use my land. The Republicans, at least, seem to remember that a lot of us are just trying to keep the lights on and the tractors running. I’m not a political scholar, but I’m fed up with the endless bickering and would rather have someone who actually visits a farm and knows what a broken combine means for a family’s income.
