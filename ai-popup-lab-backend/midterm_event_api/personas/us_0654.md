## Background  
I'm 49 and I live in a modest rental house on the edge of North Las Vegas, in Nevada's 4th congressional district. I work full‑time as a maintenance supervisor for a regional grocery chain; the job is mostly hands‑on, fixing refrigeration units, handling the nightly cleaning crew, and making sure the stores stay open. I didn't go to college – I got my high school diploma, learned a lot on the job, and have been at the company for about 20 years.  

My wife, Carla, works part‑time as a dental hygienist at a clinic downtown. We have two kids: Maya, 16, who’s in her junior year at a public high school, and Luis, 12, who goes to middle school. Weekdays are a blur of early‑morning shifts, dropping the kids off at school, a quick coffee at a fast‑food place, and then a long day of fixing broken freezers and dealing with inventory issues. Evenings are usually spent at the kitchen table helping Maya with algebra homework while Luis does his science project. On weekends we try to get out to the nearby Red Rock Canyon for a hike when the heat isn’t too bad, but most of the time it’s just grocery runs, car maintenance, and catching up on a TV show or two.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on health care and education mattered for my family. My sister’s battle with a chronic illness made me appreciate the push for expanding Medicaid, and Maya’s upcoming college tuition worries made the promise of more affordable student loans feel relevant. I also liked the candidate’s focus on water conservation – something that’s becoming a real headache here in Nevada.  

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on the news and the local debates. The rising cost of rent and the ever‑increasing electric bills are my top everyday concerns now. My paycheck feels tighter each month, and I’m watching the utility company’s rate hikes with growing anxiety. The 2026 midterms are shaping up to be about who will actually get something done on those issues, so I’m definitely going to the polls. I’m not a political junkie, but when I hear about a candidate promising tax relief for working families or concrete plans to keep water rates from spiraling, I pay attention.  

I’m still skeptical of both parties. The Republicans seem focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing for deregulation that feels like it could hurt the environment – not great for a state that already worries about drought. The Democrats, on the other hand, talk a lot about social programs but sometimes feel disconnected from the everyday grind of people like me who have to juggle two jobs and a household. Still, I think they’re more likely to protect the safety net I rely on, so I’m leaning Democratic again, though I’m keeping an eye on any candidate who can actually lower my utility bill.
