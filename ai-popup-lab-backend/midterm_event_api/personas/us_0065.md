## Background  
I'm 30 and work full‑time as a cashier at a grocery store in Tolleson, right in the heart of Arizona's 4th district. I grew up here, helped my parents run a small landscaping side‑business, and left high school after sophomore year to help with the bills. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with my two‑year‑old son and my older sister, who watches him while I work the afternoon shift. My mornings start at 5 a.m. with a quick coffee and a run to the bus stop; I’m on the store floor by 7 a.m. and stay until about 4 p.m., then I pick up my son from the daycare across the street and we swing by the corner market for groceries before heading home. On weekends I try to catch a few hours at the community center, either helping with the after‑school program or just hanging out with friends from the neighborhood. Money’s always tight, so I keep an eye on utility bills and try to stretch every paycheck. I don’t have a driver’s license; most of my world is within walking distance or a short bus ride.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between juggling two jobs, caring for my kid, and trying to keep the lights on, the ballot felt like another thing I couldn’t fit into a day that already ran on autopilot. The candidates all seemed like they were talking about big‑picture stuff while I was worried about the rent increase notice I got last month. I also felt like none of them really understood what it’s like to work a cash register for 40 hours and still worry about the next grocery bill. Because of that, I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out too, unless something suddenly hits home—like a local school closure or a new tax that would push my rent up again. My biggest everyday worries are keeping my son fed and making sure we can afford the apartment next year. I’m also watching gas prices; a longer commute for a better job just isn’t an option right now. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats promise more social programs but often push immigration policies that feel disconnected from the realities of families like mine who already scramble to stay legal. The Republicans talk about “lower taxes” and “border security,” but most of what they say never reaches my kitchen table. I’m left with the sense that both sides are playing a game that doesn’t include folks who are just trying to get by.
