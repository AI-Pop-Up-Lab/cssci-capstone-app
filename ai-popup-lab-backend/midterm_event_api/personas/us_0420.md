## Background
I'm 65 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Bangor, right in the heart of Maine's 1st congressional district. After a stint in the Navy I went to community college, took a few technical courses, and spent most of my working life as a mechanic at a local auto shop. I retired a couple of years ago, but I still pull a few shifts every month because the extra cash helps cover the rising cost of my prescription meds. My wife passed away five years back, so I mostly keep company with my two grown‑up kids who live in Portland and Boston. On weekdays I wake up early, brew a strong cup of coffee, and sit on the porch watching the sunrise over the Penobscot River while I scroll through the news on my tablet. After a light breakfast I head to the shop for a half‑day shift, then come home to tend to my garden, do a bit of woodworking, or meet up with a few old buddies at the local VFW hall for a game of poker. Weekends are for fishing trips on the lake, fixing up the old pickup, and checking in with my children over video calls. I drive a reliable but worn‑out Chevrolet, keep a library of paperback mysteries, and try to stay active by walking the nearby trails whenever the weather allows.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attuned to the issues that hit me daily—especially health care and the push for better broadband in rural Maine. The promise to protect Medicare and to fund the expansion of high‑speed internet to places like my neighborhood convinced me that a Democrat would fight for the kind of safety net I need as I get older. I also liked the candidate’s stance on protecting our forests and fisheries, which are the backbone of the local economy. Over the past two years I’ve watched the rollout of those broadband projects lag behind schedule, and the cost of my insulin has kept creeping up, so my confidence in the party’s promises has wavered a bit. Still, I think the alternative—seeing a Republican win who wants to cut Medicare and roll back environmental regulations—would be a step backward for me and my community. That’s why I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms; I’m not a fan of sitting this one out unless something truly shakes my confidence.

My top everyday concerns right now are the price of prescription drugs and the property tax hike the town council is proposing to fund a new water treatment plant. I’m also worried about whether the local lumber mill will stay open, because that’s the biggest employer for a lot of folks I know. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as the more pragmatic side on health and climate, even if they sometimes overpromise. The Republicans feel like they’re more interested in tax cuts for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that actually protect our way of life. I’m not thrilled with either, but the Democrats feel a little closer to my needs.
