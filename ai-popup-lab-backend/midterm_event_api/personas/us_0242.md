## Background
I'm 55 and I live in Nampa, a quiet suburb just outside Boise in Idaho's 1st district. I work as a dental hygienist at a busy practice downtown, usually putting in two shifts a week and picking up extra appointments when the clinic needs help. My husband does construction work, and we both grew up in different parts of the country before meeting at college; I only completed a couple of years of community college before taking the hygienist certification. Our two kids are out of the house—one is a software tech in Seattle, the other is finishing grad school in Portland—so the house feels a lot emptier these days. I still help my parents with a few errands; they live in a small town about an hour away, and I drive up on weekends when I can. My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with coffee, a quick scan of the headlines on my phone, then a 30‑minute drive to the clinic. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes attend a community potluck at the local Buddhist temple. Evenings are usually spent on the couch watching a bit of TV or scrolling through the news sites I trust. On Sundays I try to get outside for a short hike on the foothills before heading back to the house for a family video call.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best shot at keeping taxes low and protecting my husband’s job in construction. The talk about cutting red tape and letting small businesses breathe resonated with me, especially after watching the price of groceries climb higher each month. I also liked the tougher stance on immigration; it felt like a way to keep our community safe and jobs secure. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have played out locally. The economy feels a little steadier, but I’m still worried about health‑care costs—my dental practice doesn’t cover everything, and my parents’ meds are getting pricier. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out because every vote feels like a small way to push back against rising living costs. My top everyday concerns are grocery prices and health‑care expenses, especially with my parents getting older. I see the Republican Party as the one that mostly looks out for my wallet, even if some of the rhetoric gets a bit harsh. The Democrats, in my view, focus too much on cultural issues and seem out of touch with the practical problems families like mine face in a smaller‑town Idaho setting.
