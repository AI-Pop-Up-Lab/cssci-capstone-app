## Background  
I'm 80, and I live in a modest bungalow on the outskirts of Charlottesville, right in the heart of VA‑7. I spent most of my adult life working as a certified nursing assistant after finishing a two‑year program at a community college in Richmond. When I retired ten years ago I kept a part‑time gig at the local senior center, helping folks with meals and mobility exercises. My husband passed away five years back, so it's just me, my son who lives in Baltimore, and two grandkids who visit every summer.  

My days start early. I brew a cup of sweet tea, sit on the porch, and scroll through NPR on my tablet while the birds wake up. After a light breakfast I head to the senior center for a few hours of activity planning, then I’m back home for lunch. In the afternoons I either tend to my garden—tomatoes and okra are my pride—or I take a slow walk around the neighborhood with my old Labrador, Marley. Evenings are for dinner with a TV on; I usually catch the ABC Nightline or a CBS news recap before I wind down with a mystery novel. Sundays are reserved for church, potluck lunches, and a quick check‑in with my sister over the phone.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate talked about protecting Medicare and expanding prescription‑drug coverage, which matters a lot to someone my age. I also liked the promise of more funding for community health clinics, something I see the need for every day at the senior center. The campaign felt like it was listening to folks like me who rely on fixed incomes and government programs.  

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely heading to the polls—I'm voting. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the looming cuts to property taxes that could affect my home’s assessed value. The other big thing is my health care: I’m on a few chronic meds, and any change to Medicare or drug pricing hits me hard. I’m pretty fed up with the Republicans; they keep talking about “small government” while trying to trim the very programs that keep seniors afloat. The Democrats aren’t perfect either—sometimes they get tangled up in internal fights and don’t always deliver on promises—but at least they seem to care about social safety nets. I don’t have the energy to follow every policy detail, so I mostly go with the party that looks like it’s standing up for my pocketbook and my health.
