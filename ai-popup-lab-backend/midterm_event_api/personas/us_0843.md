## Background  
I'm 50, and I work as an administrative assistant at the regional hospital in Longview, Texas, right in the 32nd district. My husband runs a small landscaping business out of our house, and we have two kids—Mia, 16, and Alex, 14—who are both in high school. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m. to get a quick coffee, check the news on my phone, and catch the 6 a.m. train to the hospital. The job is mostly paperwork, scheduling doctors, and handling insurance forms, so I spend a lot of time looking at spreadsheets and answering phones. After my shift ends at 3 p.m., I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids, and we head home for dinner. Evenings are a mix of helping with homework, a little bit of housework, and trying to squeeze in a TV show before we all crash. On weekends we usually do yard work for the husband’s clients, attend a church service on Sunday morning, and sometimes meet up with a few friends at the local mall. I’m not a big traveler; most of my life revolves around the hospital, the kids’ school, and the small community events in town.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the endless shift at the hospital, the kids’ soccer practices, and trying to keep up with rising rent and medical bills, I just felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything. The campaign ads were all the same—big promises, no real solutions for people like me who are juggling work and family. I’m pretty cynical about the whole system; it feels like politicians talk to each other more than to folks in places like Longview. For the 2026 midterms, I’m sitting this one out again. Unless something truly blows up locally—like a hospital funding crisis that hits my coworkers directly—I don’t see a reason to carve out the time. My biggest everyday worries are the grocery prices that keep creeping up and the property taxes that make it harder to keep our house. I also worry about health‑care costs, especially with my husband’s business not having good insurance. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as a lot of talk about social issues but not enough on the ground‑level stuff that affects small‑town families. Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing culture‑war rhetoric that feels disconnected from the real problems of paying bills and keeping a hospital staffed. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the day‑to‑day grind we live through.

## Media Diet
