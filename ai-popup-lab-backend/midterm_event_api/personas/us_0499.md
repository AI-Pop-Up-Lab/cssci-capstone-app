## Background
I'm 80, and I live in the west side of Jackson, right near the old train tracks that run by the community college. I spent most of my adult life working as a maintenance supervisor at the technical college after I earned my associate degree in industrial mechanics. I retired about six years ago when the college closed the workshop I ran, so now my days are a lot slower. My wife passed five years ago, and I have two grown kids—one in Birmingham and the other in Memphis—who call every few weeks. My grandson comes down from Atlanta every summer and helps me tend the small garden I keep behind the house; we grow collard greens and tomatoes, and I swear the soil there is the only thing that still feels like work.

My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee and the local radio preacher, then I shuffle over to the senior center for bingo or the church basement for the weekly Bible study. Afternoons are for watching the news on the TV in the living room while I fix a leaky faucet or sort through the mail. I drive my old sedan to the grocery store on Main Street a couple of times a week, and on Sundays I sit in the pews at the historically Black church down the block. I try to stay active, but at my age I’m careful not to overdo it.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Honestly, by the time the primaries rolled around I was already feeling like the whole process was a show put on for people like me who have lived through far more than any campaign promises. The polling places were far, the lines were long, and the candidates all seemed to be talking past each other about issues that never touched my day‑to‑day life—like tech regulation or overseas trade deals. I figured my vote wouldn't change anything, especially when I could barely get out of the house without a cane and a dose of pain medication. That feeling of cynicism stuck, and unless something hits close to home—like a local school closing or a sudden hike in property taxes—I don't see myself heading to the polls again.

So for the 2026 midterms I'm sitting this one out. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that the local utility keeps raising rates on electricity, which makes it harder to keep the house warm in winter. I also worry about the state’s pension system; a lot of my friends from the union are seeing their retirement checks shrink, and I’m not sure who’s looking out for us.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they care a bit more about social programs that could help seniors, but I’m tired of the same old talking points and suspect they’re more interested in winning votes than fixing the broken infrastructure in my neighborhood. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep promising lower taxes and less government, but every time a new tax bill comes through it seems to hit the poor and retirees hardest. I don’t have a lot of faith in either side; I just want someone to actually listen to the folks who have been here for decades and make sure the roads are paved and the power stays on.

## Media Diet
I usually catch NPR in the morning while I sip coffee, then flip to CBS News or ABC News in the evening after dinner. I spend about five hours a week scrolling through their websites or watching the broadcast clips.
