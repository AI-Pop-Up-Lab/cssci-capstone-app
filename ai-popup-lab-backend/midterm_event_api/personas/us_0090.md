## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Bakersfield, right in the middle of California's 22nd district. I worked most of my adult life as a dental hygienist at a community health clinic; the associate degree I earned at a local community college got me the license I needed. I retired five years ago, but I still fill in a few shifts when the clinic is short‑staffed because I like the routine and the people.  

My husband passed away three years back, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids who live in the Valley and in San Diego. They visit every other weekend with their kids, and the house fills with the smell of tamales and the sound of Spanish‑language cartoons. I belong to the parish down the street and help run the senior‑sunday bingo nights. Most mornings I water my garden—tomatoes, peppers, a few citrus trees—that's my excuse to get outside. After breakfast I sit at the kitchen table with a mug of coffee and scroll through the news before heading out to the clinic or the grocery store. Evenings are usually a quiet TV show or a phone call with my daughter; I try to keep the house tidy for when the grandkids swing by.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about my Medicare, my prescription drug costs, and the immigration policies that affect my extended family back in Mexico. The candidate from the other side kept talking about cutting taxes, but I worry that any cut to social programs would hit seniors like me the hardest. I also liked the way the Democratic candidate talked about funding community health centers—places like the one I worked at.  

I'm definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the property tax hike the county approved last year. If the next round of lawmakers doesn't protect Medicare or at least keep drug prices from spiraling, I'm going to be really upset. I also keep an eye on the local job market because my kids are still looking for stable work, and a lot of the younger families in the neighborhood are getting squeezed out.  

When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as the more compassionate side, especially on health care and social safety nets. The Republicans, in my view, talk a lot about "personal responsibility," but that feels like a shrug when they want to cut programs that keep people afloat. I don't trust either side to be perfect, but I feel the Democrats are at least trying to address the things that matter to older folks and immigrant families. That said, I'm not thrilled about the endless political drama; sometimes I just tune out and focus on my garden.

## Media Diet  
I start my day with NPR's morning news while I sip coffee, then catch the evening headlines on ABC News around dinner time. On weekends I skim CNBC for any updates on the stock market and the economy.
