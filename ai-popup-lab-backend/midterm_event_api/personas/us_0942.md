## Background
I'm 35 and I work full‑time as a customer service representative for a regional cable provider. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the west side of Eau Claire, just a few blocks from the river trail where I like to jog when the weather's nice. My mom still lives about an hour north in a small town, so I drive up to help her with groceries every other weekend. I don't have kids yet, but I share my place with a rescued Labrador named Scout, who keeps me on my toes when I'm getting ready for work.

My day starts at 5:30 a.m. with a quick cup of cheap coffee from the corner diner, then a 20‑minute drive to the office where I spend most of my day on the phone, sorting out service outages and billing questions. Lunch is usually a sandwich I bring from home, eaten at my desk while scrolling through the news on my phone. After my shift ends at 4 p.m., I either run errands—like picking up groceries or refilling my prescription—or head to the gym for a short workout. Evenings are low‑key: I cook something simple, watch a bit of TV, and try to catch up on a show or two before bed. On weekends I sometimes help a neighbor with yard work or meet up with a few friends at the local brewery for a cheap pint.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I was hoping the party would protect my health insurance and keep tuition costs down for my younger sister, who’s still in community college. I also liked the idea of more funding for public schools, since I grew up in a district that got cutbacks. After the election, I felt a mix of relief that the president stayed in office and frustration that the promises on the campaign trail haven’t really turned into anything concrete for folks like me. The economy feels shaky, and I keep hearing about the same “big‑ticket” issues that never seem to affect my paycheck.

I'm voting this time around. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that my rent keeps creeping up faster than my wages. I also worry about property taxes in the area; my landlord keeps talking about needing to raise the rent to cover higher assessments. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who at least talk about health care and education, even if they’re slow to act. Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re more interested in tax cuts for the wealthy and pushing cultural battles that don’t help my day‑to‑day life. I’m not a political scholar, I just want policies that keep the lights on and food on the table.
