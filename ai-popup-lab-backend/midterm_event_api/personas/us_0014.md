## Background
I'm 55 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Fairbanks, in the heart of Alaska's 1st congressional district. I work full‑time as a heavy‑equipment operator for the state highway department, driving a back‑hoe and doing snow‑clearing in the winter and road‑maintenance in the summer. My wife, who retired from the local school after 30 years, keeps the garden going as long as the weather lets her, and we have two grown kids who live out of state—one in Seattle and the other in Denver. Most mornings I get up before dawn, brew a strong cup of coffee, and head out to the depot where the crew meets. After a shift that runs from 6 a.m. to 2 p.m., I swing by the hardware store to pick up a few things for the house, then stop at the diner for a quick lunch. Evenings are usually spent watching the news on the TV in the living room, fixing anything that broke during the day, or chatting with my wife on the porch while the sun sets over the river. On weekends I help my nephew with his fishing charter, haul firewood, or drive out to the community center to volunteer for the local senior‑citizen program.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in the 2024 presidential election. I felt like the whole thing was a circus—campaign ads everywhere, promises that never seemed to stick, and none of the candidates really talked about the high cost of heating my house or the long haul to get groceries stocked when the supply chain hiccups hit. Between long shifts, family obligations, and the fact that the nearest polling place is an hour's drive away, I just let the day pass. I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything, so I stayed home.

I'm sitting this 2026 midterm out too, unless something blows up locally—like a sudden cut to the state road budget that would leave the winter roads unplowed, or a major refinery closure that would take away a lot of jobs. My biggest daily worries are the price of diesel for my truck and the grocery bills that keep creeping up; when fuel prices spike, everything else follows. I also worry about the reliability of the power grid during those long, dark winters.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as promising big‑government fixes that never arrive, and Republicans as talking about low taxes but not caring about remote places like mine where the cost of living is already high. Both sides seem to forget that most Alaskans here are just trying to stay warm and keep a roof over their heads. I don't feel a strong loyalty to either; I just want policies that keep the roads clear, the power on, and the price of food reasonable.

## Media Diet
