## Background
I'm 35 and I work full‑time as a stock clerk at a big‑box store in Parkville, Maryland, right in the 2nd congressional district. My shift starts at 7 a.m., so I’m usually up by 5:30, grab a coffee from the corner deli, and catch the local bus to the warehouse. After work I swing by my mom’s house; she’s 68 and lives alone, so I help her with groceries and make sure her meds are sorted. I’m not married, and I have a 12‑year‑old son who lives with his dad a few miles away. On weekends I try to get him to the park or the library, and when I can I’m at the community center helping with after‑school tutoring. Rent is $1,350 for a two‑bedroom apartment, and I’m always watching my utility bills because the electric company keeps raising rates. I grew up in Baltimore City, went to a public high school, and never went to college – the best I could do was a certification in forklift operation, which got me the stock job. My evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV, scrolling on my phone, or cooking dinner while listening to whatever’s on the radio.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. I felt like the whole thing was a circus, and between my long shifts, taking care of my mom, and making sure my son got his school supplies, there was just no energy left to go to the polls. The ads were all the same – “vote for change” – but the candidates never seemed to talk about the things that keep me up at night, like the rising rent and the grocery prices that keep climbing even though my paycheck stays flat. I also felt like my vote wouldn’t matter; the district leans pretty solidly Democratic, and I’m not sure the party really cares about working‑class Black women like me. So I stayed home, and I haven’t changed my mind much. I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out too, unless something huge hits my neighborhood – like a major layoff at the warehouse or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces me out of my apartment. My biggest concerns right now are keeping my mom safe and healthy, and making sure I can afford the rent and the cost of food for my son. The Democratic Party talks a lot about social programs, but I’ve never seen any real help reach my block. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep yelling about “law and order” and “tax cuts” that never trickle down to folks like me. Both sides feel distant, and I’m just trying to get through the month without missing a rent check.
