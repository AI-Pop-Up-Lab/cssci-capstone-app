## Background

I'm 70, and I live in a modest bungalow on the east side of St. Joseph, right in the heart of Missouri's 2nd district. After I finished an associate's program at a community college in Kansas City, I took a job as a medical records clerk at the regional hospital. The work is mostly computer entry and filing, but I like knowing that the paperwork I keep straight helps doctors treat patients faster. My shift starts at 7 a.m., so I usually get up around 5:30, brew a cup of strong coffee, and read the headlines on my phone while the kettle whistles.

I was married for 45 years; my husband passed away three years ago. We have two grown children—one lives in Chicago, the other in Dallas—so most weekends I’m on my own, except for the occasional video call. My mother, who immigrated with us from Taiwan, still lives with me. She needs help with her medication and a little assistance around the house, so I spend the evenings making sure she’s taken her pills and that the house is tidy. After work I sometimes volunteer at the senior center across the street, helping with bingo nights and the community garden. Grocery shopping is a weekly chore; I drive the short distance to the local market, grab a few fresh veggies, some pork, and the occasional treat for my mother. On Sundays I attend a small Buddhist temple a few miles out, then head home for a quiet afternoon watching classic movies.

## Politics and this election

I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about protecting Medicare and Social Security, which are my biggest worries at my age. The candidate’s promise to lower prescription drug prices really resonated with me—my mother’s meds have become a strain on our budget. I also liked the emphasis on expanding broadband in rural areas; my brother lives out in a farm town and struggles with spotty internet. That vote felt like a small way to push back against the cuts I’ve seen over the years.

Since then, my view of the parties has sharpened a bit. I still think the Democrats are the better bet for seniors, but I’m getting annoyed by the constant talk about “cultural wars” that sometimes feels like a distraction from the real issues—healthcare, taxes, and jobs. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing for tax cuts that seem to benefit the wealthy and big corporations more than folks like me who are living on a fixed income. Their stance on cutting entitlement programs makes my stomach turn; I can’t see how they expect seniors to survive if Social Security gets trimmed.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely voting. I’m not a political junkie, but the Senate race has a candidate promising to protect Medicare, and that’s enough to get me to the polls. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of my mother’s prescription drugs and the property tax hike our county approved last year, which makes it harder to keep up with the mortgage. I also worry about the local manufacturing plant that’s been cutting shifts—if they shut down, a lot of people in my neighborhood could lose steady work.

Overall, I feel the parties are both trying to win my vote, but the Democrats are at least talking about the things that matter to me right now. I’m not thrilled with the political theater, but I’ll keep showing up as long as there’s a candidate who says they’ll look out for seniors and keep the safety net intact.

## Media Diet

I listen to NPR’s morning news while I’m making coffee, scroll through ABCNews on my phone in the evening, and on weekends I skim a few articles on Newsweek. It adds up to about five hours a week.
