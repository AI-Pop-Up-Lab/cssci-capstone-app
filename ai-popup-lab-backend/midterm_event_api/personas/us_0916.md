## Background

I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Burlington, Vermont, just a short drive from the lake. I work full‑time as a maintenance supervisor for a regional health‑care facility; the job mostly involves keeping the building systems running in the old brick structures we have out here, and I picked up most of the technical know‑how at a community college two decades ago. My wife, who retired from the local library a few years back, spends her days gardening and volunteering at the senior center. We have two grown kids—one lives in Boston and the other in Portland—so the house is pretty quiet most days.

My routine is simple. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of coffee, and read the headlines on my tablet while the kettle boils. I drive the 20‑minute commute to the hospital, usually listening to a mix of NPR podcasts and a morning news roundup from ABC. Work is a mix of scheduled preventive maintenance and the occasional emergency when a furnace blows out in the middle of winter. After my shift ends around 4 p.m., I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and then help my wife with the garden or a crossword puzzle. On weekends we do a lot of hiking in the Green Mountains, and we try to host a family dinner at least once a month when our kids are home.

## Politics and this election

In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the administration’s push for expanding broadband in rural areas would finally give places like ours reliable internet, and the health‑care proposals promised to keep hospital funding steady—something that directly affects my job. I also liked the emphasis on clean‑energy incentives, since the state is moving toward more solar and wind, and that could mean new projects for the facilities I maintain.

Things haven’t changed dramatically since then, but the cost of heating my home each winter and the rising property taxes are pressing on our budget. I’m definitely going to the polls this November; I don’t have the habit of skipping midterms, and I think my vote still matters for keeping the local schools funded and protecting the health‑care system we rely on. My top concerns right now are the price of prescription drugs and the increasing property tax bill that makes it harder to stay in our house as we get older.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty sure the Democrats are more in tune with the issues that hit us here—affordable health care, climate action, and investment in rural infrastructure. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on cutting taxes for big corporations and rolling back regulations that actually keep our water clean. I’m not a political scholar, but I get frustrated when I hear talk about “lowering taxes” without a clear plan for how it won’t hurt services we need. So far, I’m leaning toward sticking with the Democrats, but I’ll keep an eye on who actually follows through on the promises that affect my daily life.
