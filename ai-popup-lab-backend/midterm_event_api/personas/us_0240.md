## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on lower taxes and less regulation matched my outlook as a small‑business‑oriented foreman. I was also worried about immigration and felt the Republican candidate was tougher on the border, which I thought would protect my crew’s jobs from competition. The economy felt shaky after the pandemic, and I liked the promise of a steady, pro‑business environment.  

Going into the 2026 midterms, I’m voting again. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of gasoline for the trucks we drive to job sites and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on our home. I’m also watching health‑care premiums—my wife’s bakery doesn’t have a big group plan, and the costs are starting to bite.  

I’m still pretty skeptical of the Democrats. To me they seem more focused on identity politics and “green” initiatives that feel out of touch with the real‑world concerns of folks who have to pay the bills and keep a roof over their heads. The Republicans, while not perfect, usually push for lower taxes and fewer regulations, which feels more practical for someone in my line of work. I do get annoyed when the party drifts too far right on cultural issues, but overall I think they're the better bet for keeping my family’s finances stable.
