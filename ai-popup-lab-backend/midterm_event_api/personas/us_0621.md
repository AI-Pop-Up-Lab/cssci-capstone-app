## Background  
I'm 35 and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the Northeast Heights of Albuquerque, right in the part of the city that falls in NM‑1. I work full‑time as a case manager for a community health center; my bachelor's in social work helps me navigate the paperwork and advocate for clients who can’t afford medication or stable housing. My mom moved in with me a few years ago after retiring from the school district, so the place is always busy—she cooks, I do the laundry, and we both take turns watching my 7‑year‑old niece when her parents are working late. My days start early: I’m up around 5:30 am, make coffee, and check the news on my phone while the kettle boils. After a quick commute on the I‑40, I’m at the clinic by 8 am, spending the morning meeting with patients, filling out intake forms, and coordinating with local shelters. Lunch is usually a sandwich at my desk while I scroll through a few headlines. I leave work around 5 pm, swing by the grocery store to pick up fresh produce, then head home to help my niece with homework and chat with my mom about the day. Evenings are low‑key: a bit of TV, maybe a call with a friend from college, and trying to get to bed before midnight so I can do it all again tomorrow.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between juggling two jobs at the clinic, caring for my mom and niece, and trying to keep up with rent that keeps creeping up, the ballot felt like another thing I didn’t have the energy to fit into an already packed schedule. I also felt that the candidates—both parties—were making promises that never touched the day‑to‑day reality of people like me, so I just wrote it off as a lost cause. That same feeling is still with me, so I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out unless something huge happens locally, like a major cut to the community health funding that would hit my clients directly. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent and the rising cost of prescription drugs; both feel like they’re squeezing my budget from opposite ends. When I think about the parties, the Democrats get a lot of my attention because they talk about expanding Medicaid and funding for affordable housing, but I’m skeptical about whether they’ll actually follow through. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to ignore the struggles of low‑income families in the Southwest and focus on issues that feel far removed from my life, which just adds to my frustration.

## Media Diet  
I usually listen to NPR in the morning while I’m getting ready, then skim ABC News and CBS News articles during my lunch break and again in the evening after the kids are in bed. Altogether it’s about four hours a week.
