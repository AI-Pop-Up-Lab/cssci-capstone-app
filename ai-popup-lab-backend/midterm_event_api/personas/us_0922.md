## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Everett, Washington, right in the 2nd congressional district. I work full‑time as a grocery store clerk at the local Safeway, stocking shelves, ringing up customers, and sometimes covering the night shift when a coworker calls out. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I learned on the job and have been making a living that way ever since. My mother passed away a few years ago, and my two adult children live in Seattle and Spokane, so most evenings I'm just me and my rescued Labrador, Max. I get up around 5 a.m., make a quick coffee, and catch the 6:15 am bus to the store. After my shift I swing by the community center for the weekly bingo night, then head home, watch some TV, and try to get to bed before midnight. On weekends I do a bit of yard work, help a neighbor with groceries, and sometimes go to the tribal cultural events downtown.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates cared about folks like me who are just trying to get by. The ballot felt like a list of promises that never reach my grocery aisle or my rent check. I also work odd hours, so the polling places were either closed or packed when I could make it, and I just gave up. The whole process seemed designed for people with more free time and more education, which makes me feel invisible. I’m sitting this one out too; unless something blows up locally—like a sudden closure of the store where I work or a major cut to tribal services—I don’t see a reason to waste the hassle of voting again.

My biggest worries day‑to‑day are the rising price of food and the cost of my health insurance. Every time I go to the pharmacy the co‑pay climbs, and the grocery aisles keep getting pricier, which means I have to stretch my paycheck farther. I also keep an eye on property taxes because any hike would make it harder to keep the house I’ve lived in for decades. When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Republicans. Their talk about “cutting taxes” feels like a joke when my paycheck is already thin, and their stance on tribal sovereignty has never seemed supportive. The Democrats, I guess, are a little better on social programs, but I’m skeptical of their promises on the economy—most of the time it feels like they’re talking about people who don’t have to work three shifts to make ends meet. So yeah, I’m not excited about either side, but I’m more fed up with the GOP.
