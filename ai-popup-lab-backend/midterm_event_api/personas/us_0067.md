## Background
I'm 45 and I live in St. Johns, a small town on the Navajo Nation that sits inside Arizona's 2nd congressional district. I work part‑time as a cashier at the local grocery store; the hours are split between the morning shift and an evening slot a few days a week. I don't have a high school diploma – I left school when I was 16 to help my mother take care of my younger siblings after my dad passed away. Those days taught me how to stretch a paycheck and keep a house running on a shoestring.

I'm a single mom to two kids, a 12‑year‑old boy and a 9‑year‑old girl. They both go to the same elementary school that’s a bus ride away, so my mornings start at 5 a.m. with a quick breakfast, getting the kids dressed, and loading them onto the school bus. After dropping them off I head to the store, clock in, and spend the next six hours ringing up groceries, restocking shelves, and dealing with the occasional rude customer. My shift ends around 2 p.m., and I swing by the community health clinic to pick up the kids' prescriptions before heading home.

Evenings are a mix of homework help, cooking dinner on a propane stove, and trying to keep the house warm when the winter wind blows through the thin walls. Because the internet connection here is spotty, I often rely on the TV for news and a little entertainment. On weekends I help my mother with gardening, tend to the family’s small herd of goats, and sometimes drive out to the nearest town for groceries that we can't find locally. Money is always tight, and I’m constantly looking for ways to stretch what we have.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about what life is like on the reservation. The ballot was a mess of big‑ticket promises that never touched the water shortages we face or the lack of broadband that makes it impossible for my kids to do schoolwork. Between work, kids, and trying to keep the house from freezing, I just didn’t see a point in spending a few hours at the polls. I also get a lot of calls from campaign volunteers who ask me to sign petitions for issues that feel far removed from my daily grind, which adds to the feeling that the whole system is a circus.

I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic happens locally – like a major cut to the tribal water fund or a sudden spike in the price of gasoline that makes getting to work impossible – I don't plan to cast a ballot in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that we still don’t have reliable internet service, which makes it hard for my kids to keep up with school and for me to look for better-paying work. The other big worry is the looming water crisis; the wells we rely on are getting shallower each year.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats talk a lot about “reconciliation” but when the funding for tribal schools gets delayed, it feels like empty talk. The Republicans keep shouting about the border and taxes, but they never mention how the federal policies affect tribal sovereignty or the lack of infrastructure on the reservation. It’s like they’re both looking at the same map but ignoring the part where I live.
