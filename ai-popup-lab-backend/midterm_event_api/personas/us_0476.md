## Background

I'm 75, and I live in a modest bungalow on the edge of St. Joseph, Missouri, right in the 2nd congressional district. I spent most of my working life loading and unloading freight at the rail yard; I never went to college, just a high school diploma and a lot of on‑the‑job learning. I retired about ten years ago when the yard downsized, and now I get a modest pension and a bit of Social Security. My wife died five years back, so it's just me and the occasional visits from my two grown‑up kids—one lives in Kansas City, the other in Springfield. They bring their kids around Christmas and sometimes in the summer, which keeps the house from feeling too empty.

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and read the paper while the radio plays classic country. I head down to the senior center a couple of times a week for bingo and a low‑impact exercise class. In the afternoons I tend to a small vegetable garden behind the house and walk my old Labrador, Max, around the neighborhood. Evenings are for the TV—usually the local news, then a rerun of an old western, and sometimes a documentary on PBS. I try to keep the house in shape, fixing leaky faucets and shuffling through a pile of mail that includes a few flyers I never really look at.

## Politics and this election

I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was just a circus, and none of the candidates seemed to understand what a senior on a fixed income really needs. Between the long lines, the endless ads, and the feeling that my single vote wouldn't change anything, I just stayed home. The pandemic made it even harder; I didn't want to risk catching anything just to drop a ballot in a box that felt like a joke.

I'm sitting this one out again in 2026. Nothing in the news has convinced me that the system is any different now. If there were a huge local scandal—like the county hospital shutting down or a major utility rate hike that hit seniors hard—I might consider showing up, but otherwise I don't see a point. My biggest worries are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that keep eating into my pension. I also worry about the younger folks in town not finding decent work, which makes the whole community feel like it's slipping.

When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats pushing for big‑government solutions that feel disconnected from the day‑to‑day grind, and the Republicans talking about tax cuts that never seem to reach people like me. Both sides seem to be more interested in scoring points than actually fixing the little things that matter to folks who are trying to stretch a limited budget.

## Media Diet

I spend about ten hours a week with the news—Mornings I catch the local feed on CBS, evenings I scroll the ABC site on my tablet, and I listen to NPR podcasts while gardening. I also check NBC News for quick headlines before bed.
