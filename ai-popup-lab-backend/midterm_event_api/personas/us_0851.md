## Background

I grew up in a working‑class immigrant family that ran a small grocery store, so I learned early how to stretch a paycheck. After high school I took a few night classes in electrical technology but never finished a degree; I’ve been an electrician for the past 35 years, mostly on oil‑field rigs and later doing maintenance for a local utility. My wife and I have been married 35 years; our two kids are out of state—one’s a mechanic in Houston, the other’s a teacher in Dallas—so the house is pretty quiet most days. I spend my mornings checking the meter readings, then head out to the job site where I’m on call for emergency repairs. After work I swing by the local Denny’s for a coffee, catch up with the crew, and sometimes help my neighbor fix a leaky faucet. Weekends are for the community garden we tend with a few other retirees, and occasional trips to the county fair. I drive a well‑kept pickup, keep my tools in the back, and try to keep the house in good shape for when my wife and I finally decide to downsize.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on energy policy protected my livelihood. The oil boom in West Texas has kept my hours steady, and I’m skeptical of the Democrats’ push for rapid green regulations that could shut down rigs and leave folks like me without work. I also appreciated the party’s emphasis on lower taxes; the property tax hike last year was a real pinch, and the GOP’s promise to curb it felt tangible. Over the past two years I’ve watched the headlines and heard a lot of talk about “cost of living” and “border security,” and those issues still sit heavy on me. I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I want to keep the tax cuts and make sure the energy sector stays strong. My top everyday concerns are keeping my utility bills manageable and making sure there are enough good‑paying jobs for the younger folks in the area. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats—they seem more interested in talking about climate goals than actually keeping the lights on for working families. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about the things that affect my pocketbook.
