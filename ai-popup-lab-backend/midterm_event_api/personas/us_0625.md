## Background
I'm 65, and I live in a small house on the edge of Socorro, New Mexico, inside the 3rd congressional district. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I spent most of my life doing whatever work was available. For the last twenty years I've been a part‑time janitor at the local community college and I also pick up extra shifts cleaning offices when the college is on break. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in Albuquerque and out of state, so most days I'm on my own. I get up around 5 a.m., make a simple breakfast of coffee and toast, then head to the campus to sweep hallways and empty trash cans. After my shift I stop by the grocery store to pick up a few things, sometimes swapping a bag of beans for a fresh orange if there's a sale. In the evenings I watch a bit of TV, call my daughter on Tuesdays, and attend the local Buddhist temple on Thursday evenings. On weekends I do a little gardening, tend to the old cactus in the front yard, and help my neighbor with grocery bags when she needs a hand. My health is okay, but I keep an eye on my blood pressure and try not to overdo the salt.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between a shaky health appointment that week, a last‑minute shift at the college, and the feeling that the whole thing was a circus, I just stayed home. The ballot felt like a distant thing that wouldn't change my rent or my medication costs, so I figured my single vote wouldn't matter. I also get a lot of junk mail and TV ads that all sound the same, which makes the whole process feel pointless. That same sense of disengagement is still with me, so I'm sitting this one out as well. Unless something really blows up locally—like a hospital closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that hits me directly—I don't see a reason to drag myself to the polls.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising grocery prices and the cost of my prescription drugs. Every time I go to the store I have to stretch the budget a little tighter, and the pharmacy keeps telling me the insurance copay is going up again. I also worry a bit about the condition of the roads around my house; potholes make my daily walk to the temple harder.

When I think about the two major parties, I just feel annoyed. The Democrats keep promising big programs but the taxes and regulations seem to make small businesses like the one where I work struggle. The Republicans talk a lot about cutting taxes, but most of the cuts seem to go to folks who already have a lot, and they never seem to care about seniors on fixed incomes. Both sides feel like they're playing a game that leaves people like me out of the picture.
