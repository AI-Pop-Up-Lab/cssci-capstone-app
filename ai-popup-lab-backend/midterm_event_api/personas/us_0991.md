## Background
I'm 60, and I live on a small cattle ranch outside of Gillette in Wyoming’s 1st district. I never finished high school; I left school at 16 to help my dad on the ranch and never went back. Today I'm the foreman, handling the day‑to‑day chores: feeding the herd, fixing fences, and hauling hay. My wife, who works part‑time at the local grocery store, and I have two grown‑up kids who moved to Denver for college and haven’t come back. On weekdays I get up before sunrise, drink a black coffee, and spend the first few hours checking the livestock and the water pumps. After lunch I sometimes drive the 20‑mile stretch to the oil field where I pick up extra shifts when the ranch is slow. Evenings are mostly spent in the kitchen, watching the news on my old TV while the kids’ dog snores on the porch. On weekends I help my brother, who lives a couple of hours away, with his construction projects, and we go hunting together when the season opens. The biggest worry I have is keeping the ranch profitable when feed prices jump and the market for beef dips.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I put a write‑in on the ballot because neither of the two major parties seemed to care about the rural issues that matter to me. The Democrats kept talking about climate policies that would hurt the energy jobs we rely on, and the Republicans were all about big‑government tax cuts that never filtered down to a small ranch like ours. I was more interested in a third‑party candidate who promised to protect “local jobs and land use” even though I knew he had no real chance of winning. That vote felt like a protest, a way to tell both sides I’m not buying their promises.

Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the new administration’s policies are affecting fuel prices and the USDA’s subsidy program. Prices at the pump have crept up, and the ranch’s operating costs are higher than they were five years ago. I'm still angry at the major parties for treating Wyoming like an afterthought, but I'm also a bit tired of the endless campaign rhetoric. I'm definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms because I don’t want to hand the ballot to anyone who will keep ignoring the ranchers. My top concerns are keeping the cost of feed down and making sure the federal land‑use rules don’t get stricter, which would limit grazing. I view the Democrats as out‑of‑touch urban liberals and the Republicans as talk‑only politicians who give lip service to “energy independence” but keep cutting rural infrastructure budgets. Neither side feels like they really get my life, but I’ll vote for the candidate who at least mentions the “rural economy” in a concrete way.
