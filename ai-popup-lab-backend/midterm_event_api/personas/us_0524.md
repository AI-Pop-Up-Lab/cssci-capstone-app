## Background
I'm 30, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the edge of Goldsboro, North Carolina, inside the 13th congressional district. I work full‑time as a cashier at a grocery store, clocking in early for the morning shift and staying until the evening rush dies down. After my shift I usually swing by the local fast‑food joint for a cheap dinner, then head back to my place where I share the rent with my boyfriend, who works as a mechanic. We split the bills, but the rent still eats most of our paychecks, so we keep an eye on every grocery trip and try to stretch the food budget with coupons and discount apps.

My days are pretty routine: I wake up around 5:30 am, catch the bus to the store, spend eight hours scanning items, restocking shelves, and handling the occasional irate customer. On my days off I either do laundry, run errands for my mom who lives a short drive away, or scroll through my phone looking for cheap deals on household stuff. I don’t have a college degree; I left high school after freshman year to help my family with money, so most of what I know is from on‑the‑job experience and the internet. In my spare moments I watch a few reality shows and listen to some country music on the radio while I cook.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates really cared about people like me who are just trying to make rent and keep food on the table. The campaigns were all ads about big issues that felt far removed from the grocery aisles where I spend most of my day. I also work a schedule that makes it hard to find a polling place that’s open when I’m off, and the whole voter‑ID thing felt like an extra hassle I wasn’t willing to jump through. So I stayed home, and I’m not planning to change that for the 2026 midterms unless something hits close to home—like a big cut to local services or a scandal that directly affects my community.

I’m sitting this one out, too. My top everyday concerns are keeping up with rent and dealing with rising grocery prices; those are the things that keep me up at night. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes, but they never seem to care about the low‑wage workers who actually pay those taxes, and the Democrats keep promising bigger social programs that feel like they’ll just raise taxes or create more bureaucracy. Neither side seems to get why a cashier in Goldsboro needs a stable paycheck and affordable food. If a candidate could actually talk about keeping the cost of living down and not just grand rhetoric, maybe I’d pay attention, but for now I’m just watching from the sidelines.
