## Politics and this election
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between juggling two jobs, the kids’ schedules, and a feeling that none of the candidates were really listening to folks like me, voting just didn’t make it onto my radar. I’ve always thought the system is set up so that the big‑money players get the loudest voice, and when you’re working the night shift you don’t have the luxury to stay up late watching debates. That cynicism hasn’t faded; if anything, the headlines about endless partisan fights and no real solutions have reinforced my sense that my vote won’t change anything. So for the 2026 midterms I’m sitting this one out again unless something dramatic hits our town—like a factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes—that forces me to pay attention.

My biggest worries day‑to‑day are keeping up with rent and making sure groceries don’t eat up the whole paycheck. The warehouse job is steady, but overtime isn’t guaranteed, and I’m nervous about any layoffs. I also worry about the local job market for my kids when they graduate; there aren’t many well‑paying opportunities around here beyond the big distribution centers.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to promise more social programs but then raise taxes that hit renters like me. The Republicans talk about cutting taxes but push for cuts to social safety nets that help families when a wage goes down. It feels like they’re more interested in scoring points than solving the real problems on the ground.
