## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party's message on the economy and taxes lined up with my own worries. My husband runs his own business, and every time the state talks about raising the corporate tax or adding new regulations, we hear about higher costs that could hit our bottom line. I also liked the candidate's tough stance on the border; with my parents still living in Mexico, I want to see a clear, enforceable system that protects our families while still allowing legal immigration. The campaign ads on the radio and those short clips on the news sites I watch reminded me that the Democrats seemed more focused on big‑government programs that I worry will just raise my property taxes and push up the price of gas.

Since then, I've kept an eye on what the state legislature is doing, especially the proposals to raise the sales tax to fund school construction. That feels like a direct hit to my budget, so I'm leaning even more toward the GOP for the 2026 midterms. I'm definitely voting this time; I already have a ballot mailed to my address and plan to drop it off at the post office on Election Day. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of gasoline and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up each year. I also worry about healthcare premiums for my kids and whether we’ll be able to afford a decent plan if the market changes.

When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who understand small‑business owners and want to keep taxes low. The Democrats, in my view, are more about expanding government programs that sound good on paper but end up costing more for people like me. I don’t feel like either side is perfect, but the GOP feels like the safer bet for my family’s financial stability.
