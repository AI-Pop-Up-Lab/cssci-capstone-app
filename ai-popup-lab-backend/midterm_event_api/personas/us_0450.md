## Background
I'm 35, and I live in Burnsville, a suburb of Minneapolis in Minnesota's 4th congressional district. I work full‑time as a floor manager at a large home‑goods retailer. I went to a community college for a couple of years, took some business courses, but never finished a degree. My husband works night shifts at a regional hospital, so most of the day I'm running the store, handling inventory, and training the part‑time crew. We have two kids, ages 7 and 4, who go to the local public elementary school. Mornings are a rush: I drop the older one off, grab a coffee from the corner deli, and head to the warehouse. My shift ends around 6 p.m., then I pick up the younger one from after‑school care, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner. On Saturdays we do a big grocery run at the big box store, and on Sundays we attend a small evangelical church downtown. I like hiking the nearby trails when the weather's nice, but most weekends are spent catching up on laundry and the kids' soccer practices. I try to keep the house running on a tight budget because the mortgage and property taxes keep creeping up.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the Democratic policies were pushing taxes higher and ignoring the cost‑of‑living squeeze that families like mine are feeling. The Republican candidate talked about cutting the federal deficit and giving more relief to small businesses, which resonated with my day‑to‑day worries about payroll and rent. I also liked the tougher stance on immigration; it felt like a way to protect jobs in my community. Since then, I've stayed pretty skeptical of the political system—my husband and I argue about whether any candidate really cares about the middle class. Still, I think my vote matters enough to keep the pressure on the other side, so I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are grocery prices, which have jumped a lot over the past year, and the property tax bill that’s been rising each time the city re‑assesses our home. I see the Democratic Party as too focused on big‑city issues and social programs that don’t translate to my suburban life. The Republicans, in my view, at least talk about lowering taxes and reducing regulation, even if sometimes their rhetoric feels a bit over the top. I’m hoping the GOP can deliver some real tax relief without the wild promises.
