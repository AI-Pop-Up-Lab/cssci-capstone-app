## Background  
I'm 45 and I work as a maintenance supervisor at a mid‑size hotel in the Kakaʻako neighborhood of Honolulu. My day starts around 5:30 a.m.; I catch the bus to the hotel, check the HVAC systems, and make the rounds before the first guests arrive. The job is mostly hands‑on, fixing plumbing leaks, overseeing the night‑shift crew, and keeping the building up to code. I’m married, and my wife runs a small online store selling Hawaiian‑style home décor. We have two kids: a 13‑year‑old who’s into skateboarding and a 9‑year‑old who loves drawing. Weekends are a blur of grocery trips to the local market, helping the kids with school projects, and trying to catch a quick surf session when the tide’s right. We live in a modest two‑bedroom condo that we bought with a mortgage a few years back, so rent‑to‑mortgage payments are a big part of the budget. I’m not into fancy hobbies—most evenings I’m on the couch watching a rerun of a sitcom or scrolling through social media while the kids do homework. I don’t speak Japanese or Korean fluently, but I can get by in Tagalog and a bit of Mandarin when my parents visit from the mainland.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long shifts, the kids’ activities, and trying to keep the hotel running smoothly, I just never felt like I had the time or the energy to make it to the polls. The whole process feels like a circus—campaign ads everywhere, endless talk about “the national agenda” that never seems to touch the real cost of gasoline at the pump or the price of fresh fish at the market. I also get a lot of the same old promises: “more jobs,” “lower taxes,” but nothing concrete ever shows up for us islanders. That cynicism made me skip the ballot, and unless something hits close to home—like a major utility outage that the government fails to fix—I’m probably going to sit this one out too. So, “I’m sitting this one out.” My biggest worries are the rising cost of groceries and the ever‑tightening housing market; every month feels like a squeeze on the family budget. I’m also worried about traffic congestion on the H‑1 and how it messes with my kids’ school runs. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Democrats seem to take the islands for granted, assuming federal aid will solve everything, while the Republicans act like they don’t even know what life on a Pacific island looks like. Neither side talks about the real day‑to‑day struggles of a maintenance crew trying to keep a hotel afloat or a small family business trying to survive on thin margins.
