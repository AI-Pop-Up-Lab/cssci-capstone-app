## Background
I'm 35 and work as an administrative assistant at a community health clinic in Marrero, right in the heart of Louisiana's 4th district. I grew up in a small town in the Bayou region, and after high school I took a few classes at a community college but never finished a degree. My days start early: I grab a coffee from the corner donut shop, catch the 6:15 a.m. bus, and spend the morning filing paperwork, answering phones, and coordinating appointments for a mostly low‑income clientele. After my shift ends around 3 p.m., I head back home to a modest two‑bedroom apartment that I rent with my younger brother, who’s still in college. We split the rent and utilities, and on Tuesdays and Fridays we take turns cooking dinner—mostly rice, beans, and whatever's on sale. On weekends I either run errands at the local Walmart or help my aunt at her laundromat down the road. I try to stay active by walking my dog, a scrappy mixed breed named Buster, around the neighborhood park when I can. My family lives a few miles away in New Orleans, so we video‑call on holidays, but most of my social life is built around work and my small circle of friends from church and the community center.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between juggling long clinic hours, caring for my brother's rent, and the endless stream of ads promising change that never seemed to materialize, I felt like my vote was just another drop in a bucket that never overflows. The whole process felt like a circus—long lines, confusing mail‑in forms, and a feeling that the candidates were all talking past each other. I also got fed up with the constant bickering on the news; it made the whole system look like a reality‑show rather than a place where real problems get solved. Because of that, I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something really shakes up my neighborhood—like a major factory closing or a scandal that directly hits my community—I don't see a reason to bother with another ballot.

My biggest day‑to‑day worries are keeping up with grocery prices and making sure the rent doesn't jump again. The clinic's budget cuts mean my hours could be reduced, and that would ripple through everything else. I also worry about local jobs; most of the decent positions in the area are either low‑pay or require a degree I don't have.

When I think about the parties, I see both of them promising the same thing: a better economy and safer streets, but they keep cutting each other off with mud‑slinging. The Democrats seem to care more about social programs, which I appreciate, but I think they're too quick to blame everything on the other side. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about tax cuts and "law and order" while ignoring the rising cost of living for folks like me. In short, I'm fed up with the noise and don't trust either side to get my everyday concerns on the agenda.
