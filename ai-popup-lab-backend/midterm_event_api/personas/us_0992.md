## Background
I'm 30 and work as a field service technician for a regional oil‑field equipment company out of Casper. I went to a state university, got a bachelor's in mechanical engineering, but the job market here forced me into a more hands‑on role. My shift starts at 6 a.m.; I grab a coffee from the gas station on the edge of town, hop on a pickup, and spend the morning checking pressure gauges and fixing hydraulic leaks at rigs scattered across the high plains. By noon I’m usually back at the office for a quick lunch—usually a sandwich and whatever’s on the discount rack at the grocery store. After work I either hit the gym or catch up on a video game with my roommate, who’s also from out of state. We split the rent on a modest two‑bedroom apartment near the river, and on weekends we either go hunting with a couple of friends or drive up to the mountains for a day of fishing. My parents live a few hours away in Cheyenne, and I call them most Sunday evenings. I’m not married, but I do have a girlfriend who lives about an hour away; we see each other when we can, usually on a Friday night after a shift.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between rotating shifts, a broken car, and trying to keep up with rent, the whole process just felt like another thing I didn’t have time for. The ballot looked the same as every other year—lots of promises that never seem to touch the daily grind of a technician in Wyoming. I’ve always thought the system is set up so that the big‑ticket races matter more than the little things that actually affect my paycheck, my health insurance, and the price of diesel. That feeling of cynicism got stronger when the candidates kept talking about national issues while the local pipeline approvals kept getting delayed, which meant my crew was on standby for weeks. I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms. Unless there’s a sudden scandal that threatens my job directly—like a major shutdown of the rigs we service—or a local measure that could raise my property tax dramatically, I’m not planning to cast a ballot.

My top everyday concerns are keeping my job stable and making sure the price of gasoline doesn’t spike out of reach. The cost of groceries and rent are also always on my mind, especially when the winter heating bill looms. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to want to regulate the energy sector without understanding how many families here depend on it, while the Republicans keep talking about “energy independence” but ignore the fact that the industry’s already under a lot of pressure from market forces. Neither side feels like they’re listening to people who actually have to work the night shift and worry about the next paycheck.
