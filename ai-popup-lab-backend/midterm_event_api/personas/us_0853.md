## Background
I'm 79 and I live in Baytown, a small port town just outside Houston in Texas’ 32nd district. I spent most of my working life as a high‑school math teacher, so I have a four‑year college degree and a lot of chalk dust memories. I retired about five years ago, but I still keep a routine that feels a lot like a school day. I get up around 6 a.m., make a strong coffee, and read the local paper while the dog—an old Labrador named Chico—waits for his walk. After a quick stroll, I sit at the kitchen table and check my phone for headlines from NPR and ABC News, the two outlets I trust most. 

My wife passed away three years back, so it’s just me and my two adult children who live in Dallas and Austin. They call every other weekend, and I get to see my three grandchildren when they’re on summer break. I volunteer a few hours a week at the community center, teaching basic computer skills to other seniors, and I still help out with the church’s food pantry. The rest of my day is a mix of gardening, watching classic movies on TV, and occasionally meeting up with a few old teacher friends at the local Denny’s for a late‑night breakfast. Money’s tight on a fixed income, so I watch my expenses closely, especially the rising cost of my prescription meds and the property tax bill that seems to creep up every year.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about Medicare, Social Security, and immigration reform—issues that hit close to home for me and my family. I was especially glad to see a promise to protect the health benefits that keep me and my wife’s memory alive. The campaign also talked about investing in public schools, something I still care about even though I’m no longer in the classroom. 

Now that the 2026 midterms are here, I’m definitely going to the polls again. My top concerns are keeping my health coverage affordable and making sure my property taxes don’t eat up the little savings I have left. I’m also watching the local job market; the port has been hit by some layoffs, and I worry about my younger relatives who are trying to find steady work. I’ve become a bit more skeptical of the Republicans; their talk about cutting taxes sounds good until you realize most of those cuts help the wealthy and big corporations, and they’ve been pushing stricter immigration policies that feel hostile to families like mine. The Democrats, for all their flaws, still seem the better bet for protecting the safety net I rely on. I’m not a political scholar, I just want a government that won’t make my Medicare disappear and that will keep my community stable. So, I’m voting, and I’m hoping the next Congress will remember folks like me who are barely hanging on.

## Media Diet
I usually skim NPR in the mornings while I’m having coffee, then catch the evening updates from ABC News and a quick look at CBS News before bed, adding up to about seven hours a week.
