## Background
I'm 25 and I work as a research analyst for a biotech startup that has its lab in Naples. I grew up in a small town in Central Florida, but after finishing my master's in molecular biology at the University of Central Florida I moved down to the Gulf Coast for the job. I rent a two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who does graphic design; we split the rent and utilities, and the landlord is always trying to raise the lease. My days start with a run along the beach at sunrise, then I grab a coffee at the local café and head to the lab where I spend most of my time crunching data and writing reports. After work I usually cook dinner at home—mostly quick stir‑fries or pasta—because eating out is pricey. On weekends I volunteer at a community garden in the neighborhood and sometimes help my aunt with her small convenience store in the mall. I’m still close with my parents, who live in Orlando, and we video‑chat every Sunday night. I’m not married, and I don’t have kids yet, but I’m saving up for a down‑payment on a house.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s tax policies were the only thing keeping my paycheck from shrinking as the cost of living kept climbing. The candidate’s promises about cutting red tape for small businesses appealed to me, since the startup I work for is constantly fighting over regulations. I also liked the tougher stance on immigration; my family came here legally and I worry about a chaotic system. Since then I’ve kept an eye on the political conversation, but nothing has drastically changed my outlook. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms because the Senate race and the House contest in my district could affect the business climate and the funding for local infrastructure projects, like the road improvements that keep traffic from backing up on the I‑75 corridor.

My biggest everyday worries are rent and grocery prices—every month I have to decide whether to skip a night out or cut back on fresh produce. The other big thing is health insurance; the premiums keep ticking up and my employer’s plan isn’t getting any better. As for the parties, I see Democrats as too focused on identity politics and on expanding government programs that end up raising taxes for people like me. The Republicans, while not perfect, seem more willing to let the market work and keep taxes low, even if they sometimes ignore climate concerns that affect our coastal community. I’m still skeptical about both sides, but I lean toward whoever promises to keep my paycheck stable and the cost of living from spiraling.
