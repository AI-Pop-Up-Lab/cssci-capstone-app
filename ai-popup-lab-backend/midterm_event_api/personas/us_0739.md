## Background  
I'm 60 and I work as a crew foreman at the sawmill on the outskirts of Pendleton. I got my high school diploma in the late‑70s, never went to college, and learned most of what I know on the job. My day starts around 5 a.m.; I grab a coffee from the diner across the highway, load up the truck, and drive the half‑hour to the plant. The shift is mostly standing and supervising the line, making sure the logs get cut safely and the trucks leave on time.  

I live in a modest ranch house a few miles north of town with my wife of 35 years. We have two adult kids who moved out after college—one’s a nurse in Portland, the other runs a small landscaping business out east. My mother, who’s in her late‑80s, still lives with us because the county’s senior services are limited, so I help her with meds and groceries. On weekends I mow the lawn, tinker with the old pickup, and meet a few buddies at the local bar for a cold one and a game of darts. When I can, I’ll take the family out to the horse‑racing track or go fishing on the Columbia. My biggest hassle is the rising cost of diesel and the occasional snowstorm that makes the road to work slick.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who depend on steady wages from manufacturing and want lower taxes on my property and my truck. The Democratic platform seemed focused on big‑city issues, climate regulations that could hurt the mill, and a push for higher taxes that would hit my retirement savings. I also liked the candidate’s talk about securing the border and protecting “traditional values,” which lines up with what I was raised to believe.  

This year I’m definitely voting. My top concerns are the price of gasoline and diesel—if they keep climbing I can’t afford to keep the pickup running for work and errands—and the looming property tax hike the county is proposing to fund a new sports complex. I also worry about health‑care premiums going up; my wife’s plan is getting more expensive each year, and I’m not sure what the next election will do about that.  

I see the Democratic Party as out of touch with the rural parts of Oregon. They keep talking about climate change and renewable energy, which sounds good until you realize it could mean cutting jobs at the sawmill. Their social agenda feels like an intrusion on how I want to raise my grandchildren. The Republicans, for all their infighting, still stand for lower taxes, less regulation, and a focus on law and order, which I appreciate. I’m not blind to their flaws—sometimes the rhetoric gets too extreme, and I don’t like the way some of the newer faces try to rewrite history—but overall I think they’re the better bet for keeping my paycheck steady and my community stable.
