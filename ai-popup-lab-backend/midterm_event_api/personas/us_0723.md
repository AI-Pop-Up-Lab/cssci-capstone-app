## Background
I'm 25, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in Beaverton, just outside Portland. I work full‑time as a program assistant for a nonprofit that runs after‑school tutoring in Washington County. My bachelor's is in sociology, so I spend most of the day coordinating volunteer schedules, entering data, and occasionally stepping in to lead a session with the kids. My mornings start at 6 am; I jog a couple of miles around the Tualatin River trail, then catch the MAX light rail to the office. After work I swing by the grocery store on the way home, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes meet my roommate—another recent grad—who works as a junior software developer. We take turns cooking, usually something quick like stir‑fry or a big pot of chili that lasts us a few days. On weekends I help my mom, who lives a short drive away in Gresham, with her rent and a few errands; she’s retired from a school district job. I also volunteer at a local church’s food pantry once a month. In my spare time I binge‑watch shows on Netflix, scroll TikTok for a half hour, and try to stay in touch with a few old friends from college. I'm not religious, but I do attend a community meeting once a month about housing issues in the district.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between working long hours, caring for my mom, and trying to keep my own rent up to date, the whole process felt like a waste of time. The ballot was a long list of names I barely recognized, and the ads were all the same anger‑filled talking points that never seemed to address the real problems I deal with—affordable housing and grocery prices that keep climbing. I also feel like the system is rigged; the big parties keep trading the same old promises while the little things that matter to folks like me get ignored. That cynicism made me stay home, and honestly I didn't feel any personal stake in the outcome.

I'm sitting this one out, too. Nothing major has changed on my end, and unless a candidate comes out of nowhere with a concrete plan to lower rent or protect grocery subsidies, I don't see a reason to line up at the polls. My top everyday concerns are keeping my apartment affordable and making sure my next paycheck stretches far enough for food and utilities. When I think about the parties, I see the Republicans as mostly hostile to the kinds of social programs that help people in my community, and the Democrats as well‑meaning but often caught up in internal fights that leave the Black community’s specific needs on the back burner. I wish they’d focus more on real, local solutions instead of national grandstanding.
