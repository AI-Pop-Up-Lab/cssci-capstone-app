## Background  
I'm 80, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Lebanon, New Hampshire, right near the Connecticut River. I spent most of my working life as a carpenter, learning the trade after a couple of years at a community college where I took some drafting and business courses but never finished a degree. I retired about twelve years ago, and now my days are a mix of small projects around the house, tending to my garden, and taking my old Labrador for a walk around the neighborhood.  

My wife passed away five years ago, so I'm a widower. I have two grown children—one lives in Boston and the other in Portland, Maine—and they visit us a few times a year. My grandchildren come by in the summer, and I like to teach them how to use a hand plane or how to fix a leaky faucet. I volunteer a few hours a week at the local library, shelving books and helping the occasional patron with the computer. Most mornings I sit at the kitchen table with a cup of coffee, flip through the printed edition of the *Concord Monitor*, and then check the news on my tablet. Evenings are usually spent watching a news program or listening to NPR while I read a mystery novel. The routine is simple, and I like the predictability.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I didn't feel represented by the two big parties, so I cast my ballot for a third‑party candidate—a Libertarian who talked about cutting government waste and protecting personal freedoms. I was tired of the usual horse‑race rhetoric and thought a different voice might shake things up, even if it was just a symbolic gesture. The campaign didn't get much airtime, but I appreciated the honesty and the fact that they weren't trying to sell me a polished package.  

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I'm planning to vote again, but I'm still skeptical. I haven't found a candidate from the major parties who inspires confidence, so I'll probably stick with the independent or third‑party line if there's a viable option on the ballot. My top everyday concerns are my health care costs—prescriptions keep getting more expensive and my Medicare supplemental plan doesn't cover everything—and the property taxes that keep rising on my modest home. I also worry about the reliability of the power grid in winter; a prolonged outage would be a nightmare at my age.  

When I think about the Democrats and Republicans, I see both sides as more interested in scoring points than solving real problems. The Democrats talk a lot about social programs but seem to forget the budget realities, while the Republicans keep promising tax cuts that never materialize for folks like me. I feel like they're both stuck in the same old game, and that makes me lean toward anyone who promises to actually listen to ordinary people, even if they come from a smaller party.

## Media Diet  
I usually watch the morning segment on CBS News while I eat breakfast, catch up with ABC News articles during lunch, and listen to NPR in the evenings after dinner. On the weekends I tune into a couple of CNBC shows for a quick market rundown. All together that's about nine hours a week.
