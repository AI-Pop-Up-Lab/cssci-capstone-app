## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in Meridian, just outside Boise, in the part of ID‑1 that still feels a bit rural but has a growing shopping strip. I work part‑time as a dental hygienist at a small practice downtown; the job only requires an associate’s degree and a state license, which is exactly what I have. My husband retired from the fire department a few years ago, so most days we split chores—he does the yard work and I handle the bills and grocery runs. We have two grown kids; one lives in Seattle and the other is back home, helping my mother who needs a little extra care. My routine is pretty set: I start my mornings at 6 am with a cup of coffee while scrolling through NPR headlines on my phone, then head to the clinic by 7:30. After work I swing by the local farmer’s market, pick up fresh produce, and sometimes stop at the hardware store to fix the leaky faucet in the bathroom. Evenings are for TV news or a quiet dinner with my husband, and on weekends I either tend to my garden or drive out to the mountains for a hike.  

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I didn’t vote for a Democrat or Republican—I put a write‑in for the Libertarian candidate because I was fed up with the usual two‑party bickering and thought a smaller party might actually bring some fresh ideas. I wasn’t thrilled about the whole thing, but I felt like the only way to register my frustration was to cast a protest vote. That feeling hasn’t gone away; I still think the big parties are more interested in fundraising than solving real problems, but I also recognize that third‑party candidates rarely get a real shot. For the 2026 midterms I’m actually going to the polls. I’ve been reminded lately that local school board races and the Senate seat could affect my health‑care premiums and the water restrictions we’re seeing more of each summer, so I don’t want to sit this one out. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of health‑care and the way property taxes are creeping up as the city expands. I’m also worried about water availability for my garden and for the whole county. As for the parties, I see Democrats as talking a good game about climate and health but often overpromising on what they can deliver, while Republicans seem more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and pushing cultural issues that feel out of touch with folks like me who just want affordable medicine and a reliable water supply.
