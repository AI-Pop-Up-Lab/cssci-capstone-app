## Background
I'm 25 and work the night shift stacking pallets at a distribution center just outside Providence. I grew up in East Providence, the same neighborhood where my mom still lives in a two‑bedroom apartment with my 19‑year‑old sister. After high school I tried a couple of short stints at a fast‑food joint and a local car wash, but the warehouse job paid the most and gave me a steady schedule, even if it means I’m home at 6 a.m. most days. I’m not in school any more, but I take a GED‑prep class at the community center on my days off because I want a better paycheck someday.

My mornings are a blur of coffee, quick showers, and scrolling through my phone while waiting for the bus. I spend the evenings on the couch watching a mix of sports highlights and news clips before I’m out the door again. On weekends I help Mom with groceries, run errands for my sister who’s in community college, and try to catch a game at the local bar when I can afford it. The rent is $1,150, and we split utilities, so every extra dollar counts. I don’t have a car, so everything revolves around the bus schedule and the occasional ride‑share when the weather’s bad.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two jobs, caring for my sister’s school supplies, and the feeling that none of the candidates were speaking to people like me, I just didn’t bother to make a plan. The whole process feels like a far‑away thing that never changes anything for folks on the bottom rung. I also got a nasty call from a door‑knocker who tried to pressure me into signing a petition for a cause I didn’t understand, which left me even more fed up with the whole circus.

I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out too, unless something blows up locally—like a big layoff at the warehouse or a scandal that hits my community directly. My biggest worries every month are keeping up with rent and making sure there’s enough money for groceries; the price of fresh produce keeps creeping up, and I’m always watching my food stamp balance. I don’t feel any strong loyalty to either party. The Democrats seem to promise a lot about social programs but I never see the money trickle down to my block, and the Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes while ignoring the fact that my family can’t afford basic bills. If anything, I’m just annoyed that the political talk never includes people who work night shifts and juggle family responsibilities.
