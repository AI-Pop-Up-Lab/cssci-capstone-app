## Background
I'm 65, and I live in a modest bungalow on the outskirts of St. Joseph, right in the middle of Missouri's 7th district. I spent 35 years as a high‑school English teacher, which gave me a decent pension and the chance to stay involved with the local PTA even after I retired five years ago. My husband passed away three winters back, so most evenings are quiet except for the occasional call from my daughter who lives in Kansas City with her two kids. I still drive my old sedan to the grocery store on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and I try to keep up with my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few peppers—because fresh produce saves a few bucks and gives me something to do when the weather's nice.

My weekdays are pretty routine: I wake up around 6 a.m., brew a cup of coffee, and flip on the news while I read the paper. After breakfast I either tend to the garden or drop off a bag of groceries at the senior center where I volunteer twice a week. Saturdays are for church, followed by a potluck with neighbors, and Sundays I usually spend a few hours on a crossword puzzle before a light lunch with my daughter when she visits. Money's tight enough that I watch every discount, but the pension and Social Security keep the lights on.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about keeping taxes low, protecting Social Security, and standing up for traditional values. I was especially convinced by the candidate’s promise to cut back on federal spending and to support law‑and‑order policies that keep our small towns safe. I also liked the emphasis on strong borders and the promise to back local businesses, which matters when you’re trying to keep a garden store open in a town that’s already losing younger folks.

Since then, I’ve watched the news and it’s been a mixed bag. I’m still convinced that the GOP is the better choice for folks like me who are worried about rising prescription costs and property taxes that keep creeping up on our fixed income. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free college and big‑government health plans that sound good until you realize they’ll raise taxes for everyone else. I’m not thrilled with every Republican move—sometimes they seem more interested in culture wars than in fixing the potholes on Main Street—but I think they’re at least trying to keep the country on a fiscally responsible track.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are prescription drug prices and the property tax bill that’s been creeping up each year. I plan to vote for the Republican Senate candidate who’s promised to push for more transparency in drug pricing and a state‑level property tax freeze. I’m not interested in the Democratic candidate’s platform; it feels like they keep talking about big ideas that never make it to the kitchen table where we actually pay the bills.
