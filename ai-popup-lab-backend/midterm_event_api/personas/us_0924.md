## Background
I'm 50 and work as a senior policy analyst for a regional nonprofit that focuses on affordable housing in the Seattle metro area. I live in Auburn, a suburb in the 7th district, in a house I bought with my husband about fifteen years ago. We have two kids—Jenna, 17, and Malik, 14—who both go to the local high school. My day usually starts with a cup of coffee while scrolling through the headlines on my phone, then I head out at 7:30 a.m. to the office, a short drive on the freeway that’s always stuck in rush hour. After work I pick up the kids from soccer or band practice, help Malik with his math homework, and talk with Jenna about college applications. Evenings are a mix of cooking dinner, watching a news segment on my tablet, and trying to keep the house clean enough for the next day. On weekends I’m either at a community meeting about a new development project, at my church’s food pantry, or taking the family to the nearby lake for a quick paddle. I try to stay active by walking the neighborhood trails whenever the weather lets me.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attuned to the housing affordability crisis that hits my work and my neighborhood hard. The candidate’s promise to increase funding for low‑income housing and protect renters resonated with the daily stress of seeing families struggle to keep a roof over their heads. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and I’m a bit disappointed that the promised federal grant for our district got delayed. Still, I think the party’s stance on climate action and healthcare is closer to where I stand, even if I’m annoyed by the endless partisan bickering on TV. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out because the local school board race could affect the resources my kids get, and there’s a ballot measure on rent control that could make a real difference. My top concerns right now are keeping rent affordable and making sure my kids can afford college without drowning in debt. I view the Democrats as the side that at least pretends to care about those issues, while the Republicans seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which feels like a gamble I’m not willing to take.

## Media Diet
