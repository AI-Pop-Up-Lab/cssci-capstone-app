## Background
I'm 30 and I work as a construction foreman for a regional contractor that builds houses and small commercial projects around Lexington. I grew up in a nearby farm town, went to community college for a couple of years studying civil tech, but never finished a degree because I needed to start making money after my dad got sick. My wife and I bought a modest ranch‑style home on the edge of town three years ago; we have a three‑year‑old daughter who keeps us on our toes. Most mornings I wake up at 5:30 a.m., pour a cup of coffee, and listen to the radio while checking the weather before the 6 a.m. shift starts. I drive the 20‑minute commute to the job site, spend the day overseeing crews, handling the paperwork, and making sure the crew sticks to safety rules. Evenings are usually spent helping my daughter with bedtime stories, scrolling through my phone, and sometimes meeting friends at the local bar for a cheap beer after work. On weekends I try to get out to the lake for a few hours of fishing or help my wife with the garden. I’m not a college‑educated analyst or a policy wonk; I just try to keep a roof over my family’s heads and make enough to pay the bills.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between long hours on the construction site, caring for my kid, and feeling that neither candidate really cared about people like me, I just shrugged it off. The whole campaign felt like a circus, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The polls kept talking about “big ideas” that never filtered down to my paycheck, so I stayed home on Election Day. That same feeling’s still there, so I’m sitting this one out in 2026 unless something big hits my community—like a factory shutdown or a sudden jump in property taxes that forces us to move. My biggest everyday worries are the price at the pump and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up every year. I’m also annoyed by the constant talk about climate policies that seem to ignore the fact that my crew can’t afford a new diesel truck if taxes get raised. I see the Democrats as a party that wants to hand out more regulations and taxes while promising handouts that never arrive. The Republicans talk a lot about “lower taxes” and “border security,” but I’m skeptical because they rarely deliver on the promises that matter to a working‑class family in a small town. It feels like both sides are playing a game that leaves folks like me out of the loop.
