## Background
I'm 20 and I live in Owasso, a suburb on the north side of Tulsa in Oklahoma's 2nd district. I'm juggling a part‑time job at the local grocery store and taking a few classes at the community college downtown. My shift usually starts at 4 p.m., so I spend my mornings studying, doing laundry, and scrolling through my phone while I wait for the bus. On weekends I work a longer Saturday shift, then meet up with a couple of friends from my Spanish‑studies group at a taco spot near the college. My parents own a small landscaping business back in the town, and I help out on weekends when I can. The rent on my one‑bedroom apartment is tight, so I keep a tight budget, buying groceries on sale and using a bike to get around when the weather lets me. I also volunteer a few hours a month at a local community center that offers English classes to newer immigrants, which feels like a good way to give back and practice my own Spanish.

## Politics and this election
I turned out for the 2024 presidential election and voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about issues that hit my life directly—affordable college, immigration reform, and climate action. I was also swayed by the candidate’s promise to increase funding for community colleges, which would make it easier for someone like me to finish a degree without drowning in debt. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration handles student loan forgiveness and the recent push for a federal minimum wage raise; those are still top of my mind.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of rent and the uncertainty around finding a full‑time job after I graduate. I also worry about how local schools are funded, especially because my younger cousins rely on the public system. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they at least try to listen to younger, more diverse voices, even if they don’t always deliver on everything. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on tax cuts for big businesses and less on the day‑to‑day struggles of people like me. I’m not a fan of the constant bickering either, but I’m leaning toward supporting candidates who promise to protect social programs and make college more affordable.
