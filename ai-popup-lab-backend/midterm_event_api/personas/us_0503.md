## Background
I'm 74, and I live in Miles City, a small town on the high plains of eastern Montana. I retired from a job as a clerk at the local grocery store about ten years ago, but I still swing by once a week to pick up fresh produce and chat with the regulars. My husband passed away three years back, so it's mostly me and my two grown‑up kids who live in Missoula and Helena. We get a handful of visits from the grandkids in the summer, and they bring the newest video games that I pretend I understand.

My days are pretty routine. I get up early, make a strong cup of coffee, and read the newspaper while the rooster outside the window starts its morning call. After breakfast I head to the senior center for a bingo game or a knitting circle—those are the times I catch up with folks and hear the latest gossip about road repairs or the upcoming county fair. In the afternoons I tend to my garden; the lilacs and a few tomato plants keep my hands busy. Evenings are for TV, a bit of radio, and sometimes a call with my daughter to discuss her kids' school projects. I drive a well‑worn pickup that I keep in the garage for trips to the doctor or the post office. Money is tight enough that I watch every dollar, but I’m grateful for Social Security and the modest pension from my years at the store.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate promised to protect Medicare and keep my prescription drug costs from climbing any higher. After the last round of price hikes, I was fed up with trying to choose between my blood pressure meds and buying groceries. The Democratic platform also talked about expanding broadband in rural areas, which sounded good for my grandchildren trying to video‑call from out of state.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the promises are playing out. I still think the party is the better bet for seniors like me, even if I sometimes feel like they talk in circles about climate change and tax reforms that don’t touch my immediate worries. The Republicans keep pushing for lower taxes, but most of that seems to go to big corporations and wealthy landowners, not to the folks who rely on fixed incomes. I’m not thrilled with either side, but I’m leaning toward the Democrats because they’re the ones who actually talk about protecting Social Security.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my prescription drug prices low and making sure property taxes don’t skyrocket on my modest home. I also worry about the local hospital staying open; a few months ago the emergency department cut back hours, and that’s scary for anyone my age. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who at least listen to seniors, even if they’re sometimes out of touch with the daily grind. The Republicans, in my view, are more interested in cutting regulations that protect the environment and in tax cuts that don’t trickle down to people like me. I get annoyed when politicians use big‑word speeches that sound like they’re trying to impress rather than solve real problems.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m making coffee, and I catch the evening updates on ABC News and CBS News after dinner, spreading my seven hours of news across the week.
