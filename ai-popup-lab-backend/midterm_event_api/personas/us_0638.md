## Background
I'm 25 and work as a junior software developer at a modest tech startup in Rio Rancho, just outside Albuquerque. I graduated two years ago with a bachelor's in computer science, and the job mostly has me glued to a dual‑monitor setup, debugging code and pushing updates for a client‑facing app. My parents still live in Albuquerque proper, so I visit them on weekends for mom’s tacos and to help with grocery runs. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with a fellow coder from college; we split rent, utilities, and the occasional pizza order. My daily routine starts with a quick scroll through my phone while I brew coffee, then a 20‑minute drive to the office where I spend about eight hours in front of a screen. Evenings are a mix of coding side projects, gaming with friends on Discord, and trying to squeeze in a workout at the local gym before I crash on the couch and binge a Netflix series. I pay off a student loan, so every paycheck feels a little tighter than I'd like.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I just felt like the whole thing was a circus and I was too swamped with work deadlines and trying to keep my finances afloat. The candidates all sounded the same, and I couldn't see how any of them would change the fact that my rent keeps going up and my student loan balance stays stubbornly high. I also thought my single vote wouldn't matter in a swing district like NM‑3, so I stayed home and watched the results on TV later. That feeling hasn't really shifted; I still think the political system is a bit of a farce, and unless something hits close to home—like a major factory closing nearby or a sudden spike in utility rates—I'm not motivated to stand in a line at the polls. So, I'm sitting this one out too. My top everyday worries are keeping up with rent and making sure I can afford my monthly loan payment while still having enough for groceries and gas. I kind of see the Democrats as the party that talks a lot about student debt relief but never follows through, and the Republicans as the folks who keep pushing for lower taxes but ignore the rising cost of living in places like New Mexico. Both sides get on my nerves for different reasons, and I mostly just tune out the political noise unless it directly hits my wallet.
