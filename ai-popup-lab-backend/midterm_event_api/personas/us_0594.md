## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Derry, right on the edge of the Manchester–Derry corridor in New Hampshire's 1st district. I work as a senior software engineer for a fintech startup that moved its office to a renovated warehouse just outside downtown. My day starts at 6 a.m. with a coffee from the local diner, a quick scan of the news on my phone while the kids are still asleep, and then a 20‑minute drive to the office. I’m married to a high‑school Spanish teacher, and we have two kids—17‑year‑old Maya, who’s prepping for college, and 14‑year‑old Luis, who’s obsessed with video games and skateboarding. On weekends we usually hit the bike trail by the river or help out at the community garden, a little hobby that keeps us connected to the town. I take a night class in data science once a month to stay sharp, and I vote in local town meetings when something about road maintenance or school budgets comes up. My parents live in a small apartment in Boston, and I visit them a few times a year, bringing back homemade tamales for the holidays.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because the candidate talked about cutting the state income tax, which hit my paycheck hard as a software engineer, and because I appreciated the emphasis on securing the border and keeping business regulations light. I felt the Democratic platform was too focused on expanding government programs that I saw as inefficient, and I was worried about rising crime in the suburbs. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the state legislature has handled the budget and the recent proposals to raise property taxes to fund school construction. I’m still convinced that a smaller government approach is the best way to protect my family’s financial future, even if I’m not thrilled with every Republican talking point. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I don’t plan on sitting this one out unless something bizarre happens. My top everyday concerns are the property tax bill that’s creeping up each year and the price of groceries—especially meat and fresh produce, which feels like it’s climbing faster than my salary. I view the GOP as the party that at least tries to keep taxes low and lets the private sector drive growth, while I see the Democrats as more interested in expanding entitlement programs and pushing social agendas that feel disconnected from the everyday struggles of working families like mine.
