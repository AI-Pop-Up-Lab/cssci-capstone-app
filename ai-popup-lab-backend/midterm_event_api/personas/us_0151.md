## Background
I'm 49, and I live in a modest brick apartment in the North Wilmington area, not far from the river. I work the night shift as a maintenance technician at a regional hospital; the job means I'm up before dawn, checking HVAC systems, fixing broken lights, and making sure the emergency rooms stay functional. I didn't finish my associate's degree in electrical engineering because I needed to start earning money right after high school, but I picked up a lot of practical skills on the job. My wife works part‑time at a community college cafeteria, and we have two kids—Jade, 16, and Malik, 13—who both go to the same public high school. Evenings are a blur of homework help, catching a late‑night bus home, and trying to squeeze in a quick dinner of whatever's on sale. On weekends I sometimes help a neighbor with yard work or attend a church service at the local Baptist church, but most of my free time is spent scrolling on my phone while I wait for the kids' soccer games to start. The rent is steady but tight, and I keep an eye on my grocery bills because prices keep creeping up.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Between the night shift, the kids' school schedule, and trying to keep the lights on at home, the ballot felt like another thing I couldn't fit into a day already packed with emergencies and deadlines. I also feel like the candidates all promise the same thing—more jobs, lower taxes—but the reality on my street hasn't changed much. The whole process feels distant; the polling place is a community center a few miles away, and I never got a reminder or a clear reason to make the trip. That cynicism hasn't really faded, so I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic hits our neighborhood—like a hospital closure or a sudden rent hike—I don't see myself stepping into a line again.

My biggest everyday worries are paying the rent on time and keeping grocery costs from eating up the rest of the paycheck. I also keep an ear out for any local job training programs that might help me move up from maintenance to a supervisory role. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to talk a lot about health care and social programs, which matters to me, but I rarely see those promises materialize in my community. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing tax cuts and deregulation, which feels like they're ignoring the needs of working‑class families like mine. Neither side feels like they truly get what it's like to juggle two jobs and still try to give the kids a decent future.
