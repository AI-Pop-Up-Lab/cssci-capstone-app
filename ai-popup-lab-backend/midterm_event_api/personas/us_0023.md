## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Mobile, right in Alabama's 1st district. I work full‑time as a high school Spanish teacher at a public school downtown, a job I got after finishing my bachelor's in education at the University of Alabama. My husband, who retired from the Navy a few years ago, spends his days tinkering in the garage and helping me with the yard work. We have an adult daughter who moved to Atlanta for a tech job, but she comes home for holidays and helps us with groceries. Most mornings I wake up at 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and drive the 20‑minute commute while listening to the radio. After school I grade papers, coach the junior varsity soccer team twice a week, and then head home to cook dinner—usually rice, beans, and whatever the kids' favorite taco toppings are. Evenings are for the local church's Spanish‑language Bible study and for catching up on a few TV shows before bed around 10 p.m. On weekends I run errands at the big box store, help my mother with her medication pickups, and try to squeeze in a walk along the waterfront when the weather's nice.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between my husband's health appointments, the kids' college tuition deadlines, and the endless paperwork at school, I just felt like there was no room for a trip to the polling place, and the whole process seemed like a circus that never actually changed anything for people like me. I also get frustrated when the same talking points get recycled—"tax cuts" or "border security"—without any real attention to the rising cost of my insulin or the fact that my grocery bill keeps creeping up. Because of that, I'm sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something dramatic hits the local community, like a hospital closing or a major layoff at the naval shipyard. My biggest day‑to‑day worries are keeping up with my medication costs and making sure the rent on our house doesn't jump higher than we can afford. I see the Democrats as promising a lot of social programs but never delivering the funding, and the Republicans as focused on big‑ticket issues that ignore the small‑scale struggles of working families. Both parties feel disconnected from the reality of a Hispanic household in a coastal Alabama town, so I stay skeptical of any promises until I see actual help on the ground.
