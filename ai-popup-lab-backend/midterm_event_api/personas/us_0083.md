## Background
I'm 55, and I live in Modesto, right in the middle of CA‑10. I work as a dental hygienist at a small family‑run clinic downtown, a job I got after finishing a two‑year program at a community college. My day starts around 6 a.m. with a quick coffee and the news on my phone while I watch the sunrise over the river. I drive the 15‑minute stretch to the clinic, where I spend most of my morning cleaning teeth, taking X‑rays, and talking with patients about flossing. The afternoons are a mix of charting, filing insurance paperwork, and sometimes covering for the dentist when they’re out.

I'm married to a retired bus driver who now volunteers at the local senior center. We have two grown‑up kids—one a teacher in Sacramento, the other a software developer in the Bay Area—who visit a few times a year. My mother lives two counties over, and I help her with grocery trips and doctor appointments when she needs it. Evenings are usually spent cooking dinner, watching a sitcom or a game show, and then catching up on a few episodes of my favorite streaming series before heading to bed. On Sundays I attend a Baptist church service and then meet a few friends for brunch at a diner that serves the best pancakes in town.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the best chance to protect the health care we need for my mom and to keep funding for community health clinics like the one I work at. The candidate talked about expanding Medicaid and protecting Medicare, which hit close to home. I also liked the emphasis on criminal‑justice reform; my brother was caught up in the system years ago, and I’m still watching that ripple through our family.

Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the pandemic fallout and the recent push for a national paid‑family‑leave program. I’m not thrilled with every decision—there have been some budget hiccups and the rollout of the new health subsidies felt clunky—but overall I think the Democrats are trying to address the issues that matter most to folks like me. The Republican Party, on the other hand, seems more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental protections that keep our air clean in the Central Valley. I’ve heard a lot of talk about “law and order” and “border security,” but those don’t solve the rising cost of rent or the grocery price spikes that have been squeezing my paycheck.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the health‑care bills that keep creeping up as my mom needs more medication. I’m also watching property tax increases that could affect our ability to stay in the house we’ve owned for decades. In short, I’m leaning Democratic again, but I’m watching to see if the party can actually deliver on the promises that mattered to me in 2024.

## Media Diet
