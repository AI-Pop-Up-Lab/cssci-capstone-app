## Background
I'm 40 and work as an HVAC technician for a small company that services the suburbs around Upper Marlboro. I got my associate degree in mechanical systems at a community college in Prince George's County, then landed an apprenticeship that turned into a full‑time job. I live in a modest two‑bedroom townhouse in Clinton with my wife, who works part‑time at a local grocery store, and our two kids, ages 7 and 10. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., grab a coffee, and drive the 15‑minute stretch to the service depot where we load trucks and take calls for emergency repairs. The job is physically demanding, but it pays the bills and lets me be home for dinner most nights.

After work I usually help the kids with their homework, then we eat together while the TV is on. On weekends we hit the church basement for the youth basketball league I coach, and sometimes we visit my mother who lives a few miles away. When I have a spare hour I like to watch the local park’s kids play or fix up the old grill in our backyard. Money is tight enough that we watch our utility usage, and I’m always looking for a better deal on car insurance or a discount on the next HVAC certification course.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on lowering taxes and cutting red tape was the only thing that could keep my paycheck from shrinking. The Democratic candidate kept talking about big government programs that seemed like they’d end up as more taxes on the middle class, and I was tired of hearing about “social justice” while my kids’ school was dealing with budget cuts. I also liked the Republican stance on law‑and‑order; the rise in petty crime around the neighborhood made me want a tougher approach.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. My top concerns right now are the rising grocery prices that are eating into the little extra we manage to save each month, and the property tax hikes that make it harder to keep the house in the family. I’m still skeptical of the Democrats—they seem more interested in identity politics than fixing everyday problems. The GOP, while not perfect, at least talks about getting the economy moving and keeping taxes low. I do get annoyed when Republican leaders ignore the real struggles of working‑class folks like me, but I think the party is still the better bet for my family’s bottom line.
