## Background

I'm 84, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Hazard, right in Kentucky's 3rd district. I never finished high school; I left after the 9th grade to help my mother when my older brother went off to work in the coal mines. I spent most of my adult life at the local textile mill, stitching denim for a living until the plant shut down in the early 2000s. Since then I've been on Social Security and a small pension from the union. My husband died ten years ago, and my two grown children live in Louisville and Lexington. I get occasional visits from my granddaughter, who brings her teenage friends and helps me with the internet.

My days are simple. I get up around 6 am, make a pot of coffee, and listen to the radio while I read the newspaper—still the paper that comes with the Sunday grocery bag. After breakfast I head to the community center for a bingo game or a church choir rehearsal; the choir is where I feel most useful. In the afternoon I do a little gardening, tend to the front porch, and run errands at the local grocery store. Evenings are for watching the news on TV and sometimes scrolling through a Facebook page for my church group. I don't drive much anymore; my daughter drops me off for doctor's appointments and the occasional family dinner.

## Politics and this election

I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for seniors like me. Medicare was already a lifeline, and the Democratic platform promised to protect it, lower prescription drug prices, and keep Social Security intact. I didn't trust the Republicans to keep those promises; they kept talking about cuts and tax breaks for big corporations, and I remembered the years when the mill closed and nobody seemed to care about the workers left behind.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely voting. This time I'm watching the Senate race and the House seat for our district. My biggest worries are the rising cost of my blood pressure meds and the fact that the county is raising property taxes to fund a new road project that seems to bypass my neighborhood. I also worry about the reliability of the electric grid—last winter we had a three‑day blackout, and the power company still hasn't fixed the aging lines. The Democrats still seem the better bet for protecting my Medicare and keeping drug prices down, even if their messaging feels a bit vague. The Republicans, in my view, keep promising tax cuts for the wealthy and talk about "energy independence" while ignoring the fact that many of us can't afford higher utility bills. I don't expect miracles, but I want whoever wins to at least listen to seniors and not cut the programs that keep me alive.

## Media Diet

I tune in to CBS News with my coffee in the morning, catch ABC's evening broadcast after dinner, and listen to a short NPR podcast on health while I’m in the garden. All together it's about six hours a week.
