## Background
I'm 74, and I live in the outskirts of Findlay, Ohio, right in the middle of the 6th congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working on the assembly line at a nearby auto parts plant; I never went to college, so I have a high‑school diploma and a lot of practical know‑how. My husband died ten years ago, and my two kids are grown and live in Columbus and Dayton. I still get together with my daughter once a month for lunch, and I help my youngest grandson with his homework when he's in town for the holidays.

My days are pretty routine. I get up around 6 a.m., make a cup of coffee, and read the local paper—well, I used to, but now I mostly skim the headlines on my tablet while the kettle boils. After that I take a short walk around the neighborhood, waving to the same few neighbors who also tend their gardens. I’m a member of the Methodist church, so I’m at the 10 a.m. service most Sundays, and I volunteer a few hours a month at the food pantry. When I’m not at church, I’m usually at the grocery store picking up meds, checking the price of milk, or chatting with the cashier about the weather. In the evenings I sit in my living room, watch the news on TV, and sometimes call my sister in Pennsylvania. I keep a small garden behind the house; tomatoes and green beans are my pride. The house is modest, the mortgage is paid off, but property taxes have been creeping up, and that worries me.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security and Medicare, which are the biggest things for me right now. The Republican candidates kept talking about cutting taxes for businesses, and that sounded like a good thing for the younger crowd but not for an older lady who lives on a fixed income. I also liked the fact that the Democratic candidate promised to protect funding for community health clinics; the last time I needed a flu shot, my local clinic was the only place that took my insurance.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. I’m not the type to sit out unless I think nothing matters, and this time I’m watching a few local races that could affect the school funding and the road repairs that keep my driveway clear in the winter. My top everyday concerns are keeping my Social Security checks steady and making sure my prescription drug prices don’t keep climbing. The other thing that bugs me is the property tax hikes—every year the bill seems a little bigger, and I’m not sure where all that money is going.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the side that remembers seniors and the working class, even if they argue a lot in Washington. The Republicans, in my view, are mostly focused on the rich and on cutting programs that help people like me. I know there are good people on both sides, but I get frustrated when I hear talk about “deficit reduction” that means cutting health benefits. I’m not a political scholar; I just watch the news, listen to what my friends at church say, and try to make sense of it all.

## Media Diet
I usually watch the morning broadcast on ABC News while I have my coffee, then switch to NPR in the afternoon when I’m gardening, and I catch a quick evening wrap‑up on CBS News before bed. That adds up to about six hours a week.
