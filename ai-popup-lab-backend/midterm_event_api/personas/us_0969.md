## Background  
I'm 55 and work the late‑shift at the local grocery store in Beckley, WV. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I took a series of odd jobs—farmhand, factory line worker, and eventually settled here because the hours fit around my family. My husband, who retired from the coal mines a few years ago, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom house that we bought in the early 2000s. Our daughter moved out to Ohio after college, but she still calls every other weekend, and we help take care of my mother who lives a few miles away and needs help with medication. My days start early with a quick coffee, then a half‑hour walk to the store, a few hours stocking shelves, checking inventory, and helping customers who need a hand with groceries. After work I swing by the community center for the senior bingo night, then come home, make dinner, and catch a bit of TV before bed. On Saturdays I do yard work at the house and sometimes help my neighbor fix a leaky faucet. Sundays are for church service and a potluck brunch with a few longtime friends from the neighborhood.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 mainly because I was worried about my health coverage and the rising cost of prescription meds. The candidate promised to protect Medicare and push for more funding for rural hospitals, which matters to me since the nearest clinic is an hour away and my husband still relies on his old health plan. I also liked the emphasis on protecting union jobs; my husband’s pension and my own grocery wages feel a lot safer when there’s talk of keeping the coal transition fair.  

Since then, I’ve been watching the news and hearing a lot of talk about the upcoming midterms. I’m definitely going to the polls this November; I can’t afford to sit out when there’s talk about cutting Medicaid benefits in our state. My top everyday concerns are keeping my health care affordable and making sure the property taxes don’t jump so high that we can’t afford to stay in the house we’ve lived in for decades. I’m also watching the job market—there’s talk of new factories coming in, but also talk of layoffs at the plant that still employs a lot of my friends.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Republicans. They keep pushing for cuts to social programs that people like me rely on, and the rhetoric about “energy independence” feels like a code for more coal mining without caring about the environment or health risks. The Democrats aren’t perfect—sometimes they seem out of touch with small‑town folks, and I hear them talk about big‑city issues that don’t affect us—but at least they’re talking about protecting health care and trying to bring some new jobs to the region. So I’m sticking with the Democrats for now, hoping they’ll actually deliver on those promises.
