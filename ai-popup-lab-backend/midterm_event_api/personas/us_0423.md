## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a modest house on a quiet side street in Jackson, Michigan, right in the heart of the 8th district. I spent most of my working life as a nursing aide at the local hospital after finishing a two‑year program at a community college. I retired about ten years ago, but I still volunteer a few shifts at the senior center because it keeps me busy and lets me see familiar faces. My husband passed away five years back, and my two grown children live in Detroit and Grand Rapids; they call me every Sunday, and the grandkids come up for a few days in the summer. My days usually start with a cup of coffee while I flip through the morning paper, then I head out to the grocery store or the pharmacy, and in the afternoon I either attend the church Bible study group or play bingo at the community hall. Evenings are when I sit on the porch, listen to the radio, and catch up on the news before turning in around 10 p.m.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about Social Security, Medicare, and the prescription‑drug assistance programs that keep my health costs from blowing up. I also appreciated the candidate’s promise to protect funding for the community health clinic where I still volunteer. The campaign felt personal—there were flyers handed out at the senior center and a few of the volunteers knocked on my door to ask how the policies would affect seniors like me. That direct outreach made me feel heard, so I went to the poll on a rainy Tuesday afternoon and cast my ballot.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to vote again. I’m not a big political junkie, but the recent talk about a possible cut to Medicare Part B and the rising cost of insulin have me worried. My top everyday concerns are keeping my medication affordable and making sure my property taxes don’t skyrocket as the county looks to fund new road projects. I’m leaning toward the Democratic ticket because they’ve consistently pushed for stronger safety‑net programs, while the Republicans seem more interested in tax cuts for businesses and less in the day‑to‑day struggles of folks my age. I’m not thrilled with every Democratic proposal, but at this point the other side just feels like they’d leave me and my neighbors out in the cold.

## Media Diet
I usually watch the ABC News morning broadcast while I eat breakfast, tune into NPR’s “All Things Considered” in the evening, and catch up on the latest stories on the CBS News website when I’m waiting at the pharmacy. Altogether that’s about eleven hours a week.
