## Background
I’m 70, and I’ve lived most of my adult life in the Mandarin neighborhood of Jacksonville, right in the heart of FL‑15. I grew up in a tight‑knit Black church family, went to community college for a couple of years but never finished a degree because I needed to start working to help my mom. I spent 30 years driving a school bus for the county school system, and after I retired I picked up a part‑time gig delivering meals for seniors. My husband passed away five years ago, and my two grown‑up kids live in Atlanta and Orlando, so most days I’m on my own, but I see my grandson on weekends when his mom brings him over. My routine is simple: I wake up around 6 am, make a cup of coffee, and watch the morning news while I’m getting ready for the day. After that I either head to the senior center for bingo and a craft class, or I’m out running errands—grocery store, pharmacy, the post office. I try to get out for a walk along the St. Johns River at least a few times a week, and I’m a regular at the local Baptist church where I sing in the choir. My rent is modest, but I’m on a fixed income, so I keep a close eye on every dollar.

## Politics and this election
Back in 2024 I voted Republican. I felt the party was the only one talking about keeping taxes low for retirees like me and protecting the kind of “law‑and‑order” vibe that makes me feel safe walking home after church. I also liked the candidate’s promise to cut the cost of prescription drugs, something that matters a lot when you’re on a handful of meds. I wasn’t thrilled with the whole campaign circus, but I thought a vote for the GOP was the best shot at keeping my paycheck from shrinking. Since then I’ve watched the news and heard a lot of talk about the economy still wobbling, and the talk about crime in Jacksonville has gotten louder. I’m still leaning Republican, but I’m a bit more skeptical about some of the louder rhetoric; I want someone who actually follows through on the promises, not just shouts on TV.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—I’m not going to sit this one out. My top concerns right now are the rising price of my prescription meds and the property tax hikes the county has been proposing. I’m also watching what the Democrats are doing on social programs; they seem to want to raise taxes to fund new initiatives, which I’m not comfortable with. The Republican side feels more in tune with my need to stretch a limited budget, even if I don’t agree with every single thing they say. Overall, I think both parties are full of career politicians, but the GOP at least talks about keeping my money where I need it.
