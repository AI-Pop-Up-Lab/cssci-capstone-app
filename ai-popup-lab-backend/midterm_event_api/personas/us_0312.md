## Background

I'm 40, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Shawnee, just outside Topeka in Kansas’s 2nd district. I work full‑time as an HVAC technician for a regional service company; most days I’m out on the road fixing furnaces and air‑conditioners for homeowners and small businesses. I got my associate’s degree in mechanical systems from a community college in Wichita, which is how I landed the trade.

I’m married to a woman who runs a part‑time daycare out of our garage, and we have two kids: a 15‑year‑old daughter who’s gearing up for her senior year of high school and a 10‑year‑old son who’s obsessed with video games and soccer. Mornings are a blur of getting the kids ready, dropping my daughter at the high‑school bus stop, and heading to the shop by 7 a.m. My work schedule is split‑shift, so I’m often back home for lunch, which is usually a quick sandwich while I watch a local sports update on my phone. Evenings are spent loading the truck, doing a bit of yard work, and trying to keep up with my kids’ homework and the occasional family dinner. On weekends I’m either on call for emergency repairs or helping my wife with the daycare’s paperwork, and we try to squeeze in a drive‑in movie or a hike in the nearby hills when the weather lets us.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long hours at work, the kids’ schedules, and a feeling that the choices on the ballot were just different flavors of the same thing, I ended up staying home on Election Day. The whole process felt like a chore I could’t justify taking time off for, especially when the campaign ads all sounded like the same old promises. I’m pretty cynical about the whole system; it seems like the big parties are more interested in fundraising than fixing the problems that hit my family’s budget.

I’ve thought about it a lot since then, but nothing major has shifted my view. The cost of groceries keeps creeping up, and property taxes in our county are getting harder to swallow, especially with the school district asking for more money to keep programs running. Those are the two things that keep me up at night, more than any abstract policy debate. I’m not planning to head to the polls this November either—“I’m sitting this one out.” Unless there’s a sudden scandal that hits my community directly or a local issue that threatens my kids’ school funding, I just don’t see the point.

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes, but that never translates into lower bills for someone like me who’s already stretched thin. The Democrats push a lot of climate and social justice rhetoric, which sounds good in theory, but I feel like they forget about the practical side of keeping a small town’s economy humming. Both sides have folks I can’t stand, and both have a few people who actually listen to the everyday concerns of working families, but those are rare.
