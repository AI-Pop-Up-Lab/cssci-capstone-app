## Background  
I'm 55 and I work as an HVAC service technician in the Moorhead area of Minnesota's 1st congressional district. My high school diploma and a two‑year associate program in mechanical systems got me the job, and I've been on the road fixing furnaces and air conditioners for about thirty years. I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of town with my wife, who works part‑time at the local grocery store. Our two kids are both out of the house now—one’s a truck driver in North Dakota, the other is finishing a nursing program in Minneapolis. My mother, who’s in her late seventies, still lives about an hour away, and we take turns helping her with groceries and doctor visits.

My day starts around 5:30 a.m.; I grab a cup of coffee, read the headlines on my phone, then head out to the service trucks. The mornings are usually the busiest—lots of schools and offices need heating checked before the workday. I break for a quick sandwich at a diner near the depot, then spend the afternoon on calls, sometimes traveling a few counties away for larger commercial jobs. Evenings are low‑key: dinner with the wife, a couple of episodes of a sitcom, and a quick scroll through my news feeds before I’m in bed around 10 p.m. On weekends I do some yard work, mow the lawn, and help my mom with errands. I’m not a big club‑goer; my social life mostly revolves around the local church potluck and a weekly poker night at the community center.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who depend on steady wages and affordable energy. The tax cuts they pushed helped keep my paycheck from shrinking, and their stance on deregulating the energy sector meant I didn’t have to worry about new rules making the cost of parts sky‑high. I also liked the emphasis on “law and order” after hearing about a rise in property crimes in our town last year.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening in Washington, but most of my opinion still comes from the outlets I watch and the conversations at the hardware store. I’m definitely planning to vote in the 2026 midterms; I don’t want to sit this one out unless something drastic changes. My biggest worries right now are the grocery bills creeping up and the property tax hike the county board is proposing to fund a new community center. I’m also uneasy about the push for more aggressive climate regulations that could raise the cost of running a heating business.

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as out of touch with the everyday grind—always talking about green energy and student debt, but not about the guy who has to fix a broken furnace in a freezing Minnesota winter. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least seem to understand that people need jobs, low taxes, and a government that stays out of my business. I’m not a political scholar; I just want the next election to keep my paycheck steady and my bills manageable.
