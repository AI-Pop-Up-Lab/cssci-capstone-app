## Background
I'm 55 and I work as a maintenance supervisor at a manufacturing plant in Warner Robins, GA. The plant runs three shifts, so my day usually starts around 5 a.m. with a quick coffee and a look at the weather before I head to the factory floor. I make sure the machines are running, deal with the occasional breakdown, and keep the safety logs straight. After my shift ends at 2 p.m., I swing by the local grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and then head home to my wife of 30 years. We live in a modest ranch house with a small yard where my wife tends a vegetable garden—she grows collard greens and tomatoes most summers. Our two kids are both in college out of state, so the house is pretty quiet most evenings.

On weekends I go to the First Baptist Church downtown, where I help with the choir and sometimes drive the church van for seniors who need rides to appointments. I also like to watch the Braves on TV when I can catch a game, and I’m part of a local basketball league that meets at the community center every Thursday night. The routine is simple: work, family, church, a bit of sports, and trying to keep the house in shape. I don’t have a college degree, but I’ve taken a few night‑class courses on basic accounting to help with the household budget.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the candidate’s message about keeping taxes low and protecting law‑and‑order resonated with my own concerns. I’m not a political scholar, but I remember thinking that the Democratic platform seemed to promise more spending that could raise my property taxes and push up the cost of gasoline—things that bite into my paycheck. The Republican candidate also talked about supporting the manufacturing sector, which directly affects my job security at the plant.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the economy. Gas prices have been a pain, and I’ve seen the plant cut a few overtime slots, so I’m still leaning toward the party that promises to keep regulation light and taxes down. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’m going to the same precinct I used last time. My top everyday concerns are the price of groceries—especially fresh produce for my wife’s garden meals—and whether my property taxes will go up again next year. I’m pretty skeptical of the Democrats; they often sound like they want more government programs that end up costing more for folks like me. The Republicans, for the most part, seem to understand that I’m just trying to keep a roof over my family’s head and a stable job at the plant. I do wish they’d talk more about improving local infrastructure, but overall I feel they’re the better bet for my day‑to‑day life.
