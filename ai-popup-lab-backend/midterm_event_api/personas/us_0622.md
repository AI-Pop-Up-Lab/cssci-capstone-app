## Background
I'm 30, and I live in Los Lunas, just a few miles south of Albuquerque in NM‑3. I work full‑time as an administrative assistant at a community health clinic downtown. Most days I’m up by 6 am, grab a coffee from the corner stand, and drive the 20‑minute commute on the highway while the radio plays classic rock. My shift runs from 8 am to 4:30 pm, with a short lunch break where I usually run errands—pick up groceries, drop off a package, or swing by the laundromat. After work I head back home, share a small two‑bedroom apartment with a roommate who’s in grad school for engineering. We split rent and utilities, which is tight but manageable on my salary.

On weekends I like to hike the nearby Sandia Mountains when the weather’s good, or meet friends at the local taco joint for a quick bite. I’m not a big fan of big‑ticket vacations; a few nights at a motel in Santa Fe feels like enough of a break. I’m not married, no kids, and my parents live a couple of hours away in a small town near Santa Fe, so I call them on Sundays but don’t see them often. In my spare time I stream a bit of Netflix and scroll through social media, mostly Instagram and TikTok, to keep up with friends and memes.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between juggling a full‑time job, a cramped apartment, and the constant worry about rent and grocery bills, politics felt like another thing I couldn’t fit into the schedule. I also got the sense that my vote wouldn’t change anything—every campaign ad I saw seemed the same, and the candidates kept promising things that never materialized in my neighborhood. That cynicism turned into a habit; I figured why bother if the system just keeps ignoring folks like me?

I’m sitting this one out again in the 2026 midterms. Nothing dramatic has happened locally that would pull me into the booth, and the same routine of work and bills still dominates my days. My biggest everyday concerns are keeping up with rent and watching the price of groceries climb higher each month. I’m also worried about the reliability of my broadband service, which feels like a necessity for my job and staying in touch with family.

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Republicans. Their rhetoric feels out of touch with the struggles of working‑class people in New Mexico, especially on issues like immigration and health care. The Democrats seem a bit more sympathetic, but I’m not convinced they deliver on promises either. Overall, I feel both sides are stuck in the same game, and I’m skeptical that any candidate will actually prioritize the everyday problems I face.
