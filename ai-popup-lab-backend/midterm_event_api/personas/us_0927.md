## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a small bungalow on the east side of Spokane, right near the river trail. My husband died ten years ago, and my two kids moved to Seattle and Portland when they were in their thirties. I get visits from my granddaughter once a month, and we usually bake tamales together when she’s in town. I never went to college; I finished high school in the late 1950s and have been working ever since. These days I pick up a part‑time shift at the senior center, helping with the lunch line and running errands for the residents. It keeps me out of the house and lets me stay active.

My days are pretty routine. I wake up around 6 am, make a cup of coffee, and turn on the TV while I read the newspaper (the print copy I get delivered on Sundays). After breakfast I head to the senior center for a few hours, then come home for lunch. In the afternoons I tend my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few herbs—and sometimes meet up with a few neighbors for a card game at the community room. Evenings are for TV; I usually catch the local news at 6 pm, then sit down with a bowl of soup and watch the national broadcast. On Sundays I go to mass at the nearby parish and then spend a couple of hours on the porch, watching the kids play in the park across the street.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping taxes low and protecting our borders. I’m not a political scholar, but I remember hearing about the rising cost of prescription meds and how the GOP was promising to let the market figure out cheaper options. I also liked the talk about law‑and‑order and felt the Democrats were trying to change too much, too fast, especially when it comes to school curricula and the way they talk about immigration.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the price of my insulin and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up on my home. I’m also watching the local job market; a couple of factories closed a few years back and it’s left a lot of folks in the area struggling. I think the Democrats keep promising big programs but they never seem to actually lower the taxes that hit retirees like me. The Republicans, in my view, at least talk about cutting wasteful spending and giving people more control over their own money. I’m not thrilled with either side, but the GOP feels closer to what I need right now.
