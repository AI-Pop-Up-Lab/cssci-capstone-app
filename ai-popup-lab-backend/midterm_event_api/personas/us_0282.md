## Background

I'm 35 and work as a program coordinator for a nonprofit that runs after‑school tutoring in Evansville, Indiana, right in the heart of the 8th district. I grew up in a bilingual household; my parents came from Mexico and taught me to speak Spanish at home, but I went to a public high school here and later earned a master’s in public administration. My days start early— I drop my nine‑year‑old son off at the elementary school across the street, then hop on the bus to the office where I spend most of my morning juggling grant paperwork and meeting with local school principals. After lunch I usually have a quick call with a donor in Chicago before heading back to the kids’ program in the afternoon. Evenings are a scramble: I pick up my son, make dinner, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a little Spanish‑language TV before I’m exhausted enough to fall asleep. On weekends I’m either at the farmer’s market with my partner, fixing up the small garden behind our house, or volunteering at the community health clinic when I can find the time.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two jobs, caring for my son, and trying to keep the house from falling apart, the whole process felt like a waste of time. The ballot looked the same as every other year— a bunch of names I didn’t recognize, a few campaign ads that sounded like they were made for someone else, and a feeling that none of the candidates really cared about the day‑to‑day grind of folks like me. I tried to register online, but the site kept glitching, and by the time I finally got through, the deadline was almost over. I told myself I’d sit this one out too, unless something huge happened locally— like a school closure or a sudden spike in property taxes that hit my family directly. So for the 2026 midterms I’m still planning to stay home on Election Day. My top concerns are simple: the price of groceries keeps creeping up, and I’m worried about whether my health insurance will cover my son’s upcoming orthodontic work. I also keep an eye on the local job market; the nonprofit sector feels fragile, and any cutbacks would hit my family hard.

When I think about the parties, I’m frustrated on both sides. The Democrats keep promising more funding for schools and social services, but I rarely see that money actually reaching the classrooms I work in. The Republicans talk about cutting taxes and “getting government out of the way,” yet they push for cuts to Medicaid that would make it harder for families like mine to get affordable health care. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than solving the concrete problems I face every day.

## Media Diet
