## Background
I'm 55, and I live in Rhinelander, a small town up in Wisconsin's 8th district. I work as a maintenance supervisor at the regional hospital, making sure the building, the HVAC systems, and the backup generators keep running. I went to a community college for a couple of years studying mechanical systems, but I never finished a degree. My wife, who retired from the local school district, and I own the house we bought in the early 2000s. Our two kids are in their late twenties—one lives in Milwaukee working as a nurse, the other moved to Chicago for a tech job—so most evenings the house is quiet. I still drive the old Chevy pickup to the hospital each morning, grab a coffee at the diner on the way, and spend my lunch break checking the news on my phone. After work I usually stop by the gym for a quick workout, then come home to cook dinner, watch a bit of TV, and help my wife with her garden. Sundays are for church and a potluck with the neighbors. I take the occasional trip to Madison to vote or handle paperwork, but most of my life revolves around the hospital, the house, and the small community events that keep us connected.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about the health issues that hit my workplace hard. The promise to protect Medicare, expand prescription‑drug coverage, and pump money into rural hospitals was something I could get behind. I also liked the candidate’s stance on criminal‑justice reform; seeing too many young Black men from our area get tangled up in the system made me want a change. Since then, I've kept an eye on how the administration follows through, and while I’m still skeptical about how fast things move, I think the direction is right. I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the property‑tax hikes the county has been pushing to fund road repairs. I’m also nervous about the local manufacturing plant that’s considering a layoff—if that happens, a lot of families in town could be hit hard. When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones more likely to protect social programs and push for equity, even if they sometimes get bogged down in bureaucracy. Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about cutting taxes but those cuts usually end up favoring big corporations, and I don’t see much benefit for folks like me who are already stretching a paycheck. Both sides argue a lot, but in my day‑to‑day life I care more about who actually delivers on health care and local jobs than about any lofty rhetoric.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m driving, catch the ABC News evening roundup after dinner, and skim a couple of Newsweek articles on the weekend. All together it adds up to about four hours a week.
