## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Ammon, a small town that sits just north of the Snake River in Idaho's 2nd district. I grew up in a farm family that moved to Idaho when I was a teen, and I spent most of my adult life working as a clerk at the local grocery store. I never finished a degree, but I took some college courses in business and Spanish, which helped me communicate with the growing Latino community here. I retired ten years ago, and now most of my days revolve around my garden, the weekly church service, and checking in on my two adult children who live in Boise and Salt Lake City. My grandson visits every summer and helps me with the computer, which I still consider a mystery most of the time. I get up around 7 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and sit at the kitchen table while the morning news plays on the TV. After that I tend to my tomatoes, walk the short trail behind my house, and sometimes run errands for neighbors who can’t get around. In the evenings I like to knit while listening to a radio program or reading a printed newspaper. My health is decent for my age, but I have to take medication for high blood pressure and arthritis, and I try to stay active to keep the doctor happy.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Social Security and Medicare, which are the biggest things on my mind at my age. The Republican messaging about cutting taxes sounded nice, but I couldn't shake the worry that any cuts to federal health programs would hit people like me who rely on those benefits. I also liked that the Democrat promised to protect Medicare drug price negotiations, something I need because my prescription costs have gone up every year. The campaign visits in my district were few, but I saw a flyer at the community center that mentioned expanding broadband, and that would help my grandson stay connected when he helps me with the computer.

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hike the county council approved last spring. If the tax bill keeps going up, I might have to sell part of my garden to cover it, and that would be a real blow. I also worry about the shortage of home health aides; my daughter lives far away, and I need someone to help with daily chores when my arthritis flares up. I think the Democratic Party still feels more attuned to seniors and to the needs of working‑class families, while the Republicans seem focused on cutting government spending even if it means squeezing the safety net. I’m not a political analyst, but I feel like the Democrats give me a little more security in my later years. That said, I do get annoyed when both sides trade insults on TV and forget that we all have to live in the same community.

## Media Diet
I spend about nine hours a week with the news: NPR in the mornings while I eat breakfast, ABC News articles in the afternoon when I’m on the porch, and I occasionally scroll through CBS News in the evening after dinner.
