## Background
I'm 20, and I live in a modest duplex in Kenner, just outside New Orleans. I dropped out of high school at 16 and have been working full‑time at a fast‑food restaurant ever since. My shift runs from 2 p.m. to midnight, six days a week, so my days are a blur of flipping burgers, cleaning the grill, and trying to keep up with the endless rush of orders. I sleep in a narrow bedroom that I share with my mom, who works part‑time as a housekeeper, and my 15‑year‑old brother, who is still in school. On my days off I usually just hang out at the local convenience store, scroll through TikTok, or help my mom with the grocery list. I don’t have a car, so I rely on the bus to get to work and to the occasional friend's house. My rent is $650 a month, and I’m always watching my balance to make sure I can pay the electric bill. I’ve never been involved in any clubs or community groups; my world mostly revolves around work, the bus schedule, and trying to stretch my limited cash until the next paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I feel like none of the candidates ever really talked to people like me. Between two jobs, late shifts, and trying to keep the lights on, I just didn’t have the time or the energy to even look up where my polling place was. The whole process feels like a distant thing that only matters to folks with a college degree or a lot of free time. I also think the system is rigged—my friends who tried to register always got mixed messages, and the last time I tried to look up the candidates, the websites were full of jargon I didn’t get. So I stayed home, and I’m pretty sure I’ll do the same in 2026 unless something hits close to home, like a big rent hike or a local scandal that makes the news front page.

I’m sitting this one out. My biggest worries right now are paying rent and keeping groceries on the table. When I do think about politics, I see the Democrats as talking a lot about climate change and social programs but never delivering anything that helps someone working a minimum‑wage job. The Republicans, on the other hand, promise lower taxes and “real” jobs, but they also seem to ignore the fact that most of us can’t afford even basic healthcare. Both sides feel like they’re speaking a different language than the one I live in. I’m not a big fan of any party; they both seem to forget the people who are just trying to get by day by day.
