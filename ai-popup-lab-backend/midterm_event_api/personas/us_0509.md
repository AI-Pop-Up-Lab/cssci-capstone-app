## Background  
I'm 65 and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Great Falls, just a short drive from the Blackfeet Reservation where my family has lived for generations. I spent two years at a community college learning dental hygiene, and after raising three kids I took a part‑time job at the local health clinic cleaning and prepping rooms. The work keeps me on my feet, and I like the feeling of being useful, especially when the clinic helps a lot of our tribal members who otherwise have to travel far for care.

My husband passed away five years ago, so most days I'm on my own, but my daughter lives in Missoula and visits us every few months with her two grandkids. When they're here we go fishing on the Missouri River or take a drive up to the mountains for a weekend hunt. I also help organize the monthly powwow committee, which means I’m often out early setting up tables or later cleaning up after the dances.

My routine is simple. I get up around 6 a.m., brew a strong cup of coffee, and flip on the radio while I make breakfast. After a quick check of my phone for news headlines, I head to the clinic for my shift, usually from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. In the afternoon I run errands—picking up groceries, filling a prescription, or dropping off laundry at the laundromat. Evenings are for TV, a crossword, and sometimes a video call with my daughter. On Sundays I attend church, then spend a couple of hours at the community center helping the younger women learn basic computer skills.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking straight about the things that matter to me: keeping taxes low, protecting our land from over‑regulation, and standing up for a strong military. I was especially drawn to the candidate’s promise to support small‑business owners like the clinic where I work, and to push back against what I see as endless federal red tape that makes it harder for tribal enterprises to thrive.

Since then I’ve watched a lot of the same news outlets I trust, and I still think the GOP is the better choice for folks who want to keep their hard‑earned money and preserve the way of life we’ve built here. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about climate policies that would hurt ranchers and hunters, and they push for higher taxes that would squeeze retirees on fixed incomes. I’m not a political analyst, but I’ve seen enough promises from both sides to know that when it comes down to it, the Republican platform lines up more with my everyday concerns.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the looming water‑rights disputes that could affect our farms and the river we rely on for drinking water. I also worry about the shortage of health workers in our rural area; if the government keeps cutting funding for community clinics, people like me will have to watch more neighbors go without care.

Overall, I trust the GOP to keep taxes low and respect property rights, while I feel the Democrats are too focused on big‑city issues and don’t understand the realities of life out here in Montana.
