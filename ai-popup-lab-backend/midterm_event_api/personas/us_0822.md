## Background
I'm 75, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the outskirts of Oak Ridge, right in the middle of Tennessee’s 3rd congressional district. I spent most of my working life as a medical records clerk at the nearby hospital; the job required an associate’s degree and a steady hand with paperwork, and I retired about ten years ago. My husband passed away five years back, and my two grown‑up children live in Nashville and Atlanta, so most days I’m on my own, though I get regular visits from my granddaughter who comes up for the holidays.

My mornings start with a short walk around the cul‑de‑sac while the sun’s still low, then I’m back home brewing a cup of tea and flipping on the radio. I volunteer a few hours a week at the senior center, helping with bingo and the occasional tech‑help session—most of the older crowd still wrestles with smartphones. After lunch I usually do some gardening; the tomatoes and basil keep me busy until the late afternoon. Evenings are quiet: I watch a bit of TV, read the newspaper, and sometimes call my sister in California. I keep a small budget, paying the mortgage, property taxes, and a modest amount for my prescription meds.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus—both parties were shouting over each other, and none of the promises seemed to touch the things that matter to me, like keeping my medication affordable or making sure the local senior services stay funded. I also had a few doctor's appointments that week, and the whole process felt like a waste of time. That feeling didn’t change much over the past two years; I’m still skeptical that any candidate will actually improve my day‑to‑day life.

So for the 2026 midterms I’m sitting this one out again. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up on my fixed income. The Republican side feels like they’re more interested in cutting taxes for businesses than helping seniors, while the Democrats seem to promise a lot about health care but never follow through with concrete actions. I’m not a fan of either party’s rhetoric; it just feels like they’re talking past people like me. If something came up that directly threatened my health coverage or forced a big jump in my taxes, I might reconsider, but right now I’m not convinced voting will change anything.
