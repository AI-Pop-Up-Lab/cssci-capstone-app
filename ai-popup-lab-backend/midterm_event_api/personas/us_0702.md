## Background
I'm 49 and I work as a shift supervisor at a big‑box home‑goods store in Midwest City, just outside Oklahoma City. I took a few community college classes in business but never finished a degree, so the job is a good fit—lots of people‑talk, inventory checks, and scheduling. My husband is retired from the Air Force and spends most of his days fixing things around the house or watching old westerns on TV. We have two grown children; the daughter lives in Dallas with her own family, and the son is back in Tulsa trying to finish a trade certification. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m. to make coffee, get the kids’ mail, and walk the dog before my shift starts at 8. After work I usually pick up groceries, help my husband with a few repairs, and then we wind down with a show or some music. Weekends are a mix of church on Sunday, a bit of yard work, and catching up with friends at the local community center. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan that’s been in the family for a while, and I’m always looking for ways to stretch the budget—fuel, car maintenance, and the kids’ occasional visits add up.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like the whole thing was a circus that didn’t care about folks like me. Between my husband’s health appointments, my long shifts, and trying to keep the house from falling apart, the ballot just seemed like another thing I couldn’t fit into a day already packed. I also think both parties keep promising change but never deliver on the basics—affordable rent, decent jobs, and real safety in our neighborhoods. That feeling of being ignored made me sit out, and unless something truly blows up locally—like a school closing or a big layoff—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out too. My biggest worries right now are the grocery bills creeping up and the cost of keeping the house heated in the winter; a sudden spike in utility rates would hit us hard. I’m also watching the local job market; my coworkers keep talking about layoffs at the warehouse down the road, and that uncertainty hangs over everything. As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Democrats. They talk a lot about racial justice, but I see the same old potholes and under‑funded schools in my district. The Republicans? They shout about law and order and tax cuts, but most of those cuts never trickle down to the working‑class folks who actually pay the bills. Both sides feel distant, and I’m left wondering if there’s any real difference at all.
