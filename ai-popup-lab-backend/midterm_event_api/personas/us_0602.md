## Background
I'm 45 and work full‑time as a retail floor supervisor at a big‑box store in Denville, New Jersey, right in the heart of the 5th district. My husband was laid off two years ago, so I’m the main breadwinner; we’re holding down a modest mortgage on our two‑bedroom house and trying to keep the kids—ages 12 and 9—out of trouble after school. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30, making coffee, packing lunches, and shuffling the kids onto the school bus before heading to the store for the opening shift at 7. My day is a mix of stocking shelves, handling returns, and dealing with the occasional angry customer, which wears on me but pays the bills. Evenings are a blur of homework checks, dinner that’s usually something quick from the freezer, and a quick scroll through my phone before I’m exhausted enough to fall asleep around 10. On weekends I try to squeeze in a grocery run at the local market, a bit of yard work, and sometimes a drive to the park with the kids when the weather’s decent. I don’t have a lot of spare cash, so I keep an eye on any discount deals or free community events.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like none of the choices mattered to my day‑to‑day life. The whole process seemed like a circus, and between juggling work, the kids, and trying to stretch the paycheck, there wasn’t any energy left to get to a polling place. I also thought my single vote wouldn’t change anything, especially when the big‑ticket issues felt distant from the rent I’m trying to keep up with and the grocery prices that keep climbing. That same sense of cynicism is still with me, so I’m sitting this one out in the 2026 midterms unless something really hits home—like a sudden cut to a local school program or a major layoff at the store that forces us to move. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with the mortgage and making sure we can afford enough food without cutting back on basics. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to promise a lot of social programs but I hear them talking about taxes that would hit middle‑class families like mine. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep pushing big‑government cuts that feel like they’d leave services we rely on—like public transportation and community health clinics—out of reach. Both sides feel out of touch, and I’m just trying to get through the next month without a budget crisis.
