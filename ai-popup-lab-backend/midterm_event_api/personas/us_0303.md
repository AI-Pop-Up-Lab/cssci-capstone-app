## Background
I'm 80, and I live in Manhattan, Kansas, right near the campus of Kansas State University. I grew up in a farm town a few counties west, but after I finished an associate's program in office administration at a community college, I spent most of my adult life working as a medical‑records clerk at the local hospital. I retired ten years ago, but I still help the front desk on a part‑time basis because I like the routine and the paycheck. My husband died five years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live in Denver and Austin. I see my grandchildren every other weekend; they bring fresh fruit and a lot of noise, which keeps me from getting too bored. My days usually start with a cup of coffee on the porch while I watch the sunrise over the wheat fields, then a quick walk around the neighborhood to check on my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few roses. After that I sort the mail, pay the bills, and spend an hour or two on the computer checking the news or video‑chatting with my family. In the evenings I attend a senior‑center bingo night twice a week, and on Sundays I go to my local Catholic church, where I help with the choir's hymn books. I drive a small sedan, keep a list of doctor appointments on the fridge, and try to stay active enough to avoid the aches that come with my age.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about Medicare, prescription‑drug prices, and protecting Social Security—things that matter a lot when you're in my thirties of life. I also liked the candidate's promise to invest in rural broadband, which would help my grandchildren stay connected and give our small town a better chance at attracting new jobs. The campaign felt personal; I got a phone call from a volunteer who asked how the health‑care plan would affect my current prescriptions, and that made me feel heard. Since then, I've kept an eye on the news and the occasional town‑hall meeting, and I still think the Democrats are the better bet for seniors, even if I don't agree with everything they say about taxes or climate regulation.

For the 2026 midterms I'm definitely voting. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of my prescription meds and the property tax hike the county proposed to fund a new school roof. I also keep thinking about how grocery prices keep creeping up, which squeezes my fixed income. I see the Republican Party as mostly focused on cutting taxes for businesses and rolling back regulations, which sometimes hurts the safety nets I rely on. The Democrats, in my view, are more attentive to health‑care and senior services, even if they sometimes overpromise on things like climate policy that feel distant from my daily life. I'm not a political analyst; I just want the side that will keep my doctor visits affordable and my grocery bill manageable.
