## Background  
I'm 65, and I live in Sparks, just east of Reno, in Nevada's 4th congressional district. I work part‑time as an administrative assistant for the local community college, mostly answering phones, filing paperwork, and helping students with enrollment questions. I never finished a bachelor's degree, but I took a few business courses after high school and that got me the job. My husband passed away three years ago, so I'm on my own, but I have two grown‑up children who live in California and visit a few times a year.  

My day usually starts around 6 a.m. with a cup of black coffee and a quick scan of the news on my tablet while I watch the sunrise over the foothills. I head to the college at 7:30, work until about 12, then come home for lunch—usually a sandwich and the occasional take‑out from the Asian market down the street. In the afternoon I run errands, pick up groceries, or volunteer at the Buddhist temple where I help organize the senior meals program. Evenings are for TV, a bit of reading, and a walk around the neighborhood if the weather's nice. On weekends I tend to visit my kids, go to the farmers market, or meet friends for dim sum. I keep my car, a modest sedan, and try to stay on top of the maintenance because I don't want to rely on anyone else for rides.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I was worried about my health coverage and Social Security. My doctor told me I need a new heart monitor, and the cost of the device was a big part of why I leaned toward the party that promised to protect Medicare and keep prescription drug prices down. I also liked the emphasis on immigration reform; being Asian and having relatives overseas, I felt the Democrats were more compassionate about family reunification.  

Since then, my feelings have stayed pretty steady. I still think the Democrats are the better bet for seniors like me, especially when it comes to preserving Medicare and funding community health clinics. At the same time, I'm a bit annoyed that the party seems divided on some economic issues and that the debates in Congress feel more like theater than problem‑solving. I don't trust the Republicans much; their tax‑cut rhetoric sounds good until you see it trickle down to the wealthy while cutting funding for programs that help people my age. Their stance on immigration also feels hostile, and I worry about how that will affect my nieces and nephews who are trying to bring over family members.  

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription meds and the property tax hike the county approved last year, which makes it harder to keep my house in good shape. I see the Democrats as the party that at least pretends to care about seniors and immigrants, while the Republicans come across as more interested in cutting taxes for the rich and pushing a cultural agenda that doesn't reflect my life.
