## Background  
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a cashier‑shift lead at the grocery store in downtown Pocatello. I graduated high school in Boise, then moved back east to be closer to my parents, who own a small farm a few miles out of town. I live in a rented two‑bedroom apartment with my younger brother; we split the rent and utilities and take turns cooking. My mornings start around 5 am because I have to be at the store by 6 am to open the doors. After a quick coffee, I catch the local news on the radio while loading the shelves, then spend the next eight hours on the floor, ringing up groceries and handling the occasional angry customer. My evenings are short – I usually grab a quick dinner, help my brother with his college applications, and then scroll through my phone before crashing around 11 pm. On weekends I help my parents with farm chores, mostly loading hay bales or fixing fences, and I try to squeeze in a few hours at the community center where I volunteer to teach basic computer skills to seniors. I don’t have a car; I rely on my parents’ pickup for longer trips.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on the economy resonated with me. My paycheck hasn’t grown much in years, and the idea of lower taxes and fewer regulations sounded like a way to keep the grocery store from cutting hours. I also liked the talk about securing the border and protecting “American jobs,” which seemed important in a place where many people still work in agriculture or the military. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out. The price of gas and groceries keeps climbing, and the store has had to tighten its budget, which makes me nervous. Still, I think the Democratic platform pushes too much toward big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with folks like me who just want a stable job and affordable food.  

I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the stability of my job – the store is talking about cutting a shift next month. I also worry about property taxes on my parents’ farm; any increase could push them into debt. As far as the parties go, I see Republicans as the ones who at least try to keep taxes low and talk about personal responsibility, even if they sometimes ignore the struggles of working‑class families. The Democrats, in my view, keep talking about big climate plans and social programs that sound good on paper but end up raising taxes and creating more bureaucracy. I’m not a political scholar, but I feel the GOP is the safer bet for my day‑to‑day life.
