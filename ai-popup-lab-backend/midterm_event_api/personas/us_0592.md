## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s tax policies were the only thing keeping my wife’s bakery from being squeezed by rising costs, and I trusted their promise to keep regulation light for the broadband industry I work in. I also liked the emphasis on a strong national defense; it just seemed like the safer bet for the future. Since then I’ve watched the news and seen a few things that make me uneasy—like the higher gas prices that hit my commute and a couple of local school budget cuts that affect my kids—but I still think the GOP is the better option for keeping my household financially stable. I’m definitely going to the polls this November; I’ve already marked the date on my phone and set a reminder to vote early if the lines get long. My top concerns right now are the rising property taxes on our house and the grocery price spikes that make it harder to stretch the family budget. I view the Democratic Party as well‑meaning but too focused on big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with small‑town New England realities. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting taxes and limiting regulation, which lines up with what I need day‑to‑day.
