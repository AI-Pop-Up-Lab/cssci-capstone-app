## Background  
I'm 80 and I live in a modest ranch house on the outskirts of Cheyenne, right in the heart of Wyoming's 1st district. I spent most of my working life as an electrician for a regional utility; I never finished a degree, but I took a handful of courses at the community college when I was younger, which is why I list “some college.” I retired five years ago, but I still take on odd jobs—fixing wiring for neighbors, helping the local senior center with light repairs, and keeping the garden tidy. My wife passed away three years back, so it's just me and a big, lazy Labrador named Scout. My two kids live in Denver and Salt Lake City; they call every few weeks, and I try to visit them when I can afford the drive.  

My days start early. I brew a pot of coffee, listen to NPR’s morning news while the sun comes up over the prairie, then check the mail and tend to the garden. Around noon I’ll take a break for a sandwich and maybe watch a local high‑school football game on TV. In the evenings I sit on the porch, flip on the TV for the evening news on CBS or ABC, and read the paper before heading to bed. On weekends I’m at the hardware store picking up supplies, or I go to the county fair with Scout, where I’ll chat with folks about the weather and the price of feed.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I was worried about my health insurance and the rising cost of my prescription meds. The Republican platform seemed to focus on oil and deregulation, and I felt that wouldn’t help an older person trying to stay on his meds and keep his home heated. I also liked the candidate’s promise to expand rural broadband, something I’ve been waiting on for years.  

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm definitely going to the polls. My top everyday concerns are the price of my prescription drugs and getting reliable internet so I can stay in touch with my kids and order groceries online. I’m also watching property taxes; the county has talked about raising them to fund a new road, and that would bite into my fixed income.  

When I think about the parties, I see Democrats as the ones who actually talk about health care and social safety nets, even if they sometimes sound a bit out of touch with small‑town life. The Republicans, in my view, are still wrapped up in the oil business and cut‑back rhetoric that doesn’t help folks like me who need steady services. Both sides argue a lot, and I’m not impressed by the endless bickering, but I lean toward the side that seems to care more about seniors and rural issues.
