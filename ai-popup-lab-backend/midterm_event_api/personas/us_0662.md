## Background
I'm 45 and I live in the little hamlet of Tannersville, right in the heart of NY‑23. I work full‑time as a medical‑billing clerk for the county health clinic; the job mostly involves typing up insurance forms, answering phones, and trying to keep the ledger straight. I didn't finish my associate's degree in business administration, but the few college classes I took gave me enough confidence to handle the software we use. My husband runs a landscaping business that takes us out to the farms and cabins around the Catskills a lot, and we have two kids – a 16‑year‑old daughter who's busy with varsity soccer and a 9‑year‑old son who still needs a car seat on the way to school. Most mornings I drop the kids off at the local middle school, grab a coffee at the diner across the street, and then drive the half‑hour to the clinic. After work I usually swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids from practice, and we eat dinner at home while the dog, a rescued Labrador named Willow, circles the kitchen table. On weekends we either hike the nearby state parks or help my husband with a few jobs for his crew; the cash flow is tight, but we manage to keep the house paid and the kids in school. I spend my free time scrolling through social media on my phone, mostly in the evenings after the kids are in bed, and I watch a bit of TV when the kids are out playing.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican. I was frustrated with the rising property taxes on our home and the way the state seemed to keep raising the cost of utilities without giving back to rural communities. The Republican candidate promised to look at cutting wasteful spending and to protect the right to own a gun, which matters to my husband and me because we keep a rifle for hunting and feel a little uneasy about the new city‑style gun regulations being pushed statewide. I also liked the talk about supporting small businesses – my husband's landscaping crew depends on local contracts, and the idea of cutting red tape sounded good. Since then, I've kept an eye on how the state legislature is handling the budget, and I still think the Democrats are too focused on big‑city issues and don't understand the challenges of living in a place where the nearest hospital is an hour away. That said, I'm not a political junkie; I mostly care about how policies affect my family's bottom line.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the soaring grocery prices and the property tax bill that's set to go up again next year. If the state keeps squeezing us, we might have to pull my son out of his after‑school program, and that's not something I can afford. I see the Democratic Party as out of touch, always talking about climate goals and social programs that feel like they belong in Manhattan, while the Republican Party, despite its flaws, at least talks about keeping taxes low and giving more control back to local folks. I get annoyed when the news talks about "progress" without mentioning how that progress will be paid for, and I think the GOP is more honest about the trade‑offs, even if they sometimes sound harsh.
