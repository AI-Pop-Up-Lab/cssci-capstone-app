## Background
I'm 50, and I live in the Westside neighborhood of Jacksonville, right near the old industrial park that’s been turned into a strip of low‑rent apartments. I work full‑time as a cashier at a grocery store, and on the evenings I pick up extra shifts as a house cleaner for a few families in the area. My daughter, Maya, is 24 and lives with me while she finishes a nursing program; we split the rent and utilities, which keeps me on a constant budget. I also help take care of my mother, who has arthritis and needs a little assistance getting around. Most mornings I’m up by 5 am to catch the bus, grab a coffee from the corner deli, and be at the store before the rush. After my shift I swing by the laundromat, then head home to cook dinner—usually something cheap and filling like beans and rice. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, church services on Sunday mornings, and sitting on the porch with neighbors, swapping stories about the rising cost of everything. I don’t have a driver's license, so my world revolves around the bus routes and the few people I see at the store and church.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between juggling three jobs, caring for my mom, and trying to keep my daughter in school, politics just felt like another thing I couldn’t fit into a schedule already packed with bills and shift changes. I also got the sense that none of the candidates really cared about people like me—my neighborhood never seemed to be on any campaign flyer, and the promises sounded the same as every election. The whole process felt distant, like I was filling out a form that wouldn’t change my rent or my mom’s medication costs. Because of that, I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something major hits my block—like a sudden clinic closure or a big layoff at the grocery—I don’t see a reason to make the trip to the polling place.

My biggest everyday worries are keeping up with rent and making sure there’s enough money for groceries and my mom’s prescriptions. I also worry about the safety of the streets around my building; the recent break‑ins have made the evenings feel tense. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats talk a lot about helping Black communities but seem to forget about the low‑income folks who can’t even get a decent job. The Republicans keep pushing tax cuts and “law and order” rhetoric, which feels like they’re ignoring the systemic issues that make my life harder. Neither side feels like they’re listening to people who are just trying to get by.

## Media Diet
I usually watch the morning news on ABC and catch the evening wrap‑up on CBS after my shift, plus I skim headlines on NBC’s website when I’m on the bus. All together it’s about seven hours a week.
