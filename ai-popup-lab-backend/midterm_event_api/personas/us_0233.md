## Background  
I'm 35 and I live in a modest single‑family home on the edge of Sioux City, right near the river that marks the border with Nebraska. I work as a logistics coordinator for a regional trucking firm that moves corn and soybeans across the Midwest. My job is mostly desk‑based: I schedule routes, handle paperwork for drivers, and make sure the freight software stays up‑to‑date. I went to Iowa State, got a bachelor's in business administration, and landed this gig right after graduation.  

I'm married to Maria, who runs a small catering business out of our garage, and we have two kids—Ana, 7, and Luis, 4. Mornings are a blur of cereal, school buses, and trying to get the kids dressed in time for their kindergarten and first‑grade classes. After dropping them off, I drive about 20 minutes to the warehouse office, where I spend most of the day on a computer and on the phone. Lunch is usually a sandwich I bring from home while I catch up on a quick news feed on my phone.  

Evenings are a mix of helping Ana with her reading, cooking dinner with Maria (we rotate between tacos and Midwestern comfort food), and trying to keep the house in order. On weekends I help my parents with yard work back in a smaller town about an hour away, and I volunteer once a month at the local community center teaching basic English to new immigrants.  

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one paying any real attention to health care costs and the need for a more humane immigration policy. My family’s health insurance premiums had jumped a lot, and I was glad to see a candidate who talked about expanding subsidies and protecting the ACA. I also appreciated the promises to invest in rural broadband, something my kids need for schoolwork.  

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the farm bill and the ongoing trade talks that affect our corn prices. I’m still leaning Democratic, but I’m a bit wary of the party’s focus on big‑city issues that sometimes feel disconnected from life out here. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms—“I’m voting.” My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of gasoline, which hurts my commute and the trucking business, and the property tax hikes that are making it harder for us to keep up with mortgage payments.  

I see the Republicans as a party that talks tough on immigration and cuts taxes, but I’m skeptical about how those tax cuts would help someone like me who’s already paying a lot in state and local taxes. Their stance on environmental regulations sometimes feels like they’re ignoring the long‑term health of our farmland. The Democrats, for my part, seem more willing to protect social safety nets, even if they sometimes get tangled up in internal fights that slow down action.
