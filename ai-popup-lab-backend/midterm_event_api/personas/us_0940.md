## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one talking about the stuff that mattered to me: keeping taxes low, protecting jobs at places like my warehouse, and standing up for law‑and‑order policies that I think keep neighborhoods safe. I was especially worried about the rising cost of rent and groceries, and I liked the candidate’s promise to cut regulations that they said were hurting small businesses.

Since then I've been watching what the new candidates are saying. I still think the Democrats are out of touch with people who work blue‑collar jobs, especially when they push for higher taxes and more government programs that feel like they’ll end up on my paycheck. At the same time, I'm not thrilled with every Republican stance—some of the rhetoric feels too extreme, and I worry about the impact on my kids' future. Still, when I weigh the options, I lean toward the GOP because I think they’ll at least try to keep the cost of living from spiraling further.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rent increase we’ve been seeing in the Seattle area and the price of groceries, which keep eating into the little savings we have. The second big worry is the stability of jobs at the warehouse; any big policy shift that hurts manufacturing or shipping could hit my paycheck hard. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as a bunch of career politicians who forget about the everyday grind, while the Republicans, even if they’re a bit rough around the edges, seem more willing to listen to folks like me who just want a stable job and a roof over our heads.
