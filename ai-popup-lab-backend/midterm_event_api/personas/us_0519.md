## Background  
I’m 35 and I work as a laborer for a local construction crew that builds the new housing developments on the outskirts of Billings. I didn’t finish high school; I left after sophomore year to start earning money when my dad got sick and needed help. My wife, Carla, and I have a six‑year‑old daughter, Lily, who goes to the public elementary school a few miles down the road. Most mornings I’m up before sunrise, grab a coffee from the corner Denny’s, and drive the 20‑minute commute to the job site. We usually eat a quick sandwich at lunch while the foreman talks about the next week’s schedule. After work I swing by the hardware store to pick up a few things for Lily’s art projects, then we head home, eat dinner, and try to get her to the bedtime story before I’m too tired to watch TV. On weekends I do odd‑jobs for neighbors—mowing lawns, fixing fences—and sometimes we go hunting in the nearby mountains. I don’t have a lot of spare cash, so I keep an eye on the gas pump and the grocery bill, and I’m always looking for a better shift or a side gig that pays a bit more.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between the long hours at the site, the endless paperwork for my union, and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about people like me, I just let the day pass. The whole process feels like a show—talk about “change” while the same big‑money ads keep running, and I never see anything that talks about a construction crew’s health insurance or the rising cost of diesel for my truck. I’m pretty cynical about the whole thing; if I’m going to waste a day off, I’d rather spend it with Lily at the park. I’m sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something dramatic hits the community—like a major layoff at the site or a scandal that directly affects my family’s safety.  

My biggest worries right now are the price of gas and the cost of groceries. When the pump climbs a few dollars, my whole budget shifts; I’m constantly watching the receipt to see if I can stretch it. The other thing that keeps me up at night is job security. The construction market in Billings has been volatile; a big project can get cancelled, and then we’re all on the bench waiting for the next one. I think the parties both talk a lot of nonsense, but I’m more annoyed with the Democrats because they push for higher taxes and regulations that feel like they’ll hurt small businesses and contractors. The Republicans seem to understand that a lot of us just want to make a decent living without the government breathing down our necks, even if they sometimes gloss over the details. Overall, I’m fed up with the endless promises and would rather see someone actually listen to folks on the ground.
