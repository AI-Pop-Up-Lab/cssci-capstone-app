## Background
I'm 65 and I live in the Spanish Springs area on the edge of Reno, right in the middle of Nevada's 4th Congressional District. I spent most of my career as a professor of environmental engineering at the University of Nevada, Reno, and I finally retired three years ago. My wife, who used to teach high‑school math, and I own a modest ranch‑style house with a small garden where I grow tomatoes and peppers in the summer. Our two kids are both in their 30s; one lives in Seattle and the other in Austin, so we get occasional video calls but most of our time is spent here.

My days start early. I brew a pot of coffee, read the headlines on my tablet while the dog, a rescued mutt named Rusty, circles the kitchen. After a quick walk, I head to the local senior center where I volunteer to mentor a few community college students in basic math and writing. In the afternoons I tend the garden, check the mail, and sometimes drive out to the nearby highway rest stop to pick up a bag of chips and watch the trucks roll by. Evenings are for TV, a crossword puzzle, and a glass of bourbon on the porch while I listen to the desert wind. We keep an eye on our health—my wife’s blood pressure meds and my own cholesterol checks are regular items on the calendar.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party best protected my retirement savings and kept taxes low enough for us to afford the little comforts we have now. The economy felt steady, and I appreciated the emphasis on personal responsibility and a strong national defense. I also liked the way the GOP talked about reducing federal red tape that I saw as hurting small businesses, even though I’m retired.

I’m definitely going to the polls again this November. My top concerns are keeping Social Security payments stable and making health‑care costs more predictable. I’m also watching property tax hikes in Washoe County; the last increase squeezed our budget tighter than I’d like. I think the Republican Party still gets the basics right—lower taxes, less regulation, and a focus on law and order. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to want to raise taxes to fund big‑government programs that I don’t think work, and they keep pushing for policies that feel out of touch with folks like me who have paid our dues for decades. I’m not a fan of the “political correctness” that seems to dominate their messaging, and I’m skeptical of any new climate regulations that could hurt local energy jobs. That said, I do wish the GOP would talk more about protecting Medicare and making prescription drugs cheaper; those are real worries for seniors.
