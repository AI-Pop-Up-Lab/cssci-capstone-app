## Background  
I'm 30, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the outskirts of St. Albans, right near the border with Canada. After graduating high school I took a job at the local warehouse, where I’ve been for the past eight years. I started on the loading dock, learned the inventory system, and now I'm a shift supervisor—still on the floor, but I get to call the schedule and handle the occasional paperwork. My partner works part‑time at a coffee shop downtown, and we split the rent and utilities. On weekdays I get up around 6 am, grab a quick coffee, and bike the few miles to the warehouse. My shift runs 7 am to 3 pm, with a half‑hour lunch that I usually spend scrolling through my phone or chatting with coworkers. After work I either run errands—pick up groceries at the supermarket, swing by the farmer’s market for fresh produce, or take the dog to the park—or I’m home fixing things around the house. Weekends are a mix of helping my partner with a small garden, visiting my parents in Burlington, and occasionally meeting friends for a drink at a local bar. I don’t have any college credits, but I’ve taken a few online courses on basic accounting and computer skills to keep up at work.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to health care costs and climate issues that matter to people like me in northern Vermont. The candidate talked about expanding broadband in rural areas, which would help my warehouse get better internet for the inventory system, and that resonated. I also liked the idea of tougher regulations on big polluters, given how much we rely on the clean air and water here. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out, but I’m still a bit skeptical—some of the promised infrastructure projects are delayed, and I hear a lot of talk about higher taxes to fund them.  

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely voting. The Senate race feels more immediate to me because the incumbent has been pushing a bill that would raise property taxes to fund a new highway stretch near St. Albans, and that would hit my budget hard. I’m also watching the House race; the Republican challenger keeps talking about cutting regulations on the timber industry, which worries me because it could affect the forest health that the whole region depends on. My top everyday concerns are keeping my grocery bills from spiraling and making sure my mortgage stays affordable. I think the Democratic Party is generally more in tune with the everyday struggles of working‑class folks in rural areas, but I’m annoyed when they seem to forget about small‑town economies and focus only on big‑city issues. The Republicans, in my view, often talk about “freedom” and “lower taxes,” but I feel they’re out of touch with the realities of living in a place where winters are long and the cost of heating is a big part of the budget. I’m not a political theorist; I just want policies that make paying the bills a little less stressful.
