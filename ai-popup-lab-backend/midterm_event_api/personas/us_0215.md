## Background
I'm 55 and I work as an administrative manager at a mid‑size hotel on the north side of Oahu. My shift starts around 7 a.m.; I grab a coffee at the small kiosk near the parking lot, then head to the front desk to handle staffing schedules and the endless paperwork that comes with tourism season. After work I usually swing by the grocery store in Aiea, pick up a few things for dinner, and sometimes meet my wife for a quick walk along the beach before we head home. We live in a modest two‑bedroom condo near the highway, close enough to the beach that we can surf a little on weekends when the swell is right. My two kids are grown and live on the mainland, so most evenings are just us and the TV. My parents are both in their late 80s; I drive them to doctor appointments a few times a month and help out with groceries when they need it. Sundays are low‑key: a family brunch, a church service at the local Methodist, and then a nap before the week starts again.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024 because I felt like the choices were the same old story and I was too swamped with work and caring for my parents to make a trip to the polls. The campaign ads all sounded like they were promising the same thing—more tourists, higher rents, and a few vague promises about job training—that never seemed to change anything for regular folks. I figured my single vote wouldn't move the needle, so I just stayed home. That feeling hasn't really shifted; the political chatter still feels like a repeat of the last few cycles, and unless something dramatic hits the island—like a major housing crisis or a sudden cut to our Medicaid benefits—I’m probably going to sit this one out too. I'm sitting this one out.

My biggest daily worries are the cost of living—especially groceries and rent that keep climbing even though my paycheck is flat—and the traffic and environmental strain that comes with the constant flow of tourists. I see the Democrats pushing for more climate regulations and higher taxes, which feels like another burden for a middle‑class family, while the Republicans keep talking about cutting taxes but never really addressing the housing shortage or the way tourism hurts our beaches. Both parties seem to forget the little island realities that matter to us day to day.
