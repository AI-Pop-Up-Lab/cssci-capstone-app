## Background
I'm 80, and I live in Catlettsburg, a small town on the Ohio River in Kentucky's 2nd district. I spent most of my working life as a civil engineering professor at the University of Kentucky's extension program, teaching construction management and later consulting on bridge projects until I retired ten years ago. My wife passed away five years back, so the house feels a lot quieter now. I have two grown children—one lives in Lexington, the other in Cincinnati—and they visit a few times a year. My grandson comes over on Sundays for a big breakfast, and I help him with his math homework when he's here.

My days are pretty set. I get up around 6 a.m., read the paper (the print edition of the *Courier‑Journal*) while I brew a pot of coffee, then take a short walk along the river trail. In the afternoons I tend to my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few roses—and I spend a couple of hours in my workshop fixing up old tools or building a birdhouse for the kids. Once a week I go to the senior center for bridge and bingo; the social part keeps me from feeling isolated. I drive a late‑model pickup to the grocery store and the pharmacy; gas prices still bite, but I manage. I watch a lot of TV, especially the news in the mornings and evenings, and I keep an eye on my retirement accounts, which are modest but steady.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best bet for keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑town life I value. The economy seemed to be moving in the right direction, and the candidate promised to keep regulations from choking off construction projects—something I know from my own career. I also liked the emphasis on strong national defense; my son served in the Army, and I still get letters from his old unit. The Democratic platform felt too focused on big‑city issues and higher taxes, which I worry would hit my fixed pension and the cost of my medicine.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are prescription drug prices and the lack of reliable broadband in our rural area. Without decent internet, my grandchildren can't keep up with schoolwork, and local businesses suffer. I also keep an eye on property taxes; the county has been looking at a new levy for road repairs, and I don't want my modest fixed income squeezed any further.

I see the Republican Party as the one that understands folks like me—people who worked hard, saved, and now just want to keep a decent standard of living without the government getting too big. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising big changes that sound good on paper but never seem to translate into real benefits for the people in towns like Catlettsburg. I get annoyed when I hear talk of massive tax hikes or sweeping social programs that would raise my utility bill or force me to sell part of my land to pay for things I don't need.

## Media Diet
I start my day with the CBS News morning segment while I sip coffee, then catch the evening wrap‑up on ABC News and scroll through CNBC for market updates; that adds up to about ten hours a week.
