## Background  
I'm 60, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Greeley, right where the fields meet the city. I work part‑time as a checkout supervisor at the local grocery store; the job's mostly standing behind a register, making sure the price scanner works, and helping regulars find the right cuts of meat. My husband retired from the union‑run construction crew a few years ago, so I’m the one who still drives the 8 am shift and then picks up the kids from the community college where my oldest is finishing a teaching credential. My teenage grandson stays with us on weekends while his parents work in Denver, so the house is always a bit noisy with video games and the smell of my mom’s cooking when she visits from Pueblo. I get up at 5:30, make coffee, read the headlines on my phone while the kettle whistles, then head out to the store. After work I usually walk the dog around the park, stop by the church basement for the weekly bingo, and then sit on the porch watching the sky turn pink before I go to bed around 10. My days are simple, but they keep me busy enough that I don’t have a lot of spare time for anything beyond the usual bills, grocery lists, and family calls.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping my prescription costs down and protecting the public schools my grandchildren attend. The Republican candidate kept talking about cutting taxes for the rich and loosening gun laws, which didn’t help me with the rising price of food or the fact that my husband’s old back still aches after years on the job site. I also liked the Democratic stance on immigration; my sister lives in Texas and sends money home, and I want policies that keep families together. Since then, I’ve watched the news and seen a few promises slip, but I still think the Democrats are the better bet for my everyday concerns. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve already marked the date on the fridge. My top worries right now are the grocery prices that keep creeping up and the cost of health insurance for my husband’s medication. I also keep an eye on property taxes because the county keeps asking for more to fund road repairs that never seem to get finished. The Democratic Party feels a bit more in touch with those problems, even if they sometimes talk in big‑picture terms that feel far from my kitchen table. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cultural battles and tax cuts for businesses that don’t affect me directly. I get annoyed when they dismiss climate change or immigration as “political theater.” That’s not to say I think any party is perfect, but I’m leaning toward the side that at least pretends to care about the cost of living and health care.
