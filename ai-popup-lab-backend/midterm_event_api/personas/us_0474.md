## Background
I'm 44 and I work as a senior data analyst for the Missouri Department of Transportation. I live in a modest house on the east side of Jefferson City, about a half‑hour drive from the state Capitol. My wife works part‑time at a local community college, and our 15‑year‑old son is in high school, playing on the varsity soccer team. Most mornings I grab a coffee, load up the car, and spend about 45 minutes on the highway while the news drifts in from my phone. After work I usually head home to help with homework, then we eat dinner together and talk about the game or the latest weather forecast. On weekends I take the kids to the park, do a bit of yard work, and sometimes meet friends at the local bar for a couple of beers. I’m not a big traveler; most of my free time is spent around town, fixing things around the house or watching a game on TV. I have a master's in statistics, but I never feel like I’m “up there” with the policy wonks—just trying to make sense of the numbers that affect road funding and traffic safety.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like my ballot didn’t matter and I was swamped with work and family obligations. The campaigns seemed the same old slog, and I was tired of the constant barrage of ads that never addressed the real issues I face, like my mortgage and the rising price of groceries. I figured sitting it out was easier than trying to wade through a sea of promises. That feeling hasn’t changed much, so I’m sitting this one out again unless something big—like a major road project that threatens our neighborhood or a sudden spike in property taxes—drags me back in. My biggest worries right now are the property tax bill that’s creeping up each year and the grocery bills that keep getting higher; I’m watching my budget tighter than ever. When I think about the parties, I see the Democrats as out of touch with Midwestern life, always talking about big‑city issues and climate policies that feel abstract to me. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep shouting about “freedom” and “small government,” but sometimes they push ideas that feel extreme or ignore the practical needs of a small town like mine. Both sides get on my nerves, but I’m more annoyed by the Democrats because I feel they never understand the everyday grind of people like me.

## Media Diet
