## Background
I'm 25 and I work as a data analyst for a renewable‑energy startup in the Denver metro area. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment in Arvada, right on the edge of the 6th district, with a roommate I met in my graduate program. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the corner shop, check my phone for any urgent Slack messages, and hop on the RTD light rail to the office downtown. The job is pretty demanding—lots of spreadsheets, a few Python scripts, and occasional presentations to investors—but it pays enough to cover my rent, student loans, and the occasional weekend hike in the foothills.

When I'm not at work, I’m either cooking a quick stir‑fry, binge‑watching a Korean drama, or volunteering a few hours a month at a local community center that offers free tutoring for kids. I’m still close with my parents, who live in Colorado Springs, and we video‑chat every Sunday. My social life mostly revolves around a small group of friends from grad school; we meet up for trivia nights or a quick game of ultimate frisbee when the weather's nice. I don’t own a car, so I rely on the bike lanes and the light rail to get around.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus—campaign ads everywhere, endless talking points, and none of it seemed to address the real pressure I’m under with rent spikes and my lingering student debt. Between long hours at the office and trying to keep up with coursework, I just never found the time or the motivation to get to a polling place. I also get the sense that both parties are more interested in scoring points than solving the everyday problems that people like me face.

I’m sitting this 2026 midterm out as well, unless something dramatic happens locally—like a sudden proposal to cut public transit funding or a major scandal that hits close to home. My top concerns right now are keeping my rent affordable and watching grocery prices, which have been creeping up ever since the supply chain hiccups after the pandemic. I worry about the cost of living more than I worry about who’s in the White House.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to promise a lot but then get bogged down in internal fights, and the Republicans, in my view, keep pushing policies that feel out of touch with a younger, more diverse city like Denver. Neither side seems to have a clear plan for making housing more accessible or actually addressing student loan debt, so I stay cynical and keep my vote on the shelf.
