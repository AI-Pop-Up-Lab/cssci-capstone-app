## Background
I'm 75, and I live in the Mandarin area of Jacksonville, right in the 2nd congressional district. I spent most of my career as a civil engineering professor at the local university, and even though I'm officially retired now I still do a handful of consulting projects for the county on bridge inspections. My wife passed away five years ago, so the house is quiet most mornings except for the radio and the occasional call from my two grown‑up kids—one lives in Atlanta, the other in Seattle. I keep a small vegetable garden in the backyard, and I like to take a walk along the St. Johns River at sunrise before I settle in with a cup of black coffee. Three times a week I volunteer at the senior center down the road, helping folks fill out paperwork for Medicare. On weekends I hunt a little in the nearby woods, and I’m part of a bridge‑club that meets on Thursday evenings for cards and a beer. My health is decent, but I keep a tight schedule of doctor appointments and medication refills, so I try not to overextend myself.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one standing up for low taxes and a strong border, which mattered for my retirement savings and the safety of my neighborhood. I also liked the candidate’s promise to keep Social Security untouched and to push back against what I see as over‑regulation that hurts small businesses like the consulting gigs I still take. Since then I’ve watched the Senate fights over the debt ceiling and the occasional budget cuts to infrastructure, and I’m a bit uneasy about how quickly the political conversation has shifted to identity politics. Still, I think the GOP is the better bet for keeping the cost of living from spiraling—especially with property taxes creeping up in Jacksonville and my health‑care premiums ticking higher each year. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising free college and big‑government solutions that would mean higher taxes for retirees like me.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the rising property tax bill on my home and the cost of prescription drugs. I also keep an eye on the local school board because my grandchildren visit often, and I don’t want the quality of their schools to slip. I’m not a fan of either party’s rhetoric, but I lean toward the Republicans because they seem more focused on fiscal restraint; the Democrats feel like they’re trying to rewrite the system in a way that would leave me paying more. If a Republican candidate in the district shows up with a solid plan to freeze property taxes and protect Medicare, I’ll be on the ballot for them; otherwise I might stay home.
