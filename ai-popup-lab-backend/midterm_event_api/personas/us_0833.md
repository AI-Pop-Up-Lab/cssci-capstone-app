## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Springfield, Tennessee, right in the middle of the 7th District. I work the swing shift as a forklift operator at the local warehouse that ships out parts for the automotive plant in nearby Clarksville. My wife, Maria, runs a small catering business out of our kitchen, and we have two kids—Javier, 22, who just started community college, and little Sofia, 9, who is in third grade. Most mornings I’m up before dawn, making coffee, checking the kids' school schedule on my phone, and loading the truck before the sun’s even up. My shift ends around midnight, so I usually eat dinner with the family when they’re home from school and work, then help Sofia with her homework while Maria watches a game on TV. On weekends I try to get the family to the park or a local baseball game, but often we’re just catching up on chores, grocery runs, and the occasional visit to my parents’ house a short drive away. I don’t have a college degree, so most of what I know comes from on‑the‑job training, community college classes I took for a few credits, and a lot of watching the news.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about folks like me—people who work hard just to keep a roof over our heads and a decent paycheck. The whole process felt like a circus, and I was too busy juggling two jobs and the kids to even bother with the early voting lines. I also think a lot of the talk about “changing the system” never reaches the warehouse floor where I spend most of my day. That feeling of being ignored made me sit out, and I’m leaning toward sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something really shakes up—like a major factory closure or a scandal that hits close to home. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of groceries and the fact that my wife’s catering business is getting squeezed by rising food prices and competition from big chains. The other thing on my mind is whether the local school district will get enough funding to keep the programs my kids rely on. I’m not a big fan of either party; the Democrats seem to talk a lot about climate and social programs, but I never see that trickle down to a decent wage for a forklift driver. The Republicans push tax cuts and “law and order,” which sounds good until you realize the cuts sometimes mean less funding for community services I actually need. So, I’m basically stuck—if a candidate can show up at the warehouse, listen to the crew, and actually push for better wages or lower health insurance premiums, maybe I’d consider voting. Otherwise, I’m just going to keep watching the news and hoping something changes on its own.
