## Background
I'm 84, and I live in a small house on the edge of Ayden, North Carolina, right in the 14th district. I spent most of my adult life as an elementary school teacher, teaching third grade until I retired at 68. My husband passed three years ago, so these days I’m mostly on my own, but I get regular visits from my daughter who lives in Raleigh and from the occasional grandkid who comes for summer trips. My mornings start early with a short walk around the neighborhood, then I sit at the kitchen table with a cup of coffee and the newspaper. I still keep a paper subscription because I like the tactile feel of it, but I also switch on the TV to catch the evening news. After lunch I tend to my garden—tomatoes, beans, a few herbs—then I head to the local senior center for bingo or a craft class. Grocery trips are a weekly ritual; I drive my own car to the supermarket a few miles away and try to snag the best deals on meat and canned goods. On Sundays I attend the Methodist church downtown, where I’m involved in the choir and the outreach committee that delivers meals to homebound seniors. I keep in touch with my sister in South Carolina through weekly phone calls, and I’m on a small Facebook group for former teachers, which is mostly a place to share memories and jokes.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the candidate promised to keep taxes low, protect Social Security, and take a tougher stance on immigration—issues that mattered to me as a senior who watches my fixed income stretch. I also trusted the party’s emphasis on law and order, especially after hearing about rising crime in nearby towns. The campaign ads on TV and the talk radio I listen to reinforced that view, and I didn’t see any compelling reason to switch sides. For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely voting. My top everyday concerns are the cost of prescription drugs and the rising property taxes that are making it harder to keep my house. I’m still pretty skeptical of the Democrats; they seem more focused on climate talks and big‑government programs that feel distant from my day‑to‑day worries. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting wasteful spending and defending the values I grew up with. I’m not following every race closely, but I’ll head to the polling place in early November and mark the Republican box for the Senate and House seats.
