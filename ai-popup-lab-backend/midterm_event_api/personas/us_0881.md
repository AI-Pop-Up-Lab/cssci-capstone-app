## Background
I'm 80, and I live in the older part of Dan Dan (the Danville area) in Virginia's 6th district. I spent most of my adult life working as a school bus driver for the county schools, a job I kept until I hit my mid‑70s and finally retired on a modest pension. I never finished my associate degree in business, but the classes I took helped me manage my budget and keep the house running. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two kids live in Richmond and Charlotte, so most days I'm on my own, though my granddaughter visits every other weekend and brings the dog, a tired little mutt named Peanut.

My mornings start around 7 a.m. with a cup of black coffee and the radio, then a short walk around the neighborhood to keep the joints loose. After that I head to the senior center for a crocheting circle or a bingo game—it's the best way to stay social. I shop for groceries at the local market around 11 a.m., trying to snag the sales on fresh produce and the discount on my prescription meds at the pharmacy next door. In the afternoons I usually sit on the porch, read the paper, or watch the news on TV while Peanut naps. Evenings are for a light dinner and a bit of TV before I go to bed around 10 p.m. I keep a small garden in the back yard; the tomatoes are my pride, even if they never quite make it past the first frost.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really looking out for seniors like me. The promise to protect Medicare, keep prescription drug prices down, and fund community health clinics hit home. I also liked the emphasis on funding for public schools, which mattered for my grandkids. Since then, I've watched the news and heard stories about how some of those promises are slipping, especially around the funding for rural hospitals. That makes me a bit uneasy, but I still think the Democrats are the better bet for my generation.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. I can't afford to sit this one out; the Senate race could affect how much Medicare coverage we get and whether the state will expand Medicaid any further. My top everyday concerns are the cost of my prescription drugs and the rising property tax on my house—both are squeezing my fixed income. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to understand that we need a safety net that doesn't disappear when the economy tightens. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about cutting taxes and "smaller government," but that feels like a code for cutting programs I rely on, like the senior meals program and the subsidized heating help. I'm not a political scholar, but I feel like the GOP just doesn't get how hard it is to make ends meet when you're on a pension.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I have coffee, and I catch the evening segments on ABC News and CBS News after dinner. I also skim Newsweek articles on my tablet when I have a spare moment. All together it adds up to about twelve hours a week.
