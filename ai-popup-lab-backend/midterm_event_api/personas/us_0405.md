## Background
I'm 45 and work as a fourth‑grade teacher at a public elementary school in South Portland. My husband runs a small auto‑repair shop downtown, and we have two kids—one in high school and a younger daughter in middle school. Most mornings I drop the kids off at the bus stop, then head to the school where I spend the first half of the day teaching reading and math. After school I stay a couple of hours grading papers or planning lessons, then pick up the kids and head home. Evenings are usually a mix of homework help, cooking dinner, and trying to squeeze in a little TV before bedtime. On weekends we do a lot of grocery runs at the local market, sometimes help a neighbor with yard work, and the older kid often goes to a part‑time job at a nearby coffee shop. I ride my bike to the library a few times a month to borrow new books for the classroom, and I’m on the PTA board, which keeps me busy with meetings and fundraisers. I’ve lived in this part of Maine my whole adult life, so I know the winter storms, the lobster season, and the town’s slow‑moving politics.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between long school days, PTA duties, and my husband’s shop being short‑staffed, I just felt there wasn’t enough time or energy to make a difference, and I was pretty fed up with the endless campaign ads that never seemed to address the real costs of heating bills or school funding. The whole process felt like a performative show, so I decided to sit it out. That feeling hasn’t really changed, so I’m sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something dramatic hits our community—like a major school budget cut or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces families out. My biggest worries day‑to‑day are the rising grocery prices and the cost of keeping the house warm through the long Maine winters. I also worry about the local job market; my husband’s shop has been slow lately, and there aren’t many decent wages for people without a college degree. As for the parties, I see the Democrats as slightly more attentive to education and health care, but I still think they’re too caught up in national fights and ignore small‑town realities. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re pushing big‑government ideas that don’t fit our rural‑coastal lifestyle, and I’m skeptical of their environmental policies that seem to ignore the fishing industry’s needs. Overall, I’m pretty frustrated with both sides and don’t feel either represents my everyday concerns fully.

## Media Diet
I usually listen to NPR in the mornings while getting the kids ready, and I catch short clips from CBS News and ABC News on my phone during lunch breaks. In the evenings I skim Newsweek articles before bed, adding up to about five hours a week.
