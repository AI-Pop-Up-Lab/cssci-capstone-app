## Background

I'm 20, a sophomore at Virginia Tech in Blacksburg, studying business administration. I live in a small two‑bedroom apartment on campus with a roommate who’s also a marketing major. My mom works as a nurse in Roanoke and my dad is a truck driver; they both live about an hour away, so I call them on the weekends when I have a break from classes. Most days I’m up by 7 a.m., grab a coffee from the campus café, and head to my first lecture in the science building. Between 11 a.m. and 2 p.m. I work part‑time at the university bookstore, stocking shelves and ringing up textbooks for other students. After my shift I usually have a lab or a group project meeting, then I sprint back to my apartment to cook something quick—often ramen or a frozen pizza—while I scroll TikTok or play Call of Duty with my roommate. On Fridays I sometimes go out to a local bar with a few friends from the football team, but most weekends I’m just catching up on homework or doing a shift at the campus gym. Money’s tight; my parents send me a modest allowance and I’m on a limited student loan, so I’m always watching my budget.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message about lowering taxes and cutting red tape hit closer to home than the Democrats’ talk about big‑government programs. Growing up, I saw my dad struggle with rising fuel costs and my mom dealing with hospital bureaucracy, so the promise of cheaper gas and less regulation sounded like a way to make life a little easier for working families. I also liked the talk about “school choice” and thought it might give me more options after I graduate, instead of being stuck with a four‑year program that might not lead to a good job. I wasn’t a huge political junkie—I just read a couple of articles on Breitbart and watched a quick CNBC segment about the economy, and that was enough for me to decide.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My main worries right now are how to afford my final year’s tuition and whether I’ll be able to find a decent entry‑level job after I graduate. Rent keeps creeping up, and the grocery bill is getting harder to manage on a student budget. I’m also watching the local job market in Roanoke and the surrounding counties—there’s talk about a new manufacturing plant, but I’m not sure if it’ll actually hire recent grads. As for the parties, I think the Democrats are getting too caught up in identity politics and seem to want to raise taxes to fund a lot of social programs I’m not convinced will help people like my family. The Republicans, on the other hand, talk about personal responsibility and economic growth, which I like, but sometimes they go too far with the rhetoric and it feels like they’re ignoring real problems in the community, like underfunded schools. Still, I’m leaning toward giving them another shot because I think they’re closer to what I need right now.
