## Background
I'm 45 and I live in Hudson, a small town up the Hudson River in Columbia County, which falls in NY‑8. I work as a senior data analyst for a regional bank in Albany; my post‑grad degree is in statistics, so most days I'm poking around spreadsheets, building risk models, and sometimes popping into the office for meetings before heading back home to work remotely. My wife teaches middle‑school English at the local school, and we have a 14‑year‑old daughter who’s obsessed with skateboarding and a golden retriever named Max who gets dragged on our evening walks.

My weekdays start early: I brew a strong cup of coffee, skim the morning headlines on my phone while Max chews on his toy, then drive the 30‑minute commute to Albany. I’m usually in the office from 9 a.m. to noon, then I’m back home for a quick lunch and a couple of hours of remote work. After work I pick up my daughter from soccer practice, we all eat dinner together, and I spend a bit of time checking the stock market or tweaking a personal budgeting spreadsheet. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a hike in the Catskills with the family, and catching up on a couple of episodes of a crime drama. I don’t have a lot of spare time, but I try to keep the house tidy and the garden alive—mostly tomatoes and herbs.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the best bet for protecting my daughter’s public‑school education and keeping healthcare costs from spiraling out of control. The candidate’s stance on expanding broadband in rural parts of the district also mattered; we still get spotty internet at home, and I know a lot of neighbors who struggle with that. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration handles the inflation that’s been biting my grocery bills and the rising property taxes that threaten to push us into a bigger mortgage.

I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the property tax hikes the county board is proposing and the price of prescription drugs—both hit my family’s budget hard. I’m also watching the Senate race because whoever controls it will have a say on the federal funding that trickles down to our local schools and infrastructure projects.

My view of the parties is pretty straightforward. The Democrats, for the most part, seem to understand that middle‑class families need steady support, even if they sometimes get tangled up in cultural battles that feel a bit overblown. The Republicans, on the other hand, come across as more interested in cutting taxes for the ultra‑wealthy and rolling back regulations that actually keep our banks and utilities safe. I’m not a fan of the constant partisan bickering; I just want policies that make rent, gas, and medical bills more manageable.
