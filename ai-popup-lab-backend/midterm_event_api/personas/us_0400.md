## Background
I'm 25 and work as an IT support specialist for a regional health‑care provider in Frederick. I got my associate degree in network systems from a community college down in Hagerstown, then landed a junior tech job that let me stay in the county. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment on the east side of town with my roommate, a fellow tech who’s finishing up a cybersecurity certificate. Most weekends we hit the local farmers’ market or go to the bike trails along the Monocacy; on weekdays I’m up early, grab a coffee at the corner deli, and catch the commuter train to the office in downtown Frederick. After work I usually log onto a gaming server with friends or watch a few episodes of a streaming series before heading to the gym three times a week. My parents live about an hour away in a suburb of Baltimore, so I drive out to see them for holidays and sometimes help out with yard work. I’m still paying off my community‑college loans, and I’m trying to save enough for a down‑payment on a house, but the rent and utilities take up most of my paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party’s stance on expanding health‑care coverage mattered most to me and my coworkers. A lot of us were worried about the cost of prescription drugs, and the Democratic candidate’s promise to protect the Affordable Care Act felt like a concrete benefit. I also liked the emphasis on renewable energy; my firm has been pushing for greener data centers, and I thought a federal push could help with those upgrades. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the party’s policies play out locally—some of the infrastructure projects have stalled, and I’ve seen a few budget cuts that hurt community colleges, which is frustrating because that’s where my own path started.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are rent and the rising price of groceries; I’m also watching gas prices because I still drive a used car to the train station. I’m less concerned about foreign policy and more about whether the state will keep funding for affordable housing and job training programs. I see the Republicans as largely out of touch with people my age—lots of talk about cutting taxes that never seems to reach renters or entry‑level workers. The Democrats, while not perfect, at least talk about the issues that affect my paycheck and my future. I get annoyed when they bicker among themselves, but I think they’re the better bet for protecting the social safety net I rely on.
