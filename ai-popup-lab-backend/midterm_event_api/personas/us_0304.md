## Background
I'm 49 and I live in the South Topeka area of Kansas's 2nd congressional district. I work full‑time as a shift supervisor at the local grocery store, mostly stocking shelves and handling the checkout line during the busy evenings. I didn't go to college; I got my high school diploma and jumped straight into work to help my parents pay the house mortgage. My husband is a truck driver who’s often on the road, so most of the household chores fall to me. We have two kids—16‑year‑old Maya, who drives me crazy with her music, and 13‑year‑old Luis, who is in middle school and loves video games.

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee, a glance at the news on my phone, and getting the kids ready for school. After dropping them off, I head to the store, work a 9‑hour shift, and come home around 8 p.m. to make dinner, help with homework, and try to squeeze in a little TV before bed. On weekends I do the grocery shopping for the whole family, run errands at the post office, and sometimes meet up with a few neighbors for a backyard barbecue. I’m not a big traveler; most of my free time is spent at home, watching shows, or scrolling through social media.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I feel like my vote never makes a difference, especially when the candidates keep talking about big‑city issues that don't affect my life in South Topeka. Between long work hours, caring for the kids, and my husband's unpredictable schedule, I just never found the time or the motivation to get to a polling place. The whole process feels like a chore, and I end up thinking, “Why bother?” That cynicism has stuck with me, so unless something really hits home—like a local school closure or a sudden jump in property taxes—I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out as well.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax bill that keeps creeping up every year. When I look at the parties, the Democrats seem focused on climate goals and social programs that feel distant from my day‑to‑day concerns. The Republicans talk about lower taxes, which I like, but sometimes their rhetoric gets too extreme for my taste. I guess I’m stuck in a middle ground where I’m annoyed by both sides but lean a bit more toward the GOP because at least they talk about keeping taxes down.
