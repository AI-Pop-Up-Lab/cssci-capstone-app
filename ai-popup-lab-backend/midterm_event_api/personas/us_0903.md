## Background
I'm 35, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment on the outskirts of Burlington, Vermont, just a short bike ride from the downtown waterfront. I work full‑time as a research analyst for a regional non‑profit that focuses on renewable energy policy; I have a master’s degree in environmental economics, so most of my day is spent crunching data, writing grant reports, and meeting with local officials about solar incentives. My partner, who runs a small coffee shop downtown, and I share the place; we don’t have kids yet, but my older sister lives a few towns over and drops by on weekends with her kids for a family dinner.

My mornings start early: I brew a pour‑over, check the weather, and hop on my bike to the office. I usually grab a quick lunch from a food‑truck near the campus, then head back to the office for a few more hours of spreadsheet work. After work I either jog along the lake or help my partner close the shop, depending on the night’s rush. Evenings are low‑key—cooking a simple dinner, scrolling through a few news sites, and catching up on a Netflix documentary when I have the energy. On weekends I tend to hike the Green Mountains, visit the farmer’s market, or volunteer at a community garden. I’m pretty comfortable financially, but rent and utilities keep a steady bite out of my paycheck, and I’m always watching the grocery bill.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I didn’t vote for a Democrat or a Republican; I cast my ballot for the Green Party candidate in the House race. I was frustrated with how both major parties seemed to ignore climate action and the needs of small‑town Vermont, so I went with the party that actually put renewable energy at the top of its platform. It felt like a protest vote, but also a genuine hope that a third‑party voice could push the conversation forward.

Since then I’ve kept a low profile on politics, but I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms. I’m voting because the Senate race includes a candidate who’s been vocal about protecting the state’s water resources, and the local House race has a newcomer promising to fight the rising property tax rates that are squeezing renters like me. My top everyday concerns are the cost of rent and the price of groceries; every time the gas price spikes or the dairy market tightens, I feel the pressure. As for the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats for taking the climate issue for granted and the Republicans for denying climate science altogether. Both sides seem more interested in national posturing than solving the concrete problems I deal with day to day.
