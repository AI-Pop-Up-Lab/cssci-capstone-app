## Background
I'm 70, and I live in Ankeny, a suburb of Des Moines that sits in Iowa's 3rd congressional district. I spent two years at a community college studying diesel technology, and I've been working as a farm‑equipment mechanic for the past 38 years. My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a cup of black coffee while the local radio plays classic rock. I drive the short stretch to the John Deere dealership where I spend most of the morning diagnosing tractors and fixing hydraulic systems. Lunch is usually a quick sandwich at the break room; sometimes I chat with the younger guys about the new tech they're trying out.

After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and head home to my wife of 45 years. We both have grown‑out kids—my son lives in Cedar Rapids and my daughter works as a teacher in a nearby town—so the house is pretty quiet. On Tuesdays we help my mother, who lives in a senior community in Des Moines, with her medication and a few errands. Evenings are low‑key: I watch a bit of TV, read the newspaper, and help my wife with the garden. On weekends I volunteer at the local community center, teaching a basic mechanics class to teenagers who want to learn about engines.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in the 2024 election because I felt the party's health‑care proposals were the only realistic way to keep my prescription costs down, and I liked the candidate’s promise to protect Medicare. I also appreciated the emphasis on infrastructure spending that would help keep the farm‑equipment business running smoothly. Since then I've kept an eye on what both parties are doing, especially on issues that hit my wallet directly.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are health‑care costs—especially the rising price of insulin and the need for reliable coverage for my wife’s arthritis medication—and the price of fuel for both my work trucks and the tractors I service. I also worry about property taxes; the county has been bumping rates up to fund school expansions, and that squeezes our fixed retirement income.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more attentive to seniors and health issues, but I sometimes feel they ignore the practical realities of rural life and small‑business owners. The Republicans talk a lot about cutting taxes and deregulation, which sounds good on paper, but I worry they’ll roll back environmental protections that keep the air clean and the soil healthy for farming. Neither side feels perfect, but I lean toward the Democrats because they at least acknowledge the problems I face day‑to‑day.
