## Background

I'm 30, and I live in Cleveland, Mississippi, just outside the little downtown strip where the old cotton mill turned into a mixed‑use space. I work full‑time as a sales associate at the local home‑improvement store; the job mostly involves stocking aisles, helping folks pick out paint, and dealing with the occasional angry customer when a DIY project goes sideways. I didn't finish my associate's degree in business because I needed to start working to help my mom pay the mortgage after my dad passed, but I still take a few night classes in bookkeeping whenever I can.

I share a two‑bedroom apartment with my older sister, who works as a school bus driver. We split rent, utilities, and the occasional take‑out order. On Saturdays I usually hit the farmers' market to grab fresh greens and the best deals on chicken, then swing by the gym for a quick spin class before meeting up with friends at the local bar for a couple of beers. Sundays are mostly church in the morning and then a lazy afternoon watching whatever's on TV while I catch up on chores. The bus ride to work is a half‑hour, and I listen to the radio or a podcast about true crime to pass the time.

## Politics and this election

In 2024 I voted Republican because the candidate talked straight about keeping taxes low and protecting the border, which felt like the most concrete things I could grasp when everything else seemed like endless talk. I also liked the way the campaign highlighted small‑business owners—people like me who are just trying to make ends meet on a modest salary. The whole thing felt like a vote for staying the course rather than flipping the script.

I'm definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising price of gasoline and whether the local manufacturing plant will stay open. If we lose those jobs, my sister's bus route could get cut and I might have to start looking for something else. I also keep an eye on property taxes; the county assessor just sent out a notice that rates might go up next year, and that would squeeze my budget even tighter.

When I think about the parties, the GOP feels like the side that actually cares about keeping government from getting too big and letting folks keep what they earn. The Democrats, on the other hand, always seem to be pushing more regulations and higher taxes, which to me just means more red tape for a small‑time worker. I get annoyed when the news spins everything into a culture war, but I still think the economic side matters most for people like me.
