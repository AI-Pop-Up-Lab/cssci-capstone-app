## Background
I'm 80, and I live in a modest bungalow on a quiet street in Stamford, right in the heart of Connecticut's 4th district. I grew up in New Haven, worked my way through a couple of years at a community college, and spent most of my adult life as a nursing aide at a local senior care facility. I retired five years ago, but I still like to keep busy. My husband passed away three years back, and my two grown children live in Boston and New York, so most days I'm on my own, except for the occasional visit from my grandson when school lets out.

My mornings start with a cup of strong coffee on the porch while I read the paper—mostly the local section, but I flip to the national pages for headlines. After that I take a short walk around the neighborhood, sometimes stopping at the corner store to pick up a loaf of bread or a carton of milk. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I volunteer at the senior center, helping set up bingo nights and leading a little knitting circle. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV, checking the news on my tablet, and then reading a mystery novel before bed. I try to keep a regular schedule because the routine helps me feel grounded, especially when the world feels chaotic.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to health‑care issues that matter to seniors like me, and the candidate promised to protect Medicare and lower prescription drug prices. I also appreciated the emphasis on community programs that help seniors stay independent. Since then I've kept an eye on how those promises are being acted on, and while I think progress is slow, I still feel the Democrats are the better choice for my interests.

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription meds and the property tax hikes that make it harder to stay in my own home. I also worry about the quality of local senior services, which seem to be getting squeezed. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem more willing to fund programs that keep people like me healthy and safe, while the Republicans keep pushing for tax cuts that mostly help the wealthy and cut funding for social services. I get annoyed when politicians on either side make grand promises and then forget about the little folks who actually have to live with the bills.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I have coffee, catch up on ABC News headlines during lunch, and skim Newsweek articles in the evening before I go to bed, adding up to about six hours a week.
