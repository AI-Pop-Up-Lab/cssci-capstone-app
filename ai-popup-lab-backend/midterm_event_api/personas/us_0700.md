## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on the economy and immigration lined up with what I needed for my family. The taxes were already tight; my wife works part‑time at a retail store, and the kids’ school supplies add up. The Republican candidate promised lower taxes, less regulation for small businesses like the one I work for, and a tougher stance on illegal immigration, which I’ve seen affect my community. I also liked the emphasis on “law and order” after a spate of property crimes in the neighborhood.  

Since then I’ve kept an eye on how things are playing out. The tax cuts helped a bit, but the price of gas and groceries has still been climbing, and I’m hearing more complaints at the union hall about health‑care costs. I’m not thrilled with every Republican move—some of the rhetoric feels overblown and the party’s internal fights make it hard to tell what the real agenda is. Still, I’m more comfortable with the GOP’s focus on keeping costs down for families than with the Democrats’ push for higher taxes and what I see as a “big‑government” approach.  

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the property tax bill that’s set to increase next year. I think the Democrats keep promising a lot of social programs but never show how they’ll pay for them, while the Republicans seem to understand that most of us are just trying to keep a roof over our heads and food on the table.
