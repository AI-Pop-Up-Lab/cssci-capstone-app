## Background
I'm 40, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Brownsville, right near the border. I work as a shift supervisor at a local food‑processing plant; the job is mostly standing on a line, making sure the machines run smooth and the crew hits the quotas. I didn't finish my associate's degree in logistics because the plant needed extra hands, so I stayed and learned on the job. My wife works part‑time at a community health clinic, and we have two kids—Javier, 10, who rides the bus to middle school, and little Sofia, 5, who’s in pre‑K. Most mornings I grab a coffee from the corner taquería, drop the kids at school, then drive the 20‑minute commute to the plant. After my shift I help with homework, try to squeeze in a little Spanish‑language TV for the kids, and on weekends we do yard work or visit my parents in nearby Harlingen. I’m a regular at the local church's food pantry and I try to stay involved in the neighborhood association, which meets once a month to talk about street lighting and water‑service issues. Money’s tight, so I keep a close eye on the utility bills and try to stretch every paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democrat in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to the needs of working families like mine—especially on health care and immigration. The candidate promised to protect DACA recipients and to push for more community health funding, which mattered to my wife’s clinic and to my own worries about my kids’ future. I also liked the idea of a modest raise for minimum‑wage workers, even though the plant doesn’t pay that yet. Since then, I’ve been watching how the current administration handles the border and trade. I’m still leaning Democratic, but I’m a bit annoyed that the promises about school funding haven’t materialized. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I’m not a protest voter—I just want someone who will keep the pressure on the Senate to pass a decent health‑care bill and to fund the water‑infrastructure projects that keep flooding at bay. My top everyday concerns are the rising grocery prices and the property taxes that keep climbing even though my house value hasn’t gone up much. I see the Republicans as more business‑friendly, which helps the plant stay open, but I think they often ignore the immigrant community and don’t care enough about the social safety net. The Democrats, in my view, are more in touch with families like mine, even if they sometimes get tangled in internal fights.
