## Background

I got my associate degree in automotive technology from a community college in Snohomish County, and after a couple of internships I landed a full‑time gig fixing cars and doing warranty work. My day starts early—usually around 5 a.m., I grab a coffee from the corner deli, hop on my bike, and ride three miles to the shop. The shift runs till about 2 p.m., then I head back home to a modest two‑bedroom townhouse I share with my girlfriend, Maya, and our 2‑year‑old son, Jalen.

After work I sometimes swing by the local gym for a quick lift, but most evenings are spent cooking dinner, helping Jalen with his blocks, and catching up on TV or the internet while Maya watches a streaming series. On weekends we do grocery runs at the big box store, mow the lawn, and I try to fit in a few hours of overtime at the shop when the schedule allows. I’m not a big traveler; my world is mostly the neighborhood, the shop, and the occasional family barbecue at a cousin’s place in Seattle.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on taxes and small‑business support resonated with my situation. The candidate promised to cut back on regulations that make it harder for shops like mine to stay profitable, and that sounded like something that could actually help me keep my hours. I also liked the tougher stance on crime; the rising break‑ins in our area made me nervous, and I thought a stricter approach might keep neighborhoods safer.

Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out. The tax cuts did give me a little more take‑home pay, but the cost of parts has gone up, and the supply chain still feels tight. I’m still leaning Republican, but I’m a bit annoyed that the party hasn’t pushed harder on the local job‑training programs I think would help a lot of people in the district. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms—I'm voting. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent as the market squeezes us and the price of gasoline, which eats into my commute and the cost of getting the trucks we service to the shop. I see the Democrats as too focused on big‑picture climate goals that don’t translate into real help for working‑class folks in places like Everett, while the Republicans seem more attuned to my day‑to‑day concerns, even if they sometimes talk past me on cultural issues.
