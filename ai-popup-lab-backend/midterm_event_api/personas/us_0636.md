## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s stance on border security and lower taxes matched what I needed to keep my family’s budget from blowing up. The candidate promised to keep regulations light on small businesses like the one I work for, and that sounded like a win for my paycheck. I also appreciated the tough talk on immigration; living near the border makes that a daily conversation at the shop. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have played out. Gas prices have been high, which hurts my commute, and the cost of parts for my work has risen. I’m still leaning Republican, but I’m a bit annoyed that some of the rhetoric feels exaggerated and that the party isn’t doing enough on infrastructure that would help my trade. I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms; I’ve already filled out my ballot and plan to drop it off at the local post office on Election Day. My top concerns are the rising cost of gasoline and making sure there are enough skilled‑trade jobs in the area so my kids don’t have to move away to find work. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones who understand the need for a strong economy and personal responsibility, even if they sometimes get caught up in partisan drama. The Democrats, in my view, push for higher taxes and more regulation that make it harder for folks like me to keep a stable income, and they seem out of touch with the everyday struggles of working‑class families.
