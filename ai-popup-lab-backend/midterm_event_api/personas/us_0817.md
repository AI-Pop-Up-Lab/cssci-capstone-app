## Background
I'm 25 and I work as an automotive service technician at a shop on the edge of Brookings. I finished an associate’s program in automotive technology at a community college in Sioux Falls, then moved back to be closer to my parents, who run a small taquería on Main Street. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment above the shop with a roommate who’s studying nursing. My day starts around 6 a.m. with a quick coffee from the corner diner, then I head to the garage for an 8‑to‑4 shift fixing trucks and cars for local farmers and the occasional college student. After work I often swing by the family restaurant to help close up on Tuesdays and Saturdays, chopping veggies and taking orders when we get a rush. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, catching a game at the local bar, and hanging out with friends from the community college who still play pickup soccer at the park. I’m saving up for a down payment on a house, so most of my spare cash goes toward rent, utilities, and sending a little bit back to my younger siblings who are still in high school in Minnesota.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because the campaign talked about things that mattered to me: a plan to expand vocational training, a push for more affordable health coverage, and a clear stance on protecting immigrant families. I also liked the candidate’s promise to invest in rural broadband, which would make my job easier and help my parents attract more customers online. Since then I’ve stayed pretty tuned to what’s happening, even if I only have an hour a week for news. I’m definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms; I feel like my vote can still tip the balance on issues that affect my paycheck and my family’s future.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising cost of rent and the price at the pump. Gas prices have crept up enough that my commute eats into my take‑home pay, and the landlord has hinted at a rent increase next lease. I’m also watching health insurance premiums because I’m thinking about getting a family plan once I’m married. When it comes to the parties, I see Democrats as the ones more willing to talk about student debt relief, immigration reform, and funding for trade schools—things that line up with my own experience. The Republicans, in my view, keep pushing tax cuts for big businesses and talk tough on immigration, which feels like it leaves folks like me and my community out of the conversation. I’m not a political scholar, but I’m fed up with the constant bickering and wish both sides would focus more on practical solutions for everyday folks.
