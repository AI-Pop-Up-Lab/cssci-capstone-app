## Background
I'm 65, and I live in Council Bluffs, right on the Missouri River. I never finished high school; after my kids were grown I took a job stocking shelves at the local grocery store because the paycheck helped cover the rent on the small house I own. My eldest daughter moved to Des Moines years ago, and my son lives a few blocks away with his wife and two grandkids. Most mornings I get up around 5:30 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and catch the local news on the TV while the coffee brews. After that I head out to the store, usually working the 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. shift. The job’s not glamorous, but the routine keeps me moving and the hours let me be home for lunch with my son’s family when they’re around.

In the evenings I like to sit on the porch, watch the sunset over the river, and tend to my garden of tomatoes and peppers. I’m a regular at the nearby Catholic church; the Sunday Mass and the weekly bingo night are the main social outlets I have. I also volunteer a few hours a month at the senior center, helping with the bingo tables and handing out snacks. Money is tight, so I’m careful about groceries and try to stretch every dollar. I drive a 2005 pickup truck that’s seen better days, but it gets me to work and the doctor’s appointments without trouble.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for folks like me who are trying to make ends meet on a fixed income. I was tired of hearing about “big government” and the endless tax hikes that seemed to target middle‑class retirees. The candidate’s promise to keep taxes low, protect my right to own a gun, and support law‑and‑order resonated with my daily concerns. I also liked the talk about cutting red tape for small businesses, which matters to the grocery store where I work.

Since then, a lot of the same worries have stuck around. Prices at the store keep climbing, and I’m watching my prescription costs eat a bigger slice of my budget. I still think the Democrats are more interested in talking about climate change and identity politics than fixing the things that hit my wallet. I’m not a political pundit, but I feel the Republicans, even when they argue among themselves, at least keep the focus on the economy and personal responsibility. So I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms—“I’m voting,” as I tell my son when he asks. My top everyday concerns are the price of groceries and the cost of my prescription meds. I also keep an eye on property taxes because any increase would mean I’d have to cut back on food or the little extras I allow myself.

I’m not thrilled with any party’s whole platform, but I’m more annoyed by the Democrats’ push for higher taxes and what I see as “government overreach.” The Republicans, for all their flaws, still talk about keeping taxes low and giving people a chance to keep what they’ve earned. That’s the angle that matters most to me right now.
