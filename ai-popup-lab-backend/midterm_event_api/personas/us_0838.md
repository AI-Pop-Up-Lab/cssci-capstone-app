## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the outskirts of Jackson, Tennessee, right in the 8th District. I spent most of my adult life working as a diesel mechanic at the local plant; after a back injury forced me to cut back, I took a part‑time supervisory role in the maintenance department. I never finished my associate’s degree, but I did a couple of years of automotive technology classes at a community college, which is why my education level is “some college.” My wife passed away five years ago, and I’m mostly on my own now, though my two grown‑up children visit on holidays and I sometimes watch my granddaughter when her parents are at work. My days start early with a strong cup of coffee and a quick scan of the headlines on my tablet while the rooster crows outside. I drive the short walk to the senior center three times a week for bingo and a low‑impact exercise class. In the afternoons I either help the younger guys at the plant with troubleshooting or tend to my garden—tomatoes, okra, and a few pepper plants that keep the kitchen stocked. Evenings are for TV, a bit of radio, and the occasional call with my sister in Memphis. I keep the house warm with a cheap electric heater and try to stretch my Social Security checks to cover the rising utility bills.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate promised to keep taxes low, protect my pension, and crack down on crime in the city. I’m not a die‑hard ideologue; I just wanted a government that wouldn’t raise my property tax again and that would keep the factory jobs that keep my community alive. The Democratic candidate talked a lot about climate policy and big‑city infrastructure, which felt distant from the problems I see on my street. That vote felt like a practical choice, not a grand statement. 

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. The biggest things on my mind are the cost of my prescription drugs and the fact that the county is considering a new sales tax to fund a sports complex that I don’t think we need. I also worry about whether the plant will stay open when the new trade rules get enforced. I’m still leaning Republican because I think they’re more likely to keep my taxes down and protect the kind of jobs I’m used to. The Democrats, in my view, keep promising more social programs but never seem to address the concrete needs of black folks in small towns. Sometimes I’m annoyed that they talk about “justice reform” without acknowledging that a lot of us already support law‑and‑order policies. I’m not thrilled with either party, but I’ll cast my ballot for the GOP candidate this time.

## Media Diet
I spend about ten hours a week glued to the TV and my phone—NBC News in the mornings while I eat breakfast, CNBC in the evenings after work, and I skim articles on Breitbart when I have a spare moment at the senior center.
