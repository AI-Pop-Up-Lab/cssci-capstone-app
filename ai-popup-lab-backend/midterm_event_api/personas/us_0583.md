## Background
I'm 80, and I live in Derry, a quiet suburb in New Hampshire's 2nd district. I spent most of my adult life working as a cashier at the local grocery store; I retired about twelve years ago when the store closed down. My husband passed away five years ago, so I mostly keep to myself, but I go to the senior center twice a week for bingo and a craft class. My daughter lives in Boston and visits every summer, and I call her on Sundays to hear about her kids. My day usually starts with a cup of tea on the porch, watching the mail truck go by, then a short walk to the pharmacy for my blood pressure meds. I shop at the nearby market in the late morning, grab a sandwich at the deli, and spend the afternoon watching the news on the TV in the living room. Evenings are for crossword puzzles and a little radio, and I try to catch my favorite talk shows before heading to bed around ten.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about keeping taxes low and protecting the way of life we grew up with. The candidate promised to look after small‑business owners like the grocery store I used to work at, and I liked the tough talk on crime and immigration—things I hear about on the news that make me nervous. I don't follow every campaign rally, but the ads on TV and the flyers I get in the mail convinced me that the GOP was the safer bet for my pocket and my values.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries are the price of my prescription drugs and the property taxes that keep creeping up on my modest home. I also keep an eye on gas prices because I still drive to the senior center and the pharmacy. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to want more government control and higher taxes, which feels like a burden for people on fixed incomes. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about cutting wasteful spending and preserving the traditions that matter to folks like me. I get annoyed when the news talks about "progress" that feels like it ignores seniors, but I also know not every Republican is perfect. Still, I lean toward the GOP because they speak my language on taxes, law and order, and keeping the country stable.
