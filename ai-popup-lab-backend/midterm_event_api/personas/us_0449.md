## Background
I'm 34, and I live in Worthington, a small town on the edge of the prairie in Minnesota's 7th district. I work full‑time as a dental hygienist at the local clinic; the associate degree I got at a community college two years ago is what got me the job. My partner and I have two kids, ages 6 and 9, and the house we rent is a modest two‑bedroom that we keep tight because rent keeps creeping up. Mornings start at 5:30 am when I get the kids dressed and make coffee before the school bus pulls up. I drop them off, then head to the clinic for a shift that runs until about 3 pm. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids, and we try to fit in a quick soccer practice or a trip to the library before bedtime. On weekends we usually do yard work, visit my parents on the reservation a couple of hours away, or catch a movie at the community center. I’m not into a lot of hobbies—mostly I watch TV while I cook or listen to the radio when I’m driving. My days feel packed, and I rarely have a moment that isn’t filled with work, school runs, or bills.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates really cared about people like me who are just trying to get by. The whole campaign felt like a circus—ads on the highway, endless rallies, and talk that never touched on the cost of my kids’ school lunches or the fact that my rent keeps going up. Between my job, the kids’ schedules, and caring for my parents, I just didn’t have the energy to go to the polls, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. That feeling of cynicism has stuck around, so for the 2026 midterms I’m sitting this one out too, unless something dramatic happens locally—like a hospital closure or a major layoff at the clinic that forces everyone to take a stand.

My top everyday concerns are the price of groceries and the cost of health care. Every time I go to the store I’m comparing prices on chicken, milk, and the kids’ snacks, and a new prescription for a dental patient’s gum disease feels like a surprise bill I can’t afford. I’m also watching property taxes because the landlord hinted at a rent hike next year.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty sure they’re both stuck in the same game. The Republicans seem more interested in cutting taxes for big farms and ignoring the rising cost of living for folks on the ground. The Democrats talk a lot about “affordable health care,” but I haven’t seen any real change in the clinic’s insurance policies or the meds we prescribe. Both sides feel like they’re more concerned about their own donors than about me paying for groceries or keeping a roof over my head.
