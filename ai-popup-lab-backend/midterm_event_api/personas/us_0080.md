## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking straight about the border, keeping taxes low, and protecting jobs for people like me who don’t have a college background. The Democratic candidates kept promising big‑government programs that I thought would just raise my taxes and make it harder for small contractors to get work. I also liked the way the Republican candidate talked about “getting the country back to common sense.”  

I’m definitely going to the polls again in the 2026 midterms – “I’m voting.” My biggest worries right now are the rising price of gas and the fact that a lot of the construction projects I rely on are getting delayed because of supply‑chain hiccups. I’m also nervous about health‑care costs; my wife’s part‑time job doesn’t give her insurance, and I’ve been looking at the market for a decent plan. As far as the parties go, the GOP still feels like the side that cares about working‑class folks and keeps the government from getting too big. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing ideas that sound good on paper but end up hurting people who are just trying to make ends meet. I don’t trust either side completely, but I’m more comfortable with the Republicans on the issues that affect my daily life.
