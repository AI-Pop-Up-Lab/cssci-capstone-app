## Background
I'm 50, and I live in the Windsor area of Colorado's 7th district. I work as an operations supervisor for a regional construction firm that builds office parks and schools around the Front Range. My job means I’m on site most mornings, checking plans, coordinating crews, and making sure we stay on budget, then I head back to the office for paperwork in the afternoon. My wife runs a small boutique coffee shop downtown, and we have two kids—16 and 13—who both ride the school bus to the high school in Fort Collins. Most weekends we’re either at the kids’ soccer games, doing a DIY project in the garage, or loading up the truck for a quick trip to the mountains for a hike. I drive a pickup truck that I keep in the garage, and I’m usually up by 6 a.m. to beat the rush, grab a coffee, and start the day. Evenings are a mix of family dinner, a little TV, and catching up on emails from work before I finally hit the bed around 11.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s message on lowering taxes and cutting red tape for businesses matched what I see every day on the job. When I hear talk about higher corporate taxes, it feels like a direct hit to the payrolls I manage and the projects we try to get approved. I also liked the candidate’s focus on “protecting our borders,” which for me meant less competition for local jobs from people moving in without a plan. Since then I’ve stayed pretty consistent—my concerns haven’t changed much. I’m voting again in the 2026 midterms; I’ll be at the polling place on Tuesday after work. My top everyday worries are keeping up with the mortgage payments now that rates have crept up, and the price of gas for the truck and our family’s commute. I’m also watching the local job market; if construction slows, a lot of us feel the pinch fast. As for the parties, I’m fed up with the Democrats seeming to want bigger government programs that raise my taxes, and I think they’re too quick to push social agendas that don’t affect my day‑to‑day life. The GOP, for all its internal squabbles, still feels like the side that understands the need for a stable economy and less regulation, even if I don’t love every single candidate they put forward.
