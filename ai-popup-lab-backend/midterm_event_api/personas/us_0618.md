## Background  
I'm 69, and I live in a modest house on a cul‑de‑sac in Ocean Township, right in the middle of NJ‑8. I never finished high school; after dropping out at 16 I picked up a series of odd jobs—mostly working as a building custodian for a local high school and doing occasional handyman gigs for neighbors. I’m still on the payroll part‑time because the pension I get from the school district barely covers the rent on my place.  

My wife passed away five years ago, so it’s just me and my two grown‑up kids who live a few hours away in Philadelphia. I see my daughter once a month, and my son calls me every couple of weeks. On Sundays I help my grandson with his homework when I can; the kid stays with his mom in the city most of the week.  

My day starts around 5:30 a.m. with a cup of black coffee and the local paper, then I head over to the school to sweep hallways, empty trash cans, and fix squeaky doors. The work is physical, but I like the routine. After my shift I swing by the corner store for a sandwich, stop at the pharmacy to pick up my blood‑pressure meds, and then sit on my porch watching the neighborhood kids play. Evenings are for TV—usually the news on one of the channels I follow—followed by a late‑night call with my sister in Newark. When I’m not working, I’m either at the community center playing checkers or at the church basement helping with the food pantry.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus. The campaigns all sounded the same, and I never saw any of the promises trickle down to my street. Between my job, dealing with my health issues, and trying to keep up with the rising cost of my medication, voting just felt like an extra chore I didn’t have the energy for. I also get the sense that the politicians in Washington don’t really care about folks like me—people who work a few hours a day just to keep a roof over their heads.

That feeling hasn’t changed much, so I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something huge hits the local scene—like a major factory closing or a real scandal that threatens my community—I don’t see a reason to go to the polls. My biggest worries right now are the property tax bill that keeps climbing every year and the price of groceries, especially fresh produce. I also worry about my health coverage; the co‑pays keep going up and my doctor’s office is getting harder to get an appointment with.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats talk a lot about “justice” and “equality,” but I don’t see any real help for people who can’t finish high school or who are stuck in low‑pay jobs. The Republicans keep pushing tax cuts that seem to end up in the pockets of the rich, while the cost of living for folks like me just keeps rising. I’m not a political theorist; I just want the government to stop making things harder for ordinary people.
