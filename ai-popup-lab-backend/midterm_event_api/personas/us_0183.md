## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Moultrie, Georgia, right in the 3rd congressional district. I spent most of my adult life working as a clerical assistant at the local community college after finishing a two‑year associate program in office administration. I retired about ten years ago, so most days now revolve around a routine that keeps me busy enough not to feel lonely. I get up around 6 a.m., make a cup of coffee, and watch the morning news on the TV while the kettle whistles. After that I head out to the senior center for a low‑impact exercise class and then back home to tend to my little garden—tomatoes and a few herbs that I grow in the backyard.

My husband passed away five years ago, and my two adult children live in Atlanta and Miami. They call most weekends, and I try to video‑chat with them, though sometimes the connection drops. I take a daily walk around the neighborhood to keep my joints moving, stop by the local pharmacy to pick up my blood‑pressure meds, and swing by the grocery store for fresh produce. Sundays are for church; I sit in the pews of the small Spanish‑speaking congregation downtown and stay for the potluck afterward. I’m involved in the church’s volunteer group that helps deliver meals to other seniors who can’t get out.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party best understood the importance of keeping taxes low for retirees and protecting the kind of traditional values I grew up with. I was especially concerned about Social Security and Medicare being threatened by big‑government proposals, and the Republican candidate promised to defend those programs. I also liked the emphasis on border security; my family has relatives who still live in Mexico, and I worry about how illegal immigration affects the job market and public safety back home.

I’m definitely voting again in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the property tax hikes the county has been pushing to fund road repairs. I’m also watching the debate over school curricula, because I don’t want my grandchildren’s kids to be taught things that clash with what I believe about family and history. As for the parties, I see the Republicans as the ones trying to keep things stable for folks like me—low taxes, strong defense, limited government meddling. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for more regulation and higher taxes, and they seem to ignore the real struggles of seniors trying to stretch a fixed income. I’m not a political scholar, but I trust my gut and the folks I hear on the news.
