## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on a quiet street in Brockton, Massachusetts, right in the heart of the 9th district. I work part‑time as a checkout clerk at the local grocery store; the hours are flexible enough that I can still help my sister with her Spanish‑language tutoring business on the weekends. My husband passed away five years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up kids who live in Boston and Springfield. I see them a few times a year, mostly for holidays, and I keep in touch by phone most days. My mornings start with a strong cup of coffee, a quick scan of the newspaper headlines, and then a short walk around the block before heading to the store. After my shift I usually stop by the community center for a bingo game or a senior‑craft class, and on Tuesdays I attend mass at the nearby parish. I try to keep the house tidy, tend to my garden of tomatoes and rosemary, and make sure my prescriptions are refilled before the month ends. The biggest hassle lately has been the rising cost of heating oil and the occasional pothole that makes my car shake on the way to work.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one that cared about the cost of living for people like me. The Democratic candidates kept talking about big‑government solutions that seemed to ignore the fact that my paycheck barely covers rent, groceries, and my medication. I liked the Republican message about keeping taxes low, protecting the border, and supporting law‑and‑order policies that make my neighborhood feel safer at night. I also trusted the candidate’s promise to protect Social Security from cuts, even if the details were vague.

Since then, I’ve watched a few more debates and read a lot of opinion pieces, and my feelings have shifted a bit. I’m still skeptical of the Democrats; they talk a lot about climate change and student debt, but I don’t see how that helps a 70‑year‑old who’s trying to stretch a fixed income. On the other hand, some of the Republican rhetoric feels more extreme than I’m comfortable with, especially the talk about cutting some senior benefits to fund other programs. Still, my top concerns are keeping my prescription drug costs down and making sure my property taxes don’t skyrocket next year. Those are the things that will decide whether I go to the polls.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. I’m going to the local precinct on Tuesday because I want a representative who will push for lower property taxes and protect Medicare. I’m less interested in the big national issues and more focused on who will stand up for senior services in our district.
