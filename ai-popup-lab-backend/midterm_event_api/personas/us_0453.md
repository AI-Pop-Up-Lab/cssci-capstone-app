## Background  
I'm 35 and work as a data scientist for a health‑tech startup that’s based in the Twin Cities. Most days I’m on the train from St. Louis Park to downtown Minneapolis, pulling a laptop and a coffee, though we’ve been flexible about working from home a few days a week. My wife is a high‑school English teacher, and we have a six‑year‑old son who’s in second grade at the local public school. After work I usually swing by the grocery store on the way home, pick up a few things for dinner, and then help our kid with his math worksheets while my wife cooks. Weekends are a mix of soccer practice, a quick trip to the lake for a family barbecue, and trying to keep up with the house—fixing the leaky faucet in the bathroom and mowing the lawn when the weather lets us. I’m a member of the local gym, but I only get in when I can squeeze a 30‑minute treadmill run in before the kids go to bed. I try to keep up with the neighborhood association meetings because the new development plans keep popping up, and I don’t want to be blindsided by any zoning changes that could affect our property taxes.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attentive to health‑care access and climate initiatives, which mattered to me both as a data scientist in health tech and as a parent worrying about the future. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding broadband in rural parts of Minnesota, which I know some of my extended family rely on. Over the past two years I’ve watched the rollout of some of those projects, and while it’s been slower than promised, I still think the effort is worth the tax dollars. The Republican platform this cycle feels like it’s all about cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back environmental regulations, which I see as a step backward for our state’s clean‑energy goals and for the cost of living in places like St. Louis Park.

I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising grocery prices and the cost of childcare—both have been creeping up since the pandemic and they eat into what little extra we can save each month. I’m also watching property taxes because the recent assessments in our district have jumped, and I’m not sure the town will find a way to offset that without cutting services. My view of the parties is pretty lopsided: I’m frustrated with the GOP’s focus on cultural issues that feel disconnected from the day‑to‑day struggles of middle‑class families, and I’m skeptical of how the Democrats handle the national debt, but overall I think they’re more likely to push policies that help families like mine. I’m not a political junkie, but when I do think about it, the Democratic side seems the lesser of two evils for now.
