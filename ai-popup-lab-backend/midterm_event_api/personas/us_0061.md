## Background
I'm 45, and I live in Glendale, right in the heart of Arizona's 8th district. I work as a middle‑school math teacher at a public school that's about a ten‑minute drive from my house. My day starts around 5:30 am; I get the kids ready for school, toss a quick breakfast together, and then hop on the freeway to the school. The commute is always jammed, so I listen to NPR podcasts and the morning news on ABC while stuck in traffic. After school ends at 3 pm, I stay for a few hours to grade papers and run the after‑school robotics club. My husband works the night shift at a local warehouse, so when he gets home around 11 pm we both try to catch up on dinner and the kids' homework. We have two kids—16‑year‑old Maya, who’s into soccer, and 13‑year‑old Jamal, who loves video games. On weekends I’m usually at church, at the community garden, or at Maya’s soccer games. Money's tight enough that we keep an eye on the grocery bill, but we’re not renting; we own a modest three‑bedroom house that needs a new roof soon. I drive a used sedan, and my spare time is spent scrolling through social media or watching the occasional Netflix series after the kids are asleep.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party cared more about public schools and health care, both of which hit my family hard. My kids rely on the school’s free lunch program, and my husband’s job doesn’t come with health benefits, so the idea of expanding Medicaid and increasing education funding mattered to me. I also liked the candidate’s stance on criminal‑justice reform, something that resonates in my community. Since then, I’ve watched the rollout of a few education grants and some modest Medicaid expansions in the state, but progress feels slower than I’d hoped. I’m still convinced that the Democratic platform aligns better with my priorities, even though I get annoyed by the constant partisan bickering on TV.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my kids’ school funded and making sure our health insurance stays affordable. The rising cost of groceries is a close third. I’m pretty fed up with the Republican side; they keep pushing for cuts to public school budgets and want to roll back some of the health‑care expansions we’ve gotten. The Democrats, for all their flaws, at least seem to recognize the need for a safety net. I’m not a political analyst, I just want policies that keep my family from falling behind.
