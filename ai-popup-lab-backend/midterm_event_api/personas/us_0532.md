## Background
I'm 40 and I work as a delivery driver for a regional freight company. I live in Goldsboro, right in the middle of NC‑10, in a modest two‑bedroom house I bought with my wife a few years back. We have two kids, a 9‑year‑old daughter and a 6‑year‑old son, who go to the local public elementary school. My parents moved here from California when I was a teen, so I grew up speaking a mix of Mandarin at home and English at school, but I never went to college; I got my high school diploma and went straight into a series of entry‑level jobs before landing the driving gig that pays enough to keep the lights on.

My day starts early, around 5 a.m., with a quick coffee and a look at the traffic report on my phone before I head out. I spend most of the day on the road, stopping at rest areas and loading docks, and I try to eat a sandwich or something from the truck stop because there isn’t time for a proper lunch. When I get home around 7 p.m., I help the kids with homework, talk with my wife about bills, and we all watch a bit of TV before bed. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, a little yard work, and trying to catch a baseball game on the radio when the schedule allows. I’m not religious, but I do go to the community center once a month for a cultural festival with other Asian families.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I didn’t vote for either Biden or Trump; I cast my ballot for a third‑party candidate because I felt both major parties were ignoring working‑class folks like me. The candidate promised a “real” jobs plan and lower freight taxes, which sounded like something that might actually affect my paycheck. I was also angry at the constant political bickering and thought a third option could shake things up.

I’m planning to vote in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising gas prices that make my routes expensive and the uncertainty about health‑care costs for my family. I’m also keeping an eye on property taxes; the county has been talking about a hike to fund a new school wing. I’m pretty fed up with the Republicans for pushing tax cuts that never seem to reach people in my zip code, and I think the Democrats are too focused on national issues and not enough on the day‑to‑day struggles of folks who have to drive long hours to make ends meet. I’m not enthusiastic about either side, but I’ll vote for the candidate who looks like they’ll actually listen to people who work in logistics and have a family to feed.

## Media Diet
I usually catch the morning headlines from NPR while I’m sipping coffee before my route, and I scroll through NBC News and CNBC updates on my phone during breaks and on the way home, totaling about six hours a week.
