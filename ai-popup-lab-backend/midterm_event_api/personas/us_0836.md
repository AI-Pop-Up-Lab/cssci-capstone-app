## Background
I'm 35, and I work as a logistics coordinator for a regional distribution center just outside Murfreesboro. I finished a couple of years of community college studying business, but I never completed a degree because the job paid decent wages and I needed to help support my parents who still live in a small apartment in the city. I rent a one‑bedroom place a few miles north of downtown, sharing the kitchen with a roommate who does construction work. My days start early: I’m usually up by 5:30 am, scrolling through the news on my phone while I brew coffee, then I’m on the road by 6:15 am loading trucks and tracking shipments. After a shift that runs until about 3 pm, I either hit the gym for an hour or meet up with a few friends at a local BBQ joint. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, catching a Raptors game on TV, and helping my parents with paperwork for their immigration status. I don’t own a car; I rely on a company‑provided van for work and a bike for short trips around town.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hours at the warehouse, caring for my parents’ paperwork, and feeling like the candidates were just swapping the same promises, I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The campaign ads felt like noise, and the debates were just a showcase of how each side blames the other without offering real solutions for folks like me who are trying to keep a roof over our heads and a steady paycheck. That cynicism has stuck with me, so I’m sitting this one out too, unless something blows up locally—like a sudden plant closure or a major road project that messes up my commute. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of gas, which eats into my take‑home pay, and property taxes that make my rent feel like a mortgage. I also keep an eye on the job market; any hint of layoffs at the distribution center makes me uneasy.

When I think about the parties, I’m annoyed by both. The Democrats seem obsessed with identity politics and talk big about climate goals, but I don’t see any concrete help for a truck driver dealing with fuel price spikes. The Republicans keep pushing culture wars and tax cuts that sound good on paper, yet they ignore the fact that my parents are dealing with health‑care bills that keep climbing. Neither side feels like they’re speaking to the everyday grind of people who just want a stable paycheck and affordable living. So far, my plan is to stay home on Election Day, maybe drop a ballot in the drop box if a friend convinces me it’s worth it.
