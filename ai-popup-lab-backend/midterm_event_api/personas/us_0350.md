## Background
I'm 79 and I've lived my whole life in the Gentilly section of New Orleans, right in the heart of Louisiana's 1st district. I never finished high school; after working a few years at a local laundromat I married my husband, raised three kids, and spent most of my adult years doing housekeeping for a few families in the neighborhood. My husband passed away ten years ago, and now I get a modest Social Security check and a little extra from my daughter, who lives just a few blocks away and helps with the bills when she can. Most days I wake up early, make coffee, and head to the small church down the street for the 8 a.m. service. After that I walk to the corner store to pick up milk and bread, chat with the clerk about the rising price of eggs, and then sit on my porch watching the world go by while the TV murmurs the morning news. In the afternoons I tend to my garden—tomatoes, okra, a few herbs—trying to stretch every dollar. Evenings are usually spent watching the news on the TV, sometimes catching an old sitcom before turning in. I don't drive much anymore; the bus is my main way to get around when I need to go to the pharmacy or the library.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the whole thing was a circus and none of the candidates seemed to care about people like me. My days are already packed with church, health appointments, and trying to keep the rent from climbing higher, so adding a trip to the polls felt like a waste of energy. The politicians keep promising better healthcare and lower grocery prices, but the grocery store on my block keeps raising its prices and the clinic is always overbooked. I figured my single vote wouldn't change anything, so I stayed home. This year I'm feeling the same way—unless something dramatic happens locally, like a sudden closure of the community health center, I'm just going to sit this one out. I'm not planning to go to the polls.

My biggest worries right now are the cost of rent and the price of groceries. My landlord gave me a notice that the rent will go up next month, and every time I go to the store, the price of chicken and fresh produce seems to jump a few cents higher. I also worry about my health; the co-pay for my medication keeps creeping up and the nearest pharmacy is always out of stock of the insulin I need. When I think about the political parties, I see the Democrats as the ones who say they'll help the Black community but then get tangled up in infighting and don't push through real change. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to ignore my neighborhood altogether, talking about tax cuts for the rich while I struggle to pay my utility bill. Neither side feels like it's listening to someone who's been working a lifetime and now just wants a little stability.

## Media Diet
I tune into ABC News and CBS News in the mornings while I eat breakfast, and I catch NPR segments in the evening after dinner. All together I spend about nine hours a week scrolling through their websites or listening on the radio.
