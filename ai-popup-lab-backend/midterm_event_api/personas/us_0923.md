## Background  
I'm 35 and work as a dental hygienist at a busy clinic in Everett, just inside the 6th district. My husband drives a delivery truck for a regional grocery chain, and we have a six‑year‑old daughter who goes to the public elementary school down the street. Most mornings I drop her off, grab a coffee from the corner bodega, and hop on the 2‑bus to the clinic. My shift runs from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., then I pick up my daughter, swing by the grocery store for produce, and try to squeeze in a quick workout at the community center before dinner. On weekends we usually do a mix of yard work at our modest house in Lynnwood and visits to my parents, who live a short drive away in a small town near the coast. Money is tight enough that we watch every line on the utility bill, but we manage to put a little aside for the daughter's soccer league and occasional family outings to the nearby park.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one paying any real attention to affordable health care and immigration reform—issues that hit my family hard. My sister, who came here as a teenager, still worries about her status, and my job gave me a front‑row seat to patients who can’t afford basic dental work. The Democratic candidate’s promises on expanding Medicaid and protecting DACA felt like the only thing that could keep us from sliding deeper into insecurity. Since then I’ve stayed pretty engaged, but I’m also realistic: I’m not a policy wonk and I only follow the big headlines. For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely heading to the polls; I can’t afford to sit out when the state legislature is debating a new housing tax that could push our rent even higher. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of housing and the price of groceries—especially fresh produce for my daughter’s meals. I see the Democrats as the party that at least pretends to care about families like mine, even if they sometimes get lost in lofty climate goals or internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more interested in cutting taxes for the wealthy and talking tough on immigration without offering any real path for people like my sister. That makes me skeptical of their promises, even though I do appreciate when they talk about small‑business relief that could help my husband’s employer.
