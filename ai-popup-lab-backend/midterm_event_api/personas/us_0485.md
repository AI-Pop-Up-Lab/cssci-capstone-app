## Background
I'm 55 and work as a high‑school English teacher in Oxford, a small college town that sits in the 2nd Congressional District of Mississippi. I grew up in a nearby farm community, went to the University of Mississippi for my bachelor's, and came back to teach because I liked the idea of staying close to home. My husband is a retired electrician, and our two kids are out of the house—one is finishing a master's in nursing in Birmingham, the other is working as a software developer in Atlanta. Most weekdays I leave the house at 6:15 a.m., drive the 15‑minute stretch to the school, teach three periods, grade papers during lunch, and stay late once a week for faculty meetings. After work I pick up groceries at the local market, help my husband with a few handyman jobs around the house, and then we usually cook dinner together. Sundays are for church services at the Baptist congregation down the road, followed by a potluck brunch with neighbors. On the occasional weekend I’ll go fishing at the nearby lake or attend a community theater performance at the historic Lyric Theatre.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I was worried about the direction of health‑care policy and the cuts to education funding that were being floated at the state level. The candidate’s promise to protect Medicaid expansion and increase funding for public schools felt like the only realistic way to keep my students from losing resources. I also liked the emphasis on protecting voting rights after the recent Supreme Court decisions; it seemed like a concrete way to keep my voice heard. Since then, I’ve watched the fallout from those decisions and the way the state legislature has tried to push back, which has only hardened my belief that the party that stands up for those protections is the one I need to support.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of health‑care premiums for my husband’s occasional doctor visits and the property tax hikes that keep pushing my mortgage payment higher each year. I’m also watching the local job market; the factory that used to be the biggest employer in town closed a few years ago, and the replacement jobs haven’t paid as well.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like the only ones pushing for decent health‑care and decent school budgets, even if they sometimes sound like they’re talking from a big‑city perspective. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep talking about lower taxes, but those cuts end up slashing the very services my family relies on—especially in a rural state like ours. I’m not a political theorist; I just want the folks in Washington and Jackson to stop playing games and start fixing the things that matter to everyday people.

## Media Diet
I usually catch up on NPR news in the morning while driving to school, and I scroll through ABC News and Newsweek articles during my lunch break. In the evenings I unwind with a quick look at CBS News headlines before bed, which adds up to about nine hours a week.
