## Background  
I'm 25 and work full‑time as a warehouse associate for a regional shipping company in the eastern part of Sioux Falls. I finished a couple of semesters of community‑college business courses before I needed a steady paycheck, so I never got a degree. I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment about a mile from my job, sharing it with a roommate who’s in a similar stage of life. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner diner, and catch the bus to the warehouse where I spend eight hours on the loading dock. After my shift I hit the gym for an hour, then swing by the grocery store to pick up a few staples—mostly frozen pizza, beans, and whatever’s on sale. Weekends are a mix of video games, hanging out at the local bar with a few friends, and helping my roommate move furniture when we have a break in the lease. I still talk to my parents on the phone once a week; they live on a farm near Watertown, and they remind me to “save some money for a rainy day.”  

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the candidate actually talked about raising the minimum wage and expanding health coverage, things that mattered to me as a low‑wage worker trying to afford rent and a decent health plan. I also liked the fact that the campaign reached out on social media, where I spend most of my free time. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies have played out—some of the promised student‑debt relief never materialized, and the inflation spike has made grocery bills feel even tighter. Still, I think the Democrats are the only party that’s trying to address the cost‑of‑living squeeze that hits people like me.  

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and finding a stable schedule that lets me move up from the warehouse floor. I’m also watching health‑care costs; a sudden illness would be a disaster with my current insurance. As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with the Republicans; their talks about cutting taxes seem to ignore the fact that most of us can’t even afford basic utilities. The Democrats get my vote, even if I’m annoyed when they waffle on climate policy or don’t push harder for a higher wage. I’m not a political scholar, I just want policies that keep the lights on and the paycheck enough to cover rent.
