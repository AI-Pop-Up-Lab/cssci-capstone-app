## Background  
I'm 75, and I live in a little house on the edge of Miles City, right in the middle of Montana's 2nd district. I spent most of my adult life working as a diesel mechanic for the local trucking company, fixing rigs that haul grain and coal across the plains. I retired ten years ago, but I still like to keep my hands busy—sometimes I help my neighbor fix a fence or I pick up a few odd jobs fixing snowplows in the winter. My wife, Margaret, is the same age; we moved here after the war and have been together for over fifty years. Our two kids live out in Seattle and Denver, so most of the time it's just us, the dog, and the radio.  

My days start early. I brew a strong cup of coffee, read the paper on the porch, and then head to the senior center for a bingo game or a woodworking class. After lunch I might take a short drive to the feed store, pick up a few supplies, and chat with the guys about the price of diesel. In the evenings I sit on the porch, watch a bit of TV, and sometimes scroll through the news on my tablet before heading to bed around 10 p.m. The routine is simple, and the community feels like an extended family.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party understood the challenges of a rural life like mine: high fuel costs, the need for reliable infrastructure, and a government that stays out of my backyard. The Democratic candidates kept talking about big‑city problems and climate policies that seemed to ignore the fact that my truck has to run on diesel to keep the farm supply chain moving. I also liked the emphasis on protecting the Second Amendment and keeping taxes low for small landowners.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are the rising price of gasoline and the shortage of doctors in our area. If the Senate or House doesn't do something to keep fuel affordable, my wife and I will have to cut back on trips to see the grandkids. And the lack of a decent primary care clinic means I have to drive two hours for a routine check‑up, which is getting harder as I age.  

When I think about the parties, the Republicans feel like the ones who remember what life is like out here—wide open skies, hard work, and a need for personal responsibility. The Democrats, in my view, are too focused on urban issues and seem to want to regulate everything, from farms to energy production, without understanding the knock‑on effects on people like me. I’m not blind to their flaws; I’ve seen some Republican leaders act more like career politicians than true representatives, but overall I trust them more to keep my community afloat.
