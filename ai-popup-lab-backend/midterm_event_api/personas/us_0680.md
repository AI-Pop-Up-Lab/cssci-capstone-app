## Background
I'm 79, and I live in the Greece neighborhood of Rochester, New York, right near the Erie Canal. I spent most of my working life driving a school bus for the Monroe County school district, and after I retired I took a few community‑college courses in business but never finished a degree. These days I do a bit of odd‑job handyman work for neighbors—fixing leaky faucets, hanging shelves—just enough to keep my hands busy and bring in a little extra cash for the occasional grocery splurge.

I'm a widower; my wife passed away five years ago. I have two grown children—one in Albany, one in Detroit—and they visit a few times a year. My grandson stays with me for a weekend every summer, and I love showing him the old baseball field behind my house. Most mornings I sit on the porch with a cup of black coffee, listen to NPR while the sun comes up, then head to the senior center for a stretch class or a chess game. Afternoons are usually spent checking the mail, tending to a small vegetable patch, or stopping by the local library to pick up the latest issue of Newsweek. Evenings I watch the news on ABC, then read a few articles on my tablet before bed.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because the candidate promised to protect Social Security and Medicare, which are my lifelines now that I'm on a fixed income. The campaign also talked about affordable prescription drugs, something I struggle with every month. I felt the Democratic platform aligned more with my needs as a senior Black man living in a city that still has pockets of economic hardship. I wasn't thrilled with any of the rhetoric, but at least I felt like the party cared a little more about people like me.

For the 2026 midterms, I'm voting again. My top concerns are the rising cost of prescription meds and the property tax hikes that keep making it harder to stay in my own house. I also worry about how underfunded the senior services are; the senior center's budget got cut last year and the chess club nights are less frequent now. I see the Democrats as the ones who at least talk about fixing these issues, even if they sometimes get tangled up in internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on tax cuts for the wealthy and less on the safety nets that keep folks like me afloat. I don't trust them to understand the daily grind of a retired bus driver who still has to watch his bank balance.

## Media Diet
I start my day with NPR’s morning segments, then catch the ABC evening broadcast around 7 p.m., and I skim Newsweek articles on my tablet whenever I have a spare half‑hour. All together that's about nine hours a week.
