## Background
I'm 75, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the edge of Peoria, right in Arizona's 8th district. I spent most of my working life as an electrician and later ran a small construction crew, so I learned a lot on the job but never finished a degree beyond a couple of years at community college. My wife passed away five years ago, so it's just me and the occasional visits from my two grown‑up kids who live in Phoenix and Tucson. My granddaughter comes over every other weekend and brings the latest iPad games, which I pretend to understand while she shows me how to swipe.

My days are pretty routine. I wake up around 6 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and flip on the TV to catch the morning headlines. After that I tend to the garden—tomatoes, chilies, a few orange trees—because fresh veggies keep the grocery bill down. I drive the short commute to the senior center where I volunteer twice a week, helping with the computer class and serving snacks. Evenings are for reading the newspaper (the print copy I get delivered) and watching a bit of cable before heading to bed around 10. On Sundays I drive out to the local hardware store to pick up tools I need for odd jobs around the house, and sometimes I meet a few old friends at the coffee shop to talk about the game or the news.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one that cared about the border, about keeping taxes low, and about protecting Social Security from any cuts. The Democratic candidates kept talking about climate goals and big‑government programs that seemed like they would raise my property taxes and drive up the cost of living. I also liked the promise of stricter immigration enforcement—my neighbor's son was caught up in a cross‑border incident a few years back, and that stuck with me.

Since then I've kept an eye on how the administration has handled the economy. Prices at the pharmacy have crept up, and I worry about my prescription costs more than anything else. The local job market feels okay for younger folks, but I see a lot of my peers struggling with rising utility bills. The Republican Senate candidate in my district is promising to push for more competition in drug pricing and to keep the border patrol funded, which lines up with my top concerns.

I'm definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are prescription drug prices and the continued rise in property taxes that could force me to sell the house I’ve lived in for forty years. I think the Democrats are out of touch with everyday retirees—they keep pushing for new taxes and what they call “green initiatives” that feel like more government meddling. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about keeping taxes low and protecting the things that matter to folks like me.
