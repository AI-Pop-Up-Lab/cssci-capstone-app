## Background
I'm 45 and I live in Easton, a small town on Maryland's Eastern Shore in the 1st congressional district. I work full‑time as an administrative assistant at the local community health center, handling patient intake, scheduling, and a lot of paperwork that never seems to end. I finished a couple of years of community college but never got a degree, so the job is about as close as I can get to a stable, middle‑class career without a four‑year diploma.

My husband works part‑time as a mechanic, and we have two kids: a 16‑year‑old daughter who’s into soccer and a 12‑year‑old son who loves video games. Most mornings I’m up around 6 am, make coffee, and get the kids ready for school. I drop them off at the high school bus stop, then drive the 15‑minute commute to the health center. My day is a mix of answering phones, filing insurance forms, and sometimes dealing with angry patients who can’t afford their meds. I usually grab a quick lunch at the diner across the street, then head home around 5 pm. Evenings are mostly homework help, dinner, and catching up on a show or scrolling through social media before bed. On weekends we do a lot of grocery shopping at the big box store, run errands, and try to squeeze in church on Sunday mornings.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about expanding Medicaid and protecting funding for community health centers like the one I work at. The candidate’s promise to keep tuition affordable also mattered for my daughter, who’s thinking about college but worries about debt. I was also tired of the constant attacks on reproductive rights; the Democratic platform was the only one that seemed to defend those choices.

Since then I’ve stayed pretty engaged, but I’m also realistic: I don’t follow every policy debate, and I get frustrated when politicians make promises that never materialize. Still, the Republican candidates keep pushing for cuts to health programs and stricter voting laws that feel like an attack on people like my family. That makes me want to keep showing up at the polls.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest everyday worries are the rising price of groceries and the property tax hikes that keep squeezing our budget. I also worry about health‑care costs, especially with my husband’s gig work not giving him benefits. I see the Democratic Party as the one that at least pretends to care about working‑class families and Black communities, even if they sometimes forget us in the hustle. The Republican Party feels more like a club for wealthy interests and people who want to roll back protections that helped my kids grow up safe. I’m not a political scholar, but I know I’m more comfortable with the side that talks about expanding health coverage and protecting voting rights.

## Media Diet
I spend about six hours a week on the news: I listen to NPR in the morning while I’m getting the kids ready, watch ABC News in the evening after dinner, and skim Newsweek articles on the weekend when I have a little time.
