## Background
I'm 64 and I live in a modest house on the edge of Staunton, right near the foothills of the Blue Ridge. I worked most of my adult life as a maintenance supervisor at the local paper mill; the job's been winding down since the plant cut a shift last year, so now I’m mostly doing odd‑job fixes for neighbors and a few part‑time gigs at a hardware store. I never finished my associate degree in industrial technology, but I picked up enough on the job to keep the machines running.

My wife, Ellen, is retired from the school district, and we have two grown kids who live in different states. We get together for Sunday dinner when they’re in town, but most weeks it’s just us watching the game or tending to the garden. I get up around 5:30 a.m., make a coffee, and scroll through the news on my tablet while the kettle boils. After a quick breakfast, I head out to the hardware store, work until about 3 p.m., then swing by the post office, pick up the mail, and sometimes stop at the grocery store before heading home. Evenings are for the TV, a couple of beers, and a call with my brother in North Carolina. I drive a 2008 pickup that’s seen better days, and I’m always looking for a good deal on fuel because the price spikes hit my budget hard.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because the candidate promised to keep taxes low and protect the coal jobs that still linger in the region. I’m not a political analyst; I just want the government to stay out of my paycheck and not tell me what to do with my land. The campaign ads that talked about “border security” and “energy independence” sounded like what I needed. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s policies affect my community. The inflation surge last year made groceries and gas feel like a constant squeeze, and I’ve seen a few small businesses close because of higher regulation. That makes me a little more skeptical about the Democrats, who I feel keep promising help but never deliver anything that reaches people like me.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are the rising cost of living—especially gas and property taxes that keep creeping up on my fixed income—and the safety of the local schools. I think the GOP still has the best shot at keeping taxes low and not over‑regulating small businesses, even if I don’t agree with everything they say. The Democrats seem more focused on big‑city issues and climate policies that feel distant from my life in the mountains. I’m not into the whole “politics as a moral crusade” thing; I just want someone who won’t raise my utility bills and who will keep the roads in decent shape.
