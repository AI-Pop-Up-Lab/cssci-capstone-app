## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was better for keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of small‑business environment that lets a place like my clinic stay afloat. I also liked the promise of less regulation on the energy sector—Wyoming’s economy still leans heavily on mining and oil, and I’ve seen friends lose jobs when the market gets hit by new rules. I didn’t feel the Democrats were listening to folks in a place where a single mine can be the whole town’s lifeline. That said, I wasn’t thrilled about the whole campaign circus; the ads were loud and the rhetoric got personal, but I figured a vote was better than nothing.

Going into the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely planning to vote again. My husband’s health insurance is up for renewal, and I’m worried about how any changes to Medicaid or prescription drug pricing could affect us. I also keep hearing about rising gasoline prices, which bite into my commute and the kids’ travel costs. Those two things—healthcare costs and fuel prices—are what keep me up at night. I’m not a big fan of the Democratic Party; they seem to push for big‑government solutions that I think will raise taxes and hurt the local economy. The Republicans have their flaws—sometimes they get too combative or ignore the concerns of people who aren’t in the oil business—but overall I think they’re more in line with what I need to keep my family stable. If a Republican candidate can show they care about rural health clinics and keep the pipeline projects moving, I’ll stick with them.
