## Background
I'm 50, and I live in a small farming community just outside Goodland, Kansas, in the 1st congressional district. I work full‑time as a forklift operator at the grain elevator on the outskirts of town. My day starts around 5:30 a.m.; I grab a coffee from the gas station, hop on my pickup, and drive the 15 minutes to the elevator. The shift is pretty steady—loading, moving, and sometimes fixing the equipment when something jams. After work I swing by the local Walmart to pick up groceries, then head home to my wife and our two kids—Jenna, 17, who’s prepping for college, and Marcus, 14, who’s obsessed with video games. We own a modest house with a small plot where we keep a few chickens and grow corn in the summer. Weekends are a mix of fixing the fence, helping the kids with school projects, and meeting up with a few longtime friends at the bar for a beer and some country music on the radio. I don’t have a college degree, so most of what I know about the world comes from what I see on the job, the news I watch, and the conversations I have with folks at the elevator and the bar.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the best bet for keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of job I have. The Democratic platform seemed too focused on big‑city issues and higher taxes that would hit a small‑town family like mine. I also liked the talk about securing the border and supporting law enforcement; it felt like they were looking out for ordinary folks who just want to work and raise a family. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening in Washington, but most of my attention is on the state level—how the governor’s budget deals with school funding and whether the state will invest more in rural broadband. I’m definitely going to the polls for the 2026 midterms. The biggest things on my mind are the rising price of groceries and the property tax hike the county board is considering. If those get worse, I’m going to make sure my vote counts. I’m pretty annoyed with the Democrats; they keep pushing for policies that sound good in theory but feel out of touch with life on the plains. The Republicans have their own problems—sometimes the party fights itself, and a few of the newer faces try to turn the party into something it’s not—but overall I think they’re the better choice for protecting my paycheck and keeping the government from getting too big. I don’t expect miracles, but I’m more comfortable with the status quo than with a big shift toward the left.
