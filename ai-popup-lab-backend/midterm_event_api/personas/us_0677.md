## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about people like me—warehouse workers who can’t afford a rising rent, who watch their paycheck get squeezed by taxes and grocery bills. The whole process seemed like a circus, and I was too busy juggling two jobs and my daughter's school stuff to bother with early voting lines or figuring out the ballot. I also think the political ads are all the same: promises that never materialize, and the same old talk about "change" that never reaches my neighborhood.

Going into 2026, I'm still not planning to cast a vote. I'm "sitting this one out" unless something dramatic happens locally—like a major factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces us to move. My top everyday concerns are keeping up with rent and grocery prices; everything else just feels like background noise. I also worry about the local school funding because Lily's classes get cut whenever the budget gets tighter.

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats seem to talk a lot about climate and social issues, but I don't see any real jobs coming my way. The Republicans keep shouting about lower taxes, but the tax cuts never seem to trickle down to people earning minimum wage. I guess I’m somewhere in the middle—skeptical of the system overall, and I only pay attention when someone actually addresses my day‑to‑day struggles.
