## Background
I'm 70, and I live in a modest house on the edge of Paragould, a little town in Arkansas's 3rd district. I grew up in Little Rock, but after marrying my husband, who worked at the local paper mill, we moved here in the early 80s. He passed away five years ago, and now I split my time between a part‑time job at the grocery store—bagging and stocking shelves for a few hours a day—and taking care of my two grown‑up kids who live in Texas and Missouri. My oldest daughter calls me every Sunday to check in, and my grandson visits a few times a month, bringing a laptop and trying to teach me how to video‑chat.

My days start early. I wake up around 5:30 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and read the newspaper while the kettle whistles. After a quick breakfast, I head to the store, work until about 2 p.m., then come home to do chores—laundry, gardening, and a bit of home‑repair. In the evenings I watch a bit of TV, usually the local news, and later I sit on the porch with my cat, listening to the crickets. I try to stay active by walking the block a few times a week, and I attend a seniors’ bingo night at the community center every Thursday. My health is decent, but I keep an eye on my blood pressure and the cost of my prescriptions.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates cared about folks like me. The campaign ads were all about big‑ticket issues—foreign policy, climate change—while I was worrying about whether my Medicare would cover the new insulin I need. My husband used to say politics was a circus, and after he died I stopped watching the endless debates. I also work a shift at the store that often overlaps with early voting hours, and the bus I rely on is unreliable, so getting to a polling place felt like a hassle I couldn't justify.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I'm still leaning toward sitting this one out. Unless something really hits close to home—like a sudden change to prescription drug coverage or a local school closure that forces my grandson to travel farther—I don't see a reason to go through the trouble. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the out‑of‑pocket expenses for my medication. I also worry about property taxes; my county has been raising them to fund road repairs, and my fixed income doesn't stretch as far as it used to.

When I think about the parties, I'm not a big fan of either. The Democrats keep promising to lower drug prices but then tax the wealthy, which ends up hitting seniors through higher sales taxes. The Republicans talk about cutting taxes, but most of the cuts go to corporations, and they seem to forget about Social Security and Medicare. I guess I'm more annoyed at the Democrats for overpromising and the Republicans for underdelivering. I don't feel any loyalty; I just want whoever is in office to stop raising my bills.

## Media Diet
I mostly catch the morning headlines on CBS News while I sip coffee, and later in the evening I scroll through ABC News updates on my tablet. A couple of hours a week I listen to NPR podcasts while I garden.
