## Background
I'm 40 and work as a production supervisor at a midsize appliance factory just outside Conway. I went to a state university and got a bachelor's in business, which landed me the floor‑manager gig after a few years on the line. My wife and I bought a modest ranch‑style house in a quiet cul‑de‑sac a few miles out of town; we have two kids, a 10‑year‑old who loves video games and a 7‑year‑old who’s always dragging me to soccer practice. My mornings start around 5:30 a.m.; I pour a cup of coffee, check the news on my phone while the kids are still in bed, then drive the kids to the elementary school across town. After dropping them off I hit the highway for a 30‑minute commute to the plant, where I spend my day walking the line, making sure the new models meet quality standards and handling a crew of about fifteen people. I usually eat lunch in the break room, scrolling through a mix of headlines while eating a sandwich I brought from home. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up the kids, help with homework, and we eat dinner together around 6:30 p.m. Evenings are a mix of watching a bit of TV, checking the scores, and sometimes catching up on a home‑repair project in the garage. Weekends are mostly family‑oriented: a quick trip to the lake, a barbecue with neighbors, or a trip to the local farmer’s market.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the best bet for keeping taxes low and protecting jobs at the factory where I work. The economic message about cutting regulations and supporting small manufacturers resonated with me, and I was also uneasy about the immigration rhetoric coming from the other side. I didn’t think the Democrats cared about the Midwest‑South belt where factories are the lifeblood of towns like mine. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration’s trade policies have affected my plant’s supply chain; there have been a few hiccups, but overall things have held steady, so I still lean right.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely voting. The Senate race is shaping up to be a real test of whether the current leadership can keep the economy on track, and I’m also watching the gubernatorial race because the governor’s stance on state taxes will hit my paycheck directly. My top everyday worries are the rising grocery bills and the property tax hike the county proposed last month. I’m not thrilled with either party, but I feel the GOP still gets the basics right—lower taxes, less government meddling. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for more social programs and higher taxes, which I think will hurt working‑class families like mine. I’m not a political scholar; I just want a stable job, affordable food, and a roof over my head.
