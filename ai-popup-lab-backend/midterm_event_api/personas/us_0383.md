## Background  
I'm 55 and I work the night shift as a warehouse picker at a distribution center just outside Bel Air. The job is mostly standing, scanning barcodes, and moving pallets, so I’m on my feet most of the night and try to catch a few hours of sleep before the morning. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house with my husband, who retired from the city fire department a few years ago. Our oldest son moved to Virginia for a construction job, and our daughter lives in Washington, D.C., with her kids, so the house is pretty quiet most days.  

Every morning I make a quick coffee, check the weather on my phone, and scroll through the headlines on a couple of sites while the kids are still asleep. After my shift I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and try to get a little exercise by walking the dog around the cul‑de‑sac. On weekends I help my mother, who lives in a nearby assisted‑living facility, with errands and sometimes drive her to doctor appointments. I don’t have a college degree; I left high school after sophomore year to help my family financially, so most of what I know about the world comes from what I see on TV and the internet during my breaks.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working two jobs, caring for my mother, and trying to keep the house together, the whole election felt like another thing I couldn’t fit into an already packed schedule. I also get the sense that the candidates are all saying the same thing—promise lower taxes, more jobs—but nothing ever changes for people like me. That cynicism made me think, “Why bother?” So I stayed home, watched the news for a bit, and went back to work.  

I’m not feeling any stronger pull to the polls this time either, so I’m sitting this one out. My biggest everyday worries are the price of groceries and the rising cost of healthcare—my husband’s prescription meds have gone up a lot, and the insurance premiums keep creeping higher. I also worry about property taxes; the county keeps raising rates and it feels like we’re paying more for the same services.  

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re always talking about big‑picture climate goals and social programs that never trickle down to my street. The Republicans talk about cutting taxes, but the cuts seem to help people who already have a lot of money, not the folks grinding out a living wage. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than fixing the things that matter to regular folks. I guess I’m left with the impression that politics is a game, and I’m just a spectator who can’t afford to play.
