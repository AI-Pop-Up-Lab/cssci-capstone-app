## Background
I'm 84 and I live in a modest bungalow on the outskirts of Hannibal, right along the Mississippi River. I spent most of my adult life as a professor of English at a small liberal arts college in St. Louis, so I retired with a pension and a love of reading that hasn't faded. My husband passed away ten years ago, and my two grown‑up children live in St. Louis and Springfield; my daughter visits us every other weekend and helps with groceries and the occasional yard work. I’m widowed, but I have a tight‑knit circle of friends from my church—First Baptist—where I still sing in the choir on Sundays.

My days are simple. I get up around 6 a.m., make a pot of coffee, and read the newspaper (the print copy of The Kansas City Star, though I mostly skim the headlines on my tablet). After breakfast I take a short walk along the river trail, especially when the weather’s nice, then I head to the senior center where I volunteer as a literacy tutor for a few hours. In the afternoons I’m either knitting, tending to my small vegetable garden, or watching reruns of classic sitcoms on the TV. Evenings are for a quick call with my son, a crossword puzzle, and sometimes a video call with my granddaughter who lives in Chicago. I drive a reliable old sedan that I keep in good shape because I still like to run errands myself.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more attuned to the social safety nets that keep people like me afloat—Medicare, Social Security, and the community health clinics that I rely on. I also liked the candidate’s emphasis on expanding broadband in rural Missouri, which would make it easier for my granddaughter to stay connected with us. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how the administration has handled the rising cost of prescription drugs; the price hikes have made me nervous, and I’ve heard from neighbors that some local pharmacies are cutting back on services.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the price of my prescription meds and the stability of my pension payouts, especially with talk of changes to federal retirement benefits. I’m also watching the local job market because a lot of the younger folks I talk to are worried about finding steady work; that indirectly affects my family’s ability to help out with expenses. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty sure the Democrats are the ones pushing for the health and social programs I need, even if they sometimes get tangled up in internal fights. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and rolling back regulations that protect workers, which feels like it leaves folks like me out in the cold.

## Media Diet
I usually catch up on NPR news during my morning coffee and flip through ABC News and CBS News headlines on my tablet after lunch; I also skim a few stories on Newsweek in the evening before bed.
