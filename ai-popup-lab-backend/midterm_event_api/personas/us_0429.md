## Background  
I'm 30 and work as a community outreach coordinator for the tribal health clinic in Saginaw. I grew up on the reservation near Grand Rapids, moved to the city after college to be closer to the hospital where I got my public health degree, and now share a two‑bedroom apartment with my partner and our 2‑year‑old daughter. My mornings start at 5:30 a.m. with a quick coffee while I scroll through NPR headlines on my phone, then I drive the kids to preschool and head to the clinic. My job has me hopping between the clinic, local schools, and occasional town‑hall meetings where we discuss vaccination drives or food‑bank partnerships. After a 9‑to‑5 shift, I usually pick up the kids, make dinner, and try to squeeze in a little homework help before we all crash around 10 p.m. On weekends I volunteer at the tribal cultural center, help with pow‑wow prep, and sometimes meet friends at the local brewery for a few drinks. I rent my place, so rent hikes are a constant worry, and I try to save for my daughter's college fund while keeping up with grocery bills that keep climbing.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between long shifts at the clinic, caring for my toddler, and feeling like the candidates were all the same—promising better health care but never delivering—I just gave up on the whole thing. The paperwork felt like another chore, and the whole process seemed like a circus that didn't care about people like me on the reservation or the working families in Saginaw. I keep hearing about corruption and broken promises, and it makes me think my vote doesn't really move the needle. For the 2026 midterms, I'm sitting this one out as well. Unless something dramatic happens locally—like a major cut to tribal health funding or a scandal that hits my community directly—I don't see a reason to go through the hassle again.

My biggest everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and whether my job at the clinic will stay funded next year. The price of fresh produce keeps going up, and it's hard to stretch a budget when you have a kid who needs healthy meals. At the same time, the clinic's grant money is always on shaky ground, and I worry about layoffs or reduced services that would hit the community hard.

When I think about the parties, I feel most annoyed with the Republicans. Their talks about cutting taxes and deregulation feel like a slap in the face when I'm trying to keep my family fed and my clinic running. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to care about health care in theory, but I get frustrated by their endless talking points and lack of concrete action for tribal communities. Both sides feel distant, but the GOP gets a bigger punch of my irritation.

## Media Diet  
I usually listen to NPR during my commute and skim ABC News headlines while I eat lunch. In the evenings, after the kids are in bed, I watch a quick CNBC recap to see how the markets are doing and if there are any big economic stories that might affect my paycheck.
