## Background
I'm 45 and I live in Yakima, right in the middle of Washington's 5th district. I work as a customer‑service supervisor at the regional grocery chain that’s the biggest employer in town. My shift starts at 8 a.m.; I spend the first half hour walking the parking lot, checking the refrigerated sections, and making sure the deli crew is ready. After my shift ends at 4 p.m. I pick up my two kids—12‑year‑old Maya and 9‑year‑old Luis—from the after‑school program and we head home. We have a small house with a backyard where we grow a few tomatoes and a pepper plant; my husband, Carlos, works evenings at the local warehouse, so dinner is usually a quick stir‑fry or the rotisserie chicken we buy on Fridays.

On weekends I try to keep the house clean, help the kids with homework, and run errands at the farmer’s market. I take Spanish lessons at the community college once a month to stay sharp, but I never finished the degree because the schedule kept clashing with work and family. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan that’s seen better days, and I’m always watching the gas gauge. When I can, I watch a soccer match on TV with the family or listen to the local radio while I’m on the road.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Green Party candidate for Congress. I was fed up with the two‑party circus and thought a third‑party voice might push the big parties to take climate and water‑rights issues more seriously. I didn’t spend a lot of time researching the ballot; I just read a quick summary on a progressive newsletter and went with the candidate who talked about protecting the Columbia River and lowering grocery prices. It felt like a small protest, and I was surprised when the candidate got a few hundred votes in the district.

Since then I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening locally. The water restrictions have tightened, and the price of avocados and other staples keeps creeping up. Those everyday pressures have made me more interested in who can actually get something done in the state legislature. I’m planning to vote in the 2026 midterms because the Senate race includes a candidate who promised to fund irrigation projects for farms like the ones my husband’s family owns. I’m still skeptical of both parties, though: the Democrats seem to talk a lot about climate but push policies that hurt small businesses, while the Republicans keep yelling about “tax cuts” that never reach the middle class. I feel most of the time like I’m stuck between a rock and a hard place, and I’m hoping a decent centrist could at least keep the conversation from devolving into shouting.

My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of groceries and the looming water shortage that could hit our farms hard. I’m not a fan of either party’s rhetoric; I just want someone who will actually listen to people like me who are trying to put food on the table while watching the river level drop.
