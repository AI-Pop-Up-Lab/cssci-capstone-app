## Background  
I'm 50 and I work as a general laborer for a construction crew that builds houses on the outskirts of Spanish Fork. I never finished high school; I left after sophomore year to help my dad with a farm job, and the money I made there kept me from going back. My wife and I have been married 22 years; we own a modest ranch‑style home with a small yard where my kids—Jenna, 17, and Tyler, 14—play video games and sometimes help me haul tools to the job site. Most mornings I’m up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and watch the local news on the TV while the kids get ready for school. I grab a quick breakfast of eggs and toast, then drive the 20‑minute commute to the construction site. The work is physically demanding, but it pays the bills and lets me stay close to home. After a typical 10‑hour shift, I come back, shower, and help the kids with homework when I can. Evenings are usually spent watching a bit of TV, scrolling through social media on my phone, or fixing things around the house. On weekends I do yard work, take the kids to their soccer practices, and sometimes help a neighbor with a fence. Money is tight enough that we keep a careful budget, but we’re not in crisis; we just try to stretch each paycheck.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate, even though most of my friends lean Republican. I did it because the candidate promised to keep Medicare costs down and to fund a new community health clinic that would serve the rural parts of our district. My dad had a heart condition and the rising premiums were a real worry, so I felt that the Democrat’s health plan was the most concrete reason to cast a ballot. I also liked the fact that the candidate talked about protecting public lands, which matters to us because we hike and hunt in the nearby canyons.  

Now, looking at the 2026 midterms, I'm definitely going to vote again. I’m not a big fan of the political theater, but the last few months have seen a push to cut funding for the very clinic that helped my dad, and the Republicans in the state legislature have been pushing a tax hike on property that would hit homeowners like me hard. My top everyday concerns are the rising grocery prices and the looming property tax increase. I’m also still worried about health care costs; a small change in my insurance could mean I have to skip a medication.  

When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with the Republicans. They keep talking about “small government” while cutting programs I actually need, and the rhetoric feels like it’s aimed at people who don’t live in places like ours. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem to promise a lot and then get tangled up in infighting, but at least they’re trying to protect the social safety nets that matter to families like mine. I don’t follow every policy detail; I just care about the stuff that hits my wallet and my family’s health.
