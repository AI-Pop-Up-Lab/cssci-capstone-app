## Background

I'm 50 and work as a long‑haul truck driver for a regional freight company that runs out of Shreveport. I live in a modest house in Bossier City, just a few miles north of the lake, with my wife and our two kids – a 15‑year‑old son who's in his sophomore year of high school and a 12‑year‑old daughter who just started middle school. Most mornings I’m up before dawn, coffee in a paper cup, and already loading the trailer with pallets of building supplies. The road is my office; I spend about 10‑12 hours a day behind the wheel, stopping at truck stops for a quick bite and a chance to stretch my legs. When I’m home, the evenings are a mix of helping the kids with homework, watching a game on the TV, and trying to keep the house in shape – the roof leaks a little every spring, and the furnace is older than I am. Weekends are the only time I can catch up on chores, mow the lawn, and run errands at the grocery store in downtown Shreveport. My wife works part‑time at the local community college cafeteria, and we both try to keep the budget tight because the cost of living has been creeping up.

## Politics and this election

I didn’t vote in 2024. Between long hauls, family obligations, and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about folks like me, the whole thing just seemed like a waste of time. The campaign ads were all the same—big promises, empty rhetoric—and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. The ballot felt like a chore, and I ended up leaving the polling place unopened. That same sense of cynicism is still with me, so I’m planning to sit this 2026 midterm out too, unless something big hits our community—like a major factory closing or a sudden spike in property taxes—that forces me to make a stand.

My biggest everyday worries are the price at the pump and the cost of my family’s health insurance. Gasoline has been climbing faster than my paycheck, and every time the insurance premiums go up, I have to cut back on other things. I’m also watching the local property tax bill; the city’s trying to raise more money for roads, and I’m not convinced they’ll actually fix the potholes that damage my rig’s tires.

When I think about the two parties, I’m mostly annoyed. The Democrats seem to want to raise taxes and push policies that feel out of touch with a working‑class family, while the Republicans talk a lot about “bringing back jobs” but rarely deliver anything concrete in my district. Both sides feel like they’re more interested in scoring points than solving real problems, so I end up feeling like I’m stuck between two bad options.
