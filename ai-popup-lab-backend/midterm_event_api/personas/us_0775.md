## Background

I'm 50, and I live in the Federal Hill neighborhood of Providence, right in the middle of Rhode Island's 1st District. I work full‑time as a project manager for a midsize software services firm that does a lot of contract work for local hospitals. My day usually starts around 6 am; I brew a coffee, check the news on my phone while the kids are still asleep, and then drive the 15‑minute commute to the office downtown. The job is a mix of spreadsheets, client calls, and trying to keep a handful of developers on schedule, so I spend a lot of my time juggling deadlines and making sure the budget doesn't blow out.

I'm married to my high‑school sweetheart, and we have two kids. Our older daughter is a senior at a community college nearby, and our younger son just started his sophomore year at a public high school. Weekends are a blur of carpooling, grocery runs, and trying to keep the house from looking like a construction site—my wife does most of the home‑improvement projects, but I help out with the yard and the occasional plumbing fix. We own our house, which means we’re always watching the property tax bill and the mortgage balance. In my spare time I like to watch the occasional baseball game on TV and take the dog for a walk around the neighborhood park.

## Politics and this election

In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party’s economic message resonated with my situation. The tax cuts they promised would help a middle‑class family like mine keep more of what we earn, and I was skeptical of the Democrats' push for higher spending on social programs that seemed to raise my property taxes. I also liked the candidate’s tough talk on immigration and felt that a stricter border policy would protect jobs in the tech sector where I work. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on how those promises have played out. The economy feels steady enough, but I’m annoyed by what I see as a lot of political theater from both sides—especially the constant bickering over climate legislation that never seems to translate into anything concrete for my community.

I’m voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are the rising cost of health insurance for my family and the property tax hikes that keep creeping up each year. I also worry about the local job market—if the tech contracts dry up, my whole household could feel the pinch. I view the Republican Party as the one that understands the need for lower taxes and less regulation, which keeps businesses like my employer afloat. The Democrats, in my mind, are more focused on identity politics and big‑government solutions that end up increasing the tax burden on homeowners. I’m not a political scholar; I just go with what seems to affect my paycheck and my mortgage the most.
