## Background  
I'm 25 and work as a sales associate at a big‑box home‑improvement store in Mishawaka, just outside South Bend. I took a couple of community‑college classes in business after high school, but never finished a degree because I needed to start supporting my family. My mom and younger brother live with me in a two‑bedroom apartment; we split rent and utilities, and I handle most of the grocery shopping. My day starts around 6 am: I grab a coffee from the corner deli, hop on the bus, and spend about an hour on the road before my shift begins at 8. After a twelve‑hour shift that includes stocking shelves, helping customers with DIY projects, and occasional inventory counts, I catch the last bus home around 9 pm. Most evenings I’m either scrolling on my phone, watching a short YouTube video, or helping my brother with his homework. Weekends are a mix of laundry, visiting my aunt who lives a few miles out in Granger, and sometimes hanging out at the local park with friends from work. I don’t own a car, so I rely on public transit and occasional rideshares. I’m not heavily involved in any clubs or volunteer groups; my free time is mostly spent catching up on sleep and trying to stretch my limited budget.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party cared more about the things that affect my daily life—like protecting Medicaid coverage and pushing for a higher minimum wage. I also liked the candidate’s stance on immigration reform, which matters to my family since my parents came here from Mexico. I didn’t vote for the Republican because their rhetoric on border security felt hostile, and I didn’t trust their promises on tax cuts to actually reach people like me. Since then, I’ve kept an eye on what’s happening locally, especially the debate over a new warehouse development that could bring jobs but also raise traffic and rent prices. I’m still planning to vote in the 2026 midterms; I think my voice matters when it comes to school funding and the upcoming property tax referendum that could affect our apartment’s cost. My biggest worries right now are keeping up with rent and making sure groceries stay affordable—prices have been creeping up and my paycheck hasn’t. I also worry about the local job market; the store where I work has been cutting hours during slow seasons, and I’ve heard talk of automation. As for the parties, I see Democrats as the ones trying to address social safety nets and climate initiatives, even if they’re not perfect. Republicans, on the whole, feel like they’re more focused on cutting taxes for the wealthy and loosening regulations, which I worry will make my cost of living rise even more. I’m not a political scholar, just someone trying to survive and maybe get a little ahead.
