## Background  
I'm 20 years old and I grew up on the Southern Ute reservation before moving to Grand Junction for school. I’m taking a couple of business classes at Mesa State College, but I’m not on a degree track yet—just picking up credits that might help me land a better job later. Most weeks I work the evening shift at a local hardware store; it’s decent money for a kid my age, and it lets me stay close to home. I live with my younger sister, Maya, and our mom in a modest two‑bedroom house a few blocks from the downtown strip. Mom works part‑time at the county health department, and Maya is still in high school, juggling soccer practice and a part‑time gig at the supermarket. My days usually start with a quick coffee from the corner diner, then a couple of hours of classes, a shift at the store, and evenings spent gaming or hanging out with friends at the skate park. On weekends I sometimes drive out to the nearby mesas for a hike or help out with tribal events when I can.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because the party’s stance on gun rights and low taxes felt like the only thing that actually mattered to me. Growing up, hunting was a big part of family life, and I don’t want the government to tell me I can’t own a rifle. The Democrats kept talking about climate policies that sound good on paper but don’t change the fact that my community still struggles with high unemployment and limited broadband. I also liked the Republican promise to cut back on federal regulations that they say hurt small businesses—something I see every day at the hardware store when the owner complains about red tape.

For the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely going to the polls. My top concerns are getting decent, well‑paying jobs after I finish school and making sure the state allocates more money to tribal infrastructure—things like better roads and reliable internet. The other big thing is the rising rent in Grand Junction; my mom’s rent went up by 12% last year and that’s a strain on our budget. I think the Republican Party still has the best shot at keeping taxes low and giving more freedom to folks who want to start their own businesses. The Democrats, in my view, are too focused on big‑city issues and seem to forget about places like ours. I’m not a fan of the “social justice” rhetoric that feels like it’s all talk and no real help for my tribe.
