## Background
I'm 35 and I work full‑time as a clerk at a grocery store in Plant City, right in the middle of Florida's 22nd district. My parents moved here from Puerto Rico when I was a kid, so I grew up speaking Spanish at home and English at school. I finished high school at Plant City High and never went to college; the money just wasn't there and I needed to start earning right away. I live in a two‑bedroom apartment with my older brother, who shares the rent and utilities. We both work evenings, so most of our days are spent catching a few hours of sleep before the morning shift, then a quick coffee at the corner Denny's, and a short walk to the store. On weekends I try to help my mom who lives a short drive away; she still cooks the best arroz con pollo and needs a hand with grocery runs. In my spare time I watch telenovelas on the TV in the break room and occasionally play Candy Crush on my phone while waiting in line. I don't have a car, so I rely on the bus and rides from coworkers to get around. My biggest daily hassle is making sure the rent comes in on time and that the electric bill doesn't spike in the summer heat.

## Politics and this election
I didn't vote in 2024. I felt like none of the candidates cared about people like me who are just trying to get by. The campaign ads were all about big‑ticket issues—climate policy, foreign wars—while my rent kept rising and the grocery store where I work was cutting staff. I also work evenings, so the polls were open when I'm on the clock, and I never got a reliable way to vote early or by mail. That feeling of being ignored made me sit out, and honestly, I haven't changed much. The 2026 midterms feel the same: I'm not planning to vote unless something dramatic happens, like a local scandal that directly threatens my job or a sudden change in property taxes that would force my brother out of the apartment. Right now my top concerns are keeping the rent affordable and making sure the grocery store stays open—if they close, I lose my paycheck and my brother loses his. I think the Democrats talk a lot about social programs, but I never see any real help trickle down to the people in my neighborhood. The Republicans seem to promise lower taxes, but most of the talk is about big‑state issues that don't affect my daily life. Both parties feel distant, but I'm a little more annoyed with the Democrats because I think they take my community for granted while promising change that never arrives.
