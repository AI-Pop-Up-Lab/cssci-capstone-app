## Background  
I'm 49, and I live in Twin Falls, right in the heart of Idaho's 2nd congressional district. I work as a maintenance technician at the regional hospital; the job mostly involves keeping the HVAC and electrical systems running, and I picked it up after getting an associate degree in industrial maintenance at the community college in Pocatello. My wife and I have been married for 22 years, and we have two kids—our daughter is a junior in high school and our son just started eighth grade. Most mornings I’m up by 5 a.m., grab a coffee, and drive the half‑hour to the hospital where my shift starts at 6 a.m. After work I swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and then help the kids with school projects before we all settle down for a quick TV show or a YouTube video. On Saturdays I take the kids to the local baseball field, and Sundays we usually head to our church service, then spend the afternoon barbecuing with neighbors. Money is tight enough that we keep a close eye on the utility bills and try to stretch every paycheck.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between the long commute, the double shift at the hospital, and the endless paperwork for my kids' school activities, I just never found the time to get to the polls, and on top of that I felt like the choices offered didn’t really represent what mattered to me—water rights for our farms, decent wages for hospital staff, and keeping property taxes from spiraling. The whole process felt like a circus, and I figured skipping it wouldn't change anything. I'm sitting this one out too. Unless something blows up locally—like a big water‑restriction proposal or a hospital budget cut that hits my department—I don't see a reason to make the trip.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising grocery prices and the property tax bill on our house. Every week I watch the grocery receipt climb, and the county keeps talking about raising the mill levy to fund road repairs. When I think about the parties, I’m pretty annoyed with both. The Democrats keep talking about climate policies that feel like they ignore the water scarcity we deal with here, and the Republicans brag about gun rights and oil while neglecting the fact that our irrigation systems need real investment. Neither side seems to get the mix of rural, working‑class concerns that matter to me.
