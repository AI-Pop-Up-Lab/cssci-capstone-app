## Background  
I'm 35, and I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the edge of Owensboro, right in the heart of Kentucky's 5th district. I work the noon‑to‑8 p.m. shift as a cashier at the local grocery store; the job's steady enough to cover rent and put a little extra toward my daughter's school supplies. My daughter, Maya, is nine and rides the bus to a public elementary school a few blocks away. Most mornings I drop her off, grab a coffee from the corner deli, and head in for a quick shift. After work I pick her up, help with homework, and we usually eat dinner while a sitcom plays on the TV. On weekends we swing by church, where I sit in the choir and help organize the food‑bank drive. I don't have a college degree, but I took a few night classes in basic computer skills that let me handle the register and keep track of inventory. My parents live in a nearby town; I visit them once a month, bringing a casserole and some fresh produce from the farmer's market. Money is tight, so I keep a careful budget and try to stretch every paycheck.

## Politics and this election  
I didn't vote in 2024. Between juggling two jobs, my daughter's school schedule, and dealing with a busted car that needed an expensive repair, I just couldn't find the time to get to the polls. I also felt like none of the candidates really spoke to people like me—my community's concerns about affordable housing and reliable public transportation seemed to get lost in the national chatter. The whole process felt like an endless loop of promises that never turned into anything concrete, so I decided to sit it out. As for 2026, I'm sitting this one out again unless something major happens locally, like a proposed school closure or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces us to move. My top everyday worries are keeping up with rent and making sure groceries stay affordable; when prices jump, it hits my family hard. I view the Democratic Party as well‑meaning but often disconnected from the real struggles of Black families in rural Kentucky—they talk about “justice” but rarely bring jobs or decent health care to our towns. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem more focused on tax cuts for big businesses and less on supporting public schools or expanding broadband, which feels like they're ignoring the needs of people like me. So for now, I'm just trying to get through each month without having to choose between two sides that both feel out of touch.
