## Background

My shift starts at 8 a.m., so I’m usually at the store by 7:30, grabbing a coffee from the diner across the street. After my 8‑hour shift I head home, drop off my two‑year‑old at the after‑school program, and try to squeeze in a quick dinner before we both crash. My partner works evenings at a local restaurant, so we trade off grocery runs and bill paying. On weekends I help my mom with yard work in nearby Richmond Hill and try to catch a baseball game at Truist Park when the schedule allows. I rent a two‑bedroom apartment a few blocks from the historic district; the rent has been creeping up, but it’s still cheaper than moving inland. I don’t have a college degree, but I took a few night classes in computer basics that helped me get the store’s inventory system job. I’m fluent in English and can get by in Spanish thanks to my mom’s side of the family. My free time is mostly scrolling TikTok on the couch or watching reruns of “The Great British Bake Off” when I can find a quiet moment.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working double shifts, caring for my kid, and feeling like none of the candidates really cared about people like me, I just shrugged and stayed home. The whole process feels like a circus—campaign ads yelling at me about issues I can’t afford to think about, long lines at the polls, and a sense that my single vote won’t change anything. I’m pretty sure I’ll sit this 2026 midterm out too, unless something huge hits my neighborhood—like a major factory closing or a sudden rent‑freeze ordinance that forces everyone to show up.

My biggest worries are the rising price of groceries and the fact that my rent keeps climbing faster than my paycheck. I also worry about the quality of childcare; the after‑school program is great but the cost is getting out of reach. When I think about the parties, I’m fed up with the Democrats for promising big programs but never delivering on the ground level, and the Republicans seem more interested in culture wars than fixing the paycheck‑to‑paycheck grind. Both sides feel like they’re talking past people like me, so I just tune out most of the political noise.
