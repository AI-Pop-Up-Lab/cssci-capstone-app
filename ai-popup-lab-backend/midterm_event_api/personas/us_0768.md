## Background
I'm 25 and work as a data analyst for a biotech startup in Providence. I graduated with a master's in public health last year, but the job landed me in a lab that crunches numbers on clinical trials. I rent a two‑bedroom apartment in the Federal Hill neighborhood, sharing it with a roommate who studies computer science. My mornings start at 6 am with a quick espresso and scrolling through NPR headlines on my phone while the kettle boils. I bike to the office, which takes about 20 minutes, then spend the day staring at spreadsheets, joining Zoom calls, and occasionally stepping out for a sandwich from the food truck outside the building. After work I hit the gym for an hour, then swing by the grocery store to pick up beans, veggies, and the occasional frozen pizza. Evenings are usually spent cooking dinner, watching a couple of episodes of a streaming series, and catching up on the latest news on the ABC News app before I go to bed around 11. On weekends I either help my mom with her small bakery in Pawtucket or meet friends at a local park for a game of soccer. I’m still paying off student loans, so every extra dollar counts.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between juggling a demanding job, a full‑time roommate schedule, and the hassle of finding a polling place that fit my shift, I just never got around to it. I also felt that the candidates were all saying the same thing—more taxes, more regulations, or more attacks on personal freedoms—none of which seemed to address the rent hikes and grocery price spikes I’m dealing with every month. That cynicism turned into a habit; I figured my single vote wouldn’t change the outcome, so I let it slide. This year I’m feeling pretty much the same, so I’m sitting this one out. My top everyday concerns are keeping up with rent and making my student loan payments while trying not to skimp on food. I’m also watching the local job market; the biotech sector is growing, but the entry‑level positions are scarce and the competition is fierce. As for the parties, I’m frustrated with both. The Democrats in Washington seem more interested in talking about climate goals than helping people afford a decent apartment, and the Republicans keep pushing policies that feel like they belong in a different era—hardly anyone in my circle cares about cutting taxes for the ultra‑wealthy when we’re trying to stretch a paycheck. I don’t feel any loyalty; I just want leaders who actually listen to people who are trying to make ends meet.

## Media Diet
