## Background
I'm 30 and I live in Paducah, right near the Ohio River in Kentucky's 4th District. I work full‑time at a regional distribution center loading and unloading trucks; the shifts are early mornings and sometimes the night shift when they need extra hands. I took a few classes at the community college in Paducah—mostly business and a bit of computer basics—but I never finished a degree because the job paid enough to keep the rent and help my mom with her meds. I share a two‑bedroom apartment with my younger brother; we split the rent, utilities, and groceries. On weekdays I’m up by 5 a.m., grab a coffee from the corner deli, and head to the warehouse. After my shift I either cook something quick or order takeout while we binge‑watch a sitcom on the couch. Weekends are a mix of grocery runs, checking in on my mom who lives a short drive away, and hanging out with friends at the local park or the church youth group I help coordinate. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan that’s seen better days, and I’m always on the lookout for a cheap oil change or a tire sale.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Between working double shifts, taking care of my brother when his class schedule conflicted with my work, and the feeling that none of the candidates really cared about people like me, I just didn’t bother to get to the polls. The whole process felt like a hassle, and I figured my single vote wouldn’t change anything. A lot of my friends felt the same; we joked that the election was just a “big TV show” and that the politicians were all the same. That cynicism hasn’t gone away. I’m sitting this one out again in 2026 unless something hits close to home—like a local school closing or a sudden spike in property taxes that forces us to move. My top everyday concerns are keeping the rent affordable and making sure grocery prices don’t keep climbing; a sudden price jump on milk or bread can throw the whole month off. I’m also worried about reliable transportation; when the depot cuts overtime, I worry about losing the hours that keep my car running.

When I think about the parties, I’m mostly annoyed with the Republicans because they keep pushing policies that feel like they’re aimed at rural, white voters and ignore the needs of Black communities in places like Paducah. The Democrats seem more vocal about social issues, but I’m skeptical about their promises on jobs and economic relief—they talk a good game but the local factories keep shutting down. I don’t have a strong loyalty to either side; I just want someone who will actually listen to people who work the night shift and can’t afford a sudden rent hike. If a candidate shows up at the community center, talks about real jobs, and backs it up with a plan that includes affordable childcare, I might consider stepping into the booth.
