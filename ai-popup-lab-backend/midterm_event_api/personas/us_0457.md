## Background
I'm 49, and I live in a modest duplex on the west side of Duluth, right near the lakefront. I work full‑time as a research analyst for the University of Minnesota Duluth's Institute on Rural Development, where I spend most of my days crunching data on regional employment trends and writing grant proposals. I have a master's in public policy and a PhD in economics, so I’m comfortable with spreadsheets and academic jargon, but most evenings I’m just trying to get dinner on the table. My wife is a high‑school Spanish teacher, and we have two kids: a 16‑year‑old son who’s into skateboarding and a 13‑year‑old daughter who plays the violin. Weekdays we all get up around 6 am; I bike to campus, she drives to school, and the kids catch the bus. After work I usually stop by the grocery store, then we eat together while the kids finish homework. Weekends are a mix of family hikes up the bluffs, watching the Vikings on TV, and me tinkering with a home‑brew coffee roaster. I also volunteer once a month at a local community center that offers after‑school tutoring for kids in the neighborhood. Money is tight enough that we keep a close eye on the budget, but we’re not struggling to put a roof over our heads.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t cast a ballot in 2024. Between long work hours, the kids’ extracurricular schedules, and a feeling that the whole system was just a game of spin doctors, I ended up staying home on Election Day. I’ve never been the type to line up at a precinct; the whole process feels like a distant theatre where the script never changes. The candidates all sounded the same to me—talking about “change” while the same big‑corporate interests keep pulling the strings. I’m not a hardcore cynic, but after watching the same promises get broken year after year, I just tune out. That’s why I’m sitting this one out too, unless something hits close to home—like a sudden school funding cut that forces my daughter’s music program to shut down, or a local scandal that directly affects my job.

My biggest everyday worries are the rising grocery prices and the ever‑increasing property taxes on our duplex. Every paycheck feels a little thinner, and the tax bill that comes every spring makes me wonder if the county is really using that money for anything useful. When I think about the parties, the Democrats get my nod on social‑justice issues, but I’m skeptical that they’ll ever move the needle on affordable housing or healthcare in our area. The Republicans, on the other hand, keep shouting about tax cuts and “law and order,” but that rhetoric feels disconnected from the everyday grind of a middle‑class family in Duluth. Both sides seem more interested in winning the next headline than solving the problems that keep me up at night.

## Media Diet
