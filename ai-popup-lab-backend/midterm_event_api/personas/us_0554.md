## Background
I'm 30, and I live in a modest rental on the east side of Fargo, right near the Red River. I got an associate’s degree in automotive technology from a community college in Grand Forks and now work as a service technician at a local dealership. My shift starts at 7 a.m., so I’m usually up by 5:30, grab a coffee from the gas station down the street, and drive the 15‑minute commute while the radio plays classic rock. After work I head home, maybe swing by the grocery store for some beans and tortillas, then help my roommate, who’s studying nursing, with his laundry. On weekends I try to catch a game at the local bar or help my mom with paperwork over FaceTime; she lives in a small town near Bismarck and depends on me for a lot of tech help. I don’t own a car beyond the one I use for work, so I rely on the dealership’s lot for a ride home. My budget’s tight—rent, utilities, and the occasional repair bill keep me watching my spending, but I enjoy the simple routine of fixing cars and watching the river flow when I have a free evening.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I didn’t vote for a Democrat or a Republican; I cast my ballot for the Libertarian candidate because I was fed up with the two‑party circus. I felt neither side cared about the everyday grind of folks like me—high insurance premiums, the cost of tools, and the lack of decent apprenticeship programs. I thought a third‑party voice might push the big parties to listen. The experience left me a bit cynical; I didn’t see much change afterward, and the next election felt like the same old options. Still, I’m planning to vote in the 2026 midterms because the Senate race has a candidate who talks about expanding vocational training, which hits close to home. I’m not thrilled about either major party, but I’ll pick the one who seems more likely to keep my job stable and keep gas prices from soaring. My top everyday worries are making ends meet—rent and grocery prices—and keeping my health insurance affordable, especially with a family history of diabetes on my mother’s side. I view the Democrats as talkers who promise big programs but forget the small‑town grind, and the Republicans as all‑talk on taxes but indifferent to the cost of living for people on modest wages. Both sides get my irritation, but I’m leaning toward the GOP this time because they’ve at least pledged to cut some business regulations that affect repair shops.
