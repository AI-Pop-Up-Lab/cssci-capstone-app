## Background
I'm 35 and I live in a modest rental house on the edge of the Huntsville‑Madison County area, just off a two‑lane road that leads to the industrial park. I work full‑time as a forklift operator at a warehouse that ships parts for the aerospace supply chain. My shift starts at 6 a.m., so I’m usually up by 5, grabbing a quick coffee from the corner truck stop and scrolling through my phone while I wait for the truck to pull up. After the eight‑hour shift I go home, try to catch a quick nap, and then spend the evening cooking cheap meals—mostly beans, rice, and whatever is on sale at the grocery store. My mom lives a few miles away and I help her with a few chores when I can, and I have a teenage niece who stays with us on weekends while her parents work long hours in the city. I don’t have a driver’s license; I rely on the company shuttle and the occasional rideshare to get around. My weekends are a mix of watching the games on the TV in the living room, helping my cousin fix his old Chevy, and running errands for the family. I’m not in school, I don’t have any plans for higher education, and I’m just trying to keep a roof over my head and enough food on the table.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like none of the candidates really cared about people like me. The whole thing just seemed like a big circus—ads on the radio, endless rallies, and a lot of talk about “changing the system” while the price of gas and the rent keep creeping up. I work two jobs sometimes when the warehouse picks up extra shifts, so I barely have the energy to think about anything beyond getting home before dark. I also thought my vote wouldn’t matter; the district is so solidly red that the outcome feels predetermined, and I’m not convinced any Democrat would listen to a Hispanic kid who never finished high school.

I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something hits close to home—like a factory closure that puts my coworkers out of work, or a sudden spike in property taxes that makes my rent unaffordable—I’m not going to the polls. My biggest worries are paying the rent next month and keeping food on the table. The grocery bills have gone up a lot, and the minimum wage here still doesn’t stretch far enough. I also worry about the local job market—if the aerospace contracts slow down, I could lose my shift.

As for the parties, I’m pretty fed up with both. The Republicans keep talking about “low taxes” and “pro‑business,” but the tax breaks never seem to trickle down to people in the warehouse. The Democrats keep promising better wages and immigration reform, but I never see any of that in my neighborhood. I feel like they’re both playing the same game, just with different slogans. I’ve got a lot of resentment toward the system, and I mostly tune out the political noise unless it directly hits my paycheck or my mom’s health.
