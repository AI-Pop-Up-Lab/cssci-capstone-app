## Background
I'm 25 and I work full‑time as a cashier at a grocery store in Henderson, Nevada, just outside the Strip. I grew up in a small apartment with my mom and my 19‑year‑old brother; we all share the rent and split groceries. After I dropped out of high school at 16, I took a series of entry‑level jobs—dishwashing, a short stint at a call center, then the grocery gig that’s been steady for the past three years. My days start early; I’m usually up by 5 a.m. to catch the bus, work a 9‑to‑5 shift, and then head home to do laundry, help my brother with his college applications, and sometimes take a night class at a community college just to keep my mind busy. On weekends I help my mom with her small salon on the side, and we try to squeeze in a family dinner at a taquería where the staff know us by name. I watch a lot of TV in the evenings, mostly just the news clips that pop up on my phone while I’m scrolling between breaks. I don’t have a car, so I rely on ride‑shares when I need to go farther, and I’m always looking for ways to stretch the next paycheck.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024 because I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything, and honestly I was exhausted from working double shifts to make ends meet. The whole campaign felt like a circus—big promises, a lot of yelling, and none of the stuff that mattered to me day‑to‑day. I was also dealing with a family health issue that took up most of my spare time, so getting to a polling place seemed like another hassle I couldn’t fit in. I’m not planning to go to the polls this November either; unless something dramatic hits the neighborhood—like a sudden rent hike or a local scandal that directly affects my job—I’m just going to sit this one out. My biggest worries are keeping up with rent and making sure groceries stay affordable; everything else feels like a distant political debate. When I think about the parties, the Democrats talk a lot about immigration and social programs, but I don’t see any real help for low‑wage workers like me. The Republicans keep pushing tax cuts and “law and order,” but those messages never translate into a higher paycheck or cheaper rent for people in my situation. Both sides seem more interested in scoring points than solving the concrete problems I face every month.
