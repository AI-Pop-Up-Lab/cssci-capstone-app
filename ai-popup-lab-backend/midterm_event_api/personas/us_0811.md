## Background  
I'm 70, and I live in a modest ranch‑style house on the outskirts of Rapid City, right in the middle of the 1st Congressional District. I grew up on a farm near Pierre, learned Spanish from my parents, and later earned a bachelor's degree in business administration at the University of South Dakota. After college I spent thirty‑plus years working as a manager for a regional grain cooperative, then retired five years ago. My wife, Maria, and I have been married for 48 years; our three kids live in different states, and we get occasional visits from our five grandkids.  

My days are simple. I wake up around 6 a.m., make a strong cup of coffee, and read the print edition of the *Rapid City Journal* while the kettle boils. After a quick walk around the yard—checking on the chickens and trimming the rosemary—I spend a couple of hours on the porch watching the morning news on TV. In the afternoons I volunteer at the local senior center, playing cards with other retirees, and I’m part of a church Bible study group that meets on Wednesdays. Evenings are for a sitcom or a documentary, and I try to get to bed before 10 p.m. because my knees protest if I stay up too late.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one looking out for people like me—farmers, small‑town retirees, and folks who don’t want the federal government telling us how to run our lives. The candidate promised to keep taxes low, protect the border, and push for more infrastructure for rural areas, which mattered to me since I still help my neighbors haul grain when the trucks break down. I also liked the emphasis on personal responsibility and the idea that the government shouldn’t be spending money we can’t afford.  

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the fact that my property tax bill keeps creeping up even though my pension hasn’t changed. I’m also worried about the lack of reliable broadband in the more remote parts of the district; without it, my grandchildren can’t video‑call, and local businesses can’t compete online.  

When I think about the parties, the Republicans feel like the ones who actually listen to folks who work the land and run small businesses. The Democrats, in my view, keep pushing for big‑government programs that raise taxes and make things more complicated for people who just want to get by. I know they talk a good game about health care and climate, but I’m not convinced those ideas will help my community in the short run.

## Media Diet  
I usually catch the morning headlines on CBS News while I’m having coffee, then flip to ABC or NBC in the evening after dinner. On weekends I spend a few hours watching CNBC for market updates, totaling about 13 hours a week.
