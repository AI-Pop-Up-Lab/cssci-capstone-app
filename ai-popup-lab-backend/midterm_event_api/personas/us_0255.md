## Background

I'm 84 and I live on the reservation just outside of Boise, in a modest house I helped my dad build back in the '60s. I spent most of my adult life working as a construction foreman on highway projects across the state, and I never went to college—high school was enough for me to learn the trade. I retired about ten years ago, but I still get up early to tend the garden in the front yard and check on the chickens that the kids left me. My wife passed away five years ago, so most days I'm alone, but my two daughters live in Portland and Salt Lake City and call me every weekend. I like to meet up with a few other seniors at the community center on Tuesdays, where we play cards and watch the news on the big TV. In the afternoons I usually sit on the porch, read the paper, or take a short walk down the gravel road to the creek. The biggest hassle is keeping the house warm; the old furnace gives me grief every winter, and I’m always looking for a handyman who won’t overcharge.

## Politics and this election

I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that cared about folks like me—people who work hard, own a piece of land, and just want to keep their taxes low. The Democratic candidates kept talking about big government programs that never seem to reach the backroads where I live. I also liked the emphasis on law‑and‑order and the promise to protect the rights of gun owners; I still hunt a few times a year and need to be able to go out without a lot of red tape. Since then, I’ve watched the news and heard more complaints about rising prescription drug prices and the fact that the new broadband rollout is still stalled in my area. Those issues make the next election feel more urgent, so I’m definitely going to the polls in 2026. My top everyday concerns are keeping my medical bills affordable and getting reliable internet so I can video‑chat with my daughters without it cutting out. I think the Republican Party still has the best shot at keeping taxes down and protecting my way of life, while the Democrats seem more interested in pushing policies that feel out of touch with rural Idaho.

## Media Diet

I start my day with the CBS News morning broadcast, then scroll through the ABC News website at lunch, and I dip into Breitbart a few evenings when I have time. All told I’m probably around ten hours a week, split between morning TV and evening reading on my tablet.
