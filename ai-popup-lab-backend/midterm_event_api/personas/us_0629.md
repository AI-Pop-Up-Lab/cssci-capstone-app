## Background  
I’m 60 years old and work as a dental hygienist at a small clinic in Farmington, right in the heart of New Mexico’s 2nd congressional district. I went to a community college in Santa Fe and earned an associate’s degree in dental hygiene, so most of my training was hands‑on rather than academic. My husband, Carlos, retired from the railroad a few years ago, and we still live in the same ranch‑style house we bought when our kids were in high school. Our daughter, Maya, is out of state now, working as a graphic designer, and we get her on FaceTime once a week. My mother, who’s 84, lives with us; I help her with her meds and take her to the senior center on Tuesdays. My days start early— I’m usually at the clinic by 7 a.m., finish around 3 p.m., then I’m back home cooking, doing laundry, and sometimes helping a neighbor with her garden. On weekends I volunteer at the local food pantry and try to keep up with the occasional community meeting about water rights, which is a big deal out here.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt the choices were a rinse‑and‑repeat of the same promises, and the ballot didn’t give me a clear reason to go to the polls. I also missed the deadline because I was juggling Mom’s doctor appointments and a sudden plumbing leak at home. This time around I’m planning to vote. The midterms will decide who sits on the state water‑management board, and I’m tired of seeing water restrictions hit our farms and the Navajo Nation. I’m also worried about my property taxes going up again; the county has been looking at raising rates to fund road repairs, and my husband and I are on a fixed retirement income. Those two issues—water security and property taxes—are what keep me up at night. I see the Democratic Party as pushing too much “green” regulation that hurts small ranchers, while the Republicans seem to talk a lot about oil and gas without caring about the drought that’s already drying up our wells. Both parties feel distant, but I’m hoping at least one candidate will actually listen to the water‑conservation plan I helped draft for the community council.

## Media Diet  
I usually flip on Fox News while I’m making coffee in the morning, catching the headlines in about 30 minutes. On my drive to the clinic I listen to NPR’s “Morning Edition,” which fills the commute with a mix of national and local stories. In the evenings I sit at the kitchen table and scroll through Reuters on my tablet for a quick, factual wrap‑up before bed. All together that’s roughly four hours a week.

## Opinion Formation  
Recall: I’m a Recaller. When the state legislature debated a new water‑allocation bill last year, I remembered the 2012 town hall where the same issue came up and the arguments that had swayed me then. I stuck to those values and told my neighbor at the pantry that I’d vote for the candidate who promised to keep the old irrigation districts intact, just like I’d promised myself back then.
