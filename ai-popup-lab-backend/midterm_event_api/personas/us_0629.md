## Background
I'm 60, and I live in a modest ranch-style house on the edge of Rio Rancho, right in the middle of NM‑2. I work full‑time as a medical assistant at the community health clinic down the road, a job I got after finishing an associate’s program at the local community college. The work is routine—checking vitals, prepping exam rooms, and handling the paperwork for patients who often can’t afford much else. My husband retired from the railroad a few years ago, so most mornings we share a quick coffee on the porch while the sky is still pink. I drive a ten‑year‑old sedan to work, then swing by the grocery store on my way home to pick up fresh produce and a few cans of beans. 

We have two grown‑up kids; the daughter lives in Denver and the son is in Phoenix, so the house feels pretty quiet most evenings. Once a week I help my elderly mother with a few errands; she still lives in a small apartment in Albuquerque, and I’m the only one who can drive her to her doctor’s appointments. On weekends I tend to the garden—tomatoes, chilies, a few peppers—and I’m part of a senior knitting circle that meets at the local library. I’m not a big tech person, but I do own a tablet that I use mostly for checking the news and video calls with family.

## Politics and this election
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like the choices were the same old faces promising the same old things, and I was too tired after a long shift at the clinic to bother with a ballot that, in my mind, wouldn’t change my rent or the price of my insulin. The political ads just blend together, and I’ve been skeptical of both parties for years—Democrats keep talking about climate and social programs while the taxes keep creeping up, and Republicans are all about the border and “law and order” but ignore the fact that my clinic’s funding is always on the chopping block. I also had a family emergency in August that kept me from even getting to the polling place, so I just let it slide.

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I’ve decided I’m sitting this one out too. Unless something dramatic hits my community—like a sudden hospital closure or a major scandal that directly affects my paycheck—I don’t see the point in spending the time to cast a vote. My top everyday worries are keeping my healthcare costs manageable and dealing with the property tax hikes that have made it harder to keep the house in shape. I’m not a fan of either party; they both seem to forget the people who are just trying to get by, and I’m more annoyed by the endless partisan bickering than anything else.
