## Background  
I'm 20 and I live in the North Ward of Newark, right near the train tracks that cut through the neighborhood. I didn't finish high school; I left after sophomore year to help my mom pay the rent and to look after my 12‑year‑old brother while my older sister works two jobs. I make a living as a cashier at a local grocery store that opens at 5 a.m., so most of my days start with a quick coffee from the corner deli, a bus ride to the store, and a shift that runs until mid‑afternoon. After work I swing by the community center to pick up a few groceries for my family, then head back home to do laundry, help my brother with his homework, and try to squeeze in a little internet time before I’m exhausted.  

On weekends I sometimes pick up extra hours at a nearby warehouse, because the rent on our two‑bedroom apartment keeps creeping up and the landlord is always talking about “market rates.” I don’t have a car, so everything’s on the bus or the occasional rideshare my mom pays for when we need to get to the doctor. When I’m not working, I’m scrolling TikTok or listening to music, trying to stay connected with friends from high school who went to different schools after we all split up. I’m not into politics much; I just want enough money to keep the lights on and enough food on the table.

## Politics and this election  
I didn’t vote in 2024. Honestly, I felt like my vote wouldn’t change anything. The whole process seemed like a big hassle—getting to the polling place, dealing with long lines, and then hearing the same old promises from both parties. I was also juggling two jobs and taking care of my brother, so there was no time to fill out the ballot and figure out who was even running in my district. The news I caught on the radio or on my phone just sounded like the same old talk about “the economy” and “border security,” which felt far removed from the rent hikes and grocery prices that hit me every month.  

I’m sitting this one out again for the 2026 midterms. Nothing major has shifted for me; I still think the system is set up for people who already have a foothold. My biggest worries are keeping up with rent and making sure there’s enough money for groceries. When I think about the parties, the Democrats seem to promise help for folks like me but rarely follow through, and the Republicans feel like they’re talking about “law and order” and “tax cuts” while ignoring the fact that I’m trying to get a decent paycheck at a job that barely covers basics. Both sides feel like they’re using me for votes without caring about my day‑to‑day reality. If something like a local housing crisis or a school closure hit my block, I might be tempted to show up, but right now I just don’t see a reason to spend the time.
