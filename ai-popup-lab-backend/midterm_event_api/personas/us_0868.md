## Background  
I'm 75, and I live in Logan, Utah, right near the foothills of the Wasatch. I spent 35 years teaching third grade at the local elementary school before I retired five years ago. My husband died ten years back, so it's just me, my two grown‑up kids who live in Salt Lake City and Boise, and the occasional weekend visit from my three grandkids. I still drive my old Subaru to the grocery store on Tuesdays and Thursdays, but most of my day revolves around the house.  

Mornings start with a cup of black coffee, the paper (I still like the printed *Deseret News*), and a quick scan of my phone for headlines. After breakfast I tend my garden—tomatoes, lavender, a few rosemary bushes—then I check my email and sometimes answer a call from the school where my daughter teaches. Afternoons are for the senior center: bingo, a knitting circle, or helping the librarian sort donations. On Sundays I go to the United Methodist service, then I’m usually back home by early afternoon, watching whatever is on the news while I bake a pie for the rest of the week. I keep a modest budget; my Social Security check and a small pension cover most expenses, but I watch my utility bills closely.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really looking out for folks my age. Medicare‑Advantage plans were expanding, there were promises to protect Social Security from any future cuts, and the candidate talked about climate policies that could keep the air clean around the mountains we love. I also liked the emphasis on funding for community colleges and public schools—something that hits close to home after three decades in the classroom.  

Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out. I’m still convinced the Democrats are the better bet for seniors, even if I’m frustrated when the rhetoric doesn’t match the rollout. The Republicans keep pushing for tax cuts that look good on paper but feel like they leave the middle class and retirees paying more for health care and property taxes. I’m not a political scholar, just a grandmother who wants her medicine to stay affordable and her property tax bill to stay manageable.  

I'm voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday concerns are prescription drug prices and the rising cost of property taxes on my modest home. I think the Democratic Party, despite its flaws, is more likely to protect Medicare and push for drug‑price negotiations. The GOP, in my view, is more focused on deregulation and cutting taxes for businesses, which doesn’t help me or my neighbors who are on fixed incomes.

## Media Diet  
I tune into NPR in the mornings while I’m having coffee, then catch CBS News and ABC News in the evenings after dinner, totaling about ten hours a week.
