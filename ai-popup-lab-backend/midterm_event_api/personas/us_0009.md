## Background  
I'm 35 and work as a dental hygienist at the Fairbanks Community Health Clinic. I got my associate degree in dental hygiene at Alaska Technical College, then stayed in Fairbanks because my mom moved here to be closer to her sister. I live in a modest two‑bedroom house on the edge of the College neighborhood with my husband, Jamal, who drives the city’s garbage trucks, and our 4‑year‑old daughter, Maya. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m. to get Maya ready for preschool, make coffee, and catch the bus to the clinic. My shift starts at 8 a.m., and after work I usually swing by the grocery store, help Maya with her homework, and try to squeeze in a quick walk around the Chena River trail before bedtime. On weekends I like to fish at Chena Lake with Jamal, and we try to visit the local farmers market when the weather’s good. I’m a member of the local church choir, which meets Thursday evenings, and I volunteer once a month at the community center teaching basic oral health to kids.

## Politics and this election  
I voted for the Democratic ticket in 2024 because I felt the party’s health‑care platform mattered most to me. As a dental hygienist I see every day how costly prescriptions and lack of insurance hurt people, especially in rural Alaska where clinics are far apart. The Democratic candidate promised to protect Medicaid expansions and fund tele‑health services, which would make a real difference for my patients. I’m also a Black woman, and the party’s stance on criminal‑justice reform and voting‑rights protections resonated with my own concerns about equity.

For the 2026 midterms I’m definitely going to the polls – “I’m voting.” My top concerns right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and reliable broadband access for remote work and education. The drug prices keep climbing, and even though I see patients for free clinics, many can’t afford their meds. Broadband is a huge issue in AK‑1; without good internet my daughter can’t do her school projects, and my husband can’t take advantage of online training for better routes. I’m pretty annoyed with the Republicans; their push to cut funding for Medicaid and their skepticism about expanding broadband feels like they’re ignoring the day‑to‑day struggles of folks like us. The Democrats, on the other hand, seem more willing to invest in social programs, even if sometimes their messaging feels a bit out of touch with rural realities. I’m not a political theorist, I just want policies that keep my family healthy and connected.

## Media Diet  
I get most of my news from NPR’s morning podcasts while I’m driving to work, and I skim ABC News and CBS News headlines on my phone in the evenings after Maya’s bedtime. I usually spend about six hours a week across those three sites, split between a quick scroll in the morning and a longer sit‑down on the weekend.
