## Background
I'm 35 and I work as a dental hygienist at a community health clinic in Fairbanks. I got my associate degree at a local technical college, which is how I landed the job after a couple of years as a retail associate. My husband is a mechanic at the same clinic's garage, and we have a nine‑year‑old daughter who rides the school bus to the middle school downtown. Most mornings I wake up around 5:30 a.m., make coffee, and get the kids ready while the house is still quiet. I drive the 15‑minute stretch to the clinic, where I spend my day cleaning and assisting the dentist, then I head home for dinner around 5 p.m. Evenings are a mix of helping with homework, watching a bit of TV, and trying to squeeze in a jog on the trail near the river when the weather lets up. Weekends are mostly grocery trips, church on Sunday, and occasional visits to my parents who live in a small village up the road. I don’t own a car beyond the family sedan, so most errands are done on foot or by the local bus.

## Politics and this election
I voted for the Democratic candidate in 2024 because I felt the party was more likely to protect funding for the community health clinic where I work and to keep tuition affordable at the technical college I attended. I also liked the candidate’s stance on expanding broadband in rural Alaska, which would make tele‑health more reliable for my patients. Since then I’ve watched the price of electricity creep up and the cost of prescription meds stay high, so those issues are front‑of‑mind for me. I’m definitely going to vote in the 2026 midterms; I can’t afford to sit out when my daughter’s school is fighting for better heating and the clinic is lobbying for more staff. My top everyday concerns are the rising utility bills that make it hard to keep the house warm during long winters, and the availability of affordable health care for my family and my patients. I think the Democrats are generally more attentive to those problems, though sometimes they seem slow to act on the ground. The Republicans, on the other hand, feel like they’re more interested in oil and mining deals that don’t translate into better services for ordinary folks like us, and I’m skeptical about their promises on social programs.
