## Background
I'm 45 and I work as a front‑desk supervisor at a mid‑size hotel in the Aiea‑Pearl City area of Oahu. I grew up in a bilingual household, so I speak both English and Spanish at home. My husband is a subcontracted electrician, and we have two kids: Maya, 16, who drives a beat‑up Corolla to school, and Luis, 9, who still asks for extra toppings on his pizza. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m. to make coffee, check the kids’ schedules, and catch a quick walk to the nearby grocery before the hotel shift starts at 7. After work I usually swing by the kids’ school pick‑up line, then we all eat dinner together—mostly rice, beans, and whatever the kids want from the food court. On weekends we hit the beach or the local farmer’s market, and sometimes I volunteer at the community center teaching basic computer skills to older adults. The cost of rent keeps creeping up, so we’re always looking at options to stay within our budget, but we love staying close to the ocean and the family.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted for the Democratic candidate because I felt the party was the only one really talking about rising housing costs and protecting our fragile marine environment. I also appreciated the stance on immigration reform, which matters to my family and a lot of friends who are recent arrivals. The campaign ads about expanding broadband to rural parts of the islands struck a chord, since my brother lives on the Big Island and still struggles with spotty internet. Since then I’ve kept an eye on how those promises are playing out. I’m a little disappointed that the housing crisis hasn’t eased; prices are still out of reach for many locals, and the tourism boom keeps pushing everything higher. Still, I think the Democrats are more likely to push for rent control measures and stronger environmental regulations, even if the progress is slow.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top everyday worries are the ever‑rising cost of groceries and the uncertainty of the tourism‑driven job market—my husband’s work gets hit hard when cruise ships cancel. I also worry about property taxes; the county keeps raising assessments, and my mortgage feels like it’s climbing a hill I can’t get off of. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they at least listen to island issues, even if they’re sometimes tangled in national politics. The Republicans, on the other hand, seem to ignore the unique challenges we face here—like sea‑level rise and the need for sustainable tourism. I’m not a fan of the rhetoric that paints us as “tax‑burdened,” because the reality is we’re already paying a lot just to stay afloat.
