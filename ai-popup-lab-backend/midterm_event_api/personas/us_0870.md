## Background
I'm 49, and I live in a cul‑de‑sac in St. George, right on the edge of the red rocks that frame the valley. I work full‑time as a dental hygienist at a downtown clinic; the associate degree I got at a community college lets me handle the routine cleanings and patient education, and I enjoy the steady rhythm of the job. My husband runs a small landscaping business, and we have two kids – a 16‑year‑old sophomore and an 11‑year‑old who still rides the school bus. Most mornings I’m up by 5:30, making coffee, packing lunches, and getting the younger one ready for school. After a quick drive, I’m at the clinic by 7:30, working until about 3:30, then I swing by the kids’ after‑school soccer practice or help them with homework. Evenings are usually a mix of dinner, a bit of TV, and catching up on emails. On weekends we hike the nearby trails, do a bit of yard work, and try to squeeze in a church service on Sundays. The cost of living has crept up a lot in the last few years, so we keep a close eye on our budget, especially with the kids getting older and the house needing a few upgrades.

## Politics and this election
I voted Democratic in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one really pushing for affordable health care and better funding for public schools, which mattered a lot with my kids in the system. I also liked the candidate’s stance on protecting the water rights that are so crucial out here in southern Utah. Since then, I’ve stayed fairly engaged – I watch the news, read a bit online, and talk politics at the kitchen table when the kids are doing their homework. I’m still convinced that the Democrats are more attentive to the everyday struggles of families like mine, even if I’m annoyed when they seem to forget about the rural perspective. That said, I’m also wary of the Republican side; some of the rhetoric feels out of touch with the reality of water shortages and the need for decent public services, but I do appreciate the emphasis they place on small‑business tax relief, which helps my husband’s landscaping gig. I’m voting in the 2026 midterms because I think my voice still matters, especially with the upcoming congressional race that could affect water policy and federal funding for schools. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the looming water scarcity that could hit our farms and gardens hard. I see the Democrats as more progressive on social safety nets, while the GOP feels like they’re trying to protect the status quo for businesses but often ignore the environmental challenges we face out here.

## Media Diet
I tune into NPR and the CBS News podcasts during my morning commute, then catch the evening briefs on ABC News and skim a couple of articles on Newsweek after dinner. Altogether I spend about six hours a week scrolling through their sites and listening to the audio feeds.
