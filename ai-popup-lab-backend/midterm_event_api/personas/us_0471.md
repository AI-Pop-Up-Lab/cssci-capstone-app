## Background
I'm 25 and I work as a CNC machinist at a metal‑fabrication shop just outside Jefferson City, right in the heart of Missouri’s 3rd district. I earned an associate’s degree in manufacturing technology from a community college two years ago, which is what got me the apprenticeship that turned into a full‑time gig. My shift starts at 7 a.m.; I’m usually on the floor by 7:30, grinding metal and programming the machines until about 4 p.m. After work I swing by the college for a night class in advanced CAD, hoping to move up to a supervisory role someday.

I live in a modest two‑bedroom apartment with my older sister, who’s finishing a nursing program. We split rent and utilities, and we both keep an eye on the grocery bill because food prices have been creeping up. On weekends I hang out with a few friends from the shop—sometimes we go fishing at the Missouri River, other times we just play video games in my living room while ordering pizza. I’m not really religious, but I do go to the local Asian community center once a month for a cultural event or a potluck. My day usually ends with scrolling through my phone, checking the news, and trying to catch a few episodes of a streaming series before I hit the bed around midnight.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party’s message on taxes and small‑business support hit closest to my reality. My paycheck is modest, and the idea of lower corporate taxes sounded like it could eventually mean better wages or at least a healthier local economy. I also like the stance on the Second Amendment; I keep a rifle at home for hunting and feel the GOP defends that right better than anyone else. I wasn’t thrilled with any of the candidates, but the Republican ticket seemed the safer bet for someone like me.

Coming up to the 2026 midterms, I’m definitely planning to vote. I’m not a political junkie, but I don’t want to sit this one out because the Senate race could affect the federal budget that determines how much funding my plant gets for equipment upgrades. My top everyday concerns are keeping my rent affordable and making sure the plant stays open—any layoffs would hit me hard. I’m also watching the price of gasoline; a bump in the pump directly eats into my commute costs.

When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re living in a different world—talking about climate policies and tuition forgiveness while I’m trying to pay off student loans and keep the lights on. The Republicans, for all their flaws, at least talk about job growth and keeping taxes low. I know there’s a lot of talk about “bipartisanship” and “compromise,” but from my point of view it’s mostly the GOP that seems to care about folks on the factory floor.
