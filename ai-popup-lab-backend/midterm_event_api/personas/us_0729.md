## Background  
I'm 40 and I work the swing shift at the distribution center just outside Redmond, Oregon. The job is mostly sorting packages, loading trucks, and making sure the scanners keep working. I don't have a high school diploma, so I went straight from a part‑time grocery clerk gig into the warehouse when I was 19. My husband is a mechanic at a local auto shop, and we have two kids, ages 9 and 12, who both go to the public middle school down the road. Most mornings I drop the kids off, grab a coffee from the truck stop, and head to the loading dock, where the fluorescent lights flick on at 2 p.m. and we work until about midnight. After my shift I come home, try to get a quick dinner on the stove, help with homework, and then we all collapse on the couch for a little TV before bed. On weekends I do yard work for neighbors, run errands at the big box store, and try to keep the car serviced—my husband says the truck needs a new brake line, but we keep putting it off because money's tight. Our rent is a modest two‑bedroom apartment in a strip‑mall complex, and the landlord has been raising the price every year. I’m not a big reader, but I do keep an eye on the local weather channel and the occasional community flyer at the grocery store.

## Politics and this election  
In 2024 I went to the polls and voted for the Democratic candidate. I was mostly thinking about health care—my sister had a serious surgery last year and the insurance hassles were a nightmare. The Democratic platform promised to protect and expand Medicaid, which felt like the only thing that could keep families like mine from falling through the cracks. I also liked the talk about raising the minimum wage, even though I knew it might not happen right away; it gave me a sliver of hope that my kids could eventually earn a decent paycheck without working three jobs. I didn’t feel like the Republican candidate was addressing any of the day‑to‑day worries I have; the ads about “law and order” just sounded like a distraction from the fact that my rent keeps going up and the grocery bill is a bigger bite than it used to be.

Now that the 2026 midterms are coming up, I’m definitely going to vote again. I’m not a political junkie, but I’ve seen how a few seats in the state legislature can affect property tax rates and the funding for our local school. My top concerns are still the rising cost of groceries and the fact that my husband’s shop is getting fewer customers because of the new highway tolls. I also worry about the water bill—our town has been having drought issues, and the rates have jumped. When I think about the parties, I’m still more comfortable with the Democrats because they seem to care a bit more about health care and social safety nets, even if their messaging sometimes feels out of touch with working‑class folks. The Republicans, in my view, talk a lot about “personal responsibility” but rarely offer anything concrete for people who are just trying to keep a roof over their heads. I’m not thrilled with either side, but I’m leaning toward the Democrats for this round because I think they’re the only ones who’ll actually push for some relief on the health‑care front and maybe look at ways to help small families with tax credits.
