## Background
I'm 50, and I live in Owasso, a suburb just north of Tulsa in Oklahoma's 2nd district. I work full‑time as an office manager for a regional insurance agency; the job keeps me on a pretty regular 9‑to‑5 schedule, answering phones, coordinating paperwork, and making sure the team hits its monthly targets. My husband runs a small landscaping business, and between us we have two kids—one 16‑year‑old in high school and a 14‑year‑old who just started varsity soccer. Most mornings I drop the younger one off at school, grab a coffee, and scroll through my phone while I wait for the bus to the office. Evenings are a mix of homework help, a quick dinner that usually ends up being tacos or stir‑fry, and then a few episodes of whatever's on Netflix before we all wind down. On weekends we try to fit in a family hike at the nearby prairie trail, a trip to the grocery store for bulk items, and sometimes a church service on Sunday morning. I keep a modest savings account, but with the kids' college fund and a mortgage on our house, money feels tight most months.

## Politics and this election
I didn't cast a ballot in 2024. Between long work hours, my husband's unpredictable schedule, and the kids' extracurriculars, I just never found a quiet moment to fill out an absentee card or make it to the polls. On top of that, I felt like the choices were the same old promises with no real impact on my daily grind—high grocery bills, rising property taxes, and the constant worry about healthcare costs for my aging parents back in California. That mix of time pressure and disillusionment made voting feel like a wasted effort, so I sat it out. Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms, I'm leaning toward sitting this one out as well, unless something dramatic hits my community—like a major school funding cut or a local scandal that directly affects our neighborhood. My top concerns right now are the price of groceries and the property tax hike we got hit with last year; both eat into the little extra we try to set aside for college savings. As for the parties, I find myself irritated by both. The Republicans in Oklahoma push for tax cuts that never seem to trickle down to middle‑class families, while the Democrats keep talking about big‑government solutions that feel out of touch with small‑town realities. Honestly, I just want policies that keep my kids' schools funded, keep my mortgage manageable, and stop the price of milk from climbing every few months. Until I see a candidate who actually addresses those everyday worries, I'm not motivated to step into the booth.
