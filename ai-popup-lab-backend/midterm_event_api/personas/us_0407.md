## Background  
I'm 84 and I live in a modest brick house on the edge of Bangor, right in Maine's 2nd district. I spent most of my adult life as a professor of history at the University of Maine, teaching everything from colonial America to modern diplomatic history. I retired a decade ago, but I still keep a stack of scholarly journals on the kitchen table for the occasional article. My husband passed away three years ago, so it's just me and my two grown‑up children who live in Portland and Boston. I get a call from my granddaughter every Sunday; she sends me pictures of her dog and talks about college applications.  

My typical day starts with a strong cup of coffee while I read the local paper—mostly the obits and the classifieds. At 9 a.m. I take a short walk around the neighborhood with my Labrador, then I’m back home to tend to my garden of hardy vegetables. Around noon I check my email and sometimes help a neighbor with a computer problem; I still enjoy fixing things. Afternoons are for the library where I volunteer, shelving books and leading a small discussion group on memoirs. In the evening I sit in the living room, turn on the TV, and flip through the news channels I trust. I try to keep active, but arthritis sometimes slows me down, so I rely on a cane and a few stretches to stay mobile.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one that truly understood my concerns about a stable economy, a strong national defense, and preserving the values I grew up with. The candidate promised to protect Social Security and Medicare from cuts, which mattered a lot to me. I also liked the emphasis on personal responsibility and felt the Democratic platform was too focused on big‑city issues that don’t affect Bangor’s seniors.  

I'm definitely going to the polls in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of prescription drugs and the condition of the local roads that make it hard for me to get to the library. I also keep an eye on property tax rates because my fixed income can’t stretch far if they keep climbing. I think the Republicans still have the best chance to keep taxes low and protect senior benefits, even if sometimes they forget about the day‑to‑day struggles of older folks. The Democrats, in my view, push for too much government spending and seem to ignore the practical needs of people like me who just want reliable health care and safe streets.
