## Background  
I'm 65 and I live in Bangor, right in the heart of Maine's 2nd congressional district. After getting my associate's degree in electrical technology at a community college, I spent most of my career as an industrial electrician for a paper mill that shut down a few years back. Nowadays I work part‑time for the local hospital doing preventive maintenance on their generators and HVAC systems. I’m married to Rosa, and we’ve been together for 38 years. Our two kids are grown, one lives in Boston and the other in Portland, so the house is mostly quiet.  

My days start early; I’m out the door by 5:30 am, grab a coffee from the corner diner, and head to the hospital. The shift is usually a mix of fixing broken breakers and checking the backup power for the new wing. After work I swing by the hardware store to pick up supplies, then either help Rosa in the garden or sit on the porch watching the traffic on Main Street. On Tuesdays and Thursdays we go to church, and on Saturdays I meet a few old buddies at the local VFW hall for a beer and some card games. I keep a modest budget, trying to stretch my fixed retirement income while covering the mortgage and the rising cost of my prescription meds.

## Politics and this election  
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party was the only one talking about keeping taxes low and protecting the kind of jobs that used to exist in Maine’s manufacturing towns. I was especially worried about the federal push for higher energy regulations that could hurt the little factories still hanging on in the north. The candidate promised to keep the military bases funded and to push back against what I see as overreach from Washington, and that rang true for me after a decade of watching the Democrats promise more social programs but never delivering on the jobs front.  

Since then I’ve gotten a bit more skeptical of both sides. The Republicans haven’t been able to stop the rising price of insulin, and the Democrats keep talking about climate policies that feel like they’ll just raise my heating bills even more. Still, I think the GOP is the lesser of two evils when it comes to my wallet and my community’s way of life. I’m voting in the 2026 midterms – I’m not planning to sit this one out. My top everyday concerns are keeping my prescription costs down and making sure my property taxes don’t balloon as the town tries to fund new projects. I see the Democrats as too focused on big‑city issues and identity politics, while the Republicans, even if they’re not perfect, at least talk about fiscal responsibility and protecting the kind of work I used to do.
