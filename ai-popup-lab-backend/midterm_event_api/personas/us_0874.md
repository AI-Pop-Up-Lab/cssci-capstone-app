## Background
I'm 35 and work as a logistics coordinator for a regional distribution center just outside Heber City, in Utah's 3rd congressional district. I grew up on the Uintah Reservation, went to a state university and earned a bachelor's in business. After college I moved to the Provo‑Heber area for a steady paycheck and now live in a modest two‑bedroom house with my wife and our nine‑month‑old son. My wife does part‑time work at the local health clinic, and we split the chores—she handles most of the cooking while I take care of the morning school run and the occasional grocery haul.

My day starts around 5:30 am; I grab a coffee, check the weather, and head to the warehouse where I track inbound shipments and schedule drivers. The job is mostly sitting at a computer, but I have to be on my feet a lot when the trucks arrive. After a 9‑to‑5 shift I usually swing by the grocery store, pick up a few things for dinner, and try to get home before the kids' bedtime. Weekends are a mix of taking the boy to the park, helping my parents with yard work on the reservation, and catching a baseball game at the local field when I can. I’m not a big traveler; most of my free time is spent on the couch watching sports or scrolling through news on my phone.

## Politics and this election
I voted Republican in 2024 because I felt the party's stance on energy development and lower taxes aligned with my need for a stable paycheck and affordable living. The candidate promised to keep federal land policies that protect the jobs at the distribution center and to push back against what I see as over‑regulation that hurts small businesses like the one my wife’s clinic runs. I also appreciated the talk about “border security,” even though I don’t deal with immigration directly; it just felt like a clear, strong position.

I'm definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My top concerns right now are the rising cost of groceries and the looming property‑tax hike the county is debating. I’m also watching water rights talks, because any restrictions could affect our irrigation and the local farms that feed the community. I still think the Republican Party is the better bet for keeping taxes low and protecting jobs, even if some of the rhetoric feels a bit over the top. The Democrats, in my view, push too much climate‑driven regulation that could hurt the very industries that keep our town afloat, and they seem more focused on identity politics than on practical solutions for everyday folks like me.
