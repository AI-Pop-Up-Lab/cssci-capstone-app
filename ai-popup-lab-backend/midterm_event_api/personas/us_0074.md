## Background
I'm 30 and work as a construction laborer for a subcontractor that does a lot of the big‑site work around the Phoenix metro area. I live in Peoria, just outside the Loop 202 belt, in a modest two‑bedroom house I bought with my wife a few years ago. We have a three‑year‑old son who’s already in preschool, so most mornings I’m up by 5:30 a.m. to get a quick coffee, pack a lunch, and drive the half‑hour to the job site. The work is hard, but the pay is steady, and I like the feeling of actually building something you can see.

After the shift I usually swing by the local grocery store, grab a few things for dinner, and try to be home before the kids’ bedtime. Weekends are a mix of catching a game on TV, helping my son with his coloring books, and helping my wife with yard work or the occasional home repair. My parents live on the reservation about an hour north, so I drive down to see them every other month and bring them a few groceries. I don't have a college degree, so most of my knowledge comes from on‑the‑job training, the guys at the crew, and the news I watch online.

## Politics and this election
In 2024 I voted Republican because I felt the party was the only one talking about keeping gas prices low and making it easier for folks like me to keep a steady job without a bunch of new regulations. I also liked the talk about stronger border security, which I thought would help keep the community safer. Since then, I’ve seen a few things that made me uneasy—some of the rhetoric getting uglier, and a few promises that haven’t shown up in my paycheck—but I still think the GOP is the better bet for my family’s bottom line.

I’m definitely voting in the 2026 midterms. My biggest worries right now are the rising cost of gasoline and the fact that my crew’s overtime has been cut because the contractor says business is slowing. I also keep an eye on health‑care premiums because my wife’s job doesn’t cover the whole family. When I think about the parties, the Democrats feel like they’re always trying to raise taxes and push big‑government solutions that never reach the people doing the actual work. The Republicans, even when they argue a lot on TV, at least seem to care about keeping jobs and not over‑regulating us. That’s not to say I’m blind to their flaws, but in my day‑to‑day life the GOP’s priorities line up more with what I need.
